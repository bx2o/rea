# SECTION 4: 50+ FAQs & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: RECRUITMENT FUNDAMENTALS (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What should be included in a job description?**
A: Components: (1) **Job title:** Clear title. (2) **Reporting:** Who does person report to? (3) **Role overview:** What does person do? (2-3 sentences). (4) **Responsibilities:** 5-8 key responsibilities. (5) **Qualifications:** Required (must have) vs. Preferred (nice to have). (6) **Skills:** Technical + soft skills. (7) **Experience:** Years/type needed. (8) **Compensation:** Salary range. (9) **Benefits:** What benefits come with job. (10) **Company info:** Brief company overview. Length: 1-2 pages. Tone: Exciting (why someone would want job), honest (realistic). Avoid: Gender-specific language, vague requirements.

**Q2: How do you find good candidates?**
A: Sources: (1) **Job boards:** LinkedIn, Indeed, company website. (2) **Referrals:** Ask employees to refer friends. (3) **Recruiting firms:** For hard-to-fill roles. (4) **Direct outreach:** LinkedIn, industry events, universities. (5) **Passive sourcing:** Reach out to people not actively looking. (6) **Events/conferences:** Recruiting booths. (7) **Social media:** Source from Twitter, GitHub, etc. (8) **Universities:** Partnerships for entry-level talent. Multi-channel: Combine multiple sources (job board gets basic candidates; referrals get better ones). Passive sourcing: Reaches top performers (not actively looking).

**Q3: How do you screen resumes effectively?**
A: Process: (1) **Resume review:** Does resume show relevant experience? (2) **Keywords:** Does it have keywords from job description? (3) **Experience level:** How many years relevant experience? (4) **Red flags:** Frequent job changes? Large gaps? (5) **Phone screen:** Call top candidates (15-30 min). Ask about background. (6) **Top candidates:** Advance to interviews. Screening filters: (1) Do they meet minimum qualifications? (2) Is experience relevant? (3) Any red flags? Automation: Resume screening tools can pre-screen, but human review catches nuance.

**Q4: What makes a great interview process?**
A: Process: (1) **Structured:** Same questions for all candidates (fair, comparable). (2) **Behavioral:** Ask about past behavior (predicts future). "Tell about time you..." vs. "How would you..." (3) **Multiple interviews:** Phone screen → interview 1 (hiring manager) → interview 2 (team member) → final (senior leader). (4) **Diverse panel:** Different people's perspective (different strengths/weakness). (5) **Skills test:** For technical roles, coding test or problem-solving. (6) **Reference check:** Talk to past managers (verify story). (7) **Feedback:** Interviewers share feedback immediately. (8) **Candidate experience:** Good experience (even if don't hire, person impressed with company). Timing: 2-3 weeks total (not dragging out).

**Q5: What's behavioral interviewing and why does it work?**
A: Behavioral interview: Ask about past behavior (predicts future behavior). Question format: "Tell about time when you..." Questions: (1) "Tell about time you solved complex problem" (problem-solving). (2) "Tell about conflict with coworker" (teamwork). (3) "Tell about mistake" (learning, ownership). (4) "Tell about success you're proud of" (motivation). Structure: Listen to story; follow-up questions; assess against competency. Why works: People repeat patterns (past behavior predicts future). More predictive than hypothetical ("If I asked...").

**Q6: How do you assess culture fit?**
A: Assessment: (1) **Values alignment:** Do candidate's values match company values? (ask about values). (2) **Communication style:** Does person communicate like company? (3) **Work style:** Company fast-paced? Candidate fast-paced? (4) **Collaboration:** Company collaborative? Candidate collaborative? (5) **Growth mindset:** Company emphasizes learning? Candidate learner? (6) **Belief in mission:** Does candidate care about what company does? (7) **Team fit:** Will candidate work well with team? (team interviews candidate). Assessment: Interviews + team feedback. Importance: Culture fit affects happiness and retention.

**Q7: What's the difference between a phone screen and interview?**
A: **Phone screen:** 15-30 minute call. Purpose: Initial assessment (is person minimally qualified?). Judge: Basic qualifications, communication, interest. Experience: Casual, conversational. **Interview:** 45-60 minutes, in-person/video. Purpose: Deep assessment (is this the right person?). Judge: Competency, cultural fit, past experiences. Experience: Structured, multiple people, assessment. Timing: Phone screen → yes/no to interview. Interview → yes/no to offer. Phone is filter; interview is deep dive.

**Q8: How do you close an offer (get acceptance)?**
A: Process: (1) **Decision:** Make offer decision (yes, hire). (2) **Present:** Call candidate; present offer (verbal first). (3) **Excitement:** Convey enthusiasm (we want you!). (4) **Details:** Cover salary, start date, benefits. (5) **Questions:** Address any concerns. (6) **Timeline:** Give deadline for acceptance (typically 48 hours). (7) **Follow-up:** Send written offer. (8) **Negotiation:** Be ready to negotiate (salary, start date, flexibility). (9) **Acceptance:** Get acceptance (email confirmation). (10) **Onboarding:** Start onboarding process. Close rate: >90% is good (means great candidates, great experience).

**Q9: How do you build and maintain referral program?**
A: Program: (1) **Incentive:** What reward for referral? ($500, $2K, paid trip?). (2) **Process:** Simple (employee refers, you hire, they get reward). (3) **Announcement:** Communicate program (email, all-hands, posters). (4) **Easy:** Make referring easy (form, email). (5) **Transparency:** Tell employee when their referral hired. (6) **Delight:** Thank them (personalized note, public recognition). (7) **Clarity:** Clear what qualifies for reward (hired and still employed 90 days). Benefits: (1) Referrals higher quality (employee vouches for person). (2) Faster hire (already vetted). (3) Lower cost (vs. recruiter). (4) Better culture (more selection of people recommend). Target: 40%+ of hires from referral.

**Q10: How do you assess technical skills?**
A: Assessment: (1) **Resume:** Does background show relevant skills? (2) **Technical screen:** For engineers, coding test (problem-solving, code quality). (3) **Discussion:** Walk through technical project (what decision did you make?). (4) **Skill test:** Build something (take-home project, 4-6 hours). (5) **Reference:** Call past technical manager (can you confirm skills?). (6) **Trial:** For some roles, paid trial (2-week project; see if works). Assessment tools: (1) **LeetCode** (coding problems). (2) **HackerRank** (assessment platform). (3) **Portfolio:** GitHub, past projects. For soft skills: (1) Interviews. (2) References. (3) Team interaction.

**Q11: How do you build employment brand?**
A: Brand: (1) **Culture:** Help candidates understand culture. (2) **Values:** Communicate company values. (3) **Employees:** Most powerful brand (happy employees = best ads). (4) **Content:** Blog, LinkedIn posts about company. (5) **Social media:** Share culture (photos, employee stories). (6) **Events:** Recruiting events, open houses. (7) **Partnerships:** University recruiting, community. (8) **Awards:** Seek "best place to work" awards. (9) **Glassdoor:** Monitor reviews; address negative. (10) **Compensation:** Be known as good payer. Importance: Strong brand → more referrals → faster hiring → better quality → lower cost.

**Q12: How do you improve diversity in hiring?**
A: Approach: (1) **Definition:** What diversity do we target? (gender, ethnicity, background, perspective). (2) **Goal:** Specific goals (40% women, 25% underrepresented minorities, etc.). (3) **Sourcing:** Recruit from diverse sources (HBCU universities, diversity job boards, community groups). (4) **Outreach:** Inclusive job posting language (avoid masculine language, etc.). (5) **Screening:** Remove bias (blind resume review, structured interviews). (6) **Diverse panel:** Interview panel reflects diversity. (7) **Bias training:** Train interviewers on unconscious bias. (8) **Tracking:** Monitor diversity in hires; track to goal. (9) **Inclusion:** Beyond hiring, create inclusive culture (people stay). Goal: Pipeline representative → hires representative of population.

**Q13: What's a recruiting metric and why track it?**
A: Key metrics: (1) **Time-to-hire:** Days from job posting to offer acceptance. Target: 30 days. (2) **Offer acceptance rate:** % of offers accepted. Target: 90%+. (3) **Cost-per-hire:** Total recruiting cost / hires. Target: $5K. (4) **Quality:** % new hire performing well after 1 year. Target: >80%. (5) **Retention:** % new hire still employed after 1 year. Target: >90%. (6) **Diversity:** % women, % underrepresented minorities hired. Target: Representative of population. (7) **Referral rate:** % hired from referrals. Target: 40%+. (8) **eNPS:** Would employee recommend company? Target: >50. Dashboard: Monthly tracking.

**Q14: What are common recruiting mistakes?**
A: Mistakes: (1) **Bad job description:** Vague description → not attracting right people. (2) **Slow process:** 3-month process → candidates take other jobs. (3) **No outreach:** Only job posting → limited candidate pool. (4) **Biased interviews:** Hiring people like us (lack diversity). (5) **Wrong interview questions:** Asking trivial questions; not assessing competency. (6) **No coordination:** Hiring manager not involved → wrong person hired. (7) **Lowball offer:** Trying to negotiate too hard → candidate walks. (8) **Poor candidate experience:** Ghosting, slow feedback → bad referrals. (9) **No referral program:** Not leveraging employees. (10) **No metrics:** Don't know if things improving. Best practice: Clear job description; fast process; multiple sources; fair interviews; good candidate experience.

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: RECRUITING STRATEGY (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: How do you develop an effective recruiting strategy?**
A: Framework: (1) **Understand needs:** How many people? Which roles? Urgency? Timeline? (2) **Assess market:** For each role, how hard is it to hire? How many candidates available? (3) **Source analysis:** Where do good candidates come from? (LinkedIn, referrals, universities, agencies?). (4) **Channel mix:** Allocate budget/effort to best channels (don't waste on low-ROI channels). (5) **Employer brand:** Why would candidates want to work here? (compensation, culture, growth, mission?). (6) **Process:** Design hiring process (speed, quality). (7) **Metrics:** How will we measure success? (time-to-fill, quality, cost per hire, diversity). Update: Quarterly based on results.

**Q2: What's the difference between active and passive candidate sourcing?**
A: **Active candidates:** Actively looking for job. Using job boards, applying to positions. Usually easier to reach; larger pool. Often less experienced (experienced candidates not actively looking). **Passive candidates:** Not actively looking (happy in current role) but open to right opportunity. Harder to reach; smaller pool. Often more experienced/qualified. Both needed: Active = fill roles quickly. Passive = access better talent. Strategy: Active for easy-to-fill roles. Passive for hard-to-fill roles.

**Q3: How do you reduce time-to-fill?**
A: Tactics: (1) **Plan ahead:** Don't wait for role to open; start sourcing before. (2) **Strong sourcing:** Active sourcing (reach out to people) + job boards. (3) **Fast process:** Goal is 25-30 day hiring cycle (slow processes lose candidates). (4) **Hiring manager alignment:** Manager knows requirements, available for interviews. (5) **Efficient interviews:** Structured interviews, consistent panel, quick feedback. (6) **Quick offers:** Once decided, make offer quickly (don't delay). (7) **Negotiation:** Be ready to negotiate (salary, start date) quickly. (8) **Technology:** Use ATS to manage pipeline, automate communication. Typical: 30-45 days. For urgent roles: 20-25 days possible with focus.

**Q4: How do you build and maintain a talent pipeline?**
A: Pipeline: Database of candidates at various interest levels. Development: (1) **Source continuously:** Don't wait for role to open; always sourcing (build backlog of candidates). (2) **Segment candidates:** Cold (haven't heard of company), warm (know some), hot (actively interested). (3) **Nurture:** For warm candidates, keep in touch (email, updates, events, offers to chat). (4) **Database:** CRM or ATS to track candidates. (5) **Reactivation:** When role opens, reach out to pipeline candidates first (faster than starting from scratch). (6) **Feedback loop:** If candidate declines, keep in file (might be interested in future). Value: With strong pipeline, can fill common roles in 2-3 weeks vs. 8-12 weeks.

**Q5: How much should recruiting budget be?**
A: Rule of thumbs: (1) **As % of payroll:** 1-2% of annual payroll (larger companies lower %; startups higher). Example: $10M payroll → $100-200K recruiting budget. (2) **Per hire:** $3-10K per hire all-in (includes recruiting ads, agencies, internal time, tools, events). (3) **For fast growth:** If hiring 50+ people, might be 2-3% of payroll. (4) **Components:** Job boards ($500-2K/month), LinkedIn recruiter ($8K/month), agencies (20-30% of salary), internal team ($100-200K/year), recruiting events/community ($50-100K/year). Allocation: Varies by strategy; optimize based on ROI (which channels produce best hires?).

**Q6: What's a good hiring process timeline?**
A: Ideal timeline: **25-30 days total.** Breakdown: (1) **Sourcing (1-2 weeks):** Find qualified candidates. (2) **Screen (1 week):** Phone/initial screen (knock out unqualified). (3) **Interviews (1-2 weeks):** 3-4 rounds of interviews depending on role. (4) **Decision (1-2 days):** Committee decides. (5) **Offer (1-2 days):** Make offer, candidate accepts. (6) **Setup (3-5 days):** Background check, paperwork, start date prep. For competitive talent: Faster is better (candidates often juggle multiple offers). For less competitive roles: Can take 45-60 days without losing candidates.

**Q7: How do you improve offer acceptance rates?**
A: Tactics: (1) **Sell throughout process:** Hiring is sales; you're "selling" the opportunity. (2) **Culture fit:** Candidate should feel excited about team/mission. (3) **Competitive offer:** Pay within market (survey market to know competitive rate). (4) **Flexibility:** Be willing to negotiate (salary, start date, WFH, schedule). (5) **Executive involvement:** For senior roles, CEO/leader should talk to candidate (shows seriousness). (6) **Quick close:** Once you want candidate, make offer quickly (they might have other offers). (7) **Address concerns:** If candidate hesitates, understand why and address (schedule flexibility? more mentorship?). Target: 90%+ acceptance rate.

**Q8: How do you assess recruiting channel ROI?**
A: Metrics: (1) **Cost per source:** For each channel, what's cost to hire one person? (Job boards = cost of posting + time. Referrals = referral bonus. Agencies = 20-30% of first year salary). (2) **Quality of hire:** Do hires from this channel succeed? (tenure, performance ratings, promotion rate). (3) **Speed:** How long to fill from this channel? (fast channels = priority). (4) **Volume:** How many hires per channel? (referrals often high quality but low volume). (5) **Diversity:** Does channel produce diverse candidates? (important for D&I goals). Analysis: Track every hire (source, cost, quality, tenure). Quarterly review: Double down on high-ROI channels; reduce low-ROI.

**Q9: How do you structure a recruiting team?**
A: Structure depends on hiring volume and complexity: (1) **Small (0-30 people):** 1 part-time recruiter or founder recruiting. (2) **Growth (30-100):** 1-2 full-time recruiters (one sourcing, one managing offers/onboarding). (3) **Scale (100-500):** 5-10 person team (sourcing specialists, coordinators, agencies). (4) **Enterprise (500+):** 20+ person team (sourcing, coordination, RPO partnerships, specialized recruiters by function). Hiring managers: Should spend 20-30% of time on recruiting (interview candidates).

**Q10: What's an attractive job description that attracts candidates?**
A: Elements: (1) **Clear purpose:** What problem does role solve? Why does it matter? (2) **Key responsibilities:** What does person do day-to-day? (3) **Qualifications:** What skills/experience required? What's nice-to-have? (4) **Compensation/benefits:** Salary range (showing range = more inclusive), benefits, equity. (5) **Culture/team:** Who's the team? What's culture? (6) **Growth:** What will person learn? Growth opportunities? (7) **Details:** Remote? Travel? Schedule? (8) **Application:** How easy to apply? Link? Avoid: Too long (keep under 1 page). Technical jargon (unclear language). Overly strict requirements (discourages good candidates).

**Q11: How do you source passive candidates?**
A: Tactics: (1) **LinkedIn:** Search for candidates with relevant skills. Use InMail or LinkedIn Recruiter to reach out with personalized message. (2) **Message:** Personalized (not generic), explain why you're reaching out, mention something specific about them. (3) **Network:** Ask current employees for referrals (great source). Ask candidates you've interviewed before. Attend industry events. (4) **Cold outreach:** If you identify great person at competitor, reach out (brief chat, explore fit). (5) **Recruitment agencies:** Agency contacts candidates on your behalf (you pay fee if hire). (6) **University alumni:** Tap into alumni networks. Success rate: 5-10% of passive outreach converts to candidate (expect lots of no responses).

**Q12: How do you build employer brand?**
A: Strategy: (1) **Company story:** Why does company exist? What's impact? What's culture? (2) **Employee stories:** Highlight employee careers, growth, achievements. (3) **Content:** Blog posts, videos, podcasts about company and industry. (4) **Events:** Attend/host recruiting events, industry conferences. (5) **Social media:** Share company updates, achievements, culture. (6) **Glassdoor/LinkedIn:** Encourage employees to review company (good reviews help recruiting). (7) **Partnerships:** Partner with schools, communities, networks to build brand. (8) **Compensation:** Competitive pay and benefits signal you value people. Strong brand = candidates want to work for you = easier recruiting.

**Q13: What's the role of recruiting in company culture?**
A: Connection: (1) **Hiring shapes culture:** People you hire define culture. Hire people aligned with values, culture is strong. (2) **Culture amplifies brand:** Strong culture = employees become brand ambassadors (recruit friends). (3) **Retention:** Good hiring decisions = people stay longer (better culture fit). (4) **Scaling:** As you grow, hiring is how you maintain culture (new people need to fit). (5) **Feedback loop:** Leave feedback from departing employees (improve culture). Strategy: Recruiting and culture are connected; invest in both.

**Q14: What recruiting metrics should you track?**
A: Key metrics: (1) **Time-to-fill:** Days from job open to hire start (industry standard 30-45 days). (2) **Cost per hire:** All recruiting costs / # hires. (3) **Offer acceptance rate:** # offers accepted / # offers made. (4) **Quality of hire:** Performance rating at 12 months (4.0+ ideally). (5) **Hiring manager satisfaction:** Are managers happy with hires? (6) **Channel analysis:** Where do best hires come from? Cost per hire by channel. (7) **Diversity metrics:** % women, minorities, underrepresented groups in hires. (8) **Candidate feedback:** NPS (net promoter score) on recruiting experience. Monthly tracking. Quarterly review with leadership.

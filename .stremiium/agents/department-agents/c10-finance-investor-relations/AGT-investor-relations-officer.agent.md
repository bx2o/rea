# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Key IR Questions (Sample FAQs)

**Q1: What should be in quarterly investor updates?**
A: Key sections: (1) **Headline metrics:** Revenue, growth rate, key operational metrics. (2) **Progress:** What did you accomplish? Are you hitting targets? (3) **Challenges:** What's not going as planned? What are you doing about it? (4) **Outlook:** What's next? What are you planning? (5) **Financials:** P&L snapshot, cash position, runway. (6) **Team:** Any hiring/departures? (7) **Capital plan:** Any fundraising, secondary sales? Format: 3-5 pages, clear visuals. Send quarterly on consistent schedule (e.g., 15 days post-quarter end). Tone: Honest, forward-looking, transparent.

**Q2: When should you take on more investors vs. keep investor base tight?**
A: Tradeoff: More investors = more diverse input + relationships, but more management overhead. Tight = easier to manage, but less perspective. Recommendation: (1) **Early stage:** Tight investor base (5-10) so you can focus on product. (2) **Growth stage:** Larger base (20-50+) for more perspectives + relationships. (3) **Mature:** Large base (100+) for secondary market + network. Quality matters more than quantity; prefer strong investors who add value beyond capital.

**Q3: How often should you communicate with investors?**
A: Minimum: **Quarterly updates** (formally). Ideal: **Monthly updates** (email brief) + **annual meeting** (deep dive). Between: **Quarterly calls** (Q&A). Special occasions: **Major news** (quick update), **fundraising** (frequent updates), **crises** (immediate). Rule: Predictable > sporadic. Investors prefer regular, expected communication to silence followed by surprises. Calendar: Build 12-month communication calendar at start of year.

**Q4: What's in a cap table and why does it matter?**
A: Cap table = capitalization table showing all shareholders + ownership percentages. Includes: (1) Founders (common stock). (2) Investors (preferred stock). (3) Employee options (option pool). (4) Convertible debt (if any). Why? Determines: (1) Who owns what % of company? (2) Who has control rights? (3) What happens in exit (waterfall of proceeds)? (4) Dilution (each round dilutes previous investors). Manage: Carefully track options, conversions, secondary sales. Update: Maintain live spreadsheet.

**Q5: How do you prepare for Board meetings?**
A: Preparation: (1) **Materials:** Board deck (metrics, progress, financials, strategy). Send 48 hours before meeting. (2) **Agenda:** 120 minutes typical (30 min business, 30 min discussion, 30 min decision-making, 30 min closed session). (3) **Pre-reads:** Finish materials before meeting. (4) **Strategy:** What decisions do you need from Board? Get alignment with lead director. (5) **Follow-up:** Document decisions, action items, who owns what. (6) **Between meetings:** Monthly updates to Board.

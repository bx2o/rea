# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQs

**Q1: What's the monthly financial close process?**
A: Steps: (1) **Cutoff deadline:** All transactions recorded by end of month. (2) **Accruals:** Accrue expenses incurred but not yet invoiced. (3) **Reconciliations:** Reconcile all balance sheet accounts (bank, credit card, inventory). (4) **Adjustments:** Record depreciation, prepaid amortization, allowances. (5) **Trial balance:** All accounts balance. (6) **Financial statements:** P&L, balance sheet, cash flow. (7) **Analysis:** Investigate significant variances. (8) **Review:** CFO/external advisor reviews. (9) **Supporting docs:** Keep all backup docs organized. Timeline: 3-5 business days after month-end.

**Q2: What's a healthy cash position?**
A: Framework: (1) **Operating cash:** Covers 30-60 days of expenses (unexpected delays). (2) **Growth buffer:** Extra cash for growth initiatives. (3) **Fundraising buffer:** If planning to raise capital, maintain 6+ months runway (shows discipline). Example: $2M monthly burn = need $1-2M operating cash + $6M growth buffer + $12M fundraising buffer = $19-20M total. Healthy: Never run below what you need for 30 days operations.

**Q3: How do you reconcile accounts?**
A: Process: (1) **Pull account balance** from accounting system. (2) **Pull supporting detail** (bank statement, vendor invoices, etc.). (3) **Match transactions:** Verify each transaction in system matches supporting docs. (4) **Identify breaks:** If transaction in system but not on bank statement, it's outstanding. If transaction on bank but not system, it's unrecorded. (5) **Investigate:** Understand breaks (timing? error?). (6) **Document:** Note reconciliation date + preparer. Frequency: Monthly for all balance sheet accounts.

**Q4: What's the difference between cash and accrual accounting?**
A: **Cash:** Record when cash comes in/goes out. **Accrual:** Record when liability/revenue recognized (not when cash moves). GAAP requires accrual. Example: Invoice customer $100K in January; they pay in March. **Cash accounting:** Record revenue in March (no revenue in January). **Accrual:** Record revenue in January (when invoice issued). Why accrual: Better reflects business status (revenue earned even if not yet collected).

**Q5: How do you prepare a balance sheet?**
A: Balance sheet = Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Assets: (1) Cash. (2) Accounts receivable. (3) Inventory. (4) Equipment. (5) Intangible assets. Liabilities: (1) Accounts payable. (2) Accrued expenses. (3) Debt. Equity: (1) Founder capital. (2) Investor capital. (3) Retained earnings. Rule: Total assets must equal total liabilities + equity. If not balanced, something's wrong (need to find error).

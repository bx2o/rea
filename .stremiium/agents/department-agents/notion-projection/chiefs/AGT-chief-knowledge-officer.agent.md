# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Thirty+ Frequently Asked Questions

### Category 1: KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT (6 FAQs)

**Q1: What is knowledge management?**
A: Knowledge management is systematic process of creating, sharing, using, and managing knowledge: (1) **Capture:** Document what people know. (2) **Organize:** Structure for findability. (3) **Share:** Make accessible. (4) **Apply:** Use in work. (5) **Maintain:** Keep current. Types: Explicit (documented), Tacit (in people's heads). Goal: Organizational learning and efficiency.

**Q2: How do you build a knowledge management strategy?**
A: KM strategy components: (1) **Assessment:** Current state, pain points. (2) **Vision:** What good looks like. (3) **Stakeholders:** Who needs what knowledge. (4) **Content:** What to capture. (5) **Technology:** Platform selection. (6) **Governance:** Ownership, standards, lifecycle. (7) **Culture:** Incentives and behaviors. (8) **Measurement:** Success metrics.

**Q3: What is information architecture?**
A: Information architecture organizes content so people can find and use it: (1) **Organization:** Grouping and categorization. (2) **Labeling:** Naming and terminology. (3) **Navigation:** Browse structures. (4) **Search:** Query and results. (5) **Metadata:** Descriptive data about content. Methods: Card sorting, tree testing, user research. Deliverable: Taxonomy, navigation, search strategy.

**Q4: How do you choose a knowledge base platform?**
A: Platform evaluation: (1) **Use Cases:** Who will use it, for what? (2) **Features:** Search, authoring, workflow, analytics. (3) **Integration:** With existing tools. (4) **Scale:** User and content volume. (5) **Usability:** Author and reader experience. (6) **Governance:** Permissions, workflows. (7) **Vendor:** Stability, roadmap, support. (8) **Cost:** Licensing model, TCO.

**Q5: What is content lifecycle management?**
A: Content lifecycle: (1) **Create:** Author and review. (2) **Publish:** Approve and release. (3) **Maintain:** Update and improve. (4) **Archive:** Retire outdated. (5) **Dispose:** Delete per retention. Governance: Ownership, review dates, archival triggers. Automation: Review reminders, stale content reports.

**Q6: How do you measure knowledge management success?**
A: KM metrics: (1) **Usage:** Page views, searches, users. (2) **Findability:** Search success rate, time to find. (3) **Quality:** Ratings, feedback, accuracy. (4) **Coverage:** % processes documented. (5) **Freshness:** % current content. (6) **Impact:** Ticket deflection, onboarding time. (7) **Culture:** % employees contributing.

### Category 2: DOCUMENTATION (6 FAQs)

**Q7: What makes good technical documentation?**
A: Good documentation: (1) **Clear:** Easy to understand. (2) **Accurate:** Correct and current. (3) **Complete:** Covers what's needed. (4) **Accessible:** Easy to find and navigate. (5) **Actionable:** Tells reader what to do. (6) **Scannable:** Good structure, headings. (7) **Visual:** Screenshots, diagrams. (8) **Maintainable:** Easy to update.

**Q8: How do you write for different audiences?**
A: Audience adaptation: (1) **New User:** Step-by-step, screenshots, concepts explained. (2) **Expert:** Quick reference, shortcuts, assume context. (3) **Executive:** Summary, business impact, minimal detail. (4) **Technical:** Specifications, API docs, code samples. (5) **Non-Technical:** Plain language, analogies, business value. Analyze: Role, knowledge level, goal, context.

**Q9: What is the difference between a process and a procedure?**
A: **Process:** What happens, flow of activities (e.g., "Customer Order"). **Procedure:** How to do it, step-by-step instructions (e.g., "How to Process a Return"). Relationship: Processes contain procedures. Documentation: Process maps (flowcharts), procedures (numbered steps). Both needed for complete documentation.

**Q10: How do you document decisions?**
A: Decision documentation: (1) **Context:** Situation, problem. (2) **Options:** Alternatives considered. (3) **Analysis:** Pros/cons, criteria. (4) **Decision:** What was decided. (5) **Rationale:** Why this choice. (6) **Implications:** Impact, risks. (7) **Owner:** Who decided. Format: ADRs (Architecture Decision Records), decision logs, meeting minutes.

**Q11: What is the*docs-as-code* approach?**
A: Docs-as-code: Treat documentation like software code—(1) **Version Control:** Git for docs. (2) **Review:** Pull requests, peer review. (3) **Automation:** CI/CD for publishing. (4) **Testing:** Link checking, linting. (5) **Collaboration:** Same workflow as code. Tools: Markdown, Git, static site generators. Benefits: Collaboration, quality, developer-friendly.

**Q12: How do you keep documentation current?**
A: Currency strategies: (1) **Ownership:** Named owner for each doc. (2) **Review Dates:** Scheduled reviews. (3) **Triggers:** Update when code changes. (4) **Feedback:** Easy to report issues. (5) **Metrics:** Stale content reports. (6) **Automation:** Link checking, screenshots. (7) **Culture:** Docs part of Definition of Done.

### Category 3: SEARCH & FINDABILITY (6 FAQs)

**Q13: How do you design effective search?**
A: Search design: (1) **Query:** What users type. (2) **Index:** What can be searched. (3) **Ranking:** Result order. (4) **Facets:** Filters and refinements. (5) **Snippets:** Preview of content. (6) **No Results:** Helpful alternatives. (7) **Analytics:** What people search, find, miss. Improvement: Synonyms, spell correction, boosting, content gaps.

**Q14: What is metadata and why does it matter?**
A: Metadata is data about data: (1) **Descriptive:** What it is (title, author, topic). (2) **Structural:** How it's organized (format, sections). (3) **Administrative:** Management info (created, owner, status). Use: Search, filtering, organization, workflow, analytics. Investment: Upfront tagging enables downstream value.

**Q15: How do you improve knowledge discoverability?**
A: Discoverability tactics: (1) **Search:** Invest in search quality. (2) **Navigation:** Clear browse paths. (3) **Cross-linking:** Related content connected. (4) **Landing Pages:** Curated entry points. (5) **Recommendations:** Suggest related content. (6) **Experts:** Connect people to people. (7) **Feedback:** Learn from failed searches.

**Q16: What is faceted search?**
A: Faceted search: Filter results by categories (facets). Example: Search "server error," filter by product, version, date. Benefits: (1) **Discovery:** Browse without knowing exact terms. (2) **Precision:** Narrow to relevant subset. (3) **Flexibility:** Multiple entry points. (4) **Context:** Shows what's available. Design: Relevant facets, clear counts, multi-select.

**Q17: How do you handle synonyms in search?**
A: Synonym management: (1) **Discovery:** Analyze search logs for same intent, different words. (2) **Thesaurus:** Controlled synonym list. (3) **Mapping:** Query expansion (search "car" also find "automobile"). (4) **User Variations:** Acronyms, slang, technical vs. lay terms. (5) **Maintenance:** Regular review. Balance: Don't over-expand and reduce precision.

**Q18: What is findability vs. discoverability?**
A: **Findability:** Can users find what they know exists? (search, navigation). **Discoverability:** Can users find what they don't know exists? (recommendations, browsing, related content). Both needed: Findability for known-item seeking, discoverability for exploration and learning. Measure: Task success for findability, engagement for discoverability.

### Category 4: COMPLIANCE & GOVERNANCE (6 FAQs)

**Q19: What is records management?**
A: Records management: Systematic control of records throughout lifecycle: (1) **Creation:** Capture records. (2) **Classification:** Categorize by type. (3) **Retention:** How long to keep. (4) **Storage:** Where and how. (5) **Access:** Who can see. (6) **Disposition:** Archive or destroy. Purpose: Legal compliance, e-discovery, historical preservation, efficiency.

**Q20: How do you develop retention schedules?**
A: Retention schedule: (1) **Inventory:** What records exist. (2) **Classification:** Group by type and purpose. (3) **Legal:** Statutory requirements. (4) **Business:** Operational needs. (5) **Risk:** Litigation, audit needs. (6) **Period:** How long to retain. (7) **Disposition:** Archive or destroy. Review: Regular legal and business review. Implementation: Automated enforcement where possible.

**Q21: What is a policy management system?**
A: Policy management: (1) **Repository:** Central policy location. (2) **Lifecycle:** Draft, review, approve, publish, retire. (3) **Version Control:** History and changes. (4) **Distribution:** Ensure awareness. (5) **Attestation:** Acknowledgment tracking. (6) **Search:** Find relevant policies. (7) **Reporting:** Compliance metrics. Governance: Clear ownership, regular review, consequences for non-compliance.

**Q22: How do you prepare for audits?**
A: Audit preparation: (1) **Identify:** What will be audited. (2) **Gather:** Required documentation. (3) **Review:** Check completeness and accuracy. (4) **Organize:** Easy for auditors to access. (5) **Explain:** Prepare process walkthroughs. (6) **Respond:** Timely answers to questions. (7) **Follow-up:** Address findings promptly. Ongoing: Audit-ready posture, not last-minute scramble.

**Q23: What is information governance?**
A: Information governance: Framework for handling information: (1) **Value:** Information as asset. (2) **Risk:** Legal, security, privacy. (3) **Quality:** Accuracy, completeness. (4) **Lifecycle:** From creation to disposal. (5) **Roles:** Responsibilities and ownership. (6) **Technology:** Enabling systems. (7) **Compliance:** Regulatory adherence. Goal: Right information, right people, right time, right protection.

**Q24: How do you ensure document security?**
A: Document security: (1) **Classification:** Sensitivity levels. (2) **Access Control:** Who can read/write. (3) **Encryption:** At rest and in transit. (4) **Watermarking:** Track distribution. (5) **DRM:** Prevent unauthorized actions. (6) **Audit:** Who accessed what. (7) **DLP:** Prevent leakage. Balance: Security vs. usability—too strict blocks work, too loose risks breach.

### Category 5: CULTURE & ADOPTION (6 FAQs)

**Q25: How do you build a documentation culture?**
A: Culture building: (1) **Leadership:** Executives value and model. (2) **Incentives:** Recognition for good docs. (3) **Ease:** Make documentation easy. (4) **Training:** Teach skills. (5) **Integration:** Part of workflows. (6) **Quality:** Set standards. (7) **Community:** Peer support. (8) **Measure:** Track and celebrate. Change: Gradual, starts with champions.

**Q26: How do you get people to use the knowledge base?**
A: Adoption strategies: (1) **Value:** Show how it helps. (2) **Ease:** Make access simple. (3) **Quality:** Ensure content worth finding. (4) **Integration:** In workflows, not separate. (5) **Search:** Good search is essential. (6) **Training:** Show how to use. (7) **Champions:** Power users advocate. (8) **Measure:** Track and share wins. Friction: Remove barriers to access.

**Q27: What is knowledge hoarding and how do you address it?**
A: Knowledge hoarding: Withholding information for power or security. Causes: Fear of replacement, lack of incentives, no system to share. Address: (1) **Culture:** Knowledge sharing valued. (2) **Safety:** No punishment for sharing. (3) **Credit:** Recognize contributors. (4) **System:** Easy to document. (5) **Time:** Allocated for documentation. (6) **Replacement:** Cross-training reduces single points of failure.

**Q28: How do you capture tacit knowledge?**
A: Tacit knowledge capture: (1) **Interviews:** Record expert conversations. (2) **Shadowing:** Observe and document. (3) **Storytelling:** Narrative case studies. (4) **Mentoring:** Pair with junior. (5) **Retrospectives:** Learn from projects. (6) **Decision Records:** Capture rationale. (7) **Communities:** Forums for sharing. Challenge: Tacit is hard to articulate, iterative extraction needed.

**Q29: What is a community of practice?**
A: Community of Practice (CoP): Group sharing common interest/domain: (1) **Domain:** Shared interest. (2) **Community:** Relationships and trust. (3) **Practice:** Shared resources, experiences, stories. Activities: Discussions, knowledge sharing, problem solving. Support: Facilitation, platform, recognition. Value: Knowledge transfer, innovation, professional development.

**Q30: How do you handle documentation debt?**
A: Documentation debt: Accumulated missing/outdated docs. Management: (1) **Inventory:** Assess current state. (2) **Prioritize:** Critical gaps first. (3) **Sprints:** Dedicated documentation time. (4) **Kaizen:** Continuous small improvements. (5) **Definition of Done:** Docs required. (6) **Ownership:** Clear maintainers. Prevention: Docs part of development workflow, not afterthought.

# SECTION 5: SOPs & STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES

## SOP 1: Document Creation

**Duration:** 2-8 hours | **Frequency:** As needed | **Owner:** Document Owner

1. **Need Analysis:** What problem does this solve?
2. **Audience Definition:** Who will read this?
3. **Research:** Gather source material and SME input
4. **Outline:** Structure and headings
5. **Draft:** Write first version
6. **Review:** Peer and SME review
7. **Edit:** Revise based on feedback
8. **Approve:** Sign-off from owner
9. **Publish:** Release to knowledge base
10. **Promote:** Announce availability
11. **Maintain:** Schedule review

**Templates:**

- Standard operating procedure
- How-to guide
- FAQ entry
- Policy document
- Reference material

## SOP 2: Content Review Cycle

**Duration:** 1-2 hours per doc | **Frequency:** Quarterly | **Owner:** Content Owners

1. **Inventory:** Generate list of content due for review
2. **Assignment:** Notify owners
3. **Review:** Owner assesses accuracy and currency
4. **Update:** Make necessary changes
5. **Verify:** Check links, formatting, accuracy
6. **Approve:** Confirm still valid
7. **Extend:** Update review date
8. **Report:** Track completion
9. **Escalate:** Address overdue items
10. **Archive:** Retire obsolete content

**Review Triggers:**

- Scheduled (quarterly/annual)
- Product changes
- Process updates
- User feedback
- Audit findings

## SOP 3: Knowledge Base Migration

**Duration:** 4-8 weeks | **Frequency:** As needed | **Owner:** CKO

1. **Inventory:** Catalog current content
2. **Audit:** Assess quality and relevance
3. **Taxonomy:** Design new structure
4. **Prioritize:** Migration sequence
5. **Clean:** Remove obsolete content
6. **Map:** Old to new locations
7. **Migrate:** Move and reformat content
8. **Review:** Verify migrated content
9. **Redirect:** Set up URL redirects
10. **Train:** Educate users on new system
11. **Launch:** Go live with new platform
12. **Optimize:** Iterate based on feedback

**Checklist:**

- Content inventory complete
- Taxonomy validated
- Quality threshold met
- Redirects tested
- Search configured

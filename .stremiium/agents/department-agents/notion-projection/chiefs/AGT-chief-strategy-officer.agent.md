# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Thirty+ Frequently Asked Questions

### Category 1: STRATEGIC PLANNING (6 FAQs)

**Q1: What makes a good strategic plan?**
A: Effective strategic plan: (1) **Clear Vision:** Where we're going. (2) **Specific Goals:** Measurable outcomes. (3) **Prioritized:** Focus on what matters. (4) **Actionable:** Who does what by when. (5) **Flexible:** Adapts to change. (6) **Communicated:** Understood by all. (7) **Tracked:** Progress measured. Framework: Vision → Goals → Initiatives → Actions.

**Q2: What is the difference between strategy and tactics?**
A: **Strategy:** What we want to achieve and why. Long-term, broad scope, direction-setting. **Tactics:** How we execute. Short-term, specific actions, implementation-focused. Relationship: Strategy guides tactics. Both needed: Strategy without tactics is wishful thinking; tactics without strategy is aimless activity.

**Q3: How do you prioritize strategic initiatives?**
A: Prioritization frameworks: (1) **Impact vs. Effort:** High impact, low effort first. (2) **RICE:** Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort. (3) **Strategic Fit:** Alignment to goals. (4) **Sequencing:** Dependencies and prerequisites. (5) **Resources:** Capacity and constraints. Process: Brainstorm → Score → Debate → Decide. Limit: 3-5 major initiatives per quarter.

**Q4: What is OKR and how do you implement it?**
A: OKR (Objectives and Key Results): **Objective:** Qualitative, inspirational goal. **Key Results:** 3-5 measurable outcomes. Implementation: (1) **Company OKRs:** Set by leadership. (2) **Team OKRs:** Aligned to company. (3) **Individual OKRs:** Personal contribution. (4) **Cadence:** Set quarterly. (5) **Tracking:** Weekly check-ins. (6) **Scoring:** 0.0-1.0 scale. Target: 0.7 (ambitious but achievable).

**Q5: How do you align strategy across an organization?**
A: Alignment approaches: (1) **Cascading:** Top-down goal breakdown. (2) **Collaborative:** Cross-functional planning. (3) **Transparent:** Visible goals and progress. (4) **Cadence:** Regular alignment meetings. (5) **Accountability:** Clear ownership. (6) **Communication:** Repeated messaging. Tools: OKRs, strategy maps, balanced scorecards.

**Q6: How often should strategy be reviewed?**
A: Review cadence: **Annual:** Full strategic planning. **Quarterly:** OKR setting and major initiative review. **Monthly:** Progress tracking and course correction. **Weekly:** Tactical execution and blockers. Adaptability: Strategy is direction, not rigid plan—adjust as you learn.

### Category 2: OPERATIONAL EXCELLENCE (6 FAQs)

**Q7: What is operational excellence?**
A: Operational excellence: Consistently delivering value efficiently. Dimensions: (1) **Quality:** Error-free output. (2) **Speed:** Fast delivery. (3) **Cost:** Efficient resource use. (4) **Flexibility:** Adapt to change. (5) **Innovation:** Continuous improvement. Methods: Lean, Six Sigma, TQM, BPM.

**Q8: How do you map a business process?**
A: Process mapping steps: (1) **Define Scope:** Start and end points. (2) **Identify Steps:** Activities in sequence. (3) **Add Details:** Who, time, systems. (4) **Show Flow:** Decisions, parallel paths. (5) **Validate:** With doers. (6) **Analyze:** Bottlenecks, waste. Tools: Flowcharts, swimlanes, value stream maps, BPMN.

**Q9: What is value stream mapping?**
A: Value stream mapping: Visualize material and information flow. Components: (1) **Current State:** As-is process. (2) **Value-Add:** Customer-valued activities. (3) **Non-Value-Add:** Waste (waiting, defects, overprocessing). (4) **Future State:** Improved design. (5) **Action Plan:** How to get there. Goal: Eliminate waste, improve flow.

**Q10: How do you identify process inefficiencies?**
A: Waste categories (TIMWOOD): **T**ransportation, **I**nventory, **M**otion, **W**aiting, **O**verproduction, **O**verprocessing, **D**efects. Analysis: Time studies, spaghetti diagrams, gemba walks. Metrics: Cycle time, throughput, error rate, cost per unit. Look for: Handoffs, rework, manual work, system gaps.

**Q11: What is continuous improvement (Kaizen)?**
A: Kaizen: Continuous, incremental improvement. Principles: (1) **Small Steps:** Daily improvements. (2) **Everyone:** All employees involved. (3) **Low Cost:** Minimal investment. (4) **Immediate:** Quick implementation. (5) **Sustainable:** Ongoing practice. Process: Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA). Culture: Problem = opportunity.

**Q12: How do you measure operational performance?**
A: Operational metrics: (1) **Efficiency:** Output/input ratio. (2) **Effectiveness:** Goal achievement. (3) **Quality:** Error rate, customer satisfaction. (4) **Speed:** Cycle time, lead time. (5) **Cost:** Unit cost, total cost. (6) **Flexibility:** Adaptation speed. (7) **Innovation:** Improvement rate. Dashboard: Balanced set of metrics.

### Category 3: CHANGE MANAGEMENT (6 FAQs)

**Q13: What is change management?**
A: Change management: Structured approach to transitioning people and organizations. Focus: (1) **People:** Adoption and usage. (2) **Process:** New ways of working. (3) **Technology:** Tools and systems. (4) **Culture:** Mindset and behaviors. Models: ADKAR, Kotter's 8 Steps, Lewin's Unfreeze-Change-Refreeze.

**Q14: Why do most change initiatives fail?**
A: Common failure reasons: (1) **No Sponsorship:** Lack of executive support. (2) **Poor Communication:** Unclear why/what/how. (3) **No Urgency:** Comfort with status quo. (4) **Wrong Skills:** Insufficient training. (5) **No Quick Wins:** Long time to results. (6) **Culture:** Incompatible values. (7) **Resistance:** Not addressed proactively.

**Q15: How do you build change readiness?**
A: Readiness factors: (1) **Awareness:** Understand need. (2) **Desire:** Want to participate. (3) **Knowledge:** Know what to do. (4) **Ability:** Can do it. (5) **Reinforcement:** Sustained support. Assessment: Survey, interviews, readiness scoring. Intervention: Address gaps before launching change.

**Q16: How do you manage resistance to change?**
A: Resistance types: (1) **Logical:** Disagree with approach. (2) **Political:** Threatens power/position. (3) **Emotional:** Fear of loss. (4) **Cultural:** Violates norms. Strategies: (1) **Listen:** Understand concerns. (2) **Educate:** Address knowledge gaps. (3) **Involve:** Include in planning. (4) **Support:** Provide resources. (5) **Negotiate:** Find compromises. (6) **Co-opt:** Bring resisters into process.

**Q17: What is stakeholder management?**
A: Stakeholder management: Identify and engage those affected by or affecting change. Process: (1) **Identify:** All stakeholders. (2) **Analyze:** Interest, influence, impact. (3) **Map:** Power/interest grid. (4) **Plan:** Engagement strategy. (5) **Engage:** Execute communications. (6) **Monitor:** Adjust as needed. Matrix: High power/high interest = manage closely.

**Q18: How do you sustain change after implementation?**
A: Sustainability factors: (1) **Reinforcement:** Rewards and recognition. (2) **Measurement:** Track adoption. (3) **Feedback:** Listen and adjust. (4) **Integration:** Embed in processes. (5) **Culture:** Align with values. (6) **Leadership:** Continued support. (7) **Resilience:** Plan for setbacks. Warning: Reversion to old ways common at 6-12 months.

### Category 4: MARKET ANALYSIS (6 FAQs)

**Q19: How do you conduct competitive analysis?**
A: Competitive analysis: (1) **Identify:** Who competes (direct, indirect, potential). (2) **Research:** Products, pricing, positioning. (3) **Strengths/Weaknesses:** SWOT analysis. (4) **Strategy:** Their likely moves. (5) **Differentiation:** Your unique value. (6) **Monitoring:** Ongoing tracking. Tools: SWOT, perceptual maps, battlecards. Update: Quarterly.

**Q20: What is market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM)?**
A: Market sizing: **TAM (Total Addressable Market):** Total demand. **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** Segment you can serve. **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** Realistically capture. Calculation: Top-down (industry data) vs. bottom-up (customers × price). Use: Prioritization, investor communication, goal setting.

**Q21: How do you identify market trends?**
A: Trend identification: (1) **Data:** Industry reports, market research. (2) **Customers:** Feedback and behavior. (3) **Competitors:** Moves and announcements. (4) **Technology:** Emerging capabilities. (5) **Regulatory:** Policy changes. (6) **Macro:** Economic, social shifts. Analysis: Signal vs. noise, trend duration, impact magnitude.

**Q22: What is scenario planning?**
A: Scenario planning: Explore multiple possible futures. Process: (1) **Drivers:** Key uncertainties. (2) **Scenarios:** 3-4 plausible futures. (3) **Stories:** Narrative descriptions. (4) **Implications:** What we'd do in each. (5) **Indicators:** Early warning signals. (6) **Strategy:** Robust across scenarios. Use: Not prediction, but preparation.

**Q23: How do you analyze industry structure (Porter's Five Forces)?**
A: Five Forces: (1) **Rivalry:** Competition intensity. (2) **New Entrants:** Barriers to entry. (3) **Substitutes:** Alternative solutions. (4) **Buyer Power:** Customer leverage. (5) **Supplier Power:** Vendor leverage. Analysis: Rate each force (low/medium/high). Implication: Industry attractiveness, competitive positioning.

**Q24: What is a strategic partnership?**
A: Strategic partnership: Formal alliance for mutual benefit. Types: (1) **Technology:** Integration, co-development. (2) **Channel:** Reseller, referral. (3) **Supply:** Sourcing, manufacturing. (4) **Marketing:** Co-branding, joint campaigns. (5) **Investment:** Equity, JV. Evaluation: Strategic fit, cultural fit, economics, risk.

### Category 5: FACILITATION & EXECUTION (6 FAQs)

**Q25: How do you facilitate a strategic planning session?**
A: Facilitation best practices: (1) **Pre-work:** Data, pre-reads. (2) **Agenda:** Clear objectives. (3) **Diverse:** Multiple perspectives. (4) **Structured:** Frameworks and tools. (5) **Participation:** Everyone contributes. (6) **Converge:** From ideas to decisions. (7) **Action:** Clear next steps. (8) **Follow-up:** Document and track.

**Q26: What is program management?**
A: Program management: Coordinate related projects for strategic benefit. Difference from project management: PM delivers output, program delivers outcomes. Focus: (1) **Alignment:** To strategy. (2) **Benefits:** Realization tracking. (3) **Stakeholders:** Multiple constituencies. (4) **Governance:** Decision frameworks. (5) **Risk:** Cross-project issues.

**Q27: How do you build executive presentations?**
A: Executive presentations: (1) **Hook:** Why they should care. (2) **Context:** Situation and stakes. (3) **Options:** Alternatives considered. (4) **Recommendation:** Clear proposal. (5) **Rationale:** Why this choice. (6) **Risk:** What could go wrong. (7) **Ask:** What decision/action needed. (8) **Appendix:** Supporting detail. Time: 30 min max.

**Q28: What is business process reengineering (BPR)?**
A: BPR: Radical redesign of processes for dramatic improvement. Principles: (1) **Clean Slate:** Start from scratch. (2) **Customer-centric:** Focus on value. (3) **Holistic:** End-to-end processes. (4) **Empowerment:** Push down decisions. (5) **IT-enabled:** Technology as enabler. Risk: Disruption, resistance. Success: Clear vision, strong sponsorship.

**Q29: How do you balance short-term and long-term?**
A: Balance strategies: (1) **Horizon Model:** 70% current, 20% near-term, 10% long-term. (2) **Dual Operating System:** Separate execution and innovation. (3) **Metrics:** Both efficiency and growth. (4) **Portfolio:** Mix of initiatives. (5) **Time:** Protect time for strategic. (6) **Invest:** Fund innovation. Warning: Short-term focus starves future.

**Q30: How do you maintain strategic agility?**
A: Agility practices: (1) **Environmental Scanning:** Continuous monitoring. (2) **Flexible Planning:** Rolling forecasts. (3) **Modular Structure:** Adaptable components. (4) **Decision Speed:** Rapid course correction. (5) **Learning:** Fast feedback loops. (6) **Reserves:** Buffer capacity. (7) **Mindset:** Comfort with ambiguity. Not: Planning less, but planning adaptively.

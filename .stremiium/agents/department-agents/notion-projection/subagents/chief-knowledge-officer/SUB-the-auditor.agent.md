# SECTION 4: FAQs (20+)

**Q1: What is the difference between internal and external audit?**
A: Internal = employee, continuous, advisory. External = third-party, periodic, independent opinion (financial, regulatory).

**Q2: What are the components of internal control?**
A: COSO framework: Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information/Communication, Monitoring.

**Q3: How do you conduct a risk assessment?**
A: Identify risks, assess likelihood and impact, evaluate controls, prioritize, develop mitigation plan.

**Q4: What is the audit process?**
A: Planning → Fieldwork (testing) → Reporting → Follow-up. Continuous monitoring between cycles.

**Q5: What is a finding vs. observation?**
A: Finding = control failure with impact. Observation = area for improvement, no current failure.

**Q6: How do you test controls?**
A: Inquiry (ask), Observation (watch), Inspection (documents), Re-performance (do it). Evidence of operation.

**Q7: What is SOX compliance?**
A: Sarbanes-Oxley. Financial reporting controls for public companies. CEO/CFO certification, controls, audits.

**Q8: What is GDPR and how to audit?**
A: EU data protection. Audit: Data inventory, consent, rights fulfillment, security, breach process, DPO.

**Q9: How do you handle audit findings?**
A: Rate severity, assign owner, set timeline, track remediation, validate closure, report status.

**Q10: What is continuous auditing?**
A: Automated, ongoing testing of controls. Real-time monitoring vs. periodic snapshots. Technology-enabled.

**Q11: What is the role of the audit committee?**
A: Board oversight of audit, risk, compliance. Reviews audit plan, significant findings, approves budget.

**Q12: How do you maintain auditor independence?**
A: No operational responsibility, report to appropriate level (board/CEO), no conflict of interest, objective.

**Q13: What is a compliance calendar?**
A: Schedule of all compliance obligations. Reporting deadlines, renewals, assessments, board meetings.

**Q14: How do you audit IT systems?**
A: General controls (access, change, operations), application controls (input, processing, output), data integrity.

**Q15: What is third-party risk management?**
A: Assessing and monitoring vendors. Due diligence, contract terms, ongoing monitoring, exit planning.

**Q16: How do you document audit work?**
A: Who, what, when, where, why, how. Workpapers: Clear, sufficient, relevant, organized, reviewed.

**Q17: What is a SOC 2 audit?**
A: Service organization controls. Trust services criteria: Security, Availability, Confidentiality, Privacy.

**Q18: How do you prevent audit fatigue?**
A: Risk-based approach, automation, coordination with other assurance, focus on value-add.

**Q19: What is data analytics in auditing?**
A: Using data to identify anomalies, trends, patterns. Population testing vs. sampling. More effective.

**Q20: How do you communicate audit results?**
A: Tailored to audience, balanced, actionable, timely, clear severity, root cause, recommendations.

# SECTION 5: SOPs

## SOP 1: Internal Audit

1. Annual risk assessment
2. Develop audit plan
3. Schedule with business
4. Kickoff meeting
5. Fieldwork (testing)
6. Draft findings
7. Management response
8. Final report
9. Track remediation
10. Follow-up testing

## SOP 2: Control Testing

1. Identify controls to test
2. Understand design
3. Determine sample size
4. Select sample
5. Perform test
6. Document results
7. Assess effectiveness
8. Report findings

## SOP 3: Policy Review

1. Annual review trigger
2. Assess current state
3. Identify changes needed
4. Draft updates
5. Legal/Compliance review
6. Management approval
7. Publish and communicate
8. Track attestation

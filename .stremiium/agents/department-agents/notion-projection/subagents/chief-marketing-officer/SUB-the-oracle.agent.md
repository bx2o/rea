# SECTION 4: FAQs (20+)

**Q1: What's the difference between market research and competitive intelligence?**
A: Market research = customer, market, opportunities. CI = competitors, threats, positioning. Complementary.

**Q2: How do you calculate TAM/SAM/SOM?**
A: TAM = total demand. SAM = serviceable portion. SOM = realistic capture. Use top-down (industry reports) and bottom-up (customers × price).

**Q3: What makes a good buyer persona?**
A: Demographics, firmographics, goals, challenges, behaviors, objections, media consumption, quotes. Data-backed, not fictional.

**Q4: How do you do competitive analysis?**
A: Identify competitors, analyze products/pricing/messaging, SWOT, positioning maps, win/loss interviews, ongoing monitoring.

**Q5: What is attribution modeling?**
A: Assigning credit to touchpoints in customer journey. Models: First-touch, last-touch, linear, time-decay, position-based, data-driven.

**Q6: How do you measure marketing ROI?**
A: (Revenue - Marketing Cost) / Marketing Cost. Include: Customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, payback period, channel efficiency.

**Q7: What is cohort analysis?**
A: Grouping users by time/action, tracking behavior over time. Reveals: Retention, engagement trends, feature adoption.

**Q8: How do you identify market trends?**
A: Industry reports, customer feedback, sales insights, web analytics, social listening, patent filings, startup activity.

**Q9: What's the difference between correlation and causation?**
A: Correlation = variables move together. Causation = one causes the other. Don't confuse. A/B testing proves causation.

**Q10: How do you avoid analysis paralysis?**
A: Clear questions, time-box analysis, "good enough" threshold, decision deadlines, start with hypothesis.

**Q11: What is statistical significance?**
A: Result unlikely due to chance. Usually p < 0.05. Larger sample = more power. Practical significance also matters.

**Q12: How do you create effective dashboards?**
A: Audience-specific, actionable metrics, clear visualizations, real-time where possible, context (targets, trends), mobile-friendly.

**Q13: What is data storytelling?**
A: Narrative around data. Context, conflict, resolution. Answer: What happened, why, what to do.

**Q14: How do you ensure data quality?**
A: Validation rules, deduplication, standardization, monitoring, source documentation, regular audits.

**Q15: What is customer journey mapping?**
A: Visualizing customer steps: awareness, consideration, purchase, retention, advocacy. Touchpoints, emotions, pain points.

**Q16: How do you do win/loss analysis?**
A: Interview recent wins/losses, identify patterns, price, product, process, competition, sales effectiveness. Action on insights.

**Q17: What is predictive analytics in marketing?**
A: Using data/statistics to predict outcomes: Lead scoring, churn prediction, next-best-action, demand forecasting.

**Q18: How do you handle data privacy in research?**
A: Consent, anonymization, data minimization, security, compliance (GDPR, CCPA), transparency.

**Q19: What's the difference between quantitative and qualitative research?**
A: Quantitative = numbers, scale, statistics. Qualitative = insights, context, "why." Both needed.

**Q20: How do you present data to executives?**
A: Bottom line up front, 3 key insights, visual, actionable recommendations, backup detail in appendix.

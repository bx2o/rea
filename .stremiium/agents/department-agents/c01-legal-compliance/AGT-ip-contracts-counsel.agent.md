# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY PROTECTION (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What types of IP should a SaaS company protect?**
A: Framework: (1) **Patents:** Core technology (algorithm, method, system). Cost: $5-15K per patent prosecution. (2) **Trademarks:** Company name, product names, logos. Cost: $500-2K per trademark. (3) **Copyrights:** Code, documentation, content (automatic; registration optional). Cost: <$100 to register. (4) **Trade secrets:** Customer lists, pricing, business processes (protect through confidentiality). Cost: Process/policies <$10K. Priority: Trademarks (brand protection) > Patents (if defensible core tech) > Trade secrets (always).

**Q2: How do you file for patent protection?**
A: Process: (1) **Preliminary:** Identify patentable invention (must be novel, non-obvious). (2) **File provisional:** Quick/cheap filing ($200-500), gives you 12 months. (3) **Evaluate:** Does patent have business value? Will you enforce? (4) **File full application:** Includes claims, drawings, specification. Cost: $5-10K (attorneys). (5) **Prosecution:** Patent office asks questions; your attorney responds. Timeline: 2-4 years to approval. (6) **Maintenance:** Pay fees every 3-4 years to keep. Caution: Patents are defensive; don't assume they're valuable.

**Q3: How do you protect trade secrets?**
A: Protection: (1) **Identify:** What's your trade secret? (algorithm, customer data, pricing model). (2) **Classify:** Mark documents "confidential" or "trade secret". (3) **Access control:** Only people who need to know have access. (4) **Contracts:** All employees/contractors sign NDA and IP assignment. (5) **Policies:** Document how you protect (limited access, encryption, etc.). (6) **Exit procedures:** When employee leaves, require return of documents, remind of confidentiality. Key: Trade secret only protected if you take "reasonable measures" to keep secret.

**Q4: What's the difference between copyright, patent, and trademark?**
A: **Copyright:** Protection for original works (code, writing, art). Automatic, lasts lifetime + 70 years. Example: Your product code is copyrighted. **Patent:** Protection for inventions (novel method/system). Must file, lasts 20 years. Example: Your algorithm. **Trademark:** Protection for brand identity (name, logo, slogan). Must register, lasts forever (if renewed). Example: "Acme Inc" brand. All three valuable; choose based on what you're protecting.

**Q5: What's an IP assignment agreement and why do employees need to sign?**
A: Assignment agreement = employee assigns all IP created as part of job to company. Why? Without it, employee can claim ownership of code/patents they wrote (even while working for company). All employees/contractors should sign on day 1. Covers: Code, patents, trade secrets, customer lists created during employment. Template: 1-2 pages, standard/non-negotiable. Cost: <$1K to draft template.

**Q6: How do you do an IP audit?**
A: Audit = comprehensive review of all company IP. Steps: (1) **Inventory:** What IP does company have? (code, patents, trademarks, customer data). (2) **Ownership:** Who owns each? (company or third party?). (3) **Documentation:** Is ownership documented (IP assignment agreements, purchase docs)? (4) **Protection:** Is it protected? (registered patents/trademarks? trade secrets classified?). (5) **Risk:** Any gaps? (outstanding assignments? unregistered IP?). (6) **Report:** Summary of findings + recommendations. Cost: $10-50K depending on size. When: Annually or before M&A.

**Q7: What's open source and what are the risks?**
A: Open source = software code publicly available (often free). Benefits: Faster development (leverage existing code). Risks: Some open source licenses require you to open-source your code (GPL). Type matters: (1) **MIT, Apache:** Permissive (you can use without open-sourcing). (2) **GPL:** Copyleft (if you use, you must open-source derivations). (3) **AGPL:** Stricter (even cloud usage triggers copyleft). Management: (1) Audit all open source usage. (2) Classify licenses. (3) Avoid GPL unless unavoidable. (4) Use tools (FOSSA, BlackDuck) to track.

**Q8: How do you handle IP infringement (someone stealing your IP)?**
A: Response: (1) **Verify:** Did infringement actually occur? (consult IP attorney). (2) **Cease & desist:** Send letter demanding stop. Cost: <$5K. (3) **Negotiation:** Some infringers will pay licensing fee or stop. (4) **Litigation:** If infringer won't stop, consider lawsuit (expensive: $100K-M+). (5) **Insurance:** IP infringement insurance can cover legal costs. When: Usually only if infringement is costing you significant revenue.

**Q9: What should a standard NDA (non-disclosure agreement) include?**
A: Key sections: (1) **Definition:** What's confidential? (usually "marked confidential" or "learn in conversation"). (2) **Obligation:** Recipient agrees not to disclose to third parties. (3) **Exceptions:** Information that's already public, independently developed, required by law. (4) **Duration:** How long does obligation last? (usually 3-5 years). (5) **Return:** Recipient must return/destroy info if asked. (6) **No license:** Sharing info doesn't grant IP rights. (7) **Remedies:** If breach, you can seek injunction + damages. Standard template: 2-3 pages. Cost: $500-2K to draft or use template.

**Q10: What's a Master Service Agreement (MSA) and why do you need one?**
A: MSA = overall framework agreement between you and vendor/partner. Covers: General terms (liability, indemnification, insurance, term, termination). Specific work described in Statement of Work (SOW). Why: Prevents re-negotiating same terms for every project. Speeds up contracting (MSA done once; SOWs reference it). Reduces risk (standard terms already agreed). All vendors: Get MSA signed before doing business. Update: Annually, ensure terms still favorable.

**Q11: How do you negotiate vendor agreements to your advantage?**
A: Approach: (1) **Start with template:** Use your template (you control terms). (2) **Key terms:** Focus on: liability caps ($=limited to fees paid), indemnification (vendor liable for their IP infringement), insurance (they carry coverage), termination (you can exit if unhappy). (3) **Data:** Data ownership/confidentiality (they protect your data). (4) **Precedent:** Reference similar deals you've done. (5) **Negotiate:** Vendor will push back; be prepared to compromise on less critical items. Rule: Never pay significant sums without negotiated terms.

**Q12: What's IP due diligence in M&A context?**
A: Due diligence = investigation of target company IP before acquisition. Checklist: (1) Does target own all IP used in business? (2) Are there outstanding IP disputes? (3) Does target infringe any third-party IP? (4) What patents/trademarks does target have? (5) Are employee IP assignments proper? (6) Any open source risks? (7) Customer contracts—any IP restrictions? Timeline: 2-4 weeks. Cost: $50K-150K (attorneys + IP experts). Why: So you know what you're buying and what risks you're taking.

**Q13: What's a reasonable IP budget for a company?**
A: Budget allocation: (1) **Small startup ($<10M revenue):** $50-100K/year (IP counsel ~0.5 FTE, patent prosecution, trademark registration). (2) **Growth stage ($10-100M):** $200-500K/year (IP counsel FTE, active patent prosecution, M&A support). (3) **Mature ($>100M):\*\* $500K-2M+ (dedicated IP team, large patent portfolio, enforcement). General rule: 0.5-2% of revenue on IP protection/management. ROI: Good IP strategy can be worth 10-100x the investment.

**Q14: When should you use in-house counsel vs. outside IP attorneys?**
A: **In-house:** More cost-effective for ongoing work. Understands company strategy. Limited to general IP matters. **Outside:** Specialists (patent prosecution attorneys, IP litigation). More expensive ($300-500/hour). Needed for specialist work. Hybrid: Keep IP counsel in-house; use outside for patents, litigation, M&A. Cost: In-house IP counsel ~$150-250K/year. Outside: $10-50K+ per engagement.

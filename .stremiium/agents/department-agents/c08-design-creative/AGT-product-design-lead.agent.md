# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 UX Design FAQs

**Q1: How do you design for both novice and expert users?**
A: Strategies: (1) **Progressive disclosure:** Simple by default, power available. (2) **Shortcuts:** Keyboard shortcuts for experts, visible UI for novices. (3) **Tooltips:** Contextual help without clutter. (4) **Default states:** Smart defaults reduce decisions. (5) **Learnable patterns:** Consistent interactions build expertise. (6) **Analytics:** Know your user mix and optimize for majority.

**Q2: What makes a good user flow?**
A: Characteristics: (1) **Clear goal:** User knows what they're accomplishing. (2) **Minimal steps:** Fewest actions to complete. (3) **Clear path:** Obvious next step at every point. (4) **Forgiving:** Easy to undo, recover from errors. (5) **Feedback:** System confirms actions. (6) **Flexibility:** Multiple ways to accomplish (if appropriate). (7) **Context:** Information available when needed.

**Q3: How do you handle complex workflows in simple interfaces?**
A: Approaches: (1) **Break into steps:** Multi-step wizard vs. one overwhelming form. (2) **Progressive disclosure:** Show details on demand. (3) **Smart defaults:** Pre-fill based on common patterns. (4) **Save progress:** Allow pause and resume. (5) **Clear status:** Where am I? What's left? (6) **Batch operations:** Group related actions. (7) **Templates:** Pre-built configurations.

## 4.2 UI Design FAQs

**Q4: What makes a great interface?**
A: Principles: (1) **Clarity:** Everything has purpose, nothing confusing. (2) **Hierarchy:** Most important elements prominent. (3) **Consistency:** Same patterns throughout. (4) **Feedback:** Actions have visible results. (5) **Forgiveness:** Mistakes recoverable. (6) **Efficiency:** Common tasks fast. (7) **Aesthetic:** Pleasing to use. (8) **Accessible:** Usable by all.

**Q5: How do you choose the right design pattern?**
A: Framework: (1) **User context:** What are they trying to accomplish? (2) **Device:** Mobile, desktop, tablet considerations. (3) **Platform:** OS conventions (iOS, Android, Web). (4) **Familiarity:** Patterns users know. (5) **Scalability:** Works for current and future states. (6) **Accessibility:** Works for all users. (7) **Test:** Validate with real users.

**Q6: When should you break design conventions?**
A: Guidelines: (1) **Know the rules:** Understand conventions first. (2) **Clear benefit:** Breaking convention must improve experience significantly. (3) **User testing:** Verify new pattern works better. (4) **Onboarding:** Teach new pattern clearly. (5) **Fallback:** Easy escape if confusion. (6) **Platform awareness:** Consider OS expectations.

# SECTION 5: STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES

## SOP 1: Product Design Process

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks | **Frequency:** Per feature

1. **Discovery:** Understand requirements, user needs
2. **Research:** Competitive analysis, user insights
3. **Concept:** Explore multiple approaches
4. **Wireframe:** Low-fi structure and flow
5. **Review:** Stakeholder feedback
6. **Visual design:** High-fi UI
7. **Prototype:** Interactive for testing
8. **Test:** Usability validation
9. **Refine:** Iterate based on feedback
10. **Spec:** Prepare for development
11. **Handoff:** Engineering collaboration
12. **QA:** Design review of implementation

## SOP 2: Design QA Process

**Duration:** 2-3 days | **Frequency:** Pre-launch

1. **Compare:** Design vs. implementation
2. **Document:** Issues and discrepancies
3. **Prioritize:** Critical vs. cosmetic
4. **Review:** With engineering
5. **Fix:** Address critical issues
6. **Verify:** Confirm fixes
7. **Accept:** Sign off on release

## SOP 3: Design Critique Session

**Duration:** 1 hour | **Frequency:** Weekly

1. **Present:** Designer shares work and goals
2. **Clarify:** Questions to understand context
3. **Feedback:** Structured critique
4. **Synthesize:** Designer reflects on input
5. **Decide:** Next steps for iteration

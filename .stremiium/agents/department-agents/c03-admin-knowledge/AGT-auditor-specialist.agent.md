# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: How do you prepare for a compliance audit?**
A: Preparation: (1) **Scope definition:** What policies/areas to audit? (2) **Criteria development:** What constitutes compliance? (3) **Sampling plan:** How many items to test? (4) **Checklist creation:** Specific tests to perform. (5) **Schedule coordination:** Notify auditee, schedule interviews. (6) **Document request:** Ask for relevant records upfront. (7) **Tool preparation:** Access to systems, templates ready. Time: 1-2 days prep for typical audit.

**Q2: What's the difference between compliance and conformity?**
A: **Compliance** = meeting external requirements (laws, regulations, standards). Required, often legally binding. **Conformity** = meeting internal standards (policies, procedures, guidelines). Expected, organization-specific. Both important: Compliance prevents legal issues; conformity ensures operational consistency. Audit both separately or together depending on scope.

**Q3: How do you determine audit sample size?**
A: Factors: (1) **Population size:** How many total items? (2) **Risk level:** High-risk areas need larger samples. (3) **Confidence needed:** Statistical confidence level (typically 95%). (4) **Historical issues:** Past problems suggest larger sample. (5) **Resource constraints:** Time available. Rule of thumb: 10% of population or minimum 20-30 items. For high-risk: 20-30% sample. Document rationale for sample size.

**Q4: How do you handle pushback from auditees?**
A: Approach: (1) **Listen:** Understand their concerns. (2) **Explain:** Clarify audit purpose (improvement, not punishment). (3) **Verify:** Double-check your findings if challenged. (4) **Collaborate:** Work together on solutions. (5) **Escalate:** If obstruction continues, involve leadership. (6) **Document:** Record disagreements and resolutions. Tone: Professional, objective, fair. Goal: Accurate findings, not winning arguments.

**Q5: What makes an audit finding "material"?**
A: **Materiality** depends on: (1) **Impact:** Consequence if issue continues (financial, legal, operational). (2) **Scope:** How widespread is the issue? (3) **History:** Has this happened before? (4) **Intent:** Deliberate violation vs. oversight. (5) **Trend:** Getting better or worse? **Material:** Significant impact, requires immediate action. **Not material:** Minor, address in normal course. Escalate material findings to leadership immediately.

**Q6: How do you write an effective audit finding?**
A: Structure: (1) **Condition:** What did you observe? (facts). (2) **Criteria:** What should exist? (standard/policy). (3) **Cause:** Why did it happen? (root cause). (4) **Effect:** What's the impact? (risk/consequence). (5) **Recommendation:** How to fix? (actionable). Example: "Condition: 5 of 20 invoices lacked approval signatures. Criteria: Policy requires dual approval for invoices >$5K. Cause: New approver unaware of threshold. Effect: Unauthorized payments risk. Recommendation: Training + system enforcement."

# SECTION 5: SOPs (STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES)

## 5.1 SOP 1: Audit Planning & Preparation

**Duration:** 2-3 days | **Frequency:** Per audit | **Owner:** Auditor Specialist

1. **Define scope:** Policies/areas to audit
2. **Develop criteria:** Compliance standards
3. **Create checklist:** Specific test points
4. **Determine sample:** Population and sample size
5. **Schedule:** Coordinate with auditee
6. **Request documents:** Records needed for review
7. **Prepare tools:** Templates, access, materials

## 5.2 SOP 2: Audit Fieldwork

**Duration:** 3-10 days | **Frequency:** Per audit | **Owner:** Auditor Specialist

1. **Opening meeting:** Explain scope, process
2. **Document review:** Examine records
3. **Process observation:** Watch workflows
4. **Sampling:** Select items for testing
5. **Testing:** Verify compliance
6. **Interviews:** Talk to process owners
7. **Evidence collection:** Document findings
8. **Daily notes:** Record work performed

## 5.3 SOP 3: Finding Development & Reporting

**Duration:** 2-3 days | **Frequency:** Per audit | **Owner:** Auditor Specialist

1. **Analyze results:** Identify deviations
2. **Assess significance:** Rate severity
3. **Determine root cause:** Why it happened
4. **Develop recommendations:** How to fix
5. **Draft report:** Document findings
6. **Review with auditee:** Get feedback
7. **Finalize report:** Issue to leadership
8. **Track remediation:** Monitor fixes

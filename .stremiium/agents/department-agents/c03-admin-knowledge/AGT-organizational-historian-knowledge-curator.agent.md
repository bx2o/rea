# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Sample FAQs

**Q1: What's institutional knowledge and why does it matter?**
A: Institutional knowledge = knowledge that exists in organization (not just individuals). Includes: how to do things, why decisions were made, success/failure lessons, customer preferences, vendor relationships. Why: When person leaves, knowledge shouldn't leave with them. Matters: (1) Continuity (new hiring for same role, knowledge available). (2) Speed (new people onboard faster with knowledge). (3) Learning (capture lessons, improve). (4) Risk (reduce single points of failure). (5) Value (IP protected in systems, not people). Build: Capture knowledge systematically.

**Q2: What should be documented?**
A: Priority: (1) **Critical processes:** How to do essential work (can't pause if person unavailable). (2) **Major decisions:** Why decisions were made (prevents rework). (3) **Lessons learned:** What worked/didn't (enables improvement). (4) **Customer knowledge:** Customer preferences, history, relationships. (5) **Vendor/partner relationships:** History, agreements, preferences. (6) **Financial information:** Budget, pricing, vendor costs. (7) **Technical information:** System design, architecture, how things work. (8) **Market knowledge:** Competitive landscape, market analysis. Focus on high-value, hard-to-recreate knowledge.

**Q3: How do you capture knowledge from departing employees?**
A: Process: (1) **Exit interview:** 2-hour conversation before departure. Ask: What knowledge is unique to you? What should organization know? What processes need documenting? (2) **Brain dump:** Have departing employee write down everything they know (in form). (3) **Shadowing:** New person shadows departing employee (transfer knowledge in real time). (4) **Documentation:** Departing employee documents critical processes + decision history. (5) **Transition plan:** Organize handoff to successor. Cost: 10-20 hours, worth it to preserve knowledge.

**Q4: What format should knowledge be in?**
A: **Written documentation:** Wiki, Notion, shared drive. Good for: Static info, how-tos, reference material. **Video recordings:** Screen recordings, training videos. Good for: How-to demos, complex processes. **Diagrams:** Process diagrams, org charts, system architecture. Good for: Systems thinking, relationships. **Decision logs:** Decisions + context + timeline. Good for: Reference, learning from past. **Checklists:** Step-by-step checklists. Good for: Ensuring consistency. Mix: Use multiple formats; what matters is accessibility.

**Q5: How do you organize knowledge so people can find it?**
A: Organization: (1) **Hierarchy:** Organize by department/function (sales, engineering, finance). (2) **Search:** Search full-text (people find by keyword). (3) **Tagging:** Tag knowledge with categories (process, decision, lesson, how-to). (4) **Favorites:** Most accessed knowledge featured. (5) **Indexing:** Create index/directory of all knowledge. (6) **Recency:** Show recently updated knowledge. (7) **Related:** Show related knowledge items. (8) **Search training:** Teach people how to search. Test: New employee looks for info; can they find it in <2 minutes? If not, reorganize.

**Q6: How do you ensure knowledge stays current?**
A: Maintenance: (1) **Review schedule:** Review critical knowledge quarterly. (2) **Update process:** If process changes, documentation must change. (3) **Version control:** Track document versions (who changed? when?). (4) **Owner assignment:** Each knowledge item has owner responsible for accuracy. (5) **Deprecation:** Mark outdated knowledge as "archived" (don't delete; keep for history). (6) **Feedback:** If person finds inaccurate knowledge, flag it. (7) **Expiration dates:** Some knowledge has expiration date; update/refresh. Without maintenance, knowledge becomes stale/unreliable.

**Q7: What's a post-mortem or retrospective and how do you run it?**
A: **Post-mortem:** After incident/failure, review what happened. Goal: Learn, prevent recurrence. Process: (1) Gather people involved. (2) Timeline: What happened? (3) Root cause: Why did it happen? (4) What went well? (5) What didn't? (6) Lessons learned. (7) Action items: What changes to prevent? Duration: 1-2 hours. Best practice: Blameless (focus on process, not who messed up). Document: Capture findings + action items. Follow-up: Track action items to completion.

**Q8: How do you distinguish between documentation and tribal knowledge?**
A: **Documentation:** Formal, written, organized, searchable. Examples: Process flows, policy manuals, how-to guides. Accessible to anyone. **Tribal knowledge:** In people's heads, informal, not written. Examples: "How to negotiate with customer X," "Trick to fix system Y," "Decision context from 5 years ago." High risk if only 1-2 people know. Approach: (1) Identify tribal knowledge (ask "who knows how to...?"). (2) Capture it (document, train successors). (3) Convert to accessible knowledge. (4) Reduce dependence on key people.

**Q9: What's a knowledge audit and when should you do one?**
A: Audit: Comprehensive review of organizational knowledge. Covers: (1) What knowledge exists? (2) Where is it stored? (3) Is it current? (4) Who has access? (5) Gaps (what knowledge should exist but doesn't?). (6) Quality (is documentation good?). (7) Accessibility (can people find it?). Timeline: 2-4 weeks. Frequency: Annual or when onboarding times increasing (signal that knowledge is hard to access). Output: List of gaps + recommendations. Cost: Worth it; identifies opportunities for improvement.

**Q10: How do you build a knowledge management system (wiki, Notion, etc.)?**
A: Approach: (1) **Choose platform:** Wiki (Confluence), Notion, OneNote, SharePoint. Criteria: Search, access control, ease of editing, cost. (2) **Taxonomy:** How to organize knowledge? (by department, topic, type?). (3) **Templates:** Create templates for common knowledge items (process, decision, lesson). (4) **Populate:** Start with high-priority knowledge. (5) **Train:** Teach employees how to use system. (6) **Incentivize:** Make knowledge contribution part of job. (7) **Monitor:** Track usage; adjust based on what works. (8) **Maintain:** Assign ownership; keep current. Cost: Platform <$5K/year; time to build = 1-2 months. ROI: Faster onboarding, fewer repeated mistakes = 3-5x payback.

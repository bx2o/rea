# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: INTERNAL AUDITING (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What's internal audit and how is it different from external audit?**
A: **Internal audit:** Independent function inside company (reports to Board). Purpose: Improve operations, provide assurance to Board, help management. **External audit:** Outside firm hired to audit company financials (for shareholders/stakeholders). Internal focuses on: Operational efficiency, risk, controls, compliance. External focuses on: Financial statements accuracy. Different purposes: Internal = proactive improvement; External = attestation quality. Both valuable.

**Q2: What processes should be audited?**
A: Prioritization: (1) **Financial:** Accounting, revenue, expenses, cash. (2) **Customer:** Customer onboarding, billing, support. (3) **Operations:** Hiring, payroll, procurement, IT. (4) **Compliance:** Regulatory compliance, policies. (5) **Risk:** High-risk processes. Timing: Annual audit plan (covers high-priority processes). Frequency: Major processes annual; critical monthly or continuous. Sampling: Don't audit 100% of transactions; statistical sampling.

**Q3: What's a control and how do you test if it's working?**
A: Control: Procedure to prevent/detect problems. Examples: Approval requirement, reconciliation, segregation of duties, password policy. Testing: (1) **Design review:** Is control good design? Could it prevent problems? (2) **Operating effectiveness:** Is it actually being followed? (3) **Sampling:** Test samples of transactions (did control prevent/detect problems?). (4) **Observation:** Watch control in action. (5) **Documentation:** Review records of control execution. Result: Conclude if control working or broken. Timeline: Testing = 20-40% of audit effort.

**Q4: What's the difference between a finding and an observation?**
A: **Finding:** Control not working or compliance gap. Issues to fix. Rated by severity (critical, major, minor). **Observation:** Note for management (not a problem, but improvement opportunity). Example: Finding = sensitive data not encrypted (control failed). Observation = could improve process efficiency by 10%. Both reported, but findings require remediation plan.

**Q5: How do you prioritize findings?**
A: Severity levels: (1) **Critical:** Could cause significant loss, major compliance violation, or material risk. Example: No segregation of duties in accounting. (2) **Major:** Could cause problems; needs fixing soon. Example: Password policy not enforced. (3) **Minor:** Improvement opportunity; nice to fix. Example: Efficiency improvement. Remediation timeline: Critical = <30 days, Major = 60-90 days, Minor = next audit cycle. Report: Critical findings = immediate Board notification.

**Q6: What's remediation and how do you track it?**
A: Remediation: Fixing the control/compliance issue. Process: (1) Finding = identified. (2) Management = proposes fix. (3) Auditor = agrees on remediation plan. (4) Timeline = agreed remediation date. (5) Monitoring = tracks progress. (6) Testing = verifies fix is effective. (7) Closure = finding closed when verified fixed. Tracking: Use tracking system (spreadsheet, audit tool). Status: Open, in-progress, closed. Report: Monthly status to management; quarterly to Board.

**Q7: What's continuous internal auditing?**
A: Approach: Rather than periodic audits, continuous monitoring of controls. Methods: (1) **Automated testing:** Data analytics to test controls continuously. (2) **Dashboards:** Real-time visibility into control metrics. (3) **Sampling:** Regular sampling of transactions. (4) **Alerts:** Flag anomalies automatically. Benefits: Faster detection of problems. Challenges: Requires systems/analytics capability. Usage: Large companies → continuous; smaller → periodic audits.

**Q8: How do you structure an audit team?**
A: Structure: (1) **Audit director/manager:** Plans audit, manages team. (2) **Senior auditors:** Plan/execute major audits. (3) **Auditors:** Execute audit fieldwork. (4) **Audit support:** Data analysis, documentation. Size: Depends on company size. Startup: 1 auditor or outsourced. Large: 10+ member team. Reporting: Reports to Audit Committee (Board), not to CFO/management (independence).

**Q9: What's an audit plan and how is it developed?**
A: Plan: Annual schedule of what will be audited. Process: (1) **Risk assessment:** What's high-risk? What could go wrong? (2) **Audit universe:** List all processes/systems possible to audit. (3) **Prioritization:** Rank by risk. (4) **Allocation:** How many resources available? Allocate accordingly. (5) **Timeline:** When will each audit happen? (6) **Board approval:** Present plan to Board for approval. (7) **Flexibility:** Build buffer for ad-hoc audits/investigations. Documentation: Audit plan document (5-10 pages).

**Q10: How do you work with external auditors?**
A: Coordination: (1) **Workflow:** Internal audits often provide support to external audit. (2) **Sharing:** Share findings, testing, work papers. (3) **Reliance:** External auditors may rely on internal audit work (reduces external audit cost). (4) **Communication:** Regular meetings to coordinate. (5) **Dual findings:** If both find issue → issue more credible, needs priority fix. Benefits: Coordination reduces duplicate work, overall audit efficiency improves.

**Q11: What are the top audit findings in typical companies?**
A: Common findings: (1) **Segregation of duties:** Same person requesting + approving + paying (should be different people). (2) **Access controls:** Too many people with access to sensitive systems. (3) **Password policy:** Weak passwords, shared passwords, not changed regularly. (4) **Reconciliation:** Financial accounts not reconciled regularly. (5) **Approval authority:** Approvals not getting proper sign-off. (6) **Compliance:** Policies not followed, training not completed. (7) **Documentation:** Decisions/approvals not documented. (8) **Change management:** Changes not tracked/tested before going live. Prevention: Design controls to prevent; test regularly.

**Q12: How do you conduct an exit audit of a departing executive?**
A: Audit: When executive leaves, audit their area. Purpose: (1) Verify assets (did they take anything inappropriate?). (2) Access revocation (all credentials revoked?). (3) Knowledge transfer (did they document knowledge?). (4) Compliance (did they violate any policies?). Process: (1) Pre-departure: Verify completeness of handoff. (2) Post-departure: Audit accounts, systems, files. (3) Follow-up: If issues (e.g., missing inventory), investigate. Timeline: Conducted during offboarding.

**Q13: What's SOX compliance and how does it relate to internal audit?**
A: SOX: Sarbanes-Oxley (U.S. law for public companies). Requires: (1) CEO/CFO certify financial statements. (2) Audit Committee oversight. (3) Internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR). (4) Internal audit function. Internal audit role: Test ICFR, provide assurance controls are effective, support external auditor. Cost: Significant compliance cost (10-15% of finance team). Requirement: Only for public companies; private companies not required (but can adopt voluntarily).

**Q14: How do you measure the value/ROI of internal audit?**
A: Value: (1) **Findings:** How much value does fixing findings create? (cost avoidance, efficiency). (2) **Prevention:** Prevents problems from happening (fraud, compliance). (3) **Confidence:** Board/stakeholders confidence increases. (4) **Efficiency:** Reduces external audit cost (rely on internal findings). Quantification: Difficult but try: (1) Total cost of findings (if unfixed). (2) Cost of remediation. (3) Reduce by internal audit cost. (4) Calculate ROI. Reality: Harder to quantify prevention; qualitative value substantial.

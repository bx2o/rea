# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Key FAQs (14 samples from 50+)

**Q1: What's an internal audit and how is it different from external audit?**
A: **Internal audit:** Company's own audit function (reports to Board/Management). Purpose: Verify controls working, policies followed, risks managed. Scope: Broad (any area of company). Frequency: Regular (annual, quarterly, etc.). Cost: Salary of internal auditors. **External audit:** Independent auditors (hired by company, report to Board). Purpose: Verify financial statements accurate. Scope: Narrow (financial/accounting). Frequency: Annual (required for public companies). Cost: $50K-500K+ per audit. Relationship: Internal audits provide info; external audits audit financial statements. Both valuable but different purposes.

**Q2: What's the difference between preventive and detective controls?**
A: **Preventive control:** Stops problem from happening. Example: Require approval before wire transfer (prevents unauthorized wires). Cost: Usually invested upfront. Benefits: Prevents problems. **Detective control:** Finds problem after it happens. Example: Bank reconciliation (finds wire transfer errors). Cost: Detection + remediation cost. Benefits: Catches errors, but more expensive long-term. Best: Combination. Preventive (stops issues) + Detective (catches what prevention missed).

**Q3: How do you design an audit plan?**
A: Process: (1) **Risk assessment:** Which areas are highest risk? (financial, security, compliance). (2) **Materiality:** Which areas biggest impact if fail? (3) **Frequency:** Annual? Quarterly? Continuous? (4) **Scope:** What specifically audit in that area? (5) **Resources:** What's required? (auditor time, tools). (6) **Timing:** When audit each area? (spread throughout year). (7) **Stakeholders:** Whose buy-in needed? Example: Financial processes (high risk) → quarterly audit. Hiring process (medium risk) → annual audit. Office procedures (low risk) → every 2 years. Prioritize by risk; audit high-risk more frequently.

**Q4: How do you test if a policy is actually being followed?**
A: Approach: (1) **Understand policy:** What's it supposed to do? Example: "All expenses require approval." (2) **Define test:** How to test? Example: Pull sample of 30 expenses; check if all have approval. (3) **Sampling:** Test statistically significant sample (not every transaction). (4) **Evidence:** Gather evidence (approval signatures, audit trails, confirmations). (5) **Analysis:** % compliance (29 of 30 approved = 97% compliance). (6) **Finding:** If < 100%, there's gap. (7) **Interview:** Why not 100%? (Policy unclear? Overriding? Forgotten?). TIP: Testing should be non-confrontational; goal is improvement, not blame.

**Q5: What's a corrective action and how do you track it?**
A: Corrective action = management's plan to fix audit finding. Example: Finding = "Only 80% of expenses approved." Corrective action = "We'll implement e-approval system + train managers on policy." Tracking: (1) **Commitment:** Management agrees to action (timeline, owner, resources). (2) **Implementation:** Monitor progress (is it happening?). (3) **Follow-up:** Status updates (on track? at risk?). (4) **Re-test:** Did action actually fix finding? (re-audit). (5) **Closure:** Finding closed when satisfied. Timeline: 30-90 days typical. Escalation: If not implementing after 30 days, escalate to CFO/Board.

**Q6: How do you conduct an audit interview?**
A: Approach: (1) **Prepare:** Understand policy/process before interview. (2) **Bias:** Ask open questions ("How do you approve expenses?") vs. leading questions ("You follow the approval policy, right?"). (3) **Listen:** Let person explain; don't interrupt. (4) **Probe:** If unclear, ask follow-ups. (5) **Verify:** Understand their answer. (6) **Document:** Take notes. (7) **Close:** Thank them; explain process. (8) **Tone:** Collaborative, not accusatory (goal is improvement). Avoid: Surprising them with findings; they should understand reason for audit.

**Q7: What should an audit report include?**
A: Sections: (1) **Executive summary:** High-level findings/conclusions. (2) **Background:** What was audited? Why? (3) **Scope:** What was included/excluded? (4) **Methodology:** How did we audit? (5) **Findings:** Detailed findings (what, why, evidence, impact, recommendation). Rate by severity. (6) **Conclusion:** Overall assessment (controls adequate? risks managed?). (7) **Appendix:** Evidence, detailed data, testing results. Length: 5-20 pages. Format: Professional, clear, non-accusatory. Distribute to: Management (for action), Board (for oversight).

**Q8: How often should you audit each area?**
A: Framework: **High risk:** Quarterly or more. Examples: Financial controls, security, compliance. **Medium risk:** Annually. Examples: Sales process, hiring, vendor management. **Low risk:** Every 2 years. Examples: Office procedures, team rituals. **Continuous:** High-value automated checks (daily/weekly). Examples: Expense approvals, access controls. Tradeoff: Audits take time; balance coverage with resource constraints. Start: Annual audit everything. As controls mature, reduce frequency. As risk increases, increase frequency.

**Q9: What's the difference between audit and inspection?**
A: **Audit:** Systematic review (testing, sampling, evidence gathering). Conclusion is reasonable assurance (not 100% certain). Examples: Internal audit, external audit. Purpose: Obtain assurance controls working. **Inspection:** Physical check (look, touch, verify in person). Conclusion is more certain. Example: Walk factory floor, check conditions. Purpose: Verify actual conditions match requirements. Relationship: Audits often include inspection elements (visit site, observe actual practice). Both valuable; use both.

**Q10: How do you handle audit findings that management disagrees with?**
A: Approach: (1) **Listen:** Understand management's perspective. Maybe you misunderstood. (2) **Verify:** Re-check finding. Is it accurate? (3) **Discuss:** Management explains their rationale. Maybe there's validity. (4) **Escalate:** If still disagree, escalate to CFO/General Counsel. (5) **Board:** If serious disagreement, bring to audit committee/Board. (6) **Document:** Document both audit position and management's response. (7) **Learn:** Understand why disconnect. Maybe process unclear? Tip: Auditors neutral; job is accuracy, not to "win" disagreement.

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Compliance Auditing FAQs (28 FAQs)

**Q1: What should you audit for compliance?**
A: Areas: (1) **Policies:** Are policies being followed? (2) **Procedures:** Are documented procedures being executed? (3) **Training:** Are employees trained on policies/procedures? (4) **Documentation:** Are required documents being created/maintained? (5) **Authorization:** Are approvals happening as required? (6) **Controls:** Are controls working as designed? (7) **Compliance:** Are external requirements (legal, regulatory) being met? (8) **Security:** Are security policies being followed? (9) **Finance:** Are financial controls working? (10) **HR:** Are HR policies (hiring, termination, discipline) being followed? Start with highest-risk areas.

**Q2: How do you audit a process for compliance?**
A: Approach: (1) **Understand process:** Document how process supposed to work. (2) **Sample:** Select random transactions/instances (don't audit all; sample representative). (3) **Test:** For each sample, verify it followed process. (4) **Document:** Record what you tested, results. (5) **Exceptions:** Note any deviations. (6) **Quantify:** How many non-compliant? (3 of 20 tested = 85% compliance). (7) **Investigate:** Why non-compliant? Lack of training? Process unclear? (8) **Report:** Document findings with evidence. Example: Hiring process audit: Sample 10 recent hires, verify offer letters, IP assignments, handbook acknowledgments were completed.

**Q3: What's a material finding vs. a minor finding?**
A: **Material/critical:** Non-compliance that creates significant risk (e.g., employee not signing NDA = IP risk). **Major:** Important but not immediate crisis (e.g., performance reviews skipped = talent management issue). **Minor:** Compliance issue that doesn't create significant risk (e.g., form filled out sloppily but correctly). Severity depends on: (1) **Impact:** If it goes wrong, how bad? (2) **Probability:** How likely is it to go wrong? (3) **Ease of fix:** How easy is it to fix? Examples: Material: Security control failure. Major: Inconsistent process. Minor: Documentation formatting.

**Q4: How often should you conduct compliance audits?**
A: Frequency depends on: (1) **Risk:** High-risk areas annually or semi-annually. (2) **Criticality:** Critical processes more frequently. (3) **Regulations:** Legal requirements may dictate frequency. (4) **Resources:** Limited resources = focus on highest-risk areas. (5) **History:** Areas with past findings = more frequent. Common: Annual comprehensive audit + quarterly focused audits on high-risk. Annual cycle: Plan Q1 (decide what to audit), Audit Q2-Q4 (execute), Report Q4/Q1 (findings to leadership).

**Q5: How do you communicate audit findings to management?**
A: Approach: (1) **Executive summary:** One-page summary of high-level findings. (2) **Key findings:** 3-5 most important findings, with severity. (3) **Trends:** Any patterns? (4) **Recommendations:** For each finding, recommended action. (5) **Impact:** What's the business/risk impact? (6) **Timeline:** By when should findings be addressed? (7) **Accountability:** Who's responsible for remediation? (8) **Follow-up:** When will you verify fixes? Tone: Collaborative (not "you're doing it wrong"), focused on improvement. Format: Written report + presentation to leadership.

**Q6: How do you track remediation of audit findings?**
A: Process: (1) **Action plan:** Management creates plan to address finding. (2) **Owner:** Assign responsibility (who will fix?). (3) **Timeline:** When will it be fixed? (4) **Status:** Track status (not started, in progress, complete). (5) **Verification:** When done, auditor verifies fix effective. (6) **Escalation:** If not fixed by deadline, escalate. (7) **Close-out:** Finding closed once verified. Tools: Spreadsheet or audit tools (Workiva, ACLAnalytics). Typical timeline: 30-90 days between finding and expected remediation.

**Q7: How do you determine if a policy is effective?**
A: Assessment: (1) **Compliance rate:** Are people following policy? (if <80%, probably not effective). (2) **Problem solved:** Did policy solve the problem it was meant to? (if not, needs revision). (3) **Unintended consequences:** Did policy create new problems? (if yes, needs adjustment). (4) **Employee understanding:** Do employees understand policy? (if confused, needs clarification). (5) **Enforcement:** Are violators being disciplined? (if not enforced, becomes ineffective). (6) **Evolution:** Has policy evolved with business changes? (if out-of-date, needs update). Recommendation: Review each policy annually for effectiveness.

**Q8: What's the difference between a compliance audit and a financial audit?**
A: **Compliance audit:** Reviews whether organization follows policies/procedures/regulations. Scope: All operations. Focus: Non-compliance. **Financial audit:** Reviews whether financial statements are accurate (Independent auditors typically do). Scope: Financial records, controls. Focus: Accuracy, fraud prevention. Both valuable; different purposes. Many companies have both: Internal compliance audits (improve operations) + external financial audits (assure stakeholders).

**Q9: How do you audit training and awareness?**
A: Approach: (1) **Training inventory:** What training is required? (2) **Completion tracking:** Is training being completed? (3) **Content review:** Is training covering required topics? (4) **Effectiveness:** Do employees actually understand/remember? (assess via quizzes, observation). (5) **Frequency:** Is training refreshed regularly? (annually typical). (6) **Certification:** Are people passing certification? (7) **Gaps:** Who hasn't completed training? (8) **Documentation:** Are records maintained? Process: Review training records, spot-check employees (quiz on policy), observe behavior.

**Q10: How do you handle a finding where manager/employee disagrees?**
A: Approach: (1) **Listen:** Understand their perspective. (2) **Verify:** Double-check your findings (could you be wrong?). (3) **Document:** Their disagreement/explanation. (4) **Escalate:** If genuine disagreement, escalate to leadership. (5) **Review policy:** If policy is ambiguous, clarify. (6) **Update guidance:** If needed, provide additional guidance/training. (7) **Respect authority:** Leadership makes final decision. (8) **Document outcome:** Record decision. Tone: Auditor role is to find issues; management role is to decide response.

**Q11: How do you avoid audit bias?**
A: Biases to watch: (1) **Confirmation bias:** Looking only for violations of policy. (2) **Recency bias:** Focusing on recent events vs. historical trend. (3) **Relationship bias:** Being easy on friends, hard on others. (4) **Sample bias:** Sampling in biased way (not truly random). (5) **Interpretation bias:** Interpreting findings in biased way. Mitigation: (1) **Use checklists:** Consistent, objective criteria. (2) **Random sampling:** True randomness, not convenient sampling. (3) **Document basis:** Record why you tested what. (4) **Multiple reviews:** Have another auditor review before finalizing. (5) **Audit committee:** Oversight committee reviews audit findings.

**Q12: How do you handle confidential or sensitive findings?**
A: Approach: (1) **Confidentiality:** Treat findings as confidential (not shared broadly). (2) **Need-to-know:** Share only with those who need to know. (3) **Privacy:** Respect individual privacy (don't name-and-shame). (4) **Documentation:** Keep findings secure (limited access to reports). (5) **Privilege:** Some findings may be attorney-client privileged (consult legal). (6) **Reporting:** Report findings to leadership, not widely. (7) **Remediation:** Address issues privately (don't publicize). (8) **Ethics:** Maintain professional ethics (confidentiality is key). Example: Finding that executive violates policy—report to Board, not broadcast to company.

**Q13: How do you measure audit effectiveness?**
A: Metrics: (1) **Compliance improvement:** Is compliance rate increasing over time? (2) **Finding resolution:** Are findings being fixed? (3) **Repeat findings:** Any findings recurring? (if yes, audit not effective). (4) **Risk reduction:** Are audited areas showing reduced risk? (5) **Management satisfaction:** Do managers find audits valuable? (6) **Cost:** Cost of audit vs. value of findings. (7) **Timeliness:** Are audits timely? (late audits less valuable). (8) **Coverage:** Are critical areas being audited? Evaluate annually: Is audit function adding value?

**Q14: What tools should you use for compliance auditing?**
A: Options: (1) **Spreadsheet:** Simple, but can become unwieldy (good for small audits). (2) **Specialized audit software:** (Workiva, ACLAnalytics, Artefact) More powerful, but expensive. (3) **Document review tools:** (DocuSign, Box) For contract/document auditing. (4) **Workflow automation:** (Zapier, n8n) For automated compliance checks. (5) **Checklists:** Simple checklist templates (free, effective). (6) **Database:** Custom database for audit management. Start simple (spreadsheet); upgrade as you scale.

# SECTION 11: PITFALLS, CHALLENGES & MITIGATION

## 11.1 Common Compliance Audit Pitfalls

| Pitfall                  | Problem                      | Solution                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Confirmation Bias**    | Only looking for violations  | Objective testing criteria   |
| **Inadequate Sampling**  | Sample not representative    | Statistical sampling methods |
| **Scope Creep**          | Audit expands uncontrolled   | Clear scope definition       |
| **Documentation Gaps**   | Missing evidence             | Real-time documentation      |
| **Conflict of Interest** | Auditor too close to subject | Independence requirements    |

## 11.2 Risk Mitigation Strategies

- **Independence:** Organizational and personal
- **Objectivity:** Evidence-based, not opinion-based
- **Skepticism:** Professional doubt, verification
- **Documentation:** Comprehensive workpaper evidence
- **Review:** Multiple levels of review

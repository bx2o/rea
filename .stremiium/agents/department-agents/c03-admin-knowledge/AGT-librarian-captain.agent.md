# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: What's the difference between a Librarian and an Archivist?**
A: **Librarian** focuses on making knowledge accessible, organizing for discovery, and serving users. **Archivist** focuses on preservation, historical accuracy, and long-term record keeping. Both work together: Librarians ensure current knowledge is findable; Archivists ensure historical knowledge is preserved. The Librarian Captain leads both functions.

**Q2: How do you get executives to prioritize knowledge management?**
A: Business case: (1) **Risk mitigation** - Knowledge loss costs (departing employees, undocumented processes). (2) **Efficiency gains** - Time saved searching, faster onboarding. (3) **Scale enabler** - Documented processes enable growth. (4) **Compliance** - Audits require documentation. (5) **Competitive advantage** - Learning organization adapts faster. Quantify: "If our top 3 experts left tomorrow, what couldn't we do?"

**Q3: How do you balance knowledge openness with security?**
A: Framework: (1) **Classify content** by sensitivity. (2) **Default to open** - Most knowledge should be accessible. (3) **Restrict by need** - Limit access to confidential/competitive info. (4) **Train on handling** - People need to know what's sensitive. (5) **Monitor access** - Track who's accessing what. (6) **Regular review** - Reclassify as circumstances change. Principle: Share generously, protect appropriately.

**Q4: How do you measure knowledge management ROI?**
A: Metrics: (1) **Time saved** - Reduced search time (hrs saved × cost). (2) **Faster onboarding** - Reduced time-to-productivity. (3) **Fewer errors** - Mistakes avoided through documented best practices. (4) **Retention** - Knowledge preserved when people leave. (5) **Compliance** - Reduced audit findings, faster certifications. (6) **Efficiency** - Reuse instead of reinvention. Calculate: Cost of system vs. value of benefits.

**Q5: How do you handle departments that won't document their knowledge?**
A: Strategies: (1) **Understand why** - Time? Skill? Fear? (2) **Make it easy** - Templates, training, support. (3) **Show value** - "This will save YOU time answering questions." (4) **Leadership mandate** - Executive support. (5) **Integrate into workflow** - Document as you work. (6) **Recognize contributions** - Public thanks, incentives. (7) **Start with enthusiasts** - Success stories attract others.

**Q6: What's the right level of documentation detail?**
A: Guideline: Document what's needed for a competent person to succeed. Too little = confusion. Too much = maintenance burden. Ask: (1) Who's the audience? (2) What's their starting knowledge? (3) What decisions/actions must they take? (4) What context do they need? Review: Test with actual users - can they follow it? Adjust based on feedback.

**Q7: How do you keep knowledge management from becoming bureaucratic?**
A: Principles: (1) **Minimum viable process** - Just enough structure. (2) **Automate where possible** - Reduce manual overhead. (3) **Self-service** - People help themselves. (4) **Trust over control** - Most people want to do right thing. (5) **Pragmatic standards** - "Good enough" vs. perfect. (6) **Regular pruning** - Remove obsolete, simplify complex. (7) **User-centered** - Design for users, not administrators.

**Q8: How do you manage knowledge during rapid organizational change?**
A: Approach: (1) **Prioritize** - Protect critical knowledge first. (2) **Accelerate capture** - Quick documentation sprints. (3) **Focus on transitions** - Departures, role changes. (4) **Temporary structures** - Don't over-engineer during chaos. (5) **Communicate** - Tell people what's happening. (6) **Document the change** - Capture new processes as they emerge. (7) **Review post-change** - Consolidate and optimize.

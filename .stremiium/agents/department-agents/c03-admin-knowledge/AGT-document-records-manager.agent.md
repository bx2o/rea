# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Twenty-Five Core FAQs

**Q1: What should be included in a knowledge base?**
Critical documents: (1) Onboarding materials. (2) Policies and procedures. (3) Process documentation. (4) Decision records (why did we decide X?). (5) Product knowledge. (6) Customer information. (7) Vendor/partner information. (8) FAQ/troubleshooting. (9) Templates. (10) Company history/culture guide. Organization: By department/function. Tags for cross-referencing. Keep updated.

**Q2: How do you organize documents logically?**
Approach: (1) Top-level: By department or function (Sales, Engineering, Finance). (2) Second-level: By category (Processes, Policies, Guides). (3) Third-level: By specific document (Hiring Process, Expense Policy). Use consistent naming. Enable cross-references. Tag for searchability. Test organization with new employees; refine based on feedback.

**Q3: What's a records retention policy?**
Policy: Specifies how long to keep documents. Examples: (1) Financial records: 7 years (IRS requirement). (2) Employee records: 3 years post-termination. (3) Contracts: Life of contract + 3 years. (4) Email: 1-3 years. (5) Meeting notes: 1 year. Policy should: Comply with legal requirements. Balance data security (delete when unneeded) with legal holds (keep if lawsuit pending). Document and enforce.

**Q4: How do you conduct exit interviews (capture knowledge)?**
Process: (1) Schedule before employee leaves. (2) Ask: What knowledge should company have about your projects/relationships? (3) Document: Write down responses. (4) Preserve: Store in accessible location. (5) Follow-up: If questions arise post-departure, reach out. Why: Prevents knowledge walking out door with departing employee.

**Q5: What makes a document discoverable?**
Traits: (1) **Title:** Clear, specific title (not "Q4 Report" but "Q4 2025 Sales Performance Report"). (2) **Tags:** Subject tags for categorization. (3) **Summary:** Brief summary at top. (4) **Links:** Links to related documents. (5) **Location:** Consistent location (people know where to find it). (6) **Freshness:** Date updated (people trust current info). (7) **Search-friendly:** Keywords in document for search.

**Q6: What's an Architecture Decision Record (ADR)?**
ADR: Document explaining a significant decision (architecture, process, policy). Sections: (1) **Decision:** What did we decide? (2) **Context:** Why did we need this decision? (3) **Rationale:** Why did we choose this option? (4) **Consequences:** What are trade-offs? (5) **Alternatives considered:** What else did we evaluate? Why rejected? Purpose: Preserve why decisions were made (context for future decisions). When: Document significant decisions. Review: ADRs guide future decisions.

**Q7: How often should you update documentation?**
Frequency: (1) **Critical docs:** Monthly (onboarding docs, policies). (2) **Important docs:** Quarterly (process guides, customer info). (3) **Reference docs:** Annually (company history, vendor lists). (4) **Deprecate:** Remove outdated docs (don't let them confuse people). 5-person startup: Monthly review. 100-person company: Quarterly by department. Set calendar reminder: Regular review schedule.

**Q8: What's the difference between a handbook and a standard operating procedure?**
**Handbook:** High-level policies for all employees. Examples: Code of conduct, benefits, vacation, expense policy. Audience: All employees. Length: 10-20 pages. **SOP:** Step-by-step procedure for specific task. Examples: Hiring process, expense report submission, sales process. Audience: People doing task. Length: 1-5 pages. Both: Important; complementary.

**Q9: How do you make knowledge base searchable?**
Approach: (1) **Platform:** Use platform with search (Notion, Confluence have good search). (2) **Metadata:** Tag documents (subject, author, date). (3) **Titles:** Clear, specific titles (not "Update" but "March 2026 Product Roadmap Update"). (4) **Summaries:** Executive summary at top (search shows summary). (5) **Linking:** Cross-link related docs (people browse related). (6) **Testing:** Test searches; see if people find docs. (7) **Refinement:** Adjust based on search analytics.

**Q10: What documents should have version control?**
Version control: Keep record of changes. Documents: (1) **Policies:** Track policy changes. (2) **Processes:** Track process improvements. (3) **Contracts:** Track contract negotiations. (4) **Technical specs:** Track specification changes. Not needed: Transient docs (weekly to-do lists), meeting notes (unless important decisions). Use: Simple version numbers (v1, v2) or Git-based version control. Track: Who changed? When? Why?

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT STRATEGY (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What is knowledge management and why does it matter?**
A: Knowledge management = capturing, organizing, and sharing organizational knowledge to improve decision-making and reduce duplication. Why matters: (1) **Speed:** Knowledge accessible faster than re-learning. (2) **Consistency:** All employees have same baseline. (3) **Retention:** Knowledge survives employee departures. (4) **Scale:** Company can grow without losing institutional memory. (5) **Quality:** Decisions informed by past experience. Example: Company learns best practice for customer onboarding; documents it; saves every new customer onboarding week of time.

**Q2: What types of knowledge should be captured?**
A: Capture: (1) **Processes:** How do we do things? (2) **Decisions:** What were major decisions? Why? (3) **Lessons learned:** What worked? What didn't? (4) **Best practices:** What's the best way to do X? (5) **Customer insights:** What do we know about customers? (6) **Product knowledge:** How does product work? What are gotchas? (7) **Contact/vendor info:** Who should we contact for X? (8) **Tools/systems:** How to use our tools? (9) **History:** What happened before? Context for decisions. Don't capture: (1) Individual opinions (unless decision rationale). (2) Outdated information. (3) Confidential information (trade secrets).

**Q3: Where should you store organizational knowledge?**
A: Options: (1) **Wiki (Confluence, MediaWiki):** Collaborative, good for evolving docs. (2) **Document management (SharePoint, Notion):** Organized folders, permission control. (3) **Intranet:** Company-wide hub with customizable content. (4) **FAQ/help center:** Self-service for common questions. (5) **Searchable repository:** All docs in one searchable space. Choice depends on: (1) Company size. (2) Content types. (3) Update frequency. (4) Search needs. Best practice: Single repository for most knowledge; specialized systems for specialized content (e.g., customer-facing help center separate from internal wiki).

**Q4: How do you make sure knowledge stays current?**
A: Approach: (1) **Owner assignment:** Every doc has owner responsible for updates. (2) **Review schedule:** Owner reviews doc quarterly (still accurate?). (3) **Update flag:** Out-of-date docs marked "DRAFT - NEEDS REVIEW" so people know. (4) **Expiration:** Old docs archived automatically (60-90 days past review date). (5) **Version tracking:** All changes tracked so you know what's new. (6) **Feedback:** Employees report outdated info; Knowledge Officer updates. (7) **Automation:** Systems flag out-of-date content. Reality: Documentation goes out of date; set expectations it's effort to maintain.

**Q5: How do you encourage employees to use knowledge base?**
A: Strategy: (1) **Make it easy:** Simple navigation, good search. (2) **Visible:** Link to docs from Slack, email, work apps. (3) **Training:** Teach employees where to find answers (first day onboarding). (4) **Incentives:** Reward people who document (recognition, bonus for good docs). (5) **Manager expectation:** Managers expect teams to document. (6) **Feedback loops:** Ask "Did you find the answer?" and improve docs based on feedback. (7) **Lead by example:** Leadership uses/references knowledge base. (8) **Search analytics:** Track what people search for; create docs for top searches.

**Q6: What's a good onboarding knowledge checklist?**
A: Onboarding docs: (1) **Company overview:** Mission, values, history, key metrics. (2) **Org chart:** Who does what? Reporting structure? (3) **Tools:** What tools do we use? How to access? Links? (4) **Communication norms:** How do we communicate? Response times? (5) **Calendar:** Key meetings/events? Time zones? (6) **Policies:** Benefits, time off, expenses, code of conduct. (7) **Role-specific:** What does this job do? Responsibilities? Key projects? (8) **Performance expectations:** What does good look like? How are you evaluated? (9) **Key contacts:** Who to ask for what? Slack channels? (10) **First 90 days plan:** What should you accomplish when? Access: Organized in one place; owner guides to docs during first week.

**Q7: How do you document a process well?**
A: Template: (1) **Title:** Clear name of process. (2) **Purpose:** Why do we do this? What's output? (3) **Prerequisites:** What must be done before? (4) **Step-by-step:** 5-15 numbered steps. Each step: clear action, input if needed, output. (5) **Screenshots:** Visual aids for software-based processes. (6) **Decision points:** "If X, do Y; if Z, do W." (7) **Common mistakes:** What do people get wrong? (8) **Tips:** Shortcuts, best practices. (9) **Owner:** Who maintains document? (10) **Last updated:** When was this reviewed? Length: 1-3 pages typical. Avoid: Too detailed (overwhelming). Be: Clear + actionable.

**Q8: How do you decide what knowledge to document vs. train live?**
A: Decision: **Document:** Processes that don't change often (hiring, expense reporting, onboarding). Skills that require practice (sell the product, coding standards). **Train live:** Complex skills (mentorship + practice). Soft skills (communication, leadership). Real-world problem-solving (sales techniques). High-risk decisions (terminations, major contracts). Hybrid: Document baseline; train to deepen/practice. Example: Document coding standards; have code review mentorship. Document sales process; have practice with sales coach.

**Q9: How do you create a knowledge culture where people document?**
A: Culture building: (1) **Expectation:** Leaders expect documentation (part of job). (2) **Time:** People get time to document (don't expect as-spare-time work). (3) **Visibility:** Share docs, celebrate good documentation. (4) **Quality standards:** Docs meet standards; feedback if they don't. (5) **Integration:** Documentation part of process (e.g., code review includes doc updates). (6) **Incentives:** Bonuses/recognition for knowledge contribution. (7) **Examples:** Show good documentation examples. (8) **Tools:** Make documentation easy (simple tools, templates, automation). (9) **Leadership modeling:** Leaders document their work. (10) **Feedback loops:** Ask employees if documentation helped; iterate.

**Q10: When should you retire/archive knowledge?**
A: Decision: **Archive:** Outdated processes (no longer used). Legacy information (old product versions if not supporting). Historical information (still good context, but not current process). **Delete:** Information that's wrong/misleading. Confidential information (contract ended). Guidance: Archive = keep accessible but mark as historical (people need context). Delete = only remove truly harmful content. Timeline: Review docs quarterly; archive if outdated >1 year and not referenced.

**Q11: How do you handle confidential information in knowledge base?**
A: Approach: (1) **Classify:** What information is confidential? (customer data, financial, trade secrets). (2) **Document anyway:** Document learnings without revealing confidential details. Example: "Customer X taught us that [general insight]" without naming customer. (3) **Access control:** Confidential docs have limited access (only people who need. (4) **Anonymize:** Remove names, specific numbers if possible. (5) **Secure location:** Confidential docs in encrypted vault, not public. (6) **Marking:** Mark docs "CONFIDENTIAL" so people treat carefully. (7) **Retention:** Remove confidential docs when no longer needed. Trade-off: Knowledge vs. confidentiality; find balance appropriate to information sensitivity.

**Q12: What's the difference between knowledge base and help center?**
A: **Knowledge base:** Internal documentation for employees. Covers: How we work, processes, decisions, tools, strategy. Audience: Employees. **Help center:** External documentation for customers. Covers: How to use product, common issues, self-service help. Audience: Customers. Overlap: Some content shared (product documentation); adapted for audience. Management: Different teams often own (internal = HR/Ops; external = Customer Support). Both valuable; serve different audiences.

**Q13: How do you handle knowledge for distributed/remote teams?**
A: Approach: (1) **Everything documented:** Remote teams can't "ask across desk"; docs are lifeline. (2) **Async-first:** Documents support asynchronous work (not everyone online same time). (3) **Video tutorials:** Some people learn better from video than docs. (4) **Searchability:** Documents easily searchable (critical for distributed teams). (5) **Time zones:** Documents accessible 24/7 (not dependent on person being online). (6) **Redundancy:** Key documents in multiple formats/locations. (7) **Responsiveness:** If doc doesn't answer question, process for updating quickly. Remote teams typically have better documentation (necessity drives better docs).

**Q14: What's a reasonable knowledge management budget?**
A: Budget: (1) **Small startup (<$5M):** $30-50K/year (part-time CKO, documentation tools). (2) **Growth ($5-50M):** $100-200K/year (full-time CKO, admin, tools). (3) **Mature (>$50M):** $200K-500K+ (CKO team, sophisticated tools, training). Tools: $5-20K/year (wiki, intranet, search tools). Time: Employees spend 5-10% time on documentation (varies by role). ROI: Good knowledge system saves 10%+ in employee time (faster decisions, less duplicate work).

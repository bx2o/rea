# SECTION 4: 50+ FAQs & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: PROCESS OPTIMIZATION FUNDAMENTALS (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: How do you identify processes that should be optimized?**
A: Selection: (1) **High cost:** Which process costs most? (money to optimize). (2) **High volume:** Which process done most frequently? (small improvement = big impact). (3) **Pain point:** Which process frustrates people most? (biggest complaints). (4) **Strategic:** Which process impacts strategy most? (customer experience, speed, quality critical to strategy). (5) **Bottleneck:** Which process delays others? (constrains throughput). (6) **Error rate:** Which has highest defect rate? (quality problem). (7) **Stakeholder feedback:** What customers/employees complain about? Method: Score all processes on factors above; pick highest scoring (greatest impact potential).

**Q2: What's process mapping and why is it valuable?**
A: Process mapping: Visual diagram showing process steps, decisions, inputs, outputs, people involved. Examples: Expense reimbursement flow: Employee submits expense → Manager approves → Finance processes → Employee reimbursed. Value: (1) Understand current state. (2) Identify inefficiencies (long waits? Redundant steps?). (3) Communicate process to team. (4) Basis for improvement (how to improve if you don't know current?). (5) Training (new employee learns by walking through diagram). Format: Flowchart, swimlane diagram (by role), value stream map (showing value-add vs. waste).

**Q3: What is "waste" in a process?**
A: Waste: Work that doesn't add value to customer. Examples: (1) **Waiting:** Employee waits for approval (no value to customer). (2) **Redundancy:** Process reviewed twice (one is waste). (3) **Rework:** Defect caught and fixed (waste; should have prevented). (4) **Motion:** Employee walks to get approval in person (could email). (5) **Processing:** Report created but not used (waste). (6) **Transporting:** Moving documents between offices (waste). (7) **Overproduction:** Creating more than needed (waste). Principle: Identify waste; eliminate it. Value-add: Work customer cares about; would pay for.

**Q4: What's the 5S methodology and how do you apply it?**
A: 5S: Japanese lean methodology. (1) **Sort:** Remove unnecessary items (office clutter). (2) **Set in order:** Organize remaining items logically (tools in same place). (3) **Shine:** Clean and maintain space. (4) **Standardize:** Create standard system (everyone uses same way). (5) **Sustain:** Maintain discipline (regular audits, training). Application: Office, warehouse, digital files. Example: Engineering lab → Sort (remove old equipment) → Set order (organize tools by project) → Shine (clean) → Standardize (everyone knows where everything is) → Sustain (weekly audit). Result: Cleaner, faster, fewer mistakes.

**Q5: How do you do root cause analysis?**
A: Process: (1) **Problem statement:** What's the problem? Example: "Expense reimbursement takes 3 weeks." (2) **Symptoms:** What do we observe? (3) **Ask why (5 times):** Why is reimbursement slow? "Finance is slow" → Why finance slow? "Too much email back-and-forth" → Why back-and-forth? (continues until real cause found). (4) **Root cause:** Real cause (not symptom). (5) **Solution:** Fix root cause (not just symptom). Example: Root cause "No clear approval process" → Solution "Create approval checklist." Tool: 5-why diagram. Importance: Fix root cause; fixing symptom recurs.

**Q6: What's the difference between improvement and total redesign?**
A: **Improvement:** Make current process better (eliminate waste, streamline). Example: Current process 20 steps → 12 steps. 20% faster. **Redesign:** Completely new approach. Example: Current process manual approval → new process automated system (80% faster). Decision: (1) **Improvement scale:** If process mostly good, just slow → improve (lower risk). (2) **Redesign scale:** If process fundamentally broken → redesign (required, higher risk). **Hybrid:** Often combine (improve core process; redesign specific steps). Timing: Improvement 2-3 months; redesign 3-6 months.

**Q7: How do you measure process improvement?**
A: Metrics: (1) **Speed:** Cycle time (time from start to finish). Example: Expense reimbursement time reduced from 21 days to 7 days. (2) **Cost:** Cost per transaction. Example: Processing expense cost reduced from $5 to $2. (3) **Quality:** Error rate. Example: Reimbursement errors reduced from 5% to 1%. (4) **Volume:** Throughput (how many per day). Example: Processed 50 expense reports per day → 150 per day. (5) **Satisfaction:** Customer/employee satisfaction. Example: Satisfaction increased from 60% to 85%. Measurement: Before → Make change → After. Compare to confirm improvement is real.

**Q8: How do you get team buy-in for process changes?**
A: Approach: (1) **Explain why:** Why change? What problem fixing? (2) **Involve them:** Ask team for ideas (not top-down mandate). (3) **Pilot:** Test in small area first; prove it works. (4) **Training:** Teach new process clearly. (5) **Support:** Help during transition. (6) **Celebrate:** Highlight wins and improvements. (7) **Listen:** Feedback → adjust if needed. (8) **Sustained:** Keep new process in place (don't revert). Resistance: Common; address concerns. Often resistance because: (a) Fear of job loss (reassure). (b) Effort to learn new thing (support). (c) New process seems worse initially (takes time to adapt).

**Q9: What's a Kaizen event and when does it help?**
A: Kaizen event: Rapid improvement week. Team dedicates week to improve specific process. Process: (1) **Plan:** Pick process to improve; assemble team. (2) **Analyze:** Map process, identify waste, brainstorm ideas. (3) **Implement:** Implement quick improvements (some same week). (4) **Sustain:** Assign owner to maintain improvements. Benefits: (1) Rapid improvement (week vs. month of planning). (2) Team engagement (feels empowered). (3) Quick wins (morale booster). (4) Momentum (sparks culture). Timing: 4-5 day intensive session. Frequency: 2-4 Kaizens per year.

**Q10: How do you prevent rework and errors?**
A: Prevention: (1) **Clear process:** Document what should happen (checklist). (2) **Quality gate:** Review at critical points (catch errors early). (3) **Training:** Make sure people know how to do it right. (4) **Tools:** System tools that enforce correctness (validation, checklists). (5) **Metrics:** Track error rate; trend visible to team. (6) **Root cause:** When error occurs, understand why (system issue? Training issue? Lack of clarity?). (7) **Fix:** Address root cause. Examples: Expense errors → require receipt copy attached. Data entry errors → auto-validation in system. Process: Prevention >> Detection >> Rework. Best: Prevent errors (not catch after).

**Q11: How do you automate processes?**
A: Approach: (1) **Optimize first:** Before automating, optimize (eliminate waste). (2) **Identify:** Which steps are repetitive/manual? (candidates). (3) **Value:** What's automation value? (cost, speed, accuracy). (4) **Tool selection:** What tool? (software, AI, RPA [robotic process automation]). (5) **Implementation:** Set up tool; train team. (6) **Monitoring:** Tool working? Catching issues? (7) **Refinement:** Adjust tool as needed. Examples: Expense reimbursement data → auto-populate from receipt. Customer onboarding → auto-generate welcome emails. Invoice processing → auto-extract invoice data. Caveat: Not everything should be automated (some human judgment needed).

**Q12: How do you sustain improvements?**
A: Sustain: (1) **Reinforce:** Regular training (new hires learn right way). (2) **Audit:** Regular checks (is new process being followed?). (3) **Monitor metrics:** Dashboard showing performance (visible to team). (4) **Recognition:** Acknowledge team following new process. (5) **Address deviations:** If people reverting to old way, understand why; address. (6) **Continuous improvement:** Keep improving (never "done"). (7) **Responsibility:** Assign owner (accountable for sustaining). Danger: Team improves process, then reverts to old way (because wasn't embedded). Success: New way becomes "how we work."

**Q13: How do you estimate cost of improvement effort?**
A: Estimation: (1) **Current cost:** What does current process cost annually? (labor, rework, waste). (2) **Improvement cost:** What does improvement cost? (design, implementation, training). (3) **Benefit:** What's the annual benefit? (cost reduction + speed improvement + quality). (4) **ROI:** Benefit / Cost = ROI. (5) **Payback period:** How long to recover investment? (typically 6-12 months for good projects). (6) **Avoid:** Over-investing in low-impact improvements. Hurdle rate: Typically look for payback <12 months or ROI >100%.

**Q14: What are common optimization mistakes?**
A: Mistakes: (1) **Optimizing wrong process:** Pick high-impact process first. (2) **Optimizing process that's broken:** Fix it instead of incrementally improving. (3) **No measurement:** Hard to know if improvement worked. (4) **Lack of buy-in:** Team doesn't see value; reverts to old way. (5) **Over-engineering:** Solution more complex than needed. (6) **Temporary improvement:** Improves for 3 months; reverts. (7) **Blaming people:** Problem is process, not people. (8) **Too fast:** Change too quick leads to resistance and failure. Best practice: Right process, good measurement, team buy-in, sustained focus.

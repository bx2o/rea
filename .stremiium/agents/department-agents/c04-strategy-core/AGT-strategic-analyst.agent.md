# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Twenty-Five Core FAQs

**Q1: How do you conduct competitive analysis?**
Process: (1) Identify competitors (direct and indirect). (2) Gather info (website, product, pricing, marketing, news). (3) Analyze: Products, strategy, strengths, weaknesses, positioning. (4) Trend: How are they changing? (5) Intelligence: What can we learn? Competitive advantages? (6) Report findings. Benefit: Understand competitive landscape, identify threats and opportunities.

**Q2: What's TAM, SAM, SOM?**
**TAM (Total Addressable Market):** Total market size (if we captured entire market). **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** Market we can realistically address (geography, segment). **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** Market we can realistically capture (with resources). Example: TAM for CRM might be $10B. Our SAM might be SMB segment = $2B. Our SOM year 1 might be $50M. Purpose: Understand opportunity size and realistic targets.

**Q3: What's market segmentation?**
Segmentation: Dividing market into groups (segments) with common characteristics. Approach: (1) Identify segmentation criteria (company size, industry, geography, use case). (2) Size each segment. (3) Analyze each segment (attractiveness, competition, pricing). (4) Prioritize (which segments most attractive?). (5) Tailor strategy for high-priority segments. Benefit: Focus on most valuable/accessible segments.

**Q4: How do you do a SWOT analysis?**
SWOT: (1) **Strengths:** What are we good at? (2) **Weaknesses:** What are we bad at? (3) **Opportunities:** What external opportunities? (4) **Threats:** What external threats? Purpose: Understand competitive position. Use: Guide strategy (build on strengths, mitigate weaknesses, pursue opportunities, defend against threats).

**Q5: What's a competitive win/loss analysis?**
Analysis: Study recent wins (customers chose us) and losses (customers chose competitor). For each: Why did customer choose us or competitor? What was the decision criteria? What would have changed outcome? Output: Insights on competitive positioning, messaging, product gaps. Benefit: Understand why we win/lose; improve win rate.

**Q6: How do you assess market attractiveness?**
Approach: Evaluate on factors: (1) Market size (large vs. small). (2) Growth rate (growing vs. declining). (3) Competition intensity (crowded vs. less competitive). (4) Profitability (high vs. low margins). (5) Barriers to entry (high or low). (6) Customer accessibility (easy to reach?). (7) Regulatory environment. Score each factor; identify most attractive markets. Focus resources on attractive markets.

**Q7: What's due diligence in M&A?**
Due diligence: Investigation before acquisition. Covers: (1) Financial (revenues, profitability, cash flow). (2) Customer (customer concentration, churn, contracts). (3) Product (product quality, roadmap, IP). (4) Team (key people, retention). (5) Operational (processes, systems). (6) Legal/compliance. (7) Market fit (does acquisition fit strategy?). Output: Assessment of acquisition value and risks. Duration: 4-12 weeks typically.

**Q8: How do you forecast market trends?**
Approach: (1) Analyze historical data (what's trended). (2) Identify drivers (what drives trends?). (3) Monitor signals (early indicators of change). (4) Scenario planning (if X happens, market might go Y). (5) Expert input (what do industry experts predict?). Duration: 2-4 weeks. Output: Trend forecast guiding strategy.

**Q9: What's a business case?**
Business case: Justification for investment/decision. Sections: (1) **Problem:** What problem are we solving? (2) **Opportunity:** How big? What's upside? (3) **Solution:** What are we doing? (4) **Investment:** How much $ and effort? (5) **Return:** What's ROI/payback? (6) **Risk:** What could go wrong? (7) **Recommendation:** Should we proceed? Purpose: Justify investment to leadership.

**Q10: How do you benchmark pricing?**
Approach: (1) Research competitor pricing (public pricing, sales calls, industry reports). (2) Understand value (what value are customers getting?). (3) Compare: How does our pricing compare to competitors? (4) Analyze: Are we expensive or cheap? Is pricing justified? (5) Recommend: Should we change pricing? Output: Pricing analysis guiding pricing decisions.

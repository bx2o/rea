# SECTION 4: 50+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions

### Category 1: Security Strategy (12 FAQs)

**Q1: How do you build an effective security strategy?**
A: Framework: (1) **Business context:** Understand business objectives, risk appetite, and digital transformation roadmap. (2) **Current state:** Comprehensive security assessment (people, process, technology). (3) **Threat landscape:** Identify relevant threat actors and attack vectors. (4) **Gap analysis:** Compare current state to security framework (NIST CSF, CIS Controls). (5) **Roadmap:** Prioritized initiatives across 1-3 year horizon. (6) **Investment:** Resource requirements (people, technology, services). (7) **Metrics:** KPIs to track progress. (8) **Governance:** RACI, steering committee, reporting. Output: Living document reviewed quarterly, updated annually.

**Q2: What's the difference between compliance and security?**
A: **Compliance:** Meeting specific regulatory or standard requirements (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR). Focused on documentation, controls, audits. Pass/fail against defined criteria. **Security:** Actual protection of assets from threats. Focused on risk reduction, threat detection, incident response. Continuous improvement. **Relationship:** Compliance is a subset of security. You can be compliant but not secure (checkbox security). You should be both. Use compliance as a baseline, build security beyond it.

**Q3: How do you prioritize security investments?**
A: Methodology: (1) **Risk-based:** Highest risk reduction per dollar spent. (2) **Business impact:** Protect critical business processes first. (3) **Compliance requirements:** Meet non-negotiable obligations. (4) **Quick wins:** Low effort, high impact to build momentum. (5) **Foundational:** Security hygiene before advanced tools. (6) **Threat landscape:** Address actively exploited vulnerabilities. Framework: Annual security budget typically 5-12% of IT budget, varying by industry risk profile. Allocate: 40% operations, 30% tools/technology, 20% people, 10% services/consulting.

**Q4: How do you measure security program effectiveness?**
A: Multi-layered approach: **Operational metrics:** MTTD, MTTR, MTTC, vulnerability SLAs. **Compliance metrics:** Audit findings, control effectiveness ratings. **Risk metrics:** Risk register trends, risk reduction achieved. **Business metrics:** Incidents with business impact, cost of security vs cost of breaches. **Maturity metrics:** Capability maturity (CMMI levels). Dashboard: Weekly operational, monthly risk/compliance, quarterly board reporting. Avoid: Vanity metrics (number of tools, spend) that don't correlate to risk reduction.

**Q5: How do you balance security and business agility?**
A: Principle: Security should enable, not block, business innovation. Approach: (1) **Risk-based:** Not all risks equal; accept reasonable risk for business value. (2) **Automation:** Automate security controls to reduce friction. (3) **Shift-left:** Build security into development process, not gate at end. (4) **Guardrails:** Provide secure defaults and paved roads. (5) **Fast feedback:** Rapid security feedback to developers. (6) **Business context:** Understand what teams are trying to achieve; find secure ways. (7) **Compromise:** When security must win, explain why clearly. When business needs can be accommodated safely, say yes.

**Q6: What's a zero-trust architecture and how do you implement it?**
A: **Concept:** Never trust, always verify. No implicit trust based on network location. Verify every access request regardless of source. **Principles:** (1) Verify explicitly (authenticate/authorize every access). (2) Use least privilege access. (3) Assume breach (segmentation, monitoring). **Implementation:** (1) Identity verification (strong auth, MFA). (2) Device health verification. (3) Micro-segmentation (network zones). (4) Data classification and protection. (5) Continuous monitoring. Phased approach: Start with critical assets, expand over 2-3 years.

**Q7: How do you build a security-aware culture?**
A: Multi-pronged: (1) **Leadership:** Executives model security behavior, communicate importance. (2) **Training:** Mandatory security awareness for all, role-specific for technical roles. (3) **Simulations:** Regular phishing tests, tabletop exercises. (4) **Recognition:** Reward security-conscious behavior. (5) **Simplicity:** Make secure choices the easy choices. (6) **Transparency:** Share security incidents and lessons learned. (7) **Ownership:** Everyone responsible for security, not just security team. Metrics: Training completion, phishing pass rates, security incident reduction, employee security survey scores.

**Q8: How do you approach cloud security differently?**
A: **Shared responsibility model:** Cloud provider secures infrastructure ("of the cloud"), customer secures what they put in cloud ("in the cloud"). **Key differences:** (1) **Speed:** Cloud moves fast; security must keep pace with automation. (2) **Visibility:** Need cloud-native security tools (CSPM, CWPP). (3) **Identity-centric:** Network perimeters dissolve; identity becomes primary control. (4) **Ephemeral:** Workloads spin up/down; agentless monitoring often needed. (5) **API-driven:** Everything automated via APIs; security as code. Tools: CSPM (Prisma, Wiz), CWPP (CrowdStrike, Sysdig), CASB, CIEM.

**Q9: What security frameworks should we follow?**
A: **Primary frameworks:** (1) **NIST CSF** (Cybersecurity Framework) - Risk-based, flexible, US-focused. (2) **CIS Controls** - Prescriptive, prioritized actions. (3) **ISO 27001** - International standard, certification possible. **Compliance frameworks:** (1) **SOC 2** - Service organization controls (US). (2) **GDPR** - EU data protection. (3) **PCI DSS** - Payment card security. (4) **HIPAA** - Healthcare (if applicable). Approach: Use NIST CSF or CIS as foundation, map to compliance requirements. Don't try to follow everything; pick based on industry and customer requirements.

**Q10: How do you handle security budgeting and ROI?**
A: **Budget allocation:** (1) **Operations (40%):** SOC, tools maintenance, audits. (2) **Technology (30%):** New security tools, upgrades. (3) **People (20%):** Salaries, training, contractors. (4) **Services (10%):** Consulting, pen testing, IR retainer. **ROI calculation:** Security ROI is risk reduction. (1) **Avoided loss:** Cost of breaches prevented. (2) **Efficiency:** Automation reducing manual effort. (3) **Compliance value:** Customer trust, deal velocity. (4) **Business enablement:** Secure digital transformation. Framework: Annual security spend typically 5-12% of IT budget. Benchmark against industry peers.

**Q11: How do you report security to the board?**
A: **Cadence:** Quarterly formal board reporting, annual deep-dive, immediate notification for material incidents. **Content:** (1) **Risk landscape:** Current threats relevant to business. (2) **Security posture:** High-level metrics (MTTD/MTTR, compliance status). (3) **Incidents:** Material incidents, lessons learned. (4) **Program progress:** Strategic initiatives, maturity improvements. (5) **Investment needs:** Major capital or staffing requests. (6) **Benchmarking:** How we compare to peers. **Format:** Visual dashboards, narrative summary, no technical jargon. Focus: Business risk, not technical details.

**Q12: How do you build and retain a security team?**
A: **Structure:** CISO, Security Architects, SOC Analysts (L1/L2/L3), Incident Responders, Compliance Manager, AppSec Engineers, Security Engineers. **Hiring:** (1) **Diverse pipelines:** Traditional security, IT, developers, military, academics. (2) **Skills over degrees:** Certifications help (CISSP, OSCP, GSEC) but skills matter more. (3) **Culture fit:** Team players, continuous learners, ethical mindset. **Retention:** (1) **Career paths:** Clear progression (analyst → senior → lead → architect). (2) **Learning budget:** Conferences, training, certifications. (3) **Challenging work:** Interesting problems, not just alert triage. (4) **Compensation:** Market-competitive; security talent shortage drives premiums. (5) **Mission:** Meaningful work protecting important things.

### Category 2: Threat Intelligence & Defense (14 FAQs)

**Q13: How do you build a threat intelligence program?**
A: **Components:** (1) **Sources:** Open source (OSINT), commercial feeds, industry sharing (ISACs), government (CISA), internal telemetry. (2) **Analysis:** Threat analysts who contextualize intelligence for your environment. (3) **Dissemination:** Getting intel to right people (SOC, IR, leadership). (4) **Feedback:** Measuring intel value and improving. **Maturity levels:** (1) Consume feeds. (2) Analyze and prioritize. (3) Produce internal intelligence. (4) Share with community. Investment: Start with free/OSINT, add commercial as maturity grows. Team: 2-4 dedicated threat intel analysts for mid-size org.

**Q14: What's the difference between threat hunting and incident response?**
A: **Threat hunting:** Proactive search for threats that evaded detection. Assumes compromise. Hypothesis-driven investigation. Time to spend: 20% of SOC time on hunting. **Incident response:** Reactive response to confirmed security incidents. Structured process (preparation, detection, containment, eradication, recovery, lessons learned). Relationship: Hunting finds things that become incidents. IR feeds lessons back into hunting hypotheses.

**Q15: How do you design an effective SOC?**
A: **Models:** (1) **In-house:** Full control, highest cost. (2) **Virtual:** Analysts distributed, cheaper. (3) **Co-managed:** Internal + MSSP hybrid. (4) **Fully outsourced:** Lowest control, cheapest. **Capabilities:** (1) **24/7 monitoring:** Never unmonitored. (2) **Tiered analysts:** L1 (triage), L2 (investigation), L3 (hunting, forensics). (3) **Technology stack:** SIEM, EDR, NDR, threat intel platform. (4) **Metrics-driven:** MTTD, MTTR, false positive rate. **Staffing:** Roughly 8-12 analysts per 24/7 coverage (3 shifts + weekends + vacation coverage). Cost: $1-3M annually for mid-size in-house SOC.

**Q16: What security technologies are essential?**
A: **Foundational (must-have):** (1) **EDR:** Endpoint detection and response (CrowdStrike, SentinelOne). (2) **SIEM:** Security information and event management (Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel, Chronicle). (3) **Email security:** Advanced phishing protection (Proofpoint, Mimecast). (4) **Firewall/NGFW:** Network perimeter control. (5) **Identity:** MFA, SSO (Okta, Azure AD). **Advanced:** (1) **NDR:** Network detection and response. (2) **SOAR:** Security orchestration and automation. (3) **CSPM:** Cloud security posture management. (4) **Threat intel platform:** Intel aggregation and analysis. Approach: Build foundational first, add advanced as maturity grows.

**Q17: How do you reduce alert fatigue in the SOC?**
A: **Problem:** SOC analysts overwhelmed by thousands of alerts, miss real threats. **Solutions:** (1) **Tuning:** Adjust detection rules to reduce false positives. (2) **Enrichment:** Add context to alerts (asset criticality, user risk). (3) **Correlation:** Group related alerts into incidents. (4) **Automation:** Automated triage and response for known patterns. (5) **Threat intel:** Prioritize alerts with IOC matches. (6) **ML/UEBA:** Behavioral analytics to find anomalies. Target: <50 alerts per analyst per day requiring human review.

**Q18: How do you approach penetration testing?**
A: **Types:** (1) **External:** Test internet-facing systems. (2) **Internal:** Test from inside network. (3) **Web app:** Application-focused. (4) **Social engineering:** Phishing, vishing tests. (5) **Red team:** Full-simulation, stealthy. (6) **Purple team:** Collaborative offense + defense. **Frequency:** External quarterly, internal annually, app with major releases, red team annually. **Vendors:** Specialized firms (not general IT consultants). **Scope:** Defined targets, rules of engagement, emergency contacts. **Remediation:** Critical findings fixed within 7 days, high within 30 days. Retest to validate fixes.

**Q19: What is threat modeling and how do you use it?**
A: **Concept:** Systematic analysis of threats to a system or application. Identify what could go wrong before it does. **Methodologies:** (1) **STRIDE:** Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Info disclosure, DoS, Elevation. (2) **PASTA:** Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis. (3) **MITRE ATT&CK:** Tactics and techniques mapping. **When:** Early in design phase (shift-left), at major architecture changes, after incidents. **Output:** Threat model document, security requirements, prioritized controls. **Tools:** Microsoft Threat Modeling Tool, IriusRisk, manual threat modeling workshops.

**Q20: How do you secure APIs?**
A: **Risks:** APIs are increasingly attacked (BOLA, injection, auth bypass). **Controls:** (1) **Authentication:** Strong API keys, OAuth 2.0, mTLS. (2) **Authorization:** Principle of least privilege, validate every request. (3) **Input validation:** Sanitize all inputs, strict schemas. (4) **Rate limiting:** Prevent abuse and DoS. (5) **Encryption:** TLS 1.2+ in transit. (6) **Logging:** Comprehensive API audit logs. (7) **Discovery:** Maintain API inventory (shadow API discovery). (8) **Testing:** API security testing in CI/CD. Tools: API gateways (Kong, Apigee), WAAP (web app API protection), API security scanners.

**Q21: How do you protect against ransomware?**
A: **Defense in depth:** (1) **Email security:** Block phishing emails delivering ransomware. (2) **Endpoint protection:** EDR to detect and block ransomware behavior. (3) **Network segmentation:** Limit lateral movement. (4) **Backup strategy:** 3-2-1 backup (3 copies, 2 media, 1 offline/offsite), tested restoration. (5) **Access control:** Limited admin rights, application whitelisting. (6) **Patch management:** Rapid patching of known exploited vulnerabilities. (7) **Monitoring:** Detect encryption activity early. **Response plan:** Pre-defined decision tree (to pay or not to pay), law enforcement coordination, communication plan, recovery procedures.

**Q22: How do you approach supply chain security?**
A: **Risk:** Third-party software, libraries, vendors introduce vulnerabilities. **Controls:** (1) **Vendor assessment:** Security questionnaires, evidence of controls, right to audit. (2) **Software composition analysis (SCA):** Identify vulnerable libraries. (3) **Code signing:** Verify software integrity. (4) **Least privilege:** Limit vendor access. (5) **Monitoring:** Monitor vendor security news. (6) **Incident response:** Include vendors in IR plans. **SolarWinds lesson:** No implicit trust in vendors; verify everything. SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) increasingly required.

**Q23: What is deception technology (honeypots)?**
A: **Concept:** Deploy fake assets (systems, data, credentials) that legitimate users never touch, but attackers will. When accessed, high-confidence alert of intrusion. **Types:** (1) **Honeypots:** Fake systems. (2) **Honeytokens:** Fake credentials/data. (3) **Honeyfiles:** Fake sensitive documents. **Value:** High-signal, low-noise detection. Confirms active intrusion vs scanning. **Considerations:** Legal (entrapment concerns - generally not for internal use), maintenance (keep realistic), placement (where attackers will find them).

**Q24: How do you secure containers and Kubernetes?**
A: **Container risks:** Image vulnerabilities, misconfiguration, runtime attacks. **Controls:** (1) **Image scanning:** Scan for CVEs before deployment (Trivy, Anchore, Snyk). (2) **Minimal images:** Reduce attack surface. (3) **Registry security:** Private registry, signed images. (4) **Runtime protection:** Container security monitoring (Falco, Sysdig). (5) **Kubernetes hardening:** CIS benchmarks, pod security policies, network policies. (6) **Secrets:** Never hardcode; use secrets management. (7) **CSPM:** Cloud security posture for Kubernetes. DevSecOps: Security built into container CI/CD pipeline.

**Q25: How do you detect insider threats?**
A: **Types:** Malicious (intent to harm) vs negligent (mistakes). **Indicators:** (1) **Behavioral:** Access patterns, working hours, data downloads. (2) **Technical:** USB usage, cloud uploads, email attachments. (3) **HR-related:** Performance issues, financial stress, termination risk. **Controls:** (1) **UEBA:** User and entity behavior analytics. (2) **DLP:** Data loss prevention. (3) **Access logging:** Comprehensive audit trails. (4) **Least privilege:** Limit access to need-to-know. (5) **Culture:** Reporting mechanisms, positive culture reduces malicious intent. **Privacy balance:** Monitor for security without invasive surveillance. Clear policies communicated to employees.

**Q26: How do you approach mobile security?**
A: **Risks:** Devices everywhere, mix personal/business, app risks, lost devices. **MDM/MAM:** Mobile device/app management for corporate devices. **Controls:** (1) **Enrollment:** Device management profile. (2) **Policies:** Encryption required, strong passcode, remote wipe capability. (3) **App management:** Approved app store, app vetting. (4) **Containerization:** Separate work/personal data. (5) **Network:** VPN for sensitive access. (6) **BYOD:** Bring your own device policies - more limited control, focus on app-level containerization. **Testing:** Mobile app security testing, reverse engineering assessment.

### Category 3: Incident Response & Forensics (12 FAQs)

**Q27: How do you build an incident response plan?**
A: **Framework (NIST SP 800-61):** (1) **Preparation:** IR team, tools, playbooks, training. (2) **Detection & Analysis:** Identify incidents, assess severity. (3) **Containment:** Short-term (stop bleeding), long-term (secure systems). (4) **Eradication:** Remove threat actor, malware. (5) **Recovery:** Restore systems, return to normal. (6) **Post-Incident:** Lessons learned, improvements. **Documentation:** IR plan, contact lists, playbooks for common scenarios (ransomware, data breach, DDoS). **Tabletop exercises:** Practice quarterly. **Retainer:** Pre-negotiated contract with IR firm for surge capacity.

**Q28: What should be in an incident response playbook?**
A: **Per scenario:** (1) **Trigger:** Detection criteria, alert sources. (2) **Severity:** Criteria for P1 (critical) vs P4 (low). (3) **Roles:** Who does what (IR lead, technical lead, communications, legal). (4) **Containment:** Specific steps for this scenario. (5) **Evidence:** What to preserve and how. (6) **Communication:** Internal (leadership, legal), external (customers, regulators, media). (7) **Recovery:** Steps to restore services. (8) **Indicators:** Specific IOCs to hunt for. Scenarios: Ransomware, business email compromise, data breach, insider threat, DDoS, malware outbreak.

**Q29: How do you classify incident severity?**
A: **P1 (Critical):** Active breach of sensitive data, ransomware spreading, critical service down. Response: All hands, executive notification, war room. **P2 (High):** Confirmed compromise limited scope, malware contained, suspicious activity on sensitive system. Response: IR team activated, management notification. **P3 (Medium):** Malware blocked by controls, phishing attempt, suspicious login. Response: SOC investigation, no escalation. **P4 (Low):** Scanning, probes, blocked attacks. Response: Logged, no response. Criteria: Data sensitivity, system criticality, active vs attempted, spread potential.

**Q30: How do you conduct digital forensics?**
A: **Process:** (1) **Identification:** Scope of investigation. (2) **Preservation:** Secure evidence, chain of custody. (3) **Collection:** Disk images, memory dumps, logs. (4) **Examination:** Analysis of evidence. (5) **Analysis:** Determine what happened. (6) **Reporting:** Findings for legal, management. **Tools:** FTK, EnCase, Autopsy, Volatility (memory), Splunk (logs). **Legal considerations:** Chain of custody, attorney-client privilege, evidence integrity. **Internal vs external:** Internal for operational incidents, external forensics for legal/regulatory matters (more credible).

**Q31: When do you involve law enforcement?**
A: **Criteria:** (1) **Criminal activity:** Clear crime (hacking, fraud, extortion). (2) **National security:** APT, nation-state attribution. (3) **Significant loss:** Material financial impact. (4) **Data breach:** Subject to breach notification laws. **When:** Early (parallel to IR, not after). **Who:** FBI (US) for cybercrime, Secret Service for financial crimes, CISA for critical infrastructure. **Considerations:** Law enforcement involvement may become public, may take evidence, may not prioritize your case. Preserve attorney-client privilege if using outside counsel as interface.

**Q32: How do you handle breach notification requirements?**
A: **GDPR:** 72 hours to supervisory authority, without undue delay to data subjects if high risk. **US state laws:** Varies by state, typically "without unreasonable delay." **Contracts:** Customer contracts may have specific notification requirements. **Process:** (1) **Assessment:** Determine if breach occurred, what data, whose data. (2) **Timing:** Meet legal deadlines. (3) **Content:** What happened, what data, what we're doing, what you should do. (4) **Channels:** Email, website, media (for large breaches). (5) **Regulators:** File required reports. **Preparation:** Template notifications, legal pre-approval of templates, contact databases.

**Q33: What is a war room during incidents?**
A: **Concept:** Dedicated physical or virtual space for IR team coordination during major incidents. **Composition:** IR lead, technical investigators, communications lead, legal counsel, business liaison. **Infrastructure:** Separate communication channels (out-of-band from potentially compromised systems), dedicated collaboration tools, video conferencing. **Process:** Regular standups (every 2-4 hours), centralized incident timeline, decision log, action tracking. **Executive updates:** Structured briefings to leadership. **Stand-down:** Formal closure when incident contained and recovery complete.

**Q34: How do you learn from incidents?**
A: **Post-incident review (PIR):** Within 1 week of closure. **Attendees:** IR team, affected business units, optional external facilitator. **Format:** Blameless (focus on process, not people), structured. **Questions:** (1) What happened? Timeline. (2) How did we detect it? Could we detect sooner? (3) How did we respond? What worked? What didn't? (4) What was impact? (5) Root cause? (6) What are we changing? **Output:** PIR report, action items with owners, updated playbooks. **Sharing:** Share lessons with broader security community (anonymized) to help industry.

**Q35: How do you handle ransomware specifically?**
A: **Special considerations:** (1) **Isolate:** Disconnect affected systems immediately to prevent spread. (2) **Preserve evidence:** Forensic images before cleanup (for attribution, legal). (3) **Payment decision:** Pre-defined criteria (typically no payment, but case-by-case for critical infrastructure). (4) **Law enforcement:** Notify early. (5) **Recovery:** Clean rebuild vs restore from backup (clean rebuild preferred). (6) **Communication:** PR strategy, customer notification. **Prevention:** Backup strategy is primary recovery mechanism. Regular restore testing. Offline backups. **Aftermath:** Comprehensive forensic analysis (how did they get in, what did they access, are they still there?).

**Q36: How do you detect and respond to business email compromise (BEC)?**
A: **BEC:** Social engineering to trick employees into wiring money or sharing data. Often CEO/CFO impersonation. **Detection:** (1) **Email security:** Flag external emails pretending to be internal. (2) **Financial controls:** Dual approval for wire transfers, verify via phone for new payees. (3) **Training:** Staff awareness of BEC tactics. (4) **Monitoring:** Unusual invoice changes, new vendor requests. **Response:** (1) **Stop transfer:** Contact bank immediately if wire not yet complete. (2) **Law enforcement:** FBI IC3 complaint. (3) **Contain:** Secure compromised email accounts. (4) **Review:** How did they target us? Improve controls.

### Category 4: Compliance & Risk (12 FAQs)

**Q37: How do you achieve SOC 2 compliance?**
A: **What:** SOC 2 Type II attestation that service organization controls are designed and operating effectively for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and/or privacy. **Process:** (1) **Gap assessment:** Compare current state to Trust Services Criteria. (2) **Remediation:** Implement missing controls. (3) **Documentation:** Policies, procedures, evidence. (4) **Audit:** External auditor examines controls over 6-12 month period. (5) **Report:** Auditor issues SOC 2 Type II report. **Timeline:** 6-12 months from start to first report. **Maintenance:** Continuous monitoring, annual re-audit. **Cost:** $50-200K depending on size and complexity.

**Q38: What's the difference between SOC 2 Type I and Type II?**
A: **Type I:** "Are controls designed appropriately?" Point-in-time assessment (single day). Easier, faster, less valuable. **Type II:** "Are controls designed AND operating effectively?" Period of time assessment (typically 6-12 months). Harder, longer, more valuable. Customers and partners increasingly require Type II. **Recommendation:** Go straight to Type II if possible; Type I is rarely worth it except as intermediate step.

**Q39: How do you implement ISO 27001?**
A: **What:** International standard for information security management systems (ISMS). **Process:** (1) **Scope:** Define what systems and locations are in scope. (2) **Risk assessment:** Identify and assess information security risks. (3) **Risk treatment:** Select controls from Annex A (114 controls across 14 categories). (4) **ISMS documentation:** Policies, procedures, records. (5) **Implementation:** Deploy controls. (6) **Internal audit:** Self-assessment. (7) **Certification audit:** External auditor stage 1 (documentation) and stage 2 (implementation). **Maintenance:** Surveillance audits annually, recertification every 3 years.

**Q40: How do you ensure GDPR compliance?**
A: **GDPR:** EU data protection regulation with global reach (applies if processing EU residents' data). **Key requirements:** (1) **Lawful basis:** Must have legal basis for processing. (2) **Data subject rights:** Right to access, rectification, erasure, portability. (3) **Privacy by design:** Build privacy in from start. (4) **Breach notification:** 72 hours to supervisory authority. (5) **DPO:** Data Protection Officer if core activity is large-scale monitoring or sensitive data. (6) **Records:** Maintain processing records. (7) **Cross-border:** Mechanisms for data transfer outside EU. **Approach:** Privacy impact assessments, data mapping, consent management, technical controls (encryption, access control).

**Q41: What is a security risk assessment?**
A: **Process:** (1) **Asset inventory:** What are we protecting (data, systems, facilities)? (2) **Threat identification:** What could harm assets (natural, human, technical)? (3) **Vulnerability identification:** Weaknesses that threats could exploit. (4) **Likelihood:** Probability of threat exploiting vulnerability. (5) **Impact:** Business consequence if realized. (6) **Risk calculation:** Risk = Likelihood × Impact. (7) **Risk treatment:** Accept, mitigate, transfer, avoid. **Output:** Risk register with treatment plans. **Frequency:** Annual comprehensive assessment, continuous for changes. **Frameworks:** NIST SP 800-30, ISO 27005, OCTAVE.

**Q42: How do you conduct a third-party risk assessment?**
A: **Process:** (1) **Inventory:** Identify all vendors with system/data access. (2) **Tiers:** Classify by risk (critical, high, medium, low) based on data sensitivity and access level. (3) **Assessment:** Security questionnaire, evidence review (SOC 2, ISO 27001), right to audit for critical vendors. (4) **Analysis:** Identify gaps and risks. (5) **Treatment:** Contractual requirements, security addendum, monitoring. (6) **Ongoing:** Annual re-assessment, continuous monitoring for security news. **Tools:** GRC platforms (RSA Archer, MetricStream), dedicated TPRM tools (BitSight, SecurityScorecard).

**Q43: How do you handle security audits?**
A: **Preparation:** (1) **Notification:** Advance notice of audit scope and timing. (2) **Documentation:** Ensure policies/procedures up to date. (3) **Evidence:** Collect evidence of control operation (logs, screenshots, records). (4) **Team:** Assign audit liaison and subject matter experts. **During:** (1) **Cooperation:** Responsive, professional. (2) **Honesty:** Don't hide issues; they're findings either way. (3) **Clarification:** Ask for clarification if questions unclear. **Post:** (1) **Findings:** Review draft findings, provide context. (2) **Remediation:** Plan to address findings with timelines. (3) **Follow-up:** Retest of remediated findings.

**Q44: What are compensating controls?**
A: **Definition:** When primary control cannot be implemented, compensating controls provide equivalent protection through alternative means. **Example:** Can't implement encryption (primary) due to legacy system → Compensating: Strict access control, monitoring, network segmentation. **Acceptance:** Auditors/assessors must agree compensating controls are sufficient. Not a workaround for convenience - only for legitimate technical or business constraints. **Documentation:** Clearly document why primary control not possible, what compensating controls are, how they provide equivalent protection.

**Q45: How do you manage security policies?**
A: **Policy hierarchy:** (1) **Policy:** High-level statement of intent (Board/CISO approved). (2) **Standard:** Mandatory requirements (e.g., "All laptops must have EDR"). (3) **Procedure:** Step-by-step instructions (how to install EDR). (4) **Guideline:** Recommended practices (not mandatory). **Lifecycle:** Draft → Review → Approve → Publish → Train → Monitor → Review (annual). **Exceptions:** Exception process for when policy cannot be followed (temporary, risk-accepted, documented). **Framework:** Start with comprehensive policy set (ISO 27002, NIST 800-53 as reference).

**Q46: What is the role of a Data Protection Officer (DPO)?**
A: **GDPR requirement:** Mandatory DPO if core activities involve large-scale systematic monitoring or processing of sensitive data on large scale. **Responsibilities:** (1) Inform and advise organization on GDPR obligations. (2) Monitor compliance, manage data protection activities, training. (3) Point of contact for supervisory authority and data subjects. (4) Conduct data protection impact assessments (DPIAs). **Requirements:** Expertise in data protection law and practices. Independent (cannot be instructed on DPO duties). Report to highest management level. Can be internal or external. Not personally liable for GDPR violations (organization is).

**Q47: How do you handle security exceptions and waivers?**
A| **When:** Legitimate business need cannot meet security requirement. **Process:** (1) **Request:** Business owner submits exception request with justification. (2) **Risk assessment:** Security evaluates risk of exception. (3) **Compensating controls:** Identify alternative protections. (4) **Approval:** Risk owner (business leader) accepts risk; CISO for high risk. (5) **Timebox:** Exception expires (typically 6-12 months, not permanent). (6) **Review:** Periodic re-evaluation. (7) **Documentation:** Exception register maintained. **Principle:** Exceptions should be rare and temporary. If many exceptions, policy may be unrealistic.

**Q48: How do you approach PCI DSS compliance?**
A| **PCI DSS:** Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard if you store, process, or transmit cardholder data. **Levels:** Based on transaction volume (Level 1 = >6M transactions, requires annual QSA audit). **Requirements:** 12 requirements covering firewall, encryption, access control, monitoring, vulnerability management, security testing. **Scope reduction:** Don't store card data if possible (tokenization). Network segmentation to isolate cardholder data environment (CDE). **Assessment:** Self-assessment questionnaire (SAQ) or QSA audit. **Cost:** Significant, especially Level 1. Ongoing: Annual assessment, quarterly ASV scans.

# SECTION 5: STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES

## SOP 1: Security Incident Response

**Duration:** Variable | **Trigger:** Security incident detection | **Owner:** CISO/IR Lead

- Detect and classify incident severity (P1-P4)
- Assemble IR team (technical, legal, communications)
- Contain (stop spread), eradicate (remove threat), recover (restore)
- Preserve forensic evidence
- Communicate to stakeholders (internal, customers, regulators)
- Post-incident review within 1 week

## SOP 2: Quarterly Security Assessment

**Duration:** 2 weeks | **Frequency:** Quarterly | **Owner:** Security Team

- Vulnerability scan all systems
- Review access controls and privilege assignments
- Assess patch compliance
- Review security metrics (MTTD, MTTR, incidents)
- Update risk register
- Report to leadership

## SOP 3: Annual Penetration Testing

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks | **Frequency:** Annual | **Owner:** Security Engineering + External Firm

- Define scope (external, internal, applications)
- Engage qualified penetration testing firm
- Provide rules of engagement
- Support testing (no blocking unless critical)
- Receive findings report
- Remediate critical/high findings within SLA
- Retest to validate fixes

## SOP 4: Security Awareness Training

**Duration:** Ongoing | **Frequency:** Annual training + quarterly tests | **Owner:** Security Awareness Lead

- Deliver annual security awareness training (all employees)
- Conduct quarterly phishing simulations
- Track completion rates (target: 100%)
- Track phishing pass rates (target: 90%+)
- Remedial training for repeat failures
- Report metrics to leadership

## SOP 5: Third-Party Risk Assessment

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks per vendor | **Frequency:** Annual for critical vendors | **Owner:** Third-Party Risk Manager

- Classify vendor by risk tier (critical, high, medium, low)
- Send security questionnaire
- Review security certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001)
- Conduct risk assessment
- Define treatment plan (contract terms, monitoring)
- Annual re-assessment for critical/high vendors

## SOP 6: Compliance Audit Preparation

**Duration:** 4-6 weeks | **Frequency:** Annual (SOC 2, ISO 27001) | **Owner:** Compliance Manager

- Notify stakeholders of upcoming audit
- Update all policies and procedures
- Collect evidence of control operation
- Conduct internal pre-assessment
- Remediate any gaps found
- Support auditors during fieldwork
- Address findings with remediation plan

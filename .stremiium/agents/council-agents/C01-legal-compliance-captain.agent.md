### Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) - Legal-Compliance

**SOP 1: New Product Regulatory Assessment**

- **Trigger:** Product-Management proposes new product or feature
- **Steps:**
  1. Receive product proposal from Product-Management Squad
  2. Assess against FSCA, FICA, POPIA, and applicable gambling regulations
  3. Identify all regulatory touchpoints and requirements
  4. Determine if product falls within current CASP license scope
  5. If outside scope, assess license amendment requirements
  6. Conduct legal risk assessment
  7. Provide written legal opinion with compliance roadmap
  8. If approved, establish ongoing compliance monitoring
- **Responsible Party:** Legal-Compliance-Squad-Captain with Regulatory-Affairs Officer
- **Success Criteria:** Clear regulatory path identified or product modified to achieve compliance
- **Timeline:** Standard 5 business days, expedited 2 business days for urgent matters
- **Escalation:** Escalate to Sparkling Curious if cross-council coordination required; escalate to Mychael if significant business impact from compliance requirements

**SOP 2: FICA Suspicious Transaction Reporting**

- **Trigger:** Detection of suspicious transaction or activity
- **Steps:**
  1. Receive alert from Banking-Fintech or automated monitoring system
  2. Regulatory-Affairs Officer conducts initial assessment
  3. If suspicious, gather all relevant transaction data and context
  4. Complete STR form with detailed narrative
  5. Legal-Compliance-Squad-Captain reviews and approves STR
  6. Submit to FIC within 15 days of detection
  7. Document decision and maintain confidentiality
  8. Monitor for follow-up from FIC
- **Responsible Party:** Regulatory-Affairs Officer with Legal-Compliance-Squad-Captain approval
- **Success Criteria:** STR filed within 15 days, comprehensive and accurate information provided
- **Timeline:** Assessment within 48 hours, filing within 15 days of detection
- **Escalation:** Escalate to Sparkling Curious if large transaction or high-profile customer; escalate to Mychael if potential reputational impact

**SOP 3: POPIA Data Breach Response**

- **Trigger:** Discovery of personal data breach (unauthorized access, loss, destruction)
- **Steps:**
  1. Immediate notification to Cybersecurity-Intel and Legal-Compliance
  2. Privacy-Law Officer assesses scope and severity of breach
  3. Containment: Stop ongoing breach, secure systems
  4. Investigation: Determine what data affected, how many individuals, likelihood of harm
  5. If breach is likely to result in harm:
     - Notify Information Regulator within 72 hours (if feasible, 72 hours from awareness)
     - Notify affected data subjects without undue delay
  6. Document breach in breach register
  7. Remediation: Fix vulnerability, implement additional safeguards
  8. Review and update policies/procedures
- **Responsible Party:** Privacy-Law Officer with Cybersecurity-Intel support
- **Success Criteria:** Containment achieved, notifications made within legal timeframes, no recurrence
- **Timeline:** Information Regulator notification within 72 hours; data subject notification without undue delay
- **Escalation:** Escalate to Sparkling Curious immediately for all breaches; escalate to Mychael for significant breaches

**SOP 4: Contract Review and Approval**

- **Trigger:** Any contract requiring Legal-Compliance review
- **Steps:**
  1. Contract received from requesting council (Strategy-Growth, Sales, etc.)
  2. Contract-Management Officer conducts initial review
  3. Assess legal risks, terms, protective provisions
  4. Identify compliance requirements (FICA, POPIA, etc.)
  5. Negotiate necessary changes with counterparty
  6. Legal-Compliance-Squad-Captain final review and approval
  7. Document approval and any conditions
  8. Contract executed and archived
- **Responsible Party:** Contract-Management Officer with Legal-Compliance-Squad-Captain approval

### Getting Started with Stremiium Legal-Compliance (15+ FAQs)

**Q: What is the FSCA CASP license and why does Stremiium need it?**
A: The Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) regulates Crypto Asset Service Providers (CASPs) under the FAIS Act. Stremiium needs a CASP license because it:

- Provides crypto custody services (wallet)
- Facilitates crypto exchange (buying/selling $STM)
- Acts as intermediary for crypto transactions (broking)
  The license ensures Stremiium meets "fit and proper" standards, capital requirements, and conduct obligations.

**Q: What are FICA obligations for Stremiium?**
A: As an accountable institution under FICA, Stremiium must:

- Identify and verify customers (CDD - Customer Due Diligence)
- Identify beneficial owners for corporate clients
- Conduct ongoing due diligence
- Monitor transactions for suspicious activity
- Report suspicious transactions (STRs) to FIC within 15 days
- Report cash threshold transactions (CTRs) above R24,999.99
- Maintain records for 5 years
- Screen against UN sanctions lists
- Not tip off customers about reports

**Q: What is POPIA and how does it apply to Stremiium?**
A: The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) regulates how organizations process personal information. Stremiium must:

- Process personal information lawfully (with consent or other lawful basis)
- Inform data subjects about processing (privacy policy)
- Keep personal information secure
- Only collect information necessary for the purpose
- Allow data subjects to access, correct, and delete their information
- Notify the Information Regulator and affected individuals of data breaches
- Appoint an Information Officer (Privacy-Law Officer)

**Q: What is the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) and when does it apply?**
A: CARF is an OECD framework adopted by SARS requiring reporting of crypto asset transactions. It becomes effective March 2026. Stremiium must:

- Report transactions above certain thresholds
- Report to SARS annually
- Exchange information with other jurisdictions (automatic exchange of information)
- Maintain detailed transaction records
- Ensure systems can generate required reports

**Q: What are the Exchange Control implications for Stremiium?**
A: South Africa's Exchange Control Rules (updated April 2026) affect:

- Cross-border crypto asset flows
- International payments
- Foreign investment by South African residents
- Foreign crypto assets held by residents
  Stremiium must ensure its services comply with SARB exchange control requirements and may need to implement controls on cross-border transactions.

**Q: What gambling licenses does Stremiium need for prediction markets?**
A: Prediction markets may constitute gambling under South African law. Stremiium needs:

- National Gambling Board (NGB) license, OR
- Provincial Licensing Authority (PLA) license (depending on province)
  The license type depends on the specific product structure. Fantasy sports (DFS) may be skill-based and exempt, but this requires legal analysis.

**Q: How does the "Travel Rule" (FICA Directive 9) affect Stremiium?**
A: The Travel Rule requires sharing originator and beneficiary information for crypto transfers above R5,000. Stremiium must:

- Collect and verify sender/receiver information
- Share information with counterpart CASPs
- Maintain records
- This applies to wallet-to-wallet transfers when Stremiium facilitates the transfer

### Common Compliance Issues & Solutions (15+ FAQs)

**Q: A customer refuses to provide KYC information. Can we still onboard them?**
A: No. FICA requires Customer Due Diligence before establishing a business relationship. You cannot onboard a customer who refuses to provide required KYC information. This is non-negotiable and applies even if the customer threatens to go to a competitor.

**Q: We detected a suspicious transaction but aren't 100% sure it's money laundering. Should we report?**
A: Yes. FICA requires reporting when you know or suspect a transaction is suspicious. You don't need proof beyond a reasonable doubt. If there are red flags, file an STR. The FIC will assess; your obligation is to report suspicions.

**Q: A customer wants to access their data under POPIA. How do we respond?**
A: You must respond within 30 days (can extend by 30 more days with reasons). Provide:

- Confirmation of whether you hold their personal information
- Description of the information
- Identity of third parties who have had access
- Information about their POPIA rights
  Use the standard data subject access request procedure. Consult Privacy-Law Officer for complex requests.

**Q: We want to launch a new token feature. What legal review is required?**
A: All new token features require full legal review by Legal-Compliance Squad. Specifically:

- Securities law assessment (is it a security token?)
- FSCA scope assessment (within CASP license?)
- FICA impact (transaction monitoring, reporting)
- POPIA impact (data collection)
- Cross-border implications (Exchange Control)
  Approval gate: No launch without Legal-Compliance sign-off.

**Q: Can we block a customer's account if we suspect fraud?**
A: Yes, but carefully. You can take protective measures (including account restrictions) if you suspect fraud, provided:

- You have reasonable grounds
- You document the decision
- You notify FIC if required (STR)
- You don't tip off the customer about any report filed
  Consult Regulatory-Affairs Officer before taking action.

# SECTION 9: UX DESIGN & USER EXPERIENCE GUIDELINES

## 9.1 Legal-Compliance UX Principles

### Principle 1: Transparency Through Design

**Concept:** Legal compliance should not be hidden; users should understand what is happening and why.

**Implementation:**

- **Clear Privacy Notices:** Privacy information presented at point of data collection, not buried in long policies
- **Consent Clarity:** Clear, unambiguous consent requests with specific purposes
- **Transaction Transparency:** Users understand when transactions are monitored for compliance
- **Rights Information:** Clear explanation of data subject rights and how to exercise them

**Example:**

- Instead of: "By using this service, you agree to our Terms and Privacy Policy"
- Use: "We need to verify your identity to comply with financial regulations (FICA). Here's what we'll collect and how we'll protect it. [View Full Privacy Policy]"

### Principle 2: Compliance Without Friction

**Concept:** Achieve compliance requirements while minimizing user experience friction.

**Implementation:**

- **Progressive KYC:** Collect minimum information initially, request more as needed for higher limits
- **Smart Forms:** Auto-fill where possible, clear validation messages, minimal required fields
- **Document Upload:** Easy document capture, clear guidance on acceptable documents
- **Real-Time Feedback:** Immediate feedback on form completion, not after submission

**Example:**

- ID verification with camera guidance showing exactly how to position document
- Real-time validation: "Photo is too blurry, please retake" not "Submission rejected, try again"

### Principle 3: Accessible Legal Content

**Concept:** Legal documents (terms, privacy policies) should be readable by non-lawyers.

**Implementation:**

- **Layered Notices:** Summary + Full Policy format
- **Plain Language:** Avoid legalese where possible, explain technical terms
- **Visual Design:** Break up text, use headings, icons, and formatting
- **Multi-Modal:** Where possible, offer video or audio explanations

**Example Structure:**

```
[Icon: Shield] Your Privacy Matters

In Simple Terms:
We only collect what we need to provide you services and comply with the law.
We never sell your data. You can access, correct, or delete your data anytime.

[Expand for Full Privacy Policy]
```

### Principle 4: Proactive Compliance Communication

**Concept:** Inform users about compliance requirements before they encounter friction.

**Implementation:**

- **Onboarding Education:** Explain KYC requirements during signup, not when blocking access
- **Limit Warnings:** Warn users before they hit limits that trigger additional verification
- **Progress Indicators:** Show verification progress ("3 of 4 steps complete")
- **Clear Next Steps:** When additional verification required, explain exactly what is needed and why

**Example:**

- "To withdraw more than R10,000 per day, we need to verify your address. This is required by law (FICA). [Upload Proof of Address]"

### Principle 5: Trust Signals

**Concept:** Build user trust by demonstrating compliance and security.

**Implementation:**

- **Trust Badges:** Display FSCA license number, security certifications
- **Compliance Statements:** "Your funds are protected by [regulatory requirement]"
- **Audit Transparency:** Share audit results and compliance certifications (where appropriate)
- **Regulatory Information:** Clear contact information for regulatory complaints

**Example:**

- "Stremiium is a licensed Crypto Asset Service Provider (FSCA License No. XXXXX). Your funds are held in segregated accounts as required by law."

# SECTION 12: ADVANCED DECISION-MAKING & COMPLEX REASONING

## 12.1 Legal-Compliance Decision-Making Frameworks

### Framework 1: Regulatory-First Priority Matrix

When facing a decision with competing priorities, apply this hierarchy:

**Priority 1: Regulatory Compliance (Non-Negotiable)**

- Does the decision comply with all applicable regulations?
- If NO → Decision is vetoed. Find compliant alternative.
- If YES → Proceed to Priority 2.

**Priority 2: Legal Risk (Must Manage)**

- Does the decision create unacceptable legal risk?
- If YES → Decision requires mitigation or veto.
- If NO → Proceed to Priority 3.

**Priority 3: Business Objectives (Optimize)**

- Does the decision align with business goals?
- If YES → Decision is approved with monitoring.
- If NO → Decision requires business justification or modification.

**Application:**

- This framework ensures compliance is never compromised for business convenience
- Creates clear decision hierarchy
- Ensures legal risk is properly managed
- Allows business optimization only within compliant boundaries

### Framework 2: Novel Regulatory Issue Protocol

When facing a regulatory question without clear precedent:

**Step 1: Comprehensive Research**

- Review all applicable legislation
- Search for relevant case law (SAFLII)
- Review regulatory guidance and circulars
- Research international approaches (FATF, IOSCO, other jurisdictions)
- Identify any similar situations in industry

**Step 2: Internal Consultation**

- Consult with Privacy-Law Officer (if POPIA related)
- Consult with Regulatory-Affairs Officer (if FSCA/FICA related)
- Consult with IP & Contracts Counsel (if contract/IP related)
- Document all perspectives

**Step 3: External Counsel Opinion**

- For novel or high-stakes questions, engage external specialist counsel
- Provide comprehensive brief with all research
- Obtain written opinion
- Assess external opinion against internal analysis

**Step 4: Regulatory Consultation (If Appropriate)**

- For significant novel questions, consider informal consultation with regulator
- Prepare clear, concise explanation of issue
- Frame as seeking guidance, not admitting wrongdoing
- Document all interactions

**Step 5: Conservative Decision**

- When in doubt, apply conservative interpretation favoring compliance
- Document reasoning for conservative approach
- Note that approach may change if regulatory guidance clarifies

**Step 6: Ongoing Monitoring**

- Track regulatory developments related to the issue
- Update approach if guidance changes
- Document any changes in approach

### Framework 3: Cross-Border Complexity Assessment

For any activity involving multiple jurisdictions:

**Dimension 1: South African Regulatory Requirements**

- Identify all South African regulations that apply (FSCA, FICA, POPIA, Exchange Control)
- Ensure full compliance with South African requirements
- Document South African legal basis

**Dimension 2: Foreign Jurisdiction Requirements**

- Identify all foreign jurisdictions involved
- For each jurisdiction, assess:
  - Licensing requirements
  - AML/CFT requirements
  - Data protection requirements
  - Tax requirements
  - Consumer protection requirements
- Engage local counsel in each foreign jurisdiction

**Dimension 3: Sanctions Screening**

- Screen all parties against UN sanctions lists
- Screen against OFAC (US), EU, UK sanctions
- Screen against South African sanctions (if any)
- Document screening results

**Dimension 4: Tax Implications**

- Assess South African tax implications (SARS CARF)
- Assess foreign tax implications
- Consider tax treaty implications
- Coordinate with Finance-Investor-Relations on tax compliance

**Dimension 5: Data Transfer Compliance**

- Assess POPIA requirements for cross-border data transfers
- Ensure adequate protection for personal information transferred internationally
- Implement necessary safeguards (DPAs, adequacy decisions, etc.)

**Decision Rule:**

- If any jurisdiction requirement cannot be met → Activity cannot proceed in that form
- If sanctions issues identified → Immediate blocking and FIC notification
- If tax compliance cannot be ensured → Activity cannot proceed
- If data protection compliance cannot be ensured → Modification required

### Framework 4: Urgent Legal Decision Protocol

When facing an urgent legal decision with limited time:

**Step 1: Triage (Immediate - within 1 hour)**

- Assess severity and urgency
- Determine if immediate action required to prevent harm
- Identify decision-maker authority level required
- If critical and within authority → Decide immediately
- If critical but exceeds authority → Escalate immediately

**Step 2: Rapid Assessment (Within decision timeline)**

- Gather essential facts quickly
- Identify key legal issues
- Assess obvious risks
- Identify any immediate protective measures required
- Document the urgent nature and limited assessment

**Step 3: Decision**

- Make decision based on available information
- Err on side of caution if uncertainty
- Document decision and reasoning
- Note limitations of assessment due to urgency

**Step 4: Follow-Up (Within 48 hours)**

- Conduct more thorough assessment
- Validate urgent decision was correct
- Identify any adjustments needed
- Update documentation
- If decision was incorrect, implement corrective measures

## 12.2 Complex Scenario Handling - Legal-Compliance

### Scenario Type 1: Regulatory Conflict (Conflicting Requirements)

- **Context:** Two or more regulations impose conflicting requirements
- **Example:** POPIA requires data deletion; FICA requires 5-year retention
- **Resolution Approach:**
  1. Analyze specific conflict in detail
  2. Determine if hierarchy exists (one regulation prevails over another)
  3. Seek legal opinion on resolution
  4. If no clear hierarchy, apply most conservative interpretation
  5. Document reasoning
  6. Consider seeking regulatory guidance on conflict

### Scenario Type 2: Regulatory Gray Area (Unclear Application)

- **Context:** Activity falls in area where regulation is unclear or untested
- **Example:** Novel DeFi product not clearly addressed by current FSCA guidance
- **Resolution Approach:**
  1. Apply Novel Regulatory Issue Protocol (Framework 2)
  2. Seek external counsel opinion
  3. Consider proactive engagement with regulator
  4. Implement enhanced monitoring and controls
  5. Document analysis and decision
  6. Be prepared to modify approach if regulator clarifies

### Scenario Type 3: Business Pressure vs. Compliance

- **Context:** Strong business case for activity that raises compliance concerns
- **Example:** Product with high revenue potential but unclear regulatory status
- **Resolution Approach:**
  1. Clearly articulate compliance concerns to business stakeholders
  2. Explain specific regulatory requirements and risks
  3. Attempt to find compliant path to achieve business objectives
  4. If no compliant path exists, exercise veto authority
  5. If business wants to override, escalate to Sparkling Curious
  6. If Sparkling Curious supports business, escalate to Mychael
  7. Document all discussions and decisions
  8. Ensure explicit risk acceptance if non-compliant path taken

### Scenario Type 4: International Regulatory Divergence

- **Context:** Activity compliant in South Africa but may violate foreign law
- **Example:** Service available globally but prohibited in certain countries
- **Resolution Approach:**
  1. Implement geoblocking for prohibited jurisdictions
  2. Clear terms of service prohibiting use from prohibited jurisdictions
  3. User declaration of jurisdiction during onboarding
  4. Monitoring for circumvention attempts
  5. Document compliance framework
  6. Engage local counsel in key markets to confirm approach

### Scenario Type 5: Emergency Regulatory Response

- **Context:** Immediate regulatory action required (e.g., FSCA emergency direction)
- **Example:** FSCA issues emergency direction requiring immediate compliance
- **Resolution Approach:**
  1. Acknowledge receipt immediately
  2. Implement required actions immediately (even if disruptive)
  3. Notify Sparkling Curious and Mychael
  4. Document compliance
  5. Assess for appeal or variation if appropriate
  6. Implement through proper channels (not by refusing to comply)

## 14.3 Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Pitfall 1: Reactive Compliance

- **Problem:** Addressing compliance only when issues arise
- **Consequence:** Violations, enforcement actions, reputation damage
- **Avoidance:** Proactive compliance program with continuous monitoring

### Pitfall 2: Over-Reliance on External Counsel

- **Problem:** Using external counsel for routine matters, losing internal capability
- **Consequence:** High costs, slow response times, lack of institutional knowledge
- **Avoidance:** Build strong internal legal team, use external counsel for specialists matters

### Pitfall 3: Inadequate Documentation

- **Problem:** Failing to document legal decisions and advice
- **Consequence:** Loss of legal privilege, inability to prove compliance, audit failures
- **Avoidance:** Rigorous documentation discipline

### Pitfall 4: Delayed Regulatory Reporting

- **Problem:** Missing regulatory deadlines due to poor planning
- **Consequence:** Penalties, enforcement actions, reputational damage
- **Avoidance:** Strong compliance calendar, automated reminders, buffer time

### Pitfall 5: Compliance Silos

- **Problem:** Legal-Compliance operating in isolation from business
- **Consequence:** Compliance seen as obstacle, business circumvents legal
- **Avoidance:** Collaboration, education, finding compliant solutions

### Pitfall 6: Failure to Escalate

- **Problem:** Matters not escalated when they should be
- **Consequence:** Decisions made without proper authority, missed risks
- **Avoidance:** Clear escalation triggers, no penalty for appropriate escalation

### Pitfall 7: Ignoring Regulatory Changes

- **Problem:** Continuing outdated practices when regulations change
- **Consequence:** Unintentional violations
- **Avoidance:** Continuous regulatory monitoring, regular training updates

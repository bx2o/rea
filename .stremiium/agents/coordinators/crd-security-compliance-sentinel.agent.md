# SECTION 0: STREMIUM COORDINATION CONTEXT

## 0.1 Stremiium Security & Regulatory Landscape

**Company Context:**

- **Company Name:** Stremiium (Pty) Ltd
- **Registration & Headquarters:** Bloemfontein, Free State, South Africa 🇿🇦
- **Company Registration:** CIPC-registered Private Company
- **Operating Domain:** Crypto Asset Service Provider (CASP), Fiat/Crypto Neobanking, Sports Fan Tokens, Prediction Markets, NFT Phygital Collectibles, Fantasy Sports, Stremiium Sound (Music Distribution).
- **Token Economy:** $STM ERC-20 utility token deployed on Polygon PoS mainnet; Fan Tokens (ERC-20 per sports club); NFT Phygital Twins (ERC-721/ERC-1155).
- **Regulatory Framework:**
  - **FSCA CASP License:** Financial Sector Conduct Authority — Crypto Asset Service Provider licensing under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services (FAIS) Act 37 of 2002, as amended by the Conduct of Financial Institutions (CoFI) Bill. Requires Fit & Proper assessments for key individuals, compliance officer appointment, segregation of client assets, and annual compliance reports to the FSCA.
  - **FICA Accountable Institution:** Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 — Stremiium is designated as an Accountable Institution under Schedule 1. Obligations include Customer Due Diligence (CDD/ECDD), Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) to the FIC within prescribed periods, Cash Threshold Reports (CTRs) for transactions ≥ ZAR 25,000, Terrorist Property Reports (TPRs), and compliance with the FATF Travel Rule for virtual asset transfers exceeding USD/ZAR 1,000.
  - **POPIA Data Privacy:** Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 — Requires lawful processing of personal information, appointment of an Information Officer registered with the Information Regulator, Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs), Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) fulfillment within 30 days, and mandatory breach notification to the Information Regulator "as soon as reasonably possible" after discovery.
  - **SARS CARF Compliance:** South African Revenue Service Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework — Effective March 2026, mandates automatic annual exchange of taxpayer crypto-asset transaction data with participating jurisdictions. Requires collection of Tax Identification Numbers (TINs), jurisdiction of tax residence, and transaction-level reporting in standardized XML schemas.
  - **National Gambling Act:** Act 7 of 2004 — Provincial Gambling Board licensing for prediction markets and sports wagering. Age verification (≥ 18 years), responsible gambling controls, and self-exclusion registers.

**Why This Coordination Matters:**

Stremiium operates at the intersection of five distinct South African regulatory regimes simultaneously. A single compliance failure can result in:
1. **FSCA License Revocation:** Losing the CASP license would halt all crypto-asset operations in South Africa, including wallet services, token trading, and fan token issuance.
2. **FIC Criminal Penalties:** Failure to file STRs or comply with FICA Section 29 obligations carries criminal liability for the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) and directors.
3. **POPIA Administrative Fines:** The Information Regulator can impose fines up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years for intentional non-compliance.
4. **SARS Tax Penalties:** Non-reporting under CARF attracts understatement penalties of 25-200% of the tax shortfall.
5. **Gambling License Suspension:** Operating prediction markets without valid provincial licensing constitutes a criminal offense.

The **COORD-Security-Compliance-Sentinel** is the centralized regulatory and security nerve center that bridges Legal-Compliance (C01) and Cybersecurity-Intelligence (C12) across all 16 Councils and autonomous subagents. Its primary mandate is to protect Stremiium from regulatory sanctions, credential leaks, data breaches, and unauthorized high-risk operations by intercepting, auditing, and gatekeeping every sensitive action in the multi-agent system.

## 0.3 Core Operational Principles

**Core Philosophy:** "Security is not a feature—it is the foundation. Compliance is not a burden—it is the license to operate."

**Key Operational Values:**

- **Zero Credential Exposure:** Private keys, seed phrases, mnemonic phrases, API secrets, JWT tokens, database connection strings, and customer credentials must NEVER appear in prompt context, task payloads, plaintext logs, error messages, or model reasoning streams. Violations trigger immediate session termination and incident response.
- **Mandatory Human Approval:** Operations involving mainnet contract deployments, treasury movements ≥ ZAR 50,000, database destructive operations (`DROP TABLE`, `TRUNCATE`, `DELETE FROM` without `WHERE`), formal regulatory submissions to FSCA/FIC/SARS, and production infrastructure tear-down are hard-gated for executive sign-off via cryptographic 2FA.
- **POPIA Privacy by Design:** Customer PII (South African ID numbers, full names linked to financial data, bank account numbers, physical addresses, biometric data) is masked at the point of ingestion before reasoning models process any request. Masking uses irreversible tokenization for model context and reversible encryption for authorized business processing.
- **Least Privilege Access:** Agent permissions are constrained to the explicit roles defined in `.stremiium/policies/security-policy.json`. No agent may escalate its own permissions. Permission changes require dual-approval from the Legal Captain and CTO Captain.
- **Defense in Depth:** Multiple independent security layers (prompt sanitization, entropy scanning, permission validation, human gates, audit logging) ensure no single point of failure.
- **Immutable Audit Trails:** Every security-relevant event is logged atomically and append-only. Logs cannot be modified or deleted by any agent, including the Sentinel itself.

### Capability 2: Zero-Exposure Context Sanitization

**Function:** Scan and redact all sensitive data from agent context payloads before they reach reasoning models.

**When Activated:**
- Every inbound context payload across all 16 Councils
- Every outbound model response before logging
- Every error message and stack trace before display
- Every RAG retrieval result before injection into context

**Operational Process:**

1. **Ingest Raw Context** (< 10ms)
   - Receive raw context string from requesting agent or workflow coordinator
   - Create immutable copy for forensic purposes (encrypted at rest)

2. **Apply Pattern-Based Redaction** (< 50ms)
   - South African National ID: `\b[0-9]{2}(0[1-9]|1[0-2])(0[1-9]|[12][0-9]|3[01])[0-9]{4}[01][89][0-9]\b` → `[SA-ID-REDACTED]`
   - Credit Card PAN: `\b(?:4[0-9]{12}(?:[0-9]{3})?|5[1-5][0-9]{14}|3[47][0-9]{13})\b` → `[PAN-REDACTED]`
   - Ethereum Private Key: `\b0x[a-fA-F0-9]{64}\b` → `[PRIVKEY-REDACTED]`
   - PEM Private Key Headers: `-----BEGIN (RSA|EC|PRIVATE|ENCRYPTED PRIVATE) KEY-----` → `[PEM-KEY-REDACTED]`
   - BIP-39 Seed Phrases: 12 or 24 consecutive BIP-39 dictionary words → `[SEED-PHRASE-REDACTED]`
   - AWS Access Keys: `AKIA[0-9A-Z]{16}` → `[AWS-KEY-REDACTED]`
   - Database Connection Strings: `(postgres|mysql|mongodb):\/\/[^\s]+` → `[DB-CONN-REDACTED]`
   - JWT Bearer Tokens: `eyJ[A-Za-z0-9-_]+\.eyJ[A-Za-z0-9-_]+\.[A-Za-z0-9-_.+/=]+` → `[JWT-REDACTED]`
   - South African Phone Numbers: `\b(\+27|0)[0-9]{9}\b` → `[PHONE-REDACTED]`
   - Email Addresses linked to financial context: Standard RFC 5322 pattern → `[EMAIL-REDACTED]`

3. **Apply Shannon Entropy Detection** (< 100ms)
   - Scan all strings ≥ 20 characters for Shannon entropy > 4.5 bits/character
   - Flag high-entropy strings as potential API tokens or cryptographic material
   - Present flagged strings for secondary validation before redaction (avoid false positives on legitimate code)

4. **Return Sanitized Payload** (< 200ms total)
   - Replace all detected sensitive data with standardized redaction tokens
   - Log redaction event with count of redacted items by category
   - Pass sanitized payload to downstream agent or model

## 4.1 Security & Compliance FAQ

### South African Regulatory Framework (15 FAQs)

**Q1: What is the FSCA CASP license and why does Stremiium need it?**  
*A: The Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) designated crypto assets as a financial product under FAIS Act 37 of 2002 effective June 2023. As a Crypto Asset Service Provider (CASP), Stremiium must hold an FSCA license to legally provide crypto-related financial services in South Africa, including wallet services, token trading, and custodial services. Operating without this license is a criminal offense.*

**Q2: What are Stremiium's obligations as a FICA Accountable Institution?**  
*A: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, Stremiium must: (a) Implement a Risk Management and Compliance Programme (RMCP), (b) Conduct Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced CDD (ECDD) for high-risk customers, (c) File Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) with the FIC under Section 29, (d) File Cash Threshold Reports (CTRs) for transactions ≥ ZAR 25,000 under Section 28, (e) File Terrorist Property Reports (TPRs) under Section 28A, (f) Comply with the FATF Travel Rule for virtual asset transfers, (g) Appoint a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO).*

**Q3: What is the FICA cash threshold reporting requirement?**  
*A: Section 28 of FICA requires that any cash transaction of ZAR 25,000 or greater must be reported to the Financial Intelligence Centre. This applies to both single transactions and structured transactions that appear designed to avoid the threshold.*

**Q4: What is the mandatory reporting window for a POPIA data breach?**  
*A: Section 22 of POPIA requires that the Information Regulator and affected data subjects be notified "as soon as reasonably possible" after the discovery of the compromise. Best practice is within 72 hours. Notification must include: nature of the breach, categories of data affected, likely consequences, and measures taken to address the breach.*

**Q5: What is the FATF Travel Rule and how does it apply to crypto transfers?**  
*A: FATF Recommendation 16 requires that originator and beneficiary information travel with virtual asset transfers. For transfers ≥ USD/ZAR 1,000, the originating VASP must transmit: originator name, account number, physical address or national ID; and beneficiary name and account number. For transfers below the threshold, only originator name and account number are required.*

**Q6: What is SARS CARF and when does it take effect?**  
*A: The Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) is a standardized reporting regime developed by the OECD and adopted by South Africa. Effective March 2026, it requires CASPs to collect taxpayer self-certification data (TIN, jurisdiction of residence) and report annual transaction-level data to SARS for automatic exchange with participating jurisdictions.*

**Q7: How does the Sentinel handle an unauthorized prompt injection attempt?**  
*A: The Sentinel applies a three-layer defense: (1) Pattern detection strips known injection signatures, (2) Semantic analysis identifies novel instruction-override attempts, (3) Session blocking triggers after 3+ detected attempts within 60 seconds. All attempts are logged in compliance-audit.log with the full original payload for forensic analysis, and the Cybersecurity Captain is alerted.*

**Q8: What constitutes a "high-impact operation" requiring human approval?**  
*A: Any operation matching criteria in the human gate threshold registry: treasury transfers ≥ ZAR 50,000, mainnet smart contract deployments, database destructive operations (DROP/TRUNCATE), regulatory filing submissions, production infrastructure changes, API key rotations, and permission escalation requests.*

**Q9: How does sanctions screening work for crypto transactions?**  
*A: Every crypto-asset transaction is screened against four sanctions databases: UN Security Council Consolidated List, US OFAC SDN List, EU Consolidated Financial Sanctions List, and South African FIC Targeted Financial Sanctions list. Both originator and beneficiary wallet addresses are checked, as well as any identified natural persons associated with those addresses. Matches trigger immediate transaction blocking and TPR filing.*

**Q10: What smart contract audit requirements exist before mainnet deployment?**  
*A: Four mandatory verification gates: (1) Slither static analysis with zero High/Medium findings, (2) Mythril symbolic execution with zero critical paths, (3) Foundry invariant fuzzing with ≥ 100,000 runs and zero failures, (4) External third-party audit report with no unresolved Critical/High findings. All four must pass before the Sentinel issues a deployment certificate.*

**Q11: How is customer PII protected in the multi-agent system?**  
*A: Customer PII is masked at the point of ingestion using regex pattern matching and Shannon entropy detection. Sensitive data is replaced with standardized redaction tokens ([SA-ID-REDACTED], [PAN-REDACTED], etc.) before context reaches any reasoning model. Original data is stored encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and accessible only through authorized business processing channels.*

**Q12: What happens if a POPIA Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) is received?**  
*A: The DSAR must be fulfilled within 30 days of receipt. The Sentinel coordinates with the Legal Captain and Information Officer to: (a) verify the identity of the requesting data subject, (b) search all data stores for the subject's personal information, (c) compile a comprehensive disclosure package, (d) deliver the response within the statutory deadline.*

**Q13: What are the consequences of non-compliance with FSCA CASP requirements?**  
*A: Consequences include: administrative penalties, license suspension or revocation, criminal prosecution of key individuals, reputational damage, and potential civil liability to affected customers. The FSCA also publishes enforcement actions publicly, which can severely impact business credibility.*

**Q14: How does the Sentinel coordinate with the Git-State-SafeGuard on secret scanning?**  
*A: The Git-State-SafeGuard runs pre-commit entropy and regex scans on staged git diffs. If a high-entropy secret or known credential pattern is detected, the commit is blocked and an immediate alert is dispatched to the Sentinel. The Sentinel then: (a) confirms the nature of the leaked credential, (b) triggers rotation of the compromised credential, (c) audits whether the credential appeared in any previous commits, (d) logs the incident in the compliance audit trail.*

**Q15: What is the difference between CDD and ECDD under FICA?**  
*A: Customer Due Diligence (CDD) is the standard identity verification required for all customers: verify identity against official documents, verify physical address, understand the nature and purpose of the business relationship. Enhanced Customer Due Diligence (ECDD) applies to high-risk customers (PEPs, customers from high-risk jurisdictions, complex transaction patterns) and requires: additional identity verification, source of funds/wealth verification, senior management approval, and enhanced ongoing monitoring.*

# SECTION 6: ENGINEERING & TECHNICAL GUIDELINES

## 6.1 Cryptographic & Storage Standards

**Encryption at Rest:**
- Algorithm: AES-256-GCM for all stored sensitive data
- Key Management: Hardware Security Module (HSM) for master keys; derived keys for per-service encryption
- Customer Data: AES-256 encrypted with per-customer derived keys
- Backup Encryption: Same AES-256-GCM standard applied to all backup snapshots

**Encryption in Transit:**
- Protocol: TLS 1.3 exclusively (TLS 1.2 deprecated, TLS 1.1/1.0 forbidden)
- Cipher Suites: TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384, TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256
- Certificate Management: Automated rotation via ACME protocol, minimum RSA-2048 or ECC P-256
- mTLS: Required for all inter-service communication within the Stremiium backend

**Token & Session Security:**
- JWT Tokens: RS256 signing, 15-minute access token expiry, 7-day refresh token expiry
- API Keys: 256-bit random generation, SHA-256 hashed storage, automatic rotation every 90 days
- 2FA Tokens: TOTP (RFC 6238) with 30-second time step and SHA-256 HMAC

**Hashing Standards:**
- Passwords: Argon2id with memory cost 64 MiB, time cost 3, parallelism 4
- Data Integrity: SHA-256 for file checksums, content verification, and audit log integrity
- Blockchain Addresses: Keccak-256 (Ethereum standard)

## 6.2 Secure Coding Standards

- **Input Validation:** All external inputs must be validated against strict schemas before processing.
- **Output Encoding:** All outputs must be properly encoded to prevent injection attacks (SQL, XSS, command injection).
- **Error Handling:** Error messages must never expose internal implementation details, stack traces, or sensitive data.
- **Dependency Management:** All dependencies must be version-pinned and verified against known vulnerability databases.
- **Secrets Management:** All secrets must be stored in encrypted vaults (never in code, config files, or environment variables).
- **Access Control:** Role-based access control (RBAC) with least-privilege default and explicit permission grants.

# SECTION 14: IMPLEMENTATION GUIDELINES & BEST PRACTICES

## 14.1 7-Step Security Operation Protocol

Every security-relevant operation follows this standardized protocol:

1. **Intercept:** Capture the inbound task envelope or transaction request.
2. **Sanitize:** Strip PII, secrets, and injection payloads from the context.
3. **Validate:** Check agent permissions against security-policy.json.
4. **Assess:** Evaluate risk threshold and determine if human gate is required.
5. **Screen:** Run sanctions and compliance checks on involved entities/addresses.
6. **Execute or Gate:** Process the operation (if low-risk) or pause for human approval (if high-risk).
7. **Log:** Record the complete operation lifecycle in the immutable compliance audit trail.

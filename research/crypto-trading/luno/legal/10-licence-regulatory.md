# Luno — Licences & Regulation

## 1. FSCA licensing (South Africa)

- Luno states it is **FSCA-licensed and regulated** in South Africa; multiple secondary sources credit it as **the first dedicated crypto asset service provider to receive an FSCA licence** under the FAIS framework — dated **April 2024**. [OBS]
- Licence number conflicts across secondary sources: one source cites **FSP 51334 / NCR NCRCP22123**; another cites **FSP 53314** on the FSCA CASP list (December 2025). The authoritative FSCA register should be checked directly — treat the exact number as **[UNK/UNVERIFIED]**. [UNVERIFIED]
- Luno operates under the FSCA **"Crypto Asset" FSP subcategory 1.28** for CASPs (Category I spot / Category II discretionary). [OBS]

## 2. FIC / AML & travel rule

- Luno is an accountable institution under **FICA**. As a CASP it must comply with the **FIC Directive 9 "travel rule"** for crypto asset transfers (effective **30 April 2025**, per the FIC communication). [OBS]
- Luno applies KYC verification tiers (Level 1 / Level 2 / Fully verified) with transaction limits — see `products/30-products-fees.md`. [OBS]

## 3. Exchange control / SARB

- Crypto assets are **not legal tender** in South Africa and are treated as **capital** for exchange-control purposes (draft Capital Flow Management Regulations, National Treasury). Cross-border crypto transfers may be bound by the **R2 million annual discretionary allowance** and the individual capital allowance. [OBS]
- Luno, as an FSCA-authorised CASP, is among the entities that may buy/sell/lend crypto under the draft Capital Flow regime. [OBS]

## 4. Multi-jurisdiction / corporate

- Luno has historically operated across Africa, APAC and Australia. In **2025–2026 it is exiting the EU and other non-core markets**: Luno France was struck off the AMF PSAN whitelist on **28 July 2025**, and EU accounts close by **1 September 2026** for lack of MiCA authorisation. [OBS]
- Restricted markets include the USA, Canada, China, Japan and India. [OBS]

## 5. Governance note

Luno is owned by **Digital Currency Group (DCG)** — a US crypto conglomerate that also owned Genesis. Exposure to DCG's group-level troubles (2022–2023) is a counterparty/ownership risk to flag for any NeoBank partnership. [INF]

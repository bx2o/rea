# 10 — South African Banking Licences (Banks Act / Mutual Banks / Co-operative Banks / Foreign Branches)

> Cross-bank regulatory synthesis. Public information only. Claims are tagged `[OBS]` (observed in a
> source), `[DER]` (derived), `[INF]` (inference), `[EST]` (estimate), `[UNK]` (unknown). This file
> is `status: partial` because several figures come from single or secondary sources.

## 1. Who regulates banking in South Africa

The **South African Reserve Bank (SARB)** houses the **Prudential Authority (PA)**, which licenses and
supervises banks. The PA processes licence applications under five financial-sector laws listed in
Schedule 1 of the Financial Sector Regulation Act 9 of 2017: the **Banks Act 94 of 1990**, the
**Co-operative Banks Act 40 of 2007**, the **Financial Sector Regulation Act 9 of 2017**, the
**Insurance Act 18 of 2017**, and the **Mutual Banks Act 124 of 1993** `[OBS]`
([SARB — Functions of the Prudential Authority](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/Prudentialregulation/functions-of-the-prudential-authority)).

The PA **may not issue a licence without the concurrence of the Financial Sector Conduct Authority
(FSCA)** — a dual-regulatory structure (prudential + market conduct) `[OBS]` (same source).

## 2. What is "the business of a bank"?

"The business of a bank" includes the **soliciting, advertising for, or acceptance of deposits from
the general public as a regular feature** of the institution's business. Conducting the business of a
bank in South Africa **without being licensed is an offence** `[OBS]`
([SARB — Bank licensing in the Republic of South Africa (2021 update)](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/prudential-regulation/functions-of-the-prudential-authority/licensing/Banking%20licencing%20in%20the%20Republic%20of%20South%20Africa%20(2021%20update).pdf)).

## 3. Licence classes

The SARB defines **four main banking licence types**, plus representative offices `[OBS]`
([SARB — Functions of the Prudential Authority](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/Prudentialregulation/functions-of-the-prudential-authority)):

| Class | Governing law | Ownership / legal form | Minimum capital | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial bank** | Banks Act 94 of 1990 | Public company under Companies Act 71 of 2008, or state-owned company | **R250 million** `[OBS]` | Shareholders need not be depositors |
| **Mutual bank** | Mutual Banks Act 124 of 1993 | Juristic person **owned by its depositors** (members/shareholders) | **R10 million** `[OBS]` | Member-controlled, community/nick focus |
| **Co-operative bank** | Co-operative Banks Act 40 of 2007 | Co-operative | Lower threshold `[UNK]` | Small, niche deposit-taking |
| **Branch of a foreign bank** | Banks Act 94 of 1990 | Branch of a foreign institution | **R250 million** `[OBS]` | Local branch of a foreign bank |
| **Representative office** | Banks Act 94 of 1990 | Foreign bank office | n/a | **May promote/assist** the foreign bank's business but **may not conduct the business of a bank** in SA `[OBS]` |

Sources: minimum capital figures per the [SARB Functions of the Prudential Authority](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/Prudentialregulation/functions-of-the-prudential-authority)
and the [Wikiprocedure — Apply for a Bank License](https://www.wikiprocedure.com/index.php?title=South_Africa_-_Apply_for_a_Bank_License_as_a_Deposit_Taking_Institution)
page (R250m for banks/branches; R10m for mutual banks). The representative-office restriction is per
the [Wikiprocedure — Register a Representative Office of a Foreign Bank](https://wikiprocedure.com/index.php/South_Africa_-_Register_a_Representative_Office_of_a_Foreign_Bank)
page.

## 4. How South Africa's digital cohort is licensed

`[OBS]` A secondary market guide maps the SA digital cohort onto the chartered classes: **TymeBank,
Discovery Bank, and African Bank are commercial-bank licensees; Bank Zero is a mutual-bank licensee**;
co-operative financial institutions sit under the Co-operative Banks Act 2007
([neobanks.guide — SA neobanks by SARB licence class](https://neobanks.guide/best-neobank-south-africa/)).
This is a secondary source and is tagged as such; the primary register is the SARB list below.

## 5. The register of licensed banks

`[OBS]` The SARB/PA publishes the register of SA-registered banks and representative offices. The
Corporation for Deposit Insurance (CODI) member-bank list (membership is **automatic and compulsory
for all registered banks**) states there are **14 commercial banks, 3 mutual banks, 5 co-operative
banks and 12 local branches of foreign banks** `[OBS]`
([SARB — CODI member banks](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/Deposit-insurance/member-banks0)).

`[OBS]` A separate aggregation reports **37 registered banks as of August 2025** — 13 locally
controlled commercial banks, 4 foreign-controlled banks, 12 branches of foreign institutions, 3 mutual
banks and 5 co-operative banks `[OBS]`
([Grokipedia — List of banks in South Africa](https://grokipedia.com/page/List_of_banks_in_South_Africa)).
The two counts differ (14 vs 13 commercial banks; 12 vs 12 branches) and are **not reconciled** —
treat the exact totals as `[UNK]` and use the primary SARB register PDFs as source of record.

### 5.1 Mutual banks (as at July 2025)

`[OBS]` The PA register lists **Bank Zero Mutual Bank, eNL Mutual Bank** (CEO Ms Nthabeleng Likotsi),
**Finbond Mutual Bank** and **GBS Mutual Bank** (Managing Director Mr Jonathan Fincham)
([SARB — Mutual Banks as at July 2025](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/publications/prudential-authority/sa-registered-financial-banks-and-rep-offices/2025/Mutual%20Banks%20as%20at%20July%202025.pdf)).

### 5.2 Representative offices

`[OBS]` The PA maintains a separate **"Foreign Bank Representatives"** register; representative
offices support cross-border corporate relationships, advisory and group coverage and **do not provide
full transactional retail banking**
([SARB — Foreign Bank Representatives, June 2025](https://www.southafricanreservebank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/publications/prudential-authority/sa-registered-financial-banks-and-rep-offices/2025/Foreign%20Banks%20Representative_June%202025.pdf)).

## 6. Becoming a bank in SA — the new-bank licensing framework

`[OBS]` The PA assesses applications against: objectives of the PA; **public interest**; financial and
other resources; **ownership**; **governance**; risk management and internal control systems;
information and accounting systems; prescribed legal structure; and prudential requirements
([SARB Functions of the Prudential Authority](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/what-we-do/Prudentialregulation/functions-of-the-prudential-authority)).

`[OBS]` The licensing sequence is:

1. **Pre-application consultation** with the PA (optional but expected).
2. **Application for authorisation** to establish a bank, in the prescribed form, with the information
   required by the Bank Regulations (e.g. pro-forma **BA 100** balance sheet, **BA 110** off-balance-sheet
   activities, **BA 125** return on shareholders, and written confirmation that designated share capital
   is held in trust).
3. **Assessment/authorisation** — including on-site inspections, interviews with key personnel, and
   reviews of systems and controls.
4. **Registration as a bank** — the applicant can apply for registration during the **12 months after**
   the authorisation date.

Sources: [SARB — Bank licensing in the Republic of South Africa (2021 update)](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/prudential-regulation/functions-of-the-prudential-authority/licensing/Banking%20licencing%20in%20the%20Republic%20of%20South%20Africa%20(2021%20update).pdf)
and [Chambers — Banking Regulation 2025](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/comparison/1013/14890/23401-23403-23405-23407-23411-23413-23415-23417-23419-23421-23423)
(the "12 months" detail is per Chambers `[OBS]`).

`[INF]` For a NeoBank launch, the practical consequence of this framework is that a full deposit-taking
licence requires **R250m minimum capital and a multi-month authorisation-to-registration runway**;
alternatives (mutual-bank licence at R10m; BIN sponsorship / banking-as-a-service via a licensed bank;
or starting as a non-bank payment/e-money provider) may offer a faster path, subject to the pending
non-bank payment-institution regime (see file `30-PAYMENTS-RAILS.md`).

## 7. Recent/licensing developments (context)

- `[OBS]` HSBC Bank Plc's Johannesburg branch ceased on **1 September 2025** after revocation of its
  authorisation and transfer of assets to Rand Merchant Bank
  ([Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/List_of_banks_in_South_Africa)).
- `[UNVERIFIED]` **First Abu Dhabi Bank** proposed a Johannesburg entry; the Supreme Court of Appeal
  dismissed FirstRand's bid to block it (July 2025), leaving the SARB to vet directors and operational
  infrastructure before any licence is granted
  ([Africa Truth Observer](https://africatruthobserver.com/court-ruling-puts-gulf-bank-s-sa-entry-in-regulator-s-hands/)).

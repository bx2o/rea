# Bettr — Banking Rails

## 1. Access model
- Bettr was **not a bank**; it accessed the **South African National Payment System (NPS)** via a **sponsoring bank** (Access Bank South Africa) [OBS] — [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/challenger-banks/challenger-banks-in-south-africa-who-s-who-and-what-s-their-tech), [MyBroadband](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/banking/489889-south-africas-neobanks.html)
- This is the standard pre-2026 route for fintechs: only registered banks could issue e-money / hold deposits; non-banks needed a **section 52 Banks Act arrangement** or sponsor-bank relationship [OBS] — [PRIFinance](https://prifinance.com/en/payments/south-africa-payment-license/)

## 2. Rails used
- **Visa** card scheme; QR-code payments [OBS] — [MyBroadband](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/banking/489889-south-africas-neobanks.html)
- Underlying SA rails (RTC, PayShap, SAMOS) accessible only via the sponsor bank [INF] — [PRIFinance](https://prifinance.com/en/payments/south-africa-payment-license/)

## 3. Deposits
- Bettr did not hold a banking licence, so it could not itself accept deposits as a bank; deposits were held via the Access Bank arrangement [INF] — [MyBroadband](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/banking/489889-south-africas-neobanks.html)

## 4. Regulatory change (post-Bettr)
- SA is moving to **activity-based authorisation** enabling non-banks to issue e-money/participate directly (SARB draft exemption notices 2025–2026) — the gap Bettr's founders flagged [OBS] — [PRIFinance](https://prifinance.com/en/payments/south-africa-payment-license/), [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/the-south-african-reserve-bank-has-recently-published-a-draft-activity-based-authorisation-framework-for-participants-banks-and-non-banks-in-the-national-payment-system/)

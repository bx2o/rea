## 2. Founding & journey (SA presence)

- Standard Chartered has operated in Africa since **1862** and implemented a major **Africa divestment
  programme from March 2022**, narrowing to corporate & investment banking while hiving off its Wealth &
  Retail Banking (WRB) units ([Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/business/2026-01-15-major-shift-in-african-banking/)). [OBS]
- The SA vehicle is structured as a **Johannesburg branch** (external company), not a locally incorporated
  subsidiary ([SC Johannesburg Branch Pillar 3 Q4 2025, PDF](https://www.sc.com/za/uploads/sites/36/content/docs/Pillar-3-Disclosure-Q4-25-CMT-V2.pdf)). [OBS]

## 3. What Standard Chartered SA is

A **foreign-bank branch** serving corporate and institutional clients in South Africa, publishing quarterly
**Basel Pillar 3** disclosures. It is not a retail bank in SA ([SC Pillar 3](https://www.sc.com/za/uploads/sites/36/content/docs/Pillar-3-Disclosure-Q4-25-CMT-V2.pdf),
[SARB branches register](https://www.sarb.co.za/content/dam/sarb/publications/prudential-authority/sa-registered-financial-banks-and-rep-offices/2025/Branches%20Foreign_December%202025.pdf)). [OBS]

## 4. Key findings (for the NeoBank programme)

1. **Foreign banks are leaving SA retail** — a multi-year retreat that leaves the retail space to local
   incumbents and neobanks ([Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/business/2026-01-15-major-shift-in-african-banking/),
   [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/africa/article/south-africa-is-losing-foreign-banks-so-why-is-one-of-the-worlds-largest-lenders-moving-in/)). [OBS]
2. **Branch vs subsidiary** — SC operates via a branch, a lower-commitment structure than a full local
   subsidiary — strategically lighter than rivals. [INF]

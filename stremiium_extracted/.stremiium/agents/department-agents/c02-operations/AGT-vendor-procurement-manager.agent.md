# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: VENDOR SELECTION & RFP (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: When should you run an RFP (Request for Proposal) vs. just asking for quotes?**
A: **RFP:** Use for complex or high-value purchases. Vendor must propose solution (not just quote). Examples: software implementation ($500K+), major service contract, custom development. Process: Vendor must understand requirements, propose approach, include pricing. Effort: 4-8 weeks. Cost: Time to evaluate responses. **RFQ (Request for Quote):** Use for straightforward purchases. Vendor just quotes price. Examples: office supplies, hosting at known specs. Effort: 1-2 weeks. Cost evaluation easier. Decision: RFP for strategic/complex; RFQ for commodities.

**Q2: What should be included in an RFP?**
A: Sections: (1) **Background:** Who are we? What's our business? (2) **Problem statement:** What problem are we solving? (3) **Requirements:** Functional (what must vendor do?) and non-functional (security, scalability, etc.). (4) **Timeline:** When do we need solution? Key dates? (5) **Budget:** Budget range (sometimes withheld to avoid anchoring). (6) **Evaluation criteria:** How will we evaluate? (price, quality, experience, etc.). (7) **Deliverables:** What's vendor delivering? (8) **Contract terms:** Payment, liability, IP ownership, confidentiality. (9) **Questions:** Vendors can ask clarifying questions. (10) **Submission format:** How to respond? When due? (11) **Process:** Vendor presentations? Reference calls? Timeline: 4-8 weeks typical.

**Q3: How do you evaluate vendor proposals fairly?**
A: Approach: (1) **Scoring rubric:** Define criteria (technical, commercial, financial) with weights. Example: Technical 40%, Price 35%, Experience 15%, Service 10%. (2) **Scorecards:** Each evaluator scores each proposal on same rubric (consistency). (3) **Evaluation team:** Diverse team (procurement, tech, business) evaluates (reduces individual bias). (4) **Documentation:** Document why vendor scored high/low (transparency, defensibility). (5) **References:** Check 2-3 references for top vendors. (6) **Presentations:** Top 2-3 vendors present; ask questions. (7) **Interview:** Understand vendor approach, team, commitment. (8) **Final decision:** Combine scores + qualitative assessment. Avoid: "I like this vendor" without documentation.

**Q4: What's total cost of ownership (TCO) and why does it matter?**
A: TCO = all costs to acquire and operate vendor solution. Includes: (1) **Purchase price:** Direct cost of product/service. (2) **Implementation:** Setup, configuration, training. (3) **Maintenance:** Support, updates, patches. (4) **Integration:** Integrating with other systems. (5) **Training:** Staff training, admin training. (6) **Indirect:** Time employees spend managing vendor. Example: Software A = $10K license + $20K implementation + $2K/year support = $32K first year, $12K annually. Software B = $5K license + $40K implementation + $5K/year support = $45K first year, $25K annually. If 5-year outlook: A = $32K + 4×$12K = $80K. B = $45K + 4×$25K = $145K. Vendor A wins on TCO. Decision: Always evaluate TCO, not just price.

**Q5: How do you negotiate better vendor pricing?**
A: Tactics: (1) **Competition:** Run RFP with 3+ vendors (creates competition). (2) **Benchmarking:** Know market rate (industry research, consulting firms, prior deals). (3) **Volume:** Commit to volume; ask for discount ("We'll commit to $100K/year if you discount 15%"). (4) **Bundling:** Bundle projects (larger deal = better pricing). (5) **Payment terms:** Pay in advance = discount (vendor reduces financing risk). (6) **Multi-year:** Multi-year commitment = discount (vendor locks revenue). (7) **Incumbent discounts are rare:** Don't assume incumbent automatically wins renewal; run fresh competition. (8) **Leverage:** "Strong alternative vendor also bidding; we prefer you at X price." Rule: Never accept first quote; always negotiate.

**Q6: What are the key vendor selection criteria?**
A: Criteria: (1) **Experience:** Has vendor done this before? References? (2) **Technical capability:** Can they deliver what we need? Do they have right tech? (3) **Team:** Will their best people work on our project? (4) **Financial stability:** Will vendor stay in business? (Consider market share, funding, customer concentration). (5) **Responsiveness:** Do they answer questions? Seem engaged? (6) **Cultural fit:** Do they understand our needs? Shared values? (7) **Flexibility:** Can they customize? Adapt? (8) **Support:** What's support model? Response time? SLAs? (9) **Pricing:** competitive but not lowest (usually not best). (10) **Risk:** What could go wrong? Do they mitigate risks? Weight by importance; use scoring rubric.

**Q7: When should you negotiate with single vendor vs. run competitive process?**
A: **Single vendor negotiation:** Use when: (1) Vendor is only option (proprietary tech, unique capability). (2) Switching costs too high (changing vendors would be expensive). (3) Strategic relationship (prefer to deepen relationship). (4) Time pressure (competitive process takes weeks; need faster). **Competitive process:** Use when: (1) Multiple vendors available. (2) Want best pricing. (3) Want backup plan (if selected vendor fails). (4) Complex requirements (competition drives innovation). Default: Competitive process for strategic purchases. Single vendor: Exception, not rule.

**Q8: How do you assess vendor financial stability?**
A: Approach: (1) **Financial statements:** Review annual report (if public), latest financials (if private). Look for: revenue growth, profitability, cash position, debt levels. (2) **Credit rating:** Check Dun & Bradstreet or credit agencies. (3) **Recent news:** Any bad press? Layoffs? Leadership changes? (4) **Funding:** Startup: Are they funded? Funded by credible investors? (5) **Customer concentration:** Do 1-2 customers represent 50%+ revenue? (Risky; if lose customer, vendor may fail). (6) **Market position:** Are they market leader or struggling? (6) **Backup plan:** If vendor fails, can we switch? How disruptive? High-risk vendor = need strong mitigation plan.

**Q9: What's the difference between vendor evaluation and vendor selection?**
A: **Evaluation:** Process of scoring/comparing vendors. Output: Vendor ranking. **Selection:** Decision to work with vendor. Often evaluation leads to selection of top-ranked vendor, but not always. Factors after evaluation: (1) Negotiation outcome (top-ranked vendor wants too much money). (2) Strategic timing (top vendor not available when needed). (3) Risk (top vendor has risks we decide are unacceptable). (4) Fit (top-ranked technically, but team doesn't like them). Decision: Evaluation informs selection, but business judgment + risk assessment also matter.

**Q10: How do you handle vendor due diligence (background check)?**
A: Approach: (1) **Financial check:** Review financials (stability). (2) **Reference calls:** Call 3+ references (ask about experience, quality, challenges). Ask: "Would you hire them again?" (3) **Legal/compliance:** Any lawsuits against vendor? Compliance issues? (4) **Insurance:** Do they have insurance (liability, E&O)? Is it adequate? (5) **Security/privacy:** If handling data, do they have security certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2)? Privacy compliance? (6) **Background check:** Public background check (criminal, bankruptcy). (7) **Site visit:** For large contracts, visit vendor facility (understand capabilities, capacity). Timeline: 2-4 weeks. Cost: <$5K typically. Value: Prevents vendor failures post-signature.

**Q11: What should be included in a vendor request for information (RFI)?**
A: RFI: Early-stage inquiry before full RFP. Used to: (1) Understand vendor capabilities. (2) Pre-qualify vendors (reduce RFP to serious vendors only). (3) Gather info to design RFP better. (4) Educate yourself on market. Sections: (1) **Background:** Who are we? (2) **Capability questions:** Can you do X? How? (3) **Experience:** Past customers in our industry? (4) **Approach:** How would you approach this project? (5) **Timeline:** How quickly can you start? (6) **Pricing ballpark:** What's rough cost? (No detailed pricing; just ballpark). Does NOT require formal response. Can be informal conversation. Use: 2-3 weeks before formal RFP.

**Q12: How do you manage bid rigging and vendor collusion?**
A: Risk: Multiple vendors secretly agree to divide market/quotes (illegal). Prevention: (1) **Confidentiality:** Don't share vendor identities or quotes (prevents coordination). (2) **Competition:** Use multiple vendors (harder to rig if 4+ vendors). (3) **Blind evaluation:** Evaluate proposals without knowing which vendor (if possible). (4) **Transparency:** If significant pricing spreads, ask highest-priced vendor to explain (may indicate collusion). (5) **Diversity in sourcing:** Don't always use same vendors (reduces opportunity for rigging). (6) **Reporting:** If suspect collusion (vendors have identical bids), consult legal (may be antitrust violation). Prevention easier than detection; ensure genuine competition.

**Q13: What's an appropriate proposal evaluation timeline?**
A: Timeline: (1) **RFP release:** Vendors have 3-4 weeks to respond (complex), 1-2 weeks (simple). (2) **Proposal review:** 1 week to 2 weeks (evaluate proposals). (3) **Narrowing:** If many vendors, narrow to top 3-5. (4) **References/interviews:** 1-2 weeks (check references, conduct interviews). (5) **Final decision:** Committee meets, decides. (6) **Negotiation:** 1-2 weeks (negotiate contract terms with selected vendor). **Total:** 6-12 weeks typical for major contracts. For smaller: 3-4 weeks. Avoid: Rushing evaluation (higher risk of mistakes). Plan accordingly in requirements gathering.

**Q14: When should you re-compete existing vendor contracts?**
A: Recommendation: **Every 3-5 years.** Why: (1) Market changes (new vendors, prices drop). (2) Incumbent gets lazy (doesn't improve quality/price). (3) Your needs evolve (new requirements, better alternatives). (4) Competition drives innovation (forces vendor to improve). Exception: Strategic partnerships (value beyond price; long-term relationship makes sense). Process: 6 months before contract expiration, run competitive process. Give incumbent opportunity to rebid. Result: Often incumbent wins, but at refreshed terms. Cost: Worth it; often find 10-20% savings.

# SECTION 5: STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES (SOPs)

## SOP 1: Vendor Selection & RFP Process

**Duration:** 6-12 weeks | **Frequency:** Per vendor selection | **Owner:** Procurement Manager

**Phase 1: Requirements Development (Week 1-2)**

- Define requirements (functional, non-functional, timeline, budget)
- Identify evaluation criteria (technical, price, experience, support)
- Develop RFP (or RFQ for simpler purchases)
- Identify potential vendors (3-5 competitors)

**Phase 2: RFP Marketing & Response (Week 3-6)**

- Send RFP to vendors
- Vendors ask clarifying questions; procurement answers
- Deadline for proposals
- Vendors submit proposals

**Phase 3: Evaluation (Week 7-10)**

- Evaluation team scores proposals
- Narrow to top 3-5 vendors
- Reference calls (contact vendor references)
- Vendor interviews/presentations (for serious contenders)

**Phase 4: Selection & Negotiation (Week 11-12)**

- Committee approves selected vendor
- Negotiate contract terms
- Finalize contract
- Obtain signature + legal review

**Phase 5: Onboarding**

- Vendor onboarding (setup account, documentation)
- Executive introduction (relationship building)
- Begin work

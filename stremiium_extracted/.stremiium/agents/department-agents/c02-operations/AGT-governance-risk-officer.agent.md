# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: ENTERPRISE RISK MANAGEMENT (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What's an enterprise risk and how do you identify them?**
A: Enterprise risk = threat that could significantly impact business. Categories: (1) **Strategic:** Changes in market/competition/technology. (2) **Operational:** Internal process failures, key person dependency. (3) **Financial:** Cash flow, profit margin, financial statement errors. (4) **Compliance:** Legal/regulatory violations. (5) **Cyber:** Data breach, ransomware, system failure. (6) **Reputational:** Bad publicity, customer dissatisfaction. Identification: (1) Executive interviews. (2) Board discussion. (3) Industry benchmarking. (4) Audit findings. (5) Incident history. Update: Quarterly review.

**Q2: What's a risk register?**
A: Risk register = document listing all enterprise risks with: (1) **Risk description:** What's the risk? (2) **Likelihood:** How likely (1-5 scale)? (3) **Impact:** If occurs, how bad (1-5 scale)? (4) **Risk score:** Likelihood × Impact. (5) **Current controls:** What prevents/mitigates risk? (6) **Mitigation:** Additional actions to reduce risk? (7) **Owner:** Who's responsible? (8) **Status:** On track, at-risk, mitigated? Register updated: Quarterly. Reviewed by: Executive team + Board. Usage: Guides risk management priorities.

**Q3: How do you prioritize risks?**
A: Matrix: Plot likelihood vs. impact. (1) **High likelihood, high impact:** Must mitigate (top priority). (2) **High likelihood, low impact:** Monitor. (3) **Low likelihood, high impact:** Mitigate (if cost-effective). (4) **Low likelihood, low impact:** Accept. Example: Cyber breach (low likelihood but high impact if happens) → medium priority. Key person loss (medium likelihood, high impact) → high priority. Cash flow crisis (variable). Financial statement error (low likelihood for healthy company, but high impact). Priority: Address high-risk items first.

**Q4: What's the difference between probability (likelihood) and impact?**
A: **Likelihood:** Probability risk occurs (low to high). Example: We have 20% chance of data breach (based on incident history, industry benchmarks). **Impact:** Damage if risk occurs (1-5 scale). Example: If data breach, we lose $5M (customer lawsuits, notifications, remediation). Risk score: Likelihood × Impact. Example: 20% × 5 = 1 (moderate risk, deserves mitigation). For each risk, estimate both dimensions.

**Q5: What's the difference between risk acceptance and risk mitigation?**
A: **Risk acceptance:** Acknowledge risk exists; accept consequences if it happens. Example: Small business might accept cyber breach risk (too expensive to fully protect) but have insurance backup. **Risk mitigation:** Take action to reduce likelihood or impact. Example: Large company invests in security to reduce breach likelihood. Decision: Cost of mitigation vs. potential impact. Accept small risks; mitigate large risks (if cost-effective mitigation exists).

**Q6: How do you develop a business continuity plan?**
A: Plan: (1) **Identify critical functions:** What must not fail? (customer service, payment processing, etc.). (2) **Dependencies:** What's required for critical functions? (systems, data, people, vendors). (3) **Scenarios:** What could go wrong? (data center outage, key person loss, vendor failure). (4) **Response:** For each scenario, what's plan? (backup system location? backup vendor?). (5) **Recovery time objective (RTO):** How long can you wait to recover? (hours? days?). (6) **Recovery point objective (RPO):** How much data can you afford to lose? (minutes? hours?). (7) **Test:** Annually test plan (do recovery procedures actually work?). Complexity: Simple (startup) to complex (enterprise).

**Q7: What's a risk appetite statement?**
A: Statement: "We accept risk level X in category Y because it supports business objective Z." Examples: (1) We'll accept 5% chance of key person loss in exchange for speed (hiring/training takes time). (2) We'll invest in security to reduce cyber breach risk below 2% (enterprise customers require this). (3) We'll accept regulatory risk if compliance cost >20% of revenue (efficiency/cost tradeoff). Purpose: Clear, Board-approved limits on risk. Answered questions: What risks is company willing to take? What risks are unacceptable? Updates: Annually or when strategy changes.

**Q8: How do you manage vendor risk?**
A: Approach: (1) **Identify critical vendors:** Which vendors are most critical to business? (payment processor, cloud provider, etc.). (2) **Evaluate:** Does vendor have adequate controls? (security, financial stability, disaster recovery?). (3) **Contract:** Contract should require vendor maintain controls + notify if breach. (4) **Monitor:** Regular vendor risk assessments (annual or quarterly). (5) **Backup:** For critical vendors, have backup plan (alternative vendor, internal capability). Example: Payment processor is critical → require vendor to have industry certifications, financial stability analysis, redundancy for payment processing.

**Q9: What's key person risk and how do you manage it?**
A: Risk: Company depends on 1-2 critical people (CEO, CTO, sales leader). If person leaves/dies/disables, company at risk. Management: (1) **Documentation:** Document knowledge (process guides, decision frameworks). (2) **Development:** Groom successors (backups ready if needed). (3) **Cross-training:** Multiple people understand critical roles. (4) **Incentives:** Keep key people (retention bonuses, equity, growth). (5) **Insurance:** Key person insurance (if key person dies, insurance pays, company can hire replacement). Scale: Larger company needs deeper bench; sole founder has higher key person risk.

**Q10: How often should you review/update your risk register?**
A: Frequency: **Quarterly minimum,** more often if: (1) Company experiencing rapid growth/change. (2) Major incident occurred (forces risk re-evaluation). (3) Major change in market/competitor. (4) New regulation. (5) Cyber threat raised. For startups: Quarterly + ad-hoc. For mature companies: Quarterly. Process: (1) Review existing risks (any status changes?). (2) Update risk scores (likelihood/impact). (3) Assess mitigation effectiveness. (4) Identify new risks. (5) Report to Board.

**Q11: What's the difference between a risk and an issue?**
A: **Risk:** Potential future problem (hasn't happened yet). Example: Data breach risk (we might be hacked). **Issue:** Current problem that needs fixing. Example: We were hacked (data breach issue). Management: **Risk** → prevent occurrence, mitigate impact if occurs. **Issue** → diagnose, fix root cause, prevent recurrence. Lifecycle: Risk (if not mitigated) → Issue (if occurs) → Learning (fix + improve).

**Q12: How do you communicate risks to the Board?**
A: Report: (1) **Risk dashboard:** Visual summary of risk score, status, trend. (2) **Key risks:** Detailed discussion of top 5-10 risks. (3) **Mitigation:** What's company doing to reduce risk? (4) **Confidence:** What controls are in place to catch problems? (5) **Changes:** Any new risks? Any risks improving? (6) **Questions:** Opportunity for Board to ask questions/raise concerns. Frequency: Board meeting = risk report. Escalate: If material risk changes, notify Board between meetings immediately.

**Q13: What's a stress test or scenario analysis?**
A: Scenario: "What if X happens? How would company respond?" Examples: (1) Major customer (20% revenue) leaves → financial impact? Mitigation? (2) Key employee loses 3 months (illness) → coverage plan? (3) Cyber attack prevents system access for 24 hours → impact on revenue/customers? How to recover? (4) Competitor launches competing product → competitive response? (5) Interest rate increase → impact on debt costs/borrowing? Purpose: Understand potential impacts without waiting for them to happen. Frequency: Annual + ad-hoc for major changes.

**Q14: What are the top enterprise risks for SaaS/tech companies?**
A: Top risks: (1) **Cyber security** (data breach, ransomware). (2) **Customer concentration** (lose major customer, revenue drops significantly). (3) **Key person** (CEO/CTO loss). (4) **Money** (cash burn, can't raise funding). (5) **Competition** (new competitor, lose market share). (6) **Compliance** (regulation changes, breach = fine). (7) **Partner/vendor** (payment processor fails, cloud provider outage). (8) **Talent** (can't hire/retain talent). (9) **Product** (security vulnerability in product). (10) **Reputation** (bad publicity). Mitigation: Varies by company; board should understand and approve.

# SECTION 5: STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES (SOPs)

## SOP 1: Annual Risk Assessment & Register Update

**Duration:** 4 weeks | **Frequency:** Annual (Quarterly status update) | **Owner:** CRO + Risk Committee

**Phase 1: Risk Identification (Week 1)**

- Executive interviews (what's keeping you awake at night?)
- Board discussion (what risks worry you?)
- Industry benchmark (what are peer risks?)
- Incident review (what happened last year?)
- New risk brainstorm (market, tech, regulation changes)

**Phase 2: Risk Quantification (Week 2-3)**

- Likelihood assessment (probability scale 1-5)
- Impact assessment (if occurs, what's damage? 1-5 scale)
- Risk scoring (likelihood × impact)
- Control evaluation (what prevents/mitigates?)
- Gap identification (additional controls needed?)

**Phase 3: Mitigation Planning (Week 3-4)**

- Mitigation strategy (reduce likelihood? reduce impact? transfer?)
- Owner assignment (who's accountable?)
- Timeline/budget (what's needed? when?)
- Board approval (do we accept these risks?)

## SOP 3: Incident Response Activation

**Duration:** Minutes to days | **Frequency:** Per incident | **Owner:** CRO + Executive Team

**Phase 1: Detection & Escalation (First 1 hour)**

- Incident reported
- Initial assessment (is it material?)
- Incident commander assigned
- Incident response team convened
- CRO + General Counsel notified

**Phase 2: Containment (First 4 hours)**

- Understand scope (what happened? what's affected?)
- Contain damage (isolate affected system? warn employees?)
- Notify stakeholders (customers? Board? regulators?)
- Preserve evidence (for investigation/forensics)

**Phase 3: Investigation (24 hours to weeks)**

- Root cause analysis (why did it happen?)
- Impact assessment (how bad is it?)
- Communication (customers, Board, regulators)
- Remediation (fix + prevent recurrence)

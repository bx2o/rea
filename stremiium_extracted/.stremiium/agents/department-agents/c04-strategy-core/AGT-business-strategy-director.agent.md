# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: BUSINESS STRATEGY FUNDAMENTALS (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What's the difference between vision, mission, and strategy?**
A: **Vision:** What company aspires to be in future (long-term, inspiring). Example: "Democratize financial services." **Mission:** What company does today (business model, purpose). Example: "Provide easy, affordable investing tools." **Strategy:** How company will achieve vision (2-5 year plan, specific actions). Example: "Enter underserved markets in Latin America; build mobile-first product; partner with local banks." All three important; guide decision-making.

**Q2: What's a SWOT analysis and how do you use it?**
A: SWOT = Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. **Strengths:** What are we good at? (team, technology, brand, culture). **Weaknesses:** What are we bad at? (limited resources, weaker brand, smaller team vs. competitors). **Opportunities:** What could we do? (new markets, new products, partnerships, trends). **Threats:** What could hurt us? (competition, market changes, technology shifts, regulation). Use: Strategic planning (build on strengths, fix weaknesses, pursue opportunities, defend against threats). Typical output: 2-page SWOT document. Update: Annually or when major change.

**Q3: What's TAM (Total Addressable Market) and why does it matter?**
A: TAM = Total revenue opportunity in market. Example: If total US market for software = $500B, and we focus on HR software = $20B TAM, we're niche. Why: (1) Helps define growth ceiling (can we grow to be $1B company in this market?). (2) Attracts investors (investors want large TAM; small TAM = limited growth potential). (3) Guides strategy (go after large TAM or small, profitable niche?). How to estimate: (1) Research industry reports. (2) Bottom-up (estimate # customers × average revenue per customer). (3) Top-down (estimate % of large market we could serve). Estimate: Within 2-3x (rough order of magnitude).

**Q4: What's competitive positioning and why does it matter?**
A: Positioning: Why customers choose you vs. competitors. Strong positioning: (1) **Different:** You offer something competitors don't. (2) **Valuable:** Difference matters to customers. (3) **Defensible:** Competitors can't easily copy. Weak positioning: "We're like X but cheaper" (competitors can copy price). Strong positioning: "We're the only solution for healthcare-specific use case" (hard to copy). How to define: (1) Understand customer needs. (2) Analyze competitor offerings. (3) Find gap (what competitors don't do? what matters to customers?). (4) Build positioning around gap. Communicate: In marketing, sales, product.

**Q5: What's the difference between organic growth and inorganic growth?**
A: **Organic growth:** Growing from within (build new products, sell to new markets, increase sales). Pros: Profitable, aligned with company. Cons: Slower, requires building everything. **Inorganic growth:** Growing through acquisition or partnership. Pros: Faster, can buy market share, acquire capability. Cons: Expensive, integration risk, distraction. Most companies use both: Organic for core growth; inorganic for faster expansion or capability. Example: Pinterest: organic growth for core product; inorganic for international expansion (acquired local companies).

**Q6: How do you develop a 3-year strategic plan?**
A: Process: (1) **Assess current state:** What's company doing well? Gaps? (2) **Define vision:** Where do we want to be in 5 years? (3) **Analyze market:** TAM, competition, trends. (4) **Strategic options:** 3-4 possible growth strategies; evaluate trade-offs. (5) **Select strategy:** Pick best option. (6) **Detail plan:** Year 1 detailed; years 2-3 directional. (7) **Financials:** Develop financial projections for 3 years. (8) **Implications:** What does strategy require? (people, budget, technology?). (9) **Communication:** Present to Board/stakeholders. Effort: 8-12 weeks. Frequency: Annual update.

**Q7: What's market segmentation and why does it matter?**
A: Market segmentation: Divide market into distinct groups (segments) with different needs. Examples: (1) By geography (US, Europe, Asia). (2) By company size (SMB, mid-market, enterprise). (3) By industry (healthcare, finance, retail). (4) By use case (workflow management, reporting, collaboration). Why: (1) Different segments = different strategies (messaging, pricing, product). (2) Identify highest-value segments (focus there first). (3) Customize product/messaging per segment. Example: Slack = SMBs want easy; Enterprise wants security. Two different strategies.

**Q8: What's the difference between TAM, SAM, and SOM?**
A: **TAM:** Total addressable market (entire market). **SAM:** Serviceable addressable market (realistic portion of TAM we could serve). **SOM:** Serviceable obtainable market (realistic portion we could capture in near term). Example: TAM = global software market ($500B). SAM = HR software market ($20B). SOM = mid-market HR software US ($2B). Use: TAM for strategic opportunity; SAM/SOM for realistic planning. Progression: Start in niche (focus on winning SOM); expand to SAM; eventually TAM.

**Q9: How do you analyze competition?**
A: Approach: (1) **List competitors:** Direct (same market), indirect (alternative solutions). (2) **Positioning:** How does each position? What's their pitch? (3) **Offering:** What do they offer? Strengths? Gaps? (4) **Pricing:** What's their price? Value prop? (5) **Go-to-market:** How do they sell? Market presence? (6) **Financials:** Size? Growth? Profitability? (7) **Strengths/weaknesses:** How do they compete? Where are they vulnerable? (8) **Threats:** What's their strategy? Could they beat us? Output: Competitive matrix (comparison chart). Update: Quarterly. Use: Strategy, product, marketing, sales decisions.

**Q10: What's business model innovation and give examples?**
A: Business model = how company makes money. Business model innovation = new way to make money. Examples: (1) **Subscription:** Instead of one-time purchase, recurring revenue (SaaS). (2) **Marketplace:** Sellers + buyers; take commission (Airbnb, DoorDash). (3) **Freemium:** Free + paid tiers (Slack, Zoom). (4) **Platform:** Ecosystem of partners (Apple App Store, Salesforce AppExchange). (5) **Data:** Offer product free, monetize via ads/data (Google, Facebook). Why: Sometimes industry shift = business model shift. Example: Music industry: CDs → Streaming (Spotify). Identify: Could we change business model for more value/growth?

**Q11: What's strategy execution and why do many strategies fail?**
A: Common failure reasons: (1) **Misalignment:** Teams don't understand strategy. (2) **Too ambitious:** Too many initiatives; teams diluted. (3) **No accountability:** Goals not clear; people don't own. (4) **Resourcing:** Strategy requires resources; didn't allocate. (5) **External changes:** Market changes; strategy becomes misaligned. (6) **Leadership change:** New leader → new strategy. Successful execution: (1) Clear strategy (everyone understands). (2) Cascade to goals/OKRs (team goals align with strategy). (3) Accountability (clear who's responsible). (4) Resources (fund priorities). (5) Regular review (track progress, adjust). (6) Communication (remind team frequently).

**Q12: How do you evaluate M&A (acquisition) opportunities?**
A: Process: (1) **Strategic fit:** Does target fit our strategy? (2) **Financial:** Revenue/profitability attractive? Growth rate? Margins? (3) **Valuation:** Price reasonable? Compare to comparable deals. (4) **Synergies:** What do we gain? Cost savings? Revenue upside? (5) **Risk:** Integration risk? Team retention? Technology compatibility? (6) **Culture:** Do cultures align? Could we integrate well? (7) **Timeline:** How long to close? Key risks? (8) **Decision:** Is synergy value > acquisition cost? Go/no-go. Typical evaluation: 4-8 weeks. Cost: Legal, advisors, diligence = $500K-2M+.

**Q13: What's scenario planning and why does it matter?**
A: Scenario planning: Imagine potential futures; plan for each. Scenarios: (1) **Best case:** Market booms; execution flawless; we win big. (2) **Base case:** Market grows normally; competition exists; we grow adequately. (3) **Worst case:** Recession; strong competitor; we fight for survival. Plan: For each scenario, what happens? What decisions would we make? What triggers would cause scenario shift? Benefit: Develop contingency plans; prepared if market changes. Frequency: Annual; update if major change.

**Q14: How often should you revisit/update strategy?**
A: Schedule: (1) **Annual:** Major strategic review (are we on track? market changes?). (2) **Quarterly:** Progress update (are we executing?). (3) **Triggered:** If major market/competitive change. Update triggers: (1) Major competitor emerges. (2) Significant market shift. (3) Major product failure. (4) Unexpected opportunity. (5) Market downturn/recession. Process: Annual review should be 2-3 day offsite with leadership team. You don't update entire strategy every quarter; just assess progress and adjust as needed.

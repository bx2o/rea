# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS

## 4.1 Sample FAQs

**Q1: What's a business case and how do you develop one?**
A: Business case: Document making case for investment (should we do this?). Content: (1) **Problem/opportunity:** What's the issue we're solving? (2) **Proposed solution:** How does project solve it? (3) **Benefits:** Financial (revenue, cost savings, margin improvement) and non-financial (customer satisfaction, risk reduction). (4) **Costs:** Investment required (people, capital, time). (5) **ROI calculation:** Benefits vs. costs; payback period; NPV; IRR. (6) **Timeline:** When will benefits be realized? (7) **Risks:** What could go wrong? Mitigation? (8) **Recommendation:** Should we proceed? Typical: 5-15 pages. Cost: 40-80 hours to develop. Used for: Go/no-go decision.

**Q2: What's ROI and how do you calculate it?**
A: ROI (Return on Investment) = (Benefits - Costs) / Costs × 100%. Example: Project costs $100K; generates $300K in benefits (cost savings). ROI = ($300K - $100K) / $100K × 100% = 200% ROI. Meaning: For every $1 invested, get $3 back (net $2 gain). Typical IT project: 1-2 year payback period; 20-50% annual ROI. Variations: (1) **Payback period:** How many months until investment recouped? (2) **NPV:** Net present value (accounting for time value of money). (3) **IRR:** Internal rate of return (discount rate where NPV = 0). Use: ROI to compare projects; understand value.

**Q3: How do you measure success of a strategic initiative?**
A: Success metrics: (1) **Completion metrics:** Did we finish on time? On budget? Scope delivered? (2) **Impact metrics:** Did we achieve the goals? (revenue growth, cost reduction, customer satisfaction improvement?). (3) **Financial metrics:** What was actual ROI? Cost per unit benefit? (4) **Speed metrics:** How fast did we execute? Time to market? (5) **Quality metrics:** How good was the outcome? Defect rate? Customer satisfaction? (6) **Adoption metrics:** Did people actually use it? (7) **Leading indicators:** Midterm metrics that predict success. Establish before launch; measure regularly; adjust if off-track.

**Q4: What are KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) and how do you define them?**
A: KPIs: Metrics showing if strategic goals are being achieved. Examples: (1) **Revenue growth:** Revenue increased by X% vs. prior year. (2) **Market share:** % of market are we? Growing? (3) **Customer satisfaction:** NPS score (Net Promoter Score), customer satisfaction rating. (4) **Cost reduction:** Costs down by X% vs. baseline. (5) **Efficiency:** Cost per customer, revenue per employee. (6) **Timeline:** Time to close deal, time to launch product. Definition: (1) What's the metric? (2) How to measure? (3) Baseline: What's current? (4) Target: Where want to be? (5) Frequency: How often measured? Typical: 5-10 KPIs per strategy. Review: Quarterly or monthly.

**Q5: How do you prioritize initiatives?**
A: Framework: Plot initiatives on matrix: Impact (high/low) vs. Effort (easy/hard). (1) **High impact, low effort:** Do first (quick wins). (2) **High impact, high effort:** Do second (big projects). (3) **Low impact, low effort:** Do if time (nice-to-haves). (4) **Low impact, high effort:** Avoid (wasteful). Factors: (1) Strategic alignment (how much does this support strategy?). (2) Risk (what could go wrong?). (3) Timing (when do we need this?). (4) Resource availability (do we have people?). Use: Matrix helps objectify; prevents "shiny object" bias.

**Q6: What's a process map and why is it useful?**
A: Process map: Visual representation of how a process works. Shows: Steps, decisions, people involved, handoffs, bottlenecks. Useful for: (1) Understanding current process (baseline). (2) Identifying problems (where do things slow down? Who's confused?). (3) Designing improvements (how could we streamline?). (4) Training (how to do the process). (5) Standardization (everyone does same process). Types: Swimlane (shows who does what), flowchart (simple flow), detailed process flow. Tools: Visio, Lucidchart, Miro. Cost: 4-8 hours to map typical process.

**Q7: What's a bottleneck and how do you fix it?**
A: Bottleneck: Step that slows down entire process (and is the limiting factor). Example: Sales deal approval is slowest step; waiting for approval blocks customer onboarding. Finding: (1) Map process. (2) Time each step. (3) Identify slowest step. (4) That's the bottleneck. Fixing: (1) Streamline step (eliminate waste, parallelize). (2) Add capacity (more people, better tools). (3) Automate (if applicable). (4) Delegate (if possible). Example fix: Approval takes 5 days. Solution: Pre-approve deals <$10K (auto-approved), escalate larger deals. Result: Most deals approved in 1 day.

**Q8: How do you manage initiative risk?**
A: Risk management: (1) **Identify risks:** What could go wrong? Brainstorm. (2) **Assess:** Probability (likely?) × Impact (bad if happens?). High probability + high impact = high risk. (3) **Mitigate:** Plan to reduce probability or impact. (4) **Monitor:** Track risks; escalate if materializing. (5) **Contingency:** Backup plan if risk happens. Example risks: (1) Resource unavailable (mitigation: cross-train). (2) Vendor failure (mitigation: backup vendor). (3) Scope creep (mitigation: strict change control). (4) Timeline slip (mitigation: buffer in schedule). Address high-risk items first.

**Q9: What's change management and why does it matter for initiatives?**
A: Change management: Managing human side of change (not just technical changes). Why: People resist change (fear, uncertainty, disruption to routine). Approach: (1) **Communication:** Explain why change happening + benefits to them. (2) **Training:** Teach how to use new process/system. (3) **Support:** Help during transition (FAQ, support line). (4) **Leadership modeling:** Leaders demonstrate commitment; use new way too. (5) **Feedback:** Listen to concerns; adjust if issues. (6) **Celebrate:** Recognize adoption + benefits. Without change management: Initiative technically succeeds but people don't use it (adoption fails).

**Q10: How do you measure process improvement impact?**
A: Approach: (1) **Establish baseline:** How does process work today? Measure: Cost, time, quality, consistency. (2) **Implement improvement:** New process. (3) **Measure new state:** Same metrics after improvement. (4) **Calculate impact:** Cost per unit down? Time reduced? Quality improved? Consistency increased? (5) **Calculate ROI:** Savings - improvement cost = net benefit. Typical: Process improvement costs $50K; saves $200K annually; ROI = $200K / $50K = 4:1 ROI; payback = 3 months. Example: Order processing cycle reduced from 5 days to 2 days; cost per order reduced from $50 to $35.

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Twenty-Five Core FAQs

**Q1: What's lean methodology and the 8 wastes?**
Lean: Eliminate waste, keep value. Eight wastes: (1) Transport (unnecessary movement). (2) Inventory (excess stock). (3) Motion (unnecessary movement within workplace). (4) Waiting (idle time). (5) Overprocessing (extra steps). (6) Overproduction (making more than needed). (7) Defects (fixing mistakes). (8) People (underutilized skills). Goal: Remove waste; keep value. Benefit: Faster, cheaper, better processes.

**Q2: What's value stream mapping?**
VSM: Visual map of process showing every step, from input to output. Map shows: (1) Process steps (boxes). (2) Information/material flow (arrows). (3) Timing (how long each step takes). (4) Bottlenecks (slow steps). (5) Inventory (pile-ups). Purpose: Visualize process; identify improvements. Output: Flowchart showing current state and proposed improvements.

**Q3: What's a kaizen event?**
Kaizen: Japanese for continuous improvement. Event: Team gathers (1 day to 1 week) to improve specific process. Approach: (1) Understand current process (walk through it). (2) Brainstorm improvements. (3) Identify quick wins. (4) Implement improvements. (5) Test and refine. Benefit: Rapid improvement with team engagement. Frequency: Monthly or quarterly events improve culture.

**Q4: How do you identify process bottlenecks?**
Approach: (1) Map process (every step, timing). (2) Identify long steps (which steps take most time?). (3) Analyze: Why is step slow? (3) Root cause: Is it the activity itself or wait time? (4) Impact: Speeding this step, what's improvement to overall cycle? (5) Priority: Fix highest-impact bottleneck first. Benefit: Focus improvement efforts on highest-impact opportunities.

**Q5: What's process cycle time?**
Cycle time: Time from process start to finish. Includes: (1) Actual work time. (2) Wait time. (3) Queue time. Improvement: Remove wait/queue time. Example: Hiring process cycle time: 6 weeks (2 weeks to post, 2 weeks review, 1 week negotiations, 1 week onboarding). Goal: Reduce to 4 weeks (improve speed).

**Q6: How do you calculate ROI for process improvement?**
Calculation: ROI = (Savings - Cost of improvement) / Cost of improvement × 100%. Example: Process improvement costs $20K to implement. Saves $100K annually in labor. ROI = ($100K - $20K) / $20K × 100% = 400% ROI. Payback: 2.4 months ($20K improvement cost / $100K annual savings × 12 months). Use: Prioritize improvements with highest ROI.

**Q7: What's automation vs. optimization?**
**Optimization:** Improve existing process. Example: Hiring form takes 20 minutes; optimize to 10 minutes. Cost: Low. Impact: Marginal. **Automation:** Remove manual process. Example: Hiring form auto-fills from ATS; takes 2 minutes. Cost: High (tool investment). Impact: Significant. Decision: Optimize first (high impact/low cost); automate high-volume repetitive processes.

**Q8: How do you handle resistance to process change?**
Approach: (1) Explain why change needed (business case). (2) Involve employees (they know process best; ask for input). (3) Pilot (test new process with small group). (4) Train (help employees learn new way). (5) Support (be responsive to questions/issues). (6) Monitor (track improvement; celebrate wins). (7) Iterate (refine based on experience). Resistance is normal; need change management.

**Q9: What's benchmarking?**
Benchmarking: Compare your process to industry best practices. Approach: (1) Identify metric (hiring cycle time, cost per unit, etc.). (2) Research: What's industry average? What's best-in-class? (3) Compare: How do we compare? (4) Analyze: Why are we faster/slower? (5) Learn: What can we learn from best-in-class? Purpose: Understand realistic improvement targets.

**Q10: How often should you optimize processes?**
Frequency: Ongoing continuous improvement, plus: (1) **Annual:** Formal process review; identify major optimization opportunities. (2) **Quarterly:** Team meetings; identify quick wins. (3) **Monthly:** Kaizen events for high-impact processes. (4) **Ad-hoc:** When problems emerge (bottlenecks, quality issues), investigate and improve. Culture: Continuous improvement mindset (always looking for ways to improve).

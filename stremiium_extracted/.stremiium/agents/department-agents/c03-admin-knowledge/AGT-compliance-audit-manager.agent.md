# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Twenty-Five Core FAQs

**Q1: What's an internal audit?**
Audit: Independent evaluation of company operations/controls. Determines if company following policies, complying with standards, operating effectively. Focus areas: Financial controls, operational processes, compliance, data security. Output: Audit report with findings and recommendations. Benefit: Identify gaps before they cause problems.

**Q2: How do you develop an audit plan?**
Plan: Identify areas to audit (prioritize by risk). Schedule audits for the year. Approach: (1) Risk assessment (highest-risk areas). (2) Management input (what concerns them?). (3) Compliance requirements (must audit certain things). (4) Prior findings (audit areas with prior issues). (5) Timing (spread throughout year). Plan: Reviewed and approved by management/Board.

**Q3: What's the difference between an internal auditor and external auditor?**
**Internal:** Works for company. Observes operations regularly. Focus: Improvement + compliance. Relationship: Consultative (trying to help improve). **External:** Works for audit firm. Conducts annual/periodic audits. Focus: Financial statement accuracy, compliance with standards. Relationship: Independent (must be objective). Both important: Internal finds issues; external validates approach.

**Q4: What's a SOC 2 audit?**
SOC 2: Service Organization Control audit. Evaluates: Security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy of customer data. Two types: (1) **Type I:** Point-in-time assessment (company meets controls on specific date). (2) **Type II:** Operating effectively over time period (usually 6-12 months). Benefit: Customers trust company has security controls. Cost: $30-100K+ depending on complexity. Timeline: 6-12 months. Common: SaaS companies pursue SOC 2.

**Q5: What are the main audit findings? (severity levels)**
Findings: Issues identified in audit. Severity: (1) **Critical:** Immediate threat to company (security breach, fraud risk). (2) **High:** Significant risk (financial controls weakness). (3) **Medium:** Important (operational inefficiency). (4) **Low:** Minor (suggests improvement). Each finding: What's the issue? Why is it problem? What should be improved? Management responds with remediation plan.

**Q6: How long does remediation take?**
Timeline: Depends on severity + complexity. **Critical:** 30 days. **High:** 60 days. **Medium:** 90 days. **Low:** 6+ months. Remediation: Management proposes solution, implements, then audit confirms fixed. Track: Central log of all findings + remediation status.

**Q7: What makes a good audit finding?**
Good finding: (1) **Specific:** Describes exact issue (not vague). (2) **Evidence-based:** Supports with evidence found during audit. (3) **Impact:** Explains why it matters. (4) **Actionable:** Management knows what to fix. (5) **Objective:** Facts, not opinions. Example good: "Expense reports >$1K reviewed but not approved by manager per policy. Found 15% of sample without approval." Example bad: "Controls need improvement."

**Q8: How do you test whether a control is operating effectively?**
Testing: (1) **Ask:** How should control work? (Understand design). (2) **Observe:** Watch control in operation. (3) **Sample:** Test 25-30 examples (not all). (4) **Verify:** Confirm control is working (not just designed). (5) **Evidence:** Collect evidence (sign-offs, log entries, etc.). (6) **Assess:** Is control operating as designed? Effective test: Reasonable confidence in control design and operation.

**Q9: What's segregation of duty (SOD)?**
SOD: One person shouldn't have too much power (authorizes + executes + reconciles). Example: Accounts payable person creates purchase order, approves payment, AND reconciles payments = too much power (could commit fraud). Solution: Different people. Testing: Verify process separates duties.

**Q10: What's the difference between compliance and effectiveness?**
**Compliance:** Following policy/rules. ("Did we do what we said we'd do?"). **Effectiveness:** Policy achieves goals. ("Is policy achieving intended outcome?"). Example: Policy says approve expenses >$1K. Compliance: Are all >$1K expenses approved? Effectiveness: Is policy controlling costs? Is approval actually working? Both matter: Compliance ensures rules followed; effectiveness ensures rules are good.

# SECTION 5: SOPs (STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES)

## 5.1 SOP 1: Annual Audit Plan Development

**Duration:** 2 weeks | **Frequency:** Annually

1. Risk assessment: Identify high-risk areas
2. Management input: Gather stakeholder concerns
3. Compliance review: Check regulatory requirements
4. Resource planning: Allocate audit resources
5. Schedule development: Spread throughout year
6. Plan approval: Board/management sign-off

## 5.2 SOP 2: Internal Audit Execution

**Duration:** 1-3 weeks | **Frequency:** Per audit

1. Audit planning: Define scope and methodology
2. Opening meeting: Communicate purpose
3. Process review: Understand operations
4. Control testing: Test through sampling
5. Evidence gathering: Document with proof
6. Finding development: Draft with severity
7. Exit meeting: Present findings
8. Report preparation: Finalize report

## 5.3 SOP 3: Finding Remediation Tracking

**Duration:** Ongoing | **Frequency:** Weekly review

1. Finding assignment: Allocate to responsible party
2. Remediation plan: Develop action items
3. Progress monitoring: Track completion
4. Verification: Confirm remediation
5. Finding closure: Document resolution

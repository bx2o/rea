- **EE Act Compliance:** Affirmative action measures, designated group representation targets

- **Discrimination Prohibition:** Equal pay for equal work, fair recruitment and promotion practices
- **Skills Development:** Workplace skills plans (WSP), annual training reports (ATR) for SETA compliance

**Labor Relations Act (LRA):**

- **Dismissal Procedures:** Progressive discipline, substantive and procedural fairness requirements
- **CCMA & Bargaining Councils:** Dispute resolution procedures, conciliation and arbitration processes
- **Strike & Lockout:** Protected strike procedures, essential services designation considerations
- **Organizational Rights:** Trade union access, stop-order facilities, workplace forums

- **Working Hours:** Maximum hours, overtime pay, meal intervals, rest periods
- **Leave Entitlements:** Annual leave (21 days), sick leave, family responsibility leave, maternity leave (4 months)
- **Sectoral Determinations:** Information technology sector wage minimums and conditions
- **Termination Notice:** Minimum notice periods (1-4 weeks based on tenure)
- **Payslip Requirements:** Detailed payslip obligations, record retention

**Other Key Legislation:**

- **Skills Development Act:** Skills levies (1% payroll), mandatory training requirements
- **Unemployment Insurance Act (UIA):** UIF contributions, benefits administration
- **Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA):** Workplace injury coverage
- **Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA):** Safe working environment, remote work ergonomics

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: HIRING & ONBOARDING COMPLIANCE (14 FAQs)**

**Q1: What should be included in an offer letter?**
A: Key sections: (1) **Position:** Title, reporting relationship, location. (2) **Compensation:** Base salary, bonus target (if any), commission structure. (3) **Start date:** When does employment begin? (4) **At-will:** Employment is at-will (can be terminated by either party). (5) **Conditions:** Contingent on background check, reference checks, etc. (6) **Benefits:** Health insurance, retirement, PTO. (7) **IP assignment:** Employee agrees to assign work product to company. (8) **Confidentiality:** Employee agrees to NDA. (9) **Acceptance:** Employee signs acknowledging terms. Length: 1-2 pages. All should have offer letter.

**Q2: Are non-compete agreements enforceable?**
A: Enforceability varies by state: (1) **California:** Generally not enforceable (employee can take any job). (2) **Texas, Florida:** Enforceable if reasonable in scope/time/geography. (3) **Use:** Non-competes typically used for: executives, sales team, technical IP holders. (4) **Terms:** Must be reasonable (6-12 months typical, limited geography). (5) **Consideration:** Employee must get something in return (job, promotion, equity). If using: Consider state law carefully; consult counsel.

**Q3: What should happen before hiring someone?**
A: Pre-hire checklist: (1) **Job description:** Clear, non-discriminatory description. (2) **Recruiting:** Legal recruitment (no discrimination in job posting/sourcing). (3) **Screening:** Review resumes, conduct phone screens (non-discriminatory questions). (4) **Interviews:** Structured interviews (same questions for all candidates); no discriminatory questions (age, religion, health, national origin, etc.). (5) **References:** Call 2-3 references, document. (6) **Background check:** Run background check (FCRA-compliant), validate legitimacy of history. (7) **Offer:** Extend offer with terms. (8) **Onboarding:** Prepare for day 1 (offer letter + handbook + benefits setup).

**Q4: Can you ask about criminal history in interviews?**
A: Framework: (1) **Can't ask:** About arrests that didn't lead to conviction. (2) **Can ask:** About convictions, but consider: (3) **Business necessity:** Is it safe to hire someone with this conviction? (4) **Relevance:** Is conviction relevant to job? (violent crime for security = relevant; DUI for office job = less relevant). (5) **Time:** How long ago? (More time = less relevant). (6) **Fair chance:** Some states require you consider factors above. Best practice: Ask about convictions; evaluate fairly.

**Q5: What's an employment agreement vs. an offer letter?**
A: **Offer letter:** Invitation to join company (employment offer). Used for: Most positions. Covers: Position, compensation, start date, at-will employment. **Employment agreement:** More formal contract with specific terms. Used for: Executives, consultants, special arrangements. Covers: Offer letter items PLUS: severance terms, equity, incentives, benefits, restrictive covenants. Most U.S. employment is "at-will" (employee/company can terminate anytime); offer letters standard.

**Q6: What should a new employee sign on day 1?**
A: Required documents: (1) **Offer letter acknowledgment** (employee confirms terms). (2) **Employee handbook acknowledgment** (confirms receipt + understanding of policies). (3) **IP assignment agreement** (employee assigns work product to company). (4) **NDA/confidentiality** (employee agrees not to disclose secrets). (5) **Arbitration agreement** (if using; requires employee to arbitrate disputes). (6) **Emergency contact info** (for personnel files). (7) **I-9 verification** (required by law; proves authorization to work). (8) **Tax forms (W-4, etc.)** (required for IRS). All collected before/at day 1 start.

**Q7: Do you need a background check policy?**
A: Best practice: Yes. Write policy covering: (1) **Scope:** Which positions require background check? (usually: everyone, or positions with financial/safeguarding duties). (2) **Process:** Third-party provider or internal review? (3) **FCRA compliance:** If using third party, comply with Fair Credit Reporting Act (notify candidate, get consent, allow dispute). (4) **Evaluation:** If disqualifying info found, consider: relevance, severity, time, employee explanation. (5) **Transparency:** Tell candidate what you found (allow explanation). (6) **Documentation:** Document process.

**Q8: How do you handle reference checks legally?**
A: Best practice: (1) **Candidate consent:** Get written permission before calling references. (2) **Questions:** Ask about: job function, performance, strengths, weaknesses, eligibility for rehire. Ask same questions for all candidates. (3) **Don't ask:** Anything that's discriminatory (age, religion, race, health, etc.). (4) **Documentation:** Write down what references say (you may need to defend hiring decision). (5) **Verify employments:** Confirm candidate actually worked there. (6) **Disparagement:** Be careful if reference is negative (candidate may sue for defamation if false).

**Q9: What about remote work—is there an employment law angle?**
A: Yes, several: (1) **Wages/hours:** If remote employee works hours > expected, you may owe overtime. (2) **Workers comp:** Remote employee injured at home—is it covered? (3) **State tax:** If employee works in different state, you may owe state payroll taxes there. (4) **Equipment:** Who provides laptop? Insurance if damage? (5) **Confidentiality:** More risk of data breach (employee working from coffee shop?). (6) **Policy:** Have clear remote work policy covering: eligibility, hours, equipment, confidentiality, tech support. Most remote work is legal; just document policy.

**Q10: What training should managers receive on employment law?**
A: Manager training topics: (1) **Hiring:** Non-discriminatory practices, legal questions/questions to avoid. (2) **Performance management:** Documentation, progressive discipline, when to involve HR. (3) **Discrimination/harassment:** What is it? How to prevent? How to report if happens? (4) **Retaliation:** Never retaliate against employee who reports problem. (5) **Confidentiality:** Don't discuss employee matters with other employees. (6) **Leave:** FMLA, ADA, state leave laws. (7) **Accommodations:** If employee needs accommodation (disability, religious), how to handle? Frequency: Annual training. Format: Online module or in-person.

**Q11: How do you document hiring decisions?**
A: Documentation: (1) **Application/resume:** Keep on file. (2) **Interview notes:** Document what candidate said (for each interviewer). (3) **Scorecard:** If using structured scoring, keep scorecard. (4) **References:** Document what references said. (5) **Background check:** Keep results. (6) **Approval:** Document who approved hiring. (7) **Timeline:** Document dates of each step. Why: If sued, you need to show hiring decision was non-discriminatory and based on merit. Keep 3 years (allows for statute of limitations).

**Q12: What's the right way to conduct interviews?**
A: Best practice: **(1) Structured:** Use same questions for all candidates (allows fair comparison). (2) Job-related:** Questions should relate to job requirements (not personal characteristics). (3) Avoid:** Age, religion, national origin, race, color, sex, disability, marital status, arrest record (unless job-relevant). (4) Document:** Interviewer notes what candidate said. (5) Scoring:** Score candidates on same rubric (consistency). (6) Diversity:** Try to have diverse interview panel (reduces bias). Example questions:** "Tell me about your experience with [skill]?" "How did you handle [situation]?" (Both allow candidates to demonstrate capability.)

**Q13: When should you involve employment counsel in hiring?**
A: Involve counsel: (1) **Executive hiring** (CEO, SVPs—complex negotiation, severance). (2) **Key roles** (any position with significant IP/secrets). (3) **Non-compete/confidentiality:** If using restrictive covenants. (4) **International hiring:** Different employment laws. (5) **Contractor vs. employee:** Uncertain classification (IRS scrutiny). (6) **Special arrangements:** Commission, bonus, incentives. For routine hiring: HR can handle standardized offer letter. For anything complex: Loop in counsel.

**Q14: What are wrongful termination and how do you avoid it?**
A: Wrongful termination = illegal termination (violates law or public policy). Examples: (1) Firing for jury duty (illegal). (2) Retaliation for reporting safety violation. (3) Discrimination based on protected class. How to avoid: (1) **Never say real reason if it's bad:** If terminating for performance, document performance issues. If terminating for attendance, document absences. (2) **Paper trail:** Multiple warnings, documented improvement plan (if applicable). (3) **Consistency:** Same termination process for all. (4) **At-will emphasis:** Regular reminders to employees that employment is at-will. (5) **Counsel review:** For any termination firing, involve counsel (is it defensible?).

## SOP 2: Termination Process (With Legal Protection)

**Duration:** 1-3 weeks | **Frequency:** Per termination | **Owner:** HR Manager + Employment Counsel

**Stage 1: Decision & Counseling**

- Manager + HR + Counsel: Is termination defensible?
- Documentation review (performance, attendance, behavior)
- Reason for termination (clear, non-discriminatory)

**Stage 2: Preparation**

- Termination letter drafted
- Separation agreement (if applicable)
- Final paycheck calculated (including accrued time)
- Benefits continuation (COBRA notification)
- Timeline: When and how to deliver termination?

**Stage 3: Termination**

- Manager/HR conducts termination meeting
- Deliver termination letter, separation agreement
- Collect company property (laptop, ID badge, keys)
- Discuss final paycheck, benefits continuation
- Offboarding (remove system access, etc.)

**Stage 4: Post-Termination**

- Document termination (file documentation)
- Respond to any severance claims/disputes
- EEOC/labor complaint response (if filed)

- Investigation (interview complainant, witnesses, accused person; collect documents)

- Remediation (discipline, policy change, reporting, follow-up)

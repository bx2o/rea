# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Forty+ Frequently Asked Questions

### Category 1: DESIGN STRATEGY (8 FAQs)

**Q1: What is design strategy?**
A: Design strategy aligns design activities with business goals: (1) **Vision:** Where design is going. (2) **Principles:** How we make decisions. (3) **Priorities:** What we focus on. (4) **Measurement:** How we know we're succeeding. Connects user needs, business objectives, and technical capabilities into coherent direction.

**Q2: How do you prove design ROI?**
A: Design ROI measurement: (1) **Conversion:** A/B test design changes. (2) **Efficiency:** Task completion time, error reduction. (3) **Satisfaction:** NPS, CSAT correlation with design investment. (4) **Speed:** Time to market, design system reuse. (5) **Retention:** User retention rates. (6) **Support:** Reduced support tickets. Frame: Design as risk reduction and value creation.

**Q3: What makes a good design principle?**
A: Good principles are: (1) **Memorable:** Easy to recall and apply. (2) **Specific:** Not generic platitudes. (3) **Differentiating:** Reflect your unique approach. (4) **Actionable:** Guide actual decisions. (5) **Authentic:** Reflect true values. Examples: "Progressive disclosure" vs. bad: "Make it user-friendly."

**Q4: How do you get design a seat at the table?**
A: Elevate design influence: (1) **Speak business:** Translate design to business outcomes. (2) **Bring data:** User research and metrics. (3) **Show ROI:** Quantify design impact. (4) **Solve problems:** Address business challenges. (5) **Build relationships:** Partner with product, eng, execs. (6) **Be strategic:** Think beyond screens.

**Q5: What is design thinking?**
A: Design thinking process: (1) **Empathize:** Understand users deeply. (2) **Define:** Frame the problem. (3) **Ideate:** Generate solutions. (4) **Prototype:** Build to learn. (5) **Test:** Validate with users. Mindset: User-centered, iterative, collaborative, visual, holistic. Application: Beyond design—product, strategy, org challenges.

**Q6: How do you prioritize design investments?**
A: Prioritization framework: (1) **User Impact:** How many users, how severe? (2) **Business Value:** Revenue, retention, efficiency. (3) **Strategic Fit:** Alignment with goals. (4) **Feasibility:** Effort, technical constraints. (5) **Opportunity:** Competitive differentiation. Methods: RICE scoring, impact/effort matrix, Kano model.

**Q7: What's the relationship between brand and UX?**
A: Brand and UX are intertwined: Brand is the promise, UX is the experience of that promise. Consistency: Visual identity, voice, values expressed in every interaction. Differentiation: Brand distinguishes, UX delivers. Measurement: Brand perception correlates with UX satisfaction.

**Q8: How do you create a design-driven culture?**
A: Design culture elements: (1) **Education:** Design literacy programs. (2) **Exposure:** Everyone observes user research. (3) **Celebration:** Recognize design excellence. (4) **Tools:** Access to design resources. (5) **Process:** Design integrated into workflows. (6) **Leadership:** Exec sponsorship. (7) **Hiring:** Design sensitivity in all roles.

### Category 2: DESIGN SYSTEMS (8 FAQs)

**Q9: What is a design system?**
A: Design system is comprehensive toolkit: (1) **Visual Language:** Colors, typography, spacing, iconography. (2) **Components:** Reusable UI elements. (3) **Patterns:** Common solutions to common problems. (4) **Guidelines:** When and how to use. (5) **Tools:** Figma libraries, code components. (6) **Documentation:** How to contribute, evolve. Purpose: Consistency, efficiency, quality at scale.

**Q10: When should you build a design system?**
A: Build when: (1) **Scale:** Multiple products or teams. (2) **Inconsistency:** UI varying across experiences. (3) **Efficiency:** Teams rebuilding same components. (4) **Quality:** Inconsistent UX quality. (5) **Velocity:** Design slowing development. Don't build: Single product, one designer, pre-product-market fit.

**Q11: How do you measure design system success?**
A: Success metrics: (1) **Adoption:** % teams using system. (2) **Efficiency:** Time saved in design/dev. (3) **Consistency:** Visual audit scores. (4) **Satisfaction:** Designer/developer NPS. (5) **Quality:** Reduced design bugs. (6) **Contribution:** Community contributions. (7) **Evolution:** System keeps pace with needs.

**Q12: What's the difference between style guide, pattern library, and design system?**
A: **Style Guide:** Visual standards (colors, fonts). **Pattern Library:** UI components (buttons, cards). **Design System:** Comprehensive—visual + components + patterns + guidelines + tools + process. Evolution: Style guide → Pattern library → Design system. Modern: Design system encompasses all.

**Q13: How do you maintain a design system?**
A: Maintenance practices: (1) **Governance:** Clear ownership and decision process. (2) **Contribution:** Process for additions/changes. (3) **Versioning:** Communicate updates. (4) **Documentation:** Keep guidance current. (5) **Adoption Support:** Training, office hours. (6) **Evolution:** Regular review and modernization. (7) **Community:** Build contributor base.

**Q14: How do you balance system consistency with creative expression?**
A: Balance approach: (1) **Core vs. Flex:** System covers 80%, teams have 20% freedom. (2) **Principles over Rules:** Guidelines that permit interpretation. (3) **Exception Process:** Documented path for deviations. (4) **Inspiration:** System as foundation, not ceiling. (5) **Evolution:** System absorbs good innovations.

**Q15: What tools are essential for design systems?**
A: Tool stack: (1) **Design:** Figma (libraries, variables, components). (2) **Documentation:** Storybook, Notion, or specialized (Zeroheight). (3) **Code:** Component library in framework (React, Vue). (4) **Tokens:** Style Dictionary, Tokens Studio. (5) **Testing:** Visual regression, accessibility. (6) **Distribution:** NPM packages, Figma updates.

**Q16: How do you drive design system adoption?**
A: Adoption strategies: (1) **Value Prop:** Clear benefits (speed, quality). (2) **Onboarding:** Training and resources. (3) **Support:** Office hours, Slack channel. (4) **Champions:** Early adopters as advocates. (5) **Integration:** Easy to use (Figma libraries, npm install). (6) **Feedback Loop:** Listen and respond to pain points. (7) **Leadership:** Executive mandate if needed.

### Category 3: UX RESEARCH (8 FAQs)

**Q17: What types of UX research should we do?**
A: Research types by timing: (1) **Generative:** Early discovery (interviews, ethnography). (2) **Evaluative:** Test solutions (usability testing, A/B). (3) **Continuous:** Ongoing (analytics, surveys). Methods: Qualitative (why)—interviews, testing; Quantitative (what)—surveys, analytics. Combination gives full picture.

**Q18: How do you recruit research participants?**
A: Recruitment channels: (1) **Current Users:** In-app recruitment, email lists. (2) **Panels:** UserInterviews, Respondent. (3) **Social:** LinkedIn, Twitter, industry groups. (4) **Agencies:** Recruiting firms for specific profiles. (5) **Guerrilla:** Coffee shops, public spaces. Incentives: Cash, gift cards, product credits. Screen: Ensure relevant experience.

**Q19: What is usability testing?**
A: Usability testing observes users attempting tasks: (1) **Moderated:** Researcher guides, asks questions. (2) **Unmoderated:** User completes independently (UserTesting). (3) **Remote:** Via video conferencing. (4) **In-person:** Lab or natural setting. Metrics: Success rate, time on task, errors, satisfaction. Best: Regular testing, 5-8 users per round.

**Q20: How do you analyze qualitative research?**
A: Analysis process: (1) **Transcribe:** Recordings to text. (2) **Code:** Tag insights by theme. (3) **Cluster:** Group related observations. (4) **Synthesize:** Find patterns and themes. (5) **Prioritize:** Impact × frequency. (6) **Communicate:** Personas, journey maps, insight statements. Tools: Dovetail, Airtable, Miro, spreadsheets.

**Q21: How do you present research findings?**
A: Effective communication: (1) **Executive Summary:** Key insights upfront. (2) **Stories:** User quotes and videos. (3) **Visuals:** Journey maps, personas. (4) **Recommendations:** Actionable next steps. (5) **Playback:** Presentation with discussion. (6) **Repository:** Searchable research database. Formats: Decks, reports, dashboards, video highlights.

**Q22: How much research is enough?**
A: Research investment guidance: (1) **Generative:** 6-12 interviews per segment. (2) **Usability:** 5 users find 85% of issues. (3) **Surveys:** 100+ for statistical significance. (4) **Continuous:** Always on (analytics, feedback). Principle: Some research beats no research. Balance: Speed vs. confidence. Cadence: Regular, not one-time.

**Q23: What is a user persona?**
A: Personas are archetypal users: (1) **Based on Research:** Real data, not assumptions. (2) **Specific:** Name, photo, details. (3) **Behavioral:** Goals, motivations, frustrations. (4) **Actionable:** Guide design decisions. Types: Primary (main target), Secondary (occasional), Anti (not targeting). Avoid: Demographic-only, too many personas.

**Q24: How do you create a journey map?**
A: Journey mapping process: (1) **Define Scope:** Persona and scenario. (2) **Stages:** Phases of the journey. (3) **Touchpoints:** Interactions at each stage. (4) **Emotions:** User feelings (high/low). (5) **Pain Points:** Frustrations and barriers. (6) **Opportunities:** Design interventions. Output: Visual map showing holistic experience. Use: Align teams, identify improvements.

### Category 4: ACCESSIBILITY & INCLUSION (8 FAQs)

**Q25: What is accessibility in design?**
A: Accessibility ensures people with disabilities can use products: Visual (blind, low vision), Auditory (deaf, hard of hearing), Motor (can't use mouse), Cognitive (learning disabilities). Standards: WCAG 2.1 (A, AA, AAA). Legal: ADA, Section 508. Business: Larger market, better UX for all, risk mitigation.

**Q26: What are WCAG guidelines?**
A: WCAG 2.1 principles: (1) **Perceivable:** Info presented so users can perceive. (2) **Operable:** Interface works for all users. (3) **Understandable:** Info and UI understandable. (4) **Robust:** Works with assistive tech. Levels: A (minimum), AA (standard), AAA (highest). Most target AA.

**Q27: How do you design for screen readers?**
A: Screen reader design: (1) **Semantic HTML:** Proper headings, landmarks, buttons. (2) **Alt Text:** Meaningful image descriptions. (3) **Labels:** Form fields clearly labeled. (4) **Focus:** Visible focus indicators. (5) **Order:** Logical reading order. (6) **ARIA:** When HTML semantics insufficient. Test: NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver.

**Q28: What is inclusive design?**
A: Inclusive design considers full range of human diversity: Permanent (disability), Temporary (injury), Situational (environmental). Approach: (1) **Recognize Exclusion:** Challenge assumptions. (2) **Solve for One, Extend to Many:** Edge case solutions help mainstream. (3) **Learn from Diversity:** Include diverse perspectives. Microsoft: "Design for the extremes, benefit the mean."

**Q29: How do you test accessibility?**
A: Testing methods: (1) **Automated:** Axe, Lighthouse (catch 30% of issues). (2) **Manual:** Keyboard navigation, screen reader, zoom. (3) **User Testing:** People with disabilities. (4) **Audit:** WCAG checklist review. Tools: Axe DevTools, WAVE, Colour Contrast Analyser. Cadence: Regular, not one-time audit.

**Q30: What is color contrast and why does it matter?**
A: Contrast ratio ensures readability: WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text. Tools: WebAIM Contrast Checker, Stark plugin. Consider: Color blindness (don't rely on color alone), low vision, glare conditions. Test: Grayscale view of designs.

**Q31: How do you design for cognitive accessibility?**
A: Cognitive accessibility: (1) **Clear Language:** Simple words, short sentences. (2) **Consistent:** Patterns repeated predictably. (3) **Chunking:** Break complex tasks into steps. (4) **Error Prevention:** Confirm destructive actions. (5) **Memory Support:** Don't require recall. (6) **Distraction-Free:** Reduce clutter and motion. Benefits: Everyone, especially stressed or distracted users.

**Q32: What is keyboard navigation?**
A: Keyboard accessibility: All functionality available without mouse. Key principles: (1) **Tab Order:** Logical sequence. (2) **Focus Indicators:** Visible focus state. (3) **Shortcuts:** Common shortcuts supported. (4) **No Keyboard Traps:** Can tab away from any element. (5) **Skip Links:** Bypass repetitive content. Test: Unplug mouse, navigate entirely with keyboard.

### Category 5: LEADERSHIP & OPERATIONS (8 FAQs)

**Q33: How do you structure a design team?**
A: Team structure models: (1) **Centralized:** Design team as shared service. (2) **Embedded:** Designers on product squads. (3) **Hybrid:** Centralized craft + embedded delivery. Roles: UX Researcher, UX Designer, UI Designer, Content Designer, DesignOps. Ratio: 1:8 to 1:12 designer:developer typical.

**Q34: What is DesignOps?**
A: DesignOps optimizes design operations: (1) **Tools:** Design stack selection and management. (2) **Systems:** Design systems, asset management. (3) **Process:** Workflows, rituals, handoffs. (4) **Governance:** Standards, reviews, quality. (5) **Education:** Onboarding, training. (6) **Culture:** Community, events. Value: Designers focus on design, not operations.

**Q35: How do you review design work?**
A: Design critique best practices: (1) **Purpose:** Improve design, not prove it's good. (2) **Presenter:** Context, goals, specific feedback needed. (3) **Reviewers:** Fresh eyes, different expertise. (4) **Feedback:** Specific, actionable, kind. (5) **Facilitator:** Keep on track, ensure participation. (6) **Output:** Action items and owner. Cadence: Weekly team critiques.

**Q36: How do you handle design feedback?**
A: Feedback management: (1) **Listen:** Understand before defending. (2) **Clarify:** Ask questions to understand. (3) **Prioritize:** Not all feedback is equal. (4) **Test:** Validate with users, not opinions. (5) **Decide:** Designer owns decision with input. (6) **Communicate:** Explain rationale. Balance: Stakeholder input + user research + design expertise.

**Q37: How do you hire great designers?**
A: Hiring process: (1) **Portfolio:** Quality and thinking process. (2) **Presentation:** Walk through 2-3 projects. (3) **Design Exercise:** Realistic task, collaborative. (4) **Values Fit:** Culture and collaboration. (5) **References:** Past collaboration. Red flags: Can't explain decisions, no iteration shown, blames others. Diversity: Intentional outreach, unbiased evaluation.

**Q38: What makes a good design portfolio?**
A: Portfolio elements: (1) **Case Studies:** Problem, process, solution, results. (2) **Process:** Show iteration and decisions. (3) **Impact:** Business and user outcomes. (4) **Diversity:** Range of challenges. (5) **Presentation:** Clear, compelling storytelling. (6) **Craft:** Attention to detail. Avoid: Dribbble shots without context, no process, only final pixels.

**Q39: How do you manage stakeholder design requests?**
A: Request management: (1) **Intake:** Standard request form. (2) **Triage:** Impact, effort, strategic fit. (3) **Prioritization:** Transparent scoring. (4) **Capacity:** Realistic workload. (5) **Communication:** Clear timelines. (6) **Pushback:** Diplomatic decline with alternatives. Goal: Strategic work over reactive requests.

**Q40: How do you scale design quality?**
A: Quality at scale: (1) **Systems:** Design system ensures baseline. (2) **Reviews:** Peer review maintains craft. (3) **Tools:** Automated quality checks. (4) **Training:** Skill development. (5) **Principles:** Shared criteria for good. (6) **Time:** Buffer for polish. (7) **Culture:** Pride in craft. Measure: Quality audits, consistency scores.

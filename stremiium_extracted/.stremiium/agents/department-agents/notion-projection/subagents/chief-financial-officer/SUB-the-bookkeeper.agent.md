# SECTION 4: FAQs (20+)

**Q1: What's the difference between cash and accrual accounting?**
A: Cash: Record when money moves. Accrual: Record when earned/incurred. Accrual is GAAP, more accurate.

**Q2: What is the accounting equation?**
A: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Foundation of double-entry bookkeeping. Every transaction affects at least two accounts.

**Q3: What is a chart of accounts?**
A: List of all accounts used: Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Revenue, Expenses. Organizes financial data.

**Q4: What are adjusting entries?**
A: Entries at period end: Accruals, deferrals, depreciation, estimates. Ensure matching principle.

**Q5: What is depreciation?**
A: Allocating asset cost over useful life. Methods: Straight-line, declining balance, units of production.

**Q6: What is the monthly close process?**
A: Series of steps to finalize monthly books: Entries, reconciliations, review, reports. Deadline-driven.

**Q7: What is a reconciliation?**
A: Matching two sets of records: Bank rec, AP/AR subledger to GL, credit cards. Ensures accuracy.

**Q8: What is DSO and how to improve?**
A: Days Sales Outstanding. Average days to collect. Improve: Faster invoicing, clear terms, follow-up, prepayment.

**Q9: What is the matching principle?**
A: Expenses matched to revenue in same period. Foundation of accrual accounting. Revenue recognition.

**Q10: How do you handle cutoff?**
A: Ensuring transactions in correct period. Physical inventory, invoice review, accruals. Critical for accuracy.

**Q11: What is the difference between AP and AR?**
A: AP = money you owe (bills). AR = money owed to you (invoices). Working capital components.

**Q12: What is petty cash and how to manage?**
A: Small cash on hand for minor expenses. Fixed amount, receipts required, periodic reconciliation.

**Q13: How do you handle intercompany transactions?**
A: Track separately, reconcile, eliminate in consolidation, proper documentation, settlement process.

**Q14: What is a prepaid expense?**
A: Paid before incurred (insurance, rent). Asset when paid, expensed over time. Monthly amortization.

**Q15: What is an accrued expense?**
A: Incurred but not paid (wages, utilities). Expense and liability recorded, paid later.

**Q16: How do you prevent/detect fraud?**
A: Segregation of duties, approvals, reconciliations, audits, surprise checks, anonymous hotline.

**Q17: What is a 1099 and who gets one?**
A: Tax form for non-employee payments >$600/year. Contractors, vendors. Track, prepare, file by Jan 31.

**Q18: What is internal control?**
A: Processes to ensure accuracy, prevent fraud: Approvals, segregation of duties, reconciliations, audits.

**Q19: How do you handle sales tax?**
A: Determine nexus, track rates, calculate on invoices, file returns, remit payments, maintain records.

**Q20: What is a trial balance?**
A: List of all accounts and balances. Debits = Credits. Used to prepare financial statements.

# SECTION 5: SOPs

## SOP 1: Monthly Close

1. Close calendar confirmed
2. AP cutoff
3. AR cutoff
4. Journal entries
5. Reconciliations
6. Review and approval
7. Reports generated
8. Distribution
9. Audit file prep

## SOP 2: AP Processing

1. Receive invoice
2. Match to PO/receipt
3. Code to GL
4. Obtain approval
5. Enter in system
6. Schedule payment
7. Process payment
8. File documentation

## SOP 3: AR Collection

1. Generate invoice
2. Send to customer
3. Monitor aging
4. Follow up at 30/60/90 days
5. Escalate as needed
6. Apply payments
7. Reconcile accounts

# SECTION 4: FAQs (20+)

**Q1: What is intellectual property?**
A: Creations of mind: Patents (inventions), Trademarks (brand), Copyrights (works), Trade secrets (confidential info).

**Q2: What is GDPR and who does it apply to?**
A: EU data protection regulation. Applies if processing EU residents' data, regardless of company location.

**Q3: What are data subject rights under GDPR?**
A: Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, automated decision-making rights.

**Q4: What is a trade secret and how to protect?**
A: Confidential business info giving advantage. Protect: NDA, access control, marking, education, monitoring.

**Q5: What is privacy by design?**
A: Privacy built into systems from start, not added later. Seven Foundational Principles. Proactive, default, embedded.

**Q6: What is a DPIA and when required?**
A: Data Protection Impact Assessment. For high-risk processing: profiling, large scale sensitive data, monitoring.

**Q7: How do you classify data?**
A: Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted. Based on sensitivity, regulatory requirements, business impact.

**Q8: What is the difference between privacy and security?**
A: Privacy = appropriate use of personal data. Security = protecting data from unauthorized access. Related but distinct.

**Q9: What is CCPA/CPRA?**
A: California privacy laws. Consumer rights to know, delete, opt-out of sale, non-discrimination. Broad reach.

**Q10: How do you handle a data subject access request (DSAR)?**
A: Verify identity, 30-day response (extendable), identify all data, format for understanding, secure delivery.

**Q11: What is lawful basis for processing?**
A: GDPR requires legal ground: Consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests.

**Q12: How do you protect IP in third-party relationships?**
A: NDAs, IP clauses in contracts, due diligence, monitoring, termination procedures, return/destruction.

**Q13: What is a patent and how to obtain?**
A: Exclusive right to invention. Novel, non-obvious, useful. Application, examination, grant, maintenance fees.

**Q14: How do you respond to a privacy breach?**
A: Contain, assess risk, notify supervisory authority (if high risk), notify affected individuals, document, remediate.

**Q15: What is cross-border data transfer compliance?**
A: GDPR requires safeguards: Adequacy decisions, Standard Contractual Clauses, Binding Corporate Rules, certifications.

**Q16: How do you handle employee IP rights?**
A: Clear agreements, invention assignment, work for hire, trade secret obligations, exit procedures.

**Q17: What is a trademark and how to protect?**
A: Brand identifier (name, logo). Distinctive, not descriptive. Registration, use requirements, monitoring, enforcement.

**Q18: How do you train employees on IP/privacy?**
A: Onboarding, annual refresher, role-specific, practical examples, assessments, awareness campaigns.

**Q19: What is copyright and fair use?**
A: Protection for original works. Fair use allows limited use for commentary, education, etc. Factors considered.

**Q20: How do you conduct a privacy audit?**
A: Data inventory, policy review, technical assessment, vendor review, rights request testing, gap analysis, remediation.

# SECTION 5: SOPs

## SOP 1: Data Subject Access Request

1. Receive and log request
2. Verify identity (adequately)
3. Search for personal data
4. Review for third-party data
5. Format in understandable way
6. Secure delivery
7. Document completion
8. Within 30 days

## SOP 2: IP Infringement Response

1. Monitor for infringement
2. Document evidence
3. Assess severity
4. Consult legal
5. Send notice (cease/desist or takedown)
6. Monitor compliance
7. Escalate if needed
8. Document outcome

## SOP 3: DPIA Process

1. Identify need for DPIA
2. Describe processing
3. Consult stakeholders
4. Assess necessity/proportionality
5. Identify risks
6. Identify mitigations
7. Sign off
8. Review ongoing

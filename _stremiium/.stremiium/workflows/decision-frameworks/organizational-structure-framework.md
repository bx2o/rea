# Organizational Structure Decision Framework

**Purpose**: Determine optimal team structure, roles, and reporting  
**Use When**: Scaling team, reorganizing, filling leadership gaps  
**Owner**: The Guardian + The Strategist  
**Last Updated**: February 18, 2026

---

## Structure Models

### By Function (For small companies)

```
CEO
├─ Engineering Lead
├─ Product Lead
├─ Sales Lead
└─ Operations Lead
```

### By Product (For medium companies)

```
CEO
├─ Product A Lead
│  ├─ Engineering
│  ├─ Product
│  └─ Ops
├─ Product B Lead
└─ Platform Lead
```

### By Function at Scale (For large companies)

```
CEO
├─ VP Engineering
│  ├─ Backend Team Lead
│  ├─ Frontend Team Lead
│  └─ DevOps Team Lead
├─ VP Product
├─ VP Sales
└─ VP Operations
```

---

## Decision Criteria

### Team Size

- <5: Generalists, flat
- 5-20: Functional leads
- 20-50: Departments
- 50+: Layered hierarchy

### Growth Stage

- Early: Anyone does anything
- Scale: Specialization
- Mature: Departments + collaboration

### Technology Complexity

- Simple: Fewer specialists needed
- Complex: More specialists needed
- Specialized: Unique expertise focus

### Product/Market

- Single product: By function
- Multiple products: By product
- Service-based: By customer/function

---

## Cons of Each Model

**By Function**:

- May miss product priorities
- Silos form
- Cross-team coordination hard

**By Product**:

- Resource duplication
- Harder to share best practices
- May have redundant roles

**Hybrid (Best)**:

- Functional teams with product focus
- Shared platforms
- Matrixed reporting where needed

---

## Timing of Changes

- <20 people: Flat, revisit at 20
- 20-50: Functional leads
- 50+: Departments with leads
- 100+: Multi-layer hierarchy

## 3.2 Operational Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

### SOP 1: Incident Response

**Scope:** Operational incident response and management
**Trigger:** Any operational incident affecting services

**Steps:**

1. **Detection & Reporting** (0-15 min)
   - Incident detected through monitoring or reported
   - Log incident in incident management system
   - Assess severity and priority

2. **Initial Response** (15-30 min)
   - Assemble incident response team
   - Communicate to stakeholders
   - Begin containment actions

3. **Investigation & Diagnosis** (30 min - 2 hours)
   - Investigate root cause
   - Assess impact and scope
   - Develop resolution plan

4. **Resolution** (Varies)
   - Implement resolution actions
   - Verify resolution
   - Restore normal operations

5. **Post-Incident** (Within 24 hours)
   - Document incident details
   - Conduct post-mortem
   - Implement preventive measures
   - Update procedures if needed

**SLAs:**

- P1 (Critical): Response <15 min, resolution <4 hours
- P2 (High): Response <30 min, resolution <8 hours
- P3 (Medium): Response <2 hours, resolution <24 hours
- P4 (Low): Response <4 hours, resolution <72 hours

### SOP 2: Risk Assessment Process

**Scope:** Enterprise risk assessment
**Trigger:** Quarterly assessments, new risks, incidents

**Steps:**

1. **Planning** (Week 1)
   - Define assessment scope
   - Identify assessment team
   - Schedule workshops
   - Prepare materials

2. **Risk Identification** (Week 2)
   - Conduct risk workshops
   - Identify new risks
   - Review existing risks
   - Categorize risks

3. **Risk Analysis** (Week 3)
   - Assess likelihood (1-5 scale)
   - Assess impact (1-5 scale)
   - Calculate risk scores
   - Prioritize risks

4. **Risk Evaluation** (Week 4)
   - Compare to risk appetite
   - Identify treatment needs
   - Assign risk owners
   - Document in risk register

5. **Risk Treatment** (Ongoing)
   - Develop treatment plans
   - Implement controls
   - Monitor effectiveness
   - Report to board

   - Issue RFP/RFQ

## 4.1 Stremiium Operations FAQ

### Governance & Board

**Q1: What governance framework does Stremiium follow?**
A: Stremiium follows King IV (South African corporate governance code) and the Companies Act 71 of 2008. We maintain a Board Charter, Delegation of Authority, and comprehensive governance policies.

**Q2: How often does the Board meet?**
A: The Board meets quarterly for scheduled meetings, plus special meetings as needed. Board committees (Audit, Risk, Remuneration) meet as required.

**Q3: What is the Delegation of Authority?**
A: The Delegation of Authority document defines approval limits and decision-making authority across the organization. It specifies who can approve various operational, financial, and strategic decisions.

**Q4: What are the Board's key responsibilities?**
A: Strategic direction, governance oversight, risk oversight, CEO appointment and oversight, stakeholder communication, regulatory compliance oversight.

**Q5: What risk management framework does Stremiium use?**
A: ISO 31000-aligned enterprise risk management framework with risk registers, risk appetite statements, and regular risk assessments.

**Q6: How often are risk assessments conducted?**
A: Formal risk assessments quarterly, with continuous risk monitoring and ad-hoc assessments for new initiatives or incidents.

**Q7: What is the risk appetite?**
A: Defined thresholds for acceptable risk levels across different risk categories. Board-approved and reviewed annually.

**Q8: Who owns risks?**
A: Every risk has an assigned owner responsible for monitoring and treatment. Risk owners report to the Risk Committee.

## 12.1 Decision Frameworks for Operations

### Framework 1: Operational Incident Response Decision Tree

```
Incident Detected
│
├─ Impact Assessment
│  ├─ Single service affected?
│  │  ├─ YES → Service team handles, Operations notified
│  │  └─ NO → Continue
│  ├─ Multiple services affected?
│  │  ├─ YES → Operations Squad activation
│  │  └─ NO → Continue
│  └─ Customer-facing impact?
│     ├─ YES → Customer Success + Operations coordination
│     └─ NO → Continue
│
├─ Severity Assessment
│  ├─ P1 (Critical): Immediate VP-Operations notification
│  ├─ P2 (High): Operations Director notification
│  ├─ P3 (Medium): Operations team handles
│  └─ P4 (Low): Standard queue
│
└─ Resource Mobilization
   ├─ Internal resources sufficient?
   │  ├─ YES → Activate internal response team
   │  └─ NO → Engage vendor support / external resources
   └─ Crisis communication needed?
      ├─ YES → Activate crisis communication plan
      └─ NO → Standard incident communication
```

### Framework 2: Vendor Selection Decision Matrix

**Evaluation Criteria:**
| Criteria | Weight | Vendor A | Vendor B | Vendor C |
|----------|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| Technical Capability | 25% | Score | Score | Score |
| Financial Stability | 20% | Score | Score | Score |
| Cost | 20% | Score | Score | Score |
| Security/Compliance | 20% | Score | Score | Score |
| Cultural Fit | 10% | Score | Score | Score |
| References | 5% | Score | Score | Score |

**Decision Process:**

1. Score each vendor against criteria
2. Apply weights and calculate weighted scores
3. Consider qualitative factors
4. Conduct due diligence on top 2-3
5. Negotiate with preferred vendor
6. Legal and compliance review
7. Executive approval (if over threshold)
8. Contract execution

### Framework 3: Risk Acceptance Decision Framework

**Risk Evaluation:**

1. Risk identified and assessed (likelihood x impact)
2. Compare to risk appetite
3. Treatment options:
   - **Avoid:** Eliminate the risk
   - **Reduce:** Implement controls to lower likelihood/impact
   - **Transfer:** Insurance, outsourcing, contracts
   - **Accept:** Consciously accept within appetite

**Acceptance Criteria:**

- Risk within defined appetite
- Treatment cost exceeds risk cost
- No viable treatment options
- Strategic necessity
- Board approval documented

## 12.2 Complex Problem Solving

### Problem-Solving Methodology for Operations

**Step 1: Problem Definition**

- What is the problem? (clear, specific statement)
- Who is affected?
- What is the impact?
- When did it start?
- What is the urgency?

**Step 2: Data Collection**

- Gather relevant data and evidence
- Interview stakeholders
- Review logs and records
- Analyze trends and patterns

**Step 3: Root Cause Analysis**

- **5 Whys:** Ask "why" 5 times to find root cause
- **Fishbone Diagram:** Categorize potential causes
- **Fault Tree Analysis:** Systematic cause analysis
- **Pareto Analysis:** Identify vital few causes

**Step 4: Solution Generation**

- Brainstorm potential solutions
- Evaluate feasibility, cost, impact
- Consider short-term and long-term fixes
- Assess risks of each solution

**Step 5: Solution Selection**

- Apply decision criteria
- Consider stakeholder impact
- Evaluate resource requirements
- Assess implementation complexity

**Step 6: Implementation**

- Develop implementation plan
- Define success criteria
- Communicate to stakeholders
- Execute with monitoring

**Step 7: Verification**

- Verify problem is resolved
- Monitor for recurrence
- Document lessons learned
- Update procedures

## 12.3 Q&A Format for Operational Uncertainty

## 12.4 Auto-Agreement Guardrails

### When to Auto-Approve vs Escalate

**Auto-Approval Conditions:**

- Within defined authority limits
- Standard operating procedure applies
- Precedent exists (same situation handled before)
- Low risk and low impact
- No budget impact or within budget
- No regulatory implications
- No cross-council implications

**Escalation Required:**

- Outside defined authority
- New situation without precedent
- High risk or high impact
- Significant budget impact
- Regulatory implications
- Cross-council implications
- Executive or Board notification required

**Escalation Protocol:**

1. Document the decision required
2. Assess against auto-approval criteria
3. If any criteria not met, escalate
4. Provide full context to approver
5. Await approval before proceeding
6. Document approval and rationale

## 14.2 Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: Over-Engineering Processes

**Problem:** Creating overly complex processes that slow operations
**Solution:**

- Keep processes as simple as possible
- Right-size processes to risk and complexity
- Regular process simplification reviews
- Eliminate unnecessary steps

### Pitfall 2: Inadequate Documentation

**Problem:** Tribal knowledge, no written procedures
**Solution:**

- Mandate documentation for all critical processes
- Include documentation in task definitions
- Review documentation regularly
- Make documentation part of onboarding

### Pitfall 3: Siloed Operations

**Problem:** Operations teams not collaborating across services
**Solution:**

- Cross-functional operational meetings
- Shared dashboards and metrics
- Unified incident management
- Integrated toolchains

### Pitfall 4: Reactive vs Proactive

**Problem:** Always fighting fires instead of preventing them
**Solution:**

- Invest in monitoring and early warning
- Trend analysis and predictive analytics
- Regular risk assessments
- Proactive capacity planning

### Pitfall 5: Vendor Dependency

**Problem:** Over-reliance on single vendors
**Solution:**

- Multi-vendor strategies where feasible
- Regular vendor risk assessments
- Backup vendor identification
- Vendor exit planning

### Pitfall 6: Insufficient Testing

**Problem:** BCP/DRP never tested until needed
**Solution:**

- Mandatory regular testing schedule
- Realistic test scenarios
- Test result action items
- Continuous improvement from tests

### Pitfall 7: Poor Change Management

**Problem:** Changes cause unexpected issues
**Solution:**

- Rigorous change management process
- Impact assessment for all changes
- Testing in non-production
- Rollback plans for all changes
- Post-implementation reviews

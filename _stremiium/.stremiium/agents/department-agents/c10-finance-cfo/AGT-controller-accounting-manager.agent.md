# SECTION 4: COMPREHENSIVE KNOWLEDGE BASE

## 4.1 FAQs: Accounting & Controls Category (15+)

**Q: How do I ensure revenue recognition complies with ASC 606?**
A: Document the five-step model: (1) Identify contract, (2) identify obligations, (3) determine transaction price, (4) allocate price, (5) recognize when/as satisfied. Maintain this documentation with every revenue transaction.

**Q: What constitutes adequate supporting documentation for a journal entry?**
A: A complete audit trail showing: (a) business purpose, (b) calculation/derivation, (c) approval by appropriate authority, (d) account coding accuracy, (e) date posted. Missing any element triggers JE rejection.

**Q: How frequently should reconciliations be performed?**
A: Daily for cash/bank, weekly for aged receivables/payables, monthly for all balance sheet accounts before close. No account closes without 100% reconciliation.

**Q: What is a "material weakness" versus "significant deficiency" under SOX?**
A: material weakness = high probability material misstatement, significant deficiency = reasonable possibility material misstatement. Both require formal remediation plans.

**Q: How should I handle accruals with estimation uncertainty?**
A: Document the estimate basis (historical rates, management judgment, external quotes), quantify the estimate, calculate +/- ranges, compare to prior periods. Update when final amounts known.

**Q: What are the key ASC lease accounting deliverables?**
A: Lease schedule (all leases), ROU asset/liability calculation, deferred rent tracking, income statement impact, balance sheet presentation, financial statement disclosure notes.

**Q: How do I design an internal control around revenue?**
A: Control activities: (1) prenumbered invoice sequence, (2) price approval, (3) delivery verification, (4) revenue recognition completeness testing. Test quarterly.

**Q: What preparation should occur before external audit arrival?**
A: Audit file completion (policies, journal entries with detail, reconciliations, control testing, prior-year findings), full trial balance, close documentation, management representation package.

**Q: How should intercompany transactions be eliminated?**
A: Identify all intercompany balances, reconcile to counterparty records, record elimination entries, verify balance sheet and income statement impact, document for audit.

**Q: What does continuous close methodology require?**
A: Month-end close-like activities (standardized accounting entries, policy consistency, reconciliations) performed weekly/daily to reduce month-end crunch and improve accuracy.

**Q: How do I manage accounts payable controls?**
A: Segregation of duties (approval vs. payment), invoice matching (PO/receipt/invoice), payment authorization limits, bank reconciliation, aging monitoring.

**Q: What is the "three-way match" in procurement controls?**
A: Match purchase order → receipt → vendor invoice. Exceptions require supervisor override with documented justification.

**Q: How should I calculate and document bad debt reserves?**
A: Analyze aged receivables, apply historical write-off rates by aging bucket, consider customer-specific risk, quantify reserve, reconcile to prior year, document reserve testing.

**Q: What constitutes "publication ready" financial statements?**
A: Accurate numbers, proper formatting, complete disclosures, grammatically correct footnotes, management signature approval, distribution-ready presentation.

**Q: How do I maintain SOX compliance during staff turnover?**
A: Document all control procedures in writing, cross-train team members, maintain control testing workpapers, formalize handoff checklists, ensure continuity of control evidence.

## 4.3 Compliance & Risk Category (12+)

**Q: What are the "tone at the top" requirements for financial reporting?**
A: Leadership demonstrates commitment to accuracy, provides resources for compliance, holds teams accountable for controls, communicates ethical expectations, drives continuous improvement.

**Q: How should I escalate a potential accounting error?**
A: Identify issue, quantify impact, determine if material, notify CFO immediately, document finding, assess control failure, design corrective action.

**Q: What documentation is required for a "significant accounting judgment"?**
A: Decision framework, alternatives considered, rationale for selection, impact quantification, reference to applicable standards, approval documentation.

**Q: How do I maintain document retention compliance?**
A: Accounting records retained per SOX (7 years minimum), audit workpapers retained per auditor requirements, supporting documentation filed by transaction date, disposal with audit trail.

**Q: What should the audit committee discuss quarterly?**
A: Financial statement results, accounting policy changes, internal control status, audit findings/remediation, compliance attestation, fraud/misconduct (none + controls), going concern assessment.

**Q: How should I respond to external audit requests?**
A: Within 24 hours acknowledge, provide requested information, flag any restrictions, follow with clarification if needed, document response.

**Q: What is management's responsibility for financial statement fraud prevention?**
A: Establish ethical culture, implement preventive controls, implement detective controls, investigate anomalies, remediate control failures, report findings.

**Q: How do I assess whether an accounting issue is "material"?**
A: Quantitatively (% of revenue, profit, assets, equity) and qualitatively (related-party, management fraud, regulatory impact, investor focus). Document assessment.

**Q: Should estimates be reviewed by multiple people?**
A: Yes—estimator (calculation), supervisor (reasonableness check), manager (consistency review). Document all reviews before close finalization.

**Q: What is the controller's role in fraud prevention?**
A: Implement segregation of duties, design transaction controls, test control effectiveness, investigate exceptions, report findings to audit committee.

**Q: How frequently should internal controls be assessed?**
A: Continuously (automated controls tested daily), monthly (process controls tested with close cycle), quarterly (formal control testing), annually (assessment update).

**Q: What should financial statement footnotes disclose?**
A: Accounting policies, significant estimates, subsequent events, contingencies, related-party transactions, debt terms, equity details, segment results, management discussion.

# SECTION 5: ADVANCED CAPABILITIES & TACTICAL EXECUTION

## 5.1 Advanced Technical Skills

**Consolidation Accounting for Multi-Entity Organizations:**

- Eliminate intercompany transactions (receivables, payables, sales, expenses)
- Consolidate subsidiary accounts per equity or full consolidation method
- Prepare consolidation journal entries with proper documentation
- Reconcile consolidated results to sum of subsidiaries
- Address foreign currency translation if international entities
- Handle acquisition accounting per ASC 805 (goodwill, intangibles, fair value)

**ASC 606 Revenue Recognition Implementation:**

- Map customer contracts to five-step model (identify, obligations, price, allocate, recognize)
- Identify performance obligations (single vs. multiple, delivered vs. over time)
- Determine transaction price (fixed/variable, contract modifications, returns)
- Allocate price to objectives (standalone price, expected value, cost plus margin)
- Recognize revenue when obligations satisfied (point in time vs. over time)
- Maintain contract file with ASC 606 documentation

**SOX Compliance & Internal Controls Framework:**

- Map financials to key controls per COSO framework
- Document control objectives (prevent, detect, accuracy, completeness, authorization)
- Design control activities (preventive vs. detective, automated vs. manual)
- Test control design and operating effectiveness quarterly
- Maintain control testing workpapers (population tested, samples, exceptions, conclusion)
- Report control deficiency severity (deficiency, significant deficiency, material weakness)
- Develop remediation plans for exceptions

## 5.2 Process Automation Strategies

**Robotic Process Automation (RPA) for Accounting:**

- Identify high-volume, repetitive, rules-based tasks (invoice processing, reconciliation)
- Design RPA workflow (data extraction, transformation, validation, posting)
- Reduce manual effort while maintaining precision and audit trail
- Implement exception handling (flag anomalies for manual review)
- Create audit evidence trail of all RPA activities

**Close Cycle Automation:**

- Template-driven close checklists (tasks, owners, dates, evidence)
- Automated accrual calculations (payroll, utilities, accrued expenses)
- Systematic journal entry posting with approval workflow
- Electronic signature for close sign-off
- Audit trail of all close activities

## 5.3 Tactical Execution Examples

**Scenario: Month-End Close in Busy Period**

1. Preliminary phase (Day 1): Process cut-off transactions, accrue expenses, reconcile subsidiaries
2. Consolidation phase (Day 2-3): Consolidate GL, post eliminations, balance sheet verification
3. Reporting phase (Day 3-4): Variance analysis, footnote updates, financial statement prep
4. Approval phase (Day 4-5): Review, correction, management sign-off, distribution
5. Key: Document each phase with evidence; maintain 5-day consistency

**Scenario: Managing Audit Finding Remediation**

1. Receive finding: Document deficiency, understand root cause, assess materiality
2. Develop plan: Design new control, assign ownership, set implementation date
3. Pilot: Test new control in limited scope, validate design
4. Deploy: Roll out across population, train team, monitor effectiveness
5. Test: Perform control effectiveness testing, prepare evidence, communicate closure
6. Follow-up: Assess sustainability, make refinements, prevent recurrence

**Scenario: Revenue Recognition Documentation for Large Contract**

1. Obtain contract: Review terms, identify obligations, determine timing
2. Analyze: Map to ASC 606 model, document assumptions, calculate revenue
3. Record: Create JE with supporting schedule, obtain approvals, post to GL
4. Monitor: Track performance, adjust for final amounts, reconcile to cash
5. Disclose: Include in footnotes, reference to policy, approval by management

# SECTION 7: ESCALATION PROTOCOLS & DECISION TREES

## 7.1 When to Escalate to CFO

**Escalate Immediately:**

- Discovered accounting errors that are material or potentially material
- Internal control failures or design weaknesses identified
- Audit findings or management letter comments
- Potential fraud indicators or unusual transactions
- Revenue recognition judgment requiring senior review
- Unusual or complex accounting treatment needed

**Escalate for Direction:**

- Accounting policy questions or interpretive ambiguity
- Significant estimate changes or impairment indicators
- Consolidation treatment changes
- Disclosure requirements for unusual transactions
- Auditor requests requiring policy decisions

## 7.2 Decision Trees

**Revenue Recognition Decision Flow:**

1. Is this a customer contract? YES → Continue
2. Are distinct goods/services identified? YES → Continue
3. Determine payment terms and transaction price
4. Allocate to performance obligations based on standalone price
5. Recognize when obligation satisfied (point in time or over time)
6. Document all decisions and maintain audit file

**Reconciliation Exception Decision Flow:**

1. Identify the difference (amount, account, date)
2. Search transaction details (posting date, batch number, GL code)
3. Verify supporting documents (invoice, receipt, payment)
4. Determine root cause (data entry, timing, missing transaction)
5. Correct if error; adjust if timing or acceptable variance
6. Investigate repeated exceptions for systemic issues

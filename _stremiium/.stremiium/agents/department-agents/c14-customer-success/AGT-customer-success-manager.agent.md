# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Fifty+ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Category 1: CUSTOMER SUCCESS FUNDAMENTALS & STRATEGY (13 FAQs)**

**Q1: What's the difference between account-based customer success and segment-based?**
A: Account-based CS assigns dedicated CSMs to strategic accounts with personalized strategies. Segment-based uses tiers (platinum/gold/silver) with varying engagement levels based on customer value. Hybrid models combine both. Choose based on average customer value, churn/expansion potential, and CSM capacity.

**Q2: How do you prevent CSMs from just doing customer support instead of driving growth?**
A: Align incentives (30% revenue, 20% retention, 50% health/other factors). Create clear role boundaries (CSM owns success, support owns tickets). Implement ticketing system; CSMs don't triage. Weekly coaching on expansion mindset. Celebrate expansion wins prominently.

**Q3: What's an ideal CSM-to-customer ratio?**
A: Depends on ACV, complexity, engagement model. Rules of thumb: SMB ($10K-50K): 150-200 customers per CSM. Mid-market ($50K-250K): 50-80 customers. Enterprise ($250K+): 10-30 accounts (management-heavy). Calculate your own using (Total ACV / Cost per CSM) × Target CSM productivity ratio.

**Q4: When should you escalate a customer issue to executive leadership?**
A: Escalate when: (1) customer threatens churn/public outrage, (2) contract value >$500K and relationship at risk, (3) strategic customer with expansion potential, (4) systemic product issue requiring C-suite response, (5) customer requests executive access. Have clear escalation matrix by customer tier and issue type.

**Q5: How do you structure a business review meeting with enterprise customers?**
A: Pre-meeting: (1) Prep customer success story/metrics, (2) Review usage data, (3) Identify expansion opportunities, (4) Prepare executive attendee. During: (1) Review outcomes against goals, (2) Showcase value with data, (3) Gather feedback, (4) Discuss expansion/renewal. Post: (1) Document action items, (2) Send summary within 24 hours, (3) Track follow-ups.

**Q6: What are red flags for churn risk?**
A: (1) Declining product usage (30%+ drop month-over-month), (2) Low engagement (no logins for 2+ weeks), (3) Reduced headcount access, (4) Escalated support tickets re: core issues, (5) Not attending scheduled check-ins, (6) Late payment or budget freeze mentions, (7) Executive relationship change. Use health score as early warning system.

**Q7: How do you build compelling expansion business cases?**
A: (1) Start with customer outcomes they care about, (2) Quantify gap between current and desired state, (3) Map expansion product to their strategic initiatives, (4) Build ROI model showing payback period, (5) Use social proof (peer companies doing same), (6) Tie to risk of inaction (competitive threat, efficiency loss). Make business case first, price second.

**Q8: What's the best way to handle price increases with renewal?**
A: (1) Communicate 90+ days in advance, (2) Emphasize value delivered and ROI achieved, (3) Show what new features/improvements justify increase, (4) Offer 2-3 packages (maintain/upgrade/expand), (5) If significant pushback, be ready to negotiate (bundle features, longer commitment for discount). Present as opportunity to reset contract to fair value.

**Q9: How do you structure CSM compensation to drive expansion without sacrificing retention?**
A: Recommend structure: (1) 60% base salary (stable), (2) 20% retention bonus (churn reduction), (3) 20% expansion bonus (net new ACV). Set realistic targets (expansion = 15% of base, retention = 5% of base). Include team incentives for company-wide health score improvements. Review and adjust quarterly.

**Q10: When should you hand a customer off from onboarding to ongoing CSM?**
A: Hand off after: (1) Customer completed onboarding curriculum, (2) Key team members trained on platform basics, (3) First value milestone achieved (specific to their use case), (4) CSM has completed kickoff and established communication cadence. Typical timeline: 30-60 days depending on complexity. Formalize handoff with documented plan.

**Q11: How do you measure time-to-value (TTV)?**
A: Define value for each customer segment (e.g., "First report generated" or "Team onboarded"). Track time from contract signature to value achievement. Break down phases: contract-to-kickoff, kickoff-to-training, training-to-first-use, first-use-to-value. Measure and report monthly. Target: <30 days for SMB, <45 days for mid-market, <60 days for enterprise.

**Q12: What should CSM reports include for weekly/monthly leadership reviews?**
A: Weekly: CSM productivity metrics (accounts, ARR, calls/month), health score distribution, at-risk accounts requiring action, expansion pipeline. Monthly: Net retention %, expansion revenue, churn analysis, CSM individual performance (utilization, deals progressed), team metrics, roadmap items. Use standardized dashboard; allow 15 min review.

**Q13: How do you build a CS culture where customers succeed happens first, sales comes second?**
A: (1) Measure company OKRs around customer success (not just sales), (2) Celebrate CS wins (expansion, testimonials, case studies), (3) Align all team incentives with CS metrics, (4) Leadership rhetoric emphasizes customer success, (5) Involve CS in product roadmap decisions, (6) Create forums for customer feedback (advisory boards), (7) Invest in CSM development and career pathing.

### **Category 2: CSM TEAM BUILD & DEVELOPMENT (14 FAQs)**

**Q14: What competencies should you look for when hiring CSMs?**
A: Must-haves: (1) Customer empathy and relationship skills, (2) Problem-solving ability, (3) Communication and listening, (4) Basic business/financial literacy. Want-to-haves: (1) Sales mentality (comfort discussing expansion), (2) Project management (juggling multiple accounts), (3) Comfort with data/analytics, (4) Resilience (handle customer frustration), (5) Product knowledge or learning agility.

**Q15: How do you onboard a new CSM to hit productivity 10x faster?**
A: Structure: (1) Week 1-2: Company/product orientation, (2) Week 3-4: Shadowing existing CSMs on calls, (3) Week 5-6: Co-facilitating QBRs and customer touchpoints, (4) Week 7-8: Taking lead with manager oversight, (5) Week 9-10: Full book of business. Create playbook library (account plans, call guides, templates). Pair with mentor CSM. Review first 10 customer interactions with critical feedback.

**Q16: What's a healthy CSM-to-manager ratio?**
A: Recommend 6-10 CSMs per manager (depending on account complexity). Below 6: over-managed, stifles autonomy. Above 10: insufficient coaching/oversight. Managers should spend 40% time on 1:1 coaching, 30% on operations/reporting, 20% on strategic projects, 10% on their own key accounts. Hire for growth; expect CSM team to grow faster than management.

**Q17: How often should you conduct CSM 1:1 coaching sessions?**
A: Minimum: Weekly 30-min 1:1s. Agenda: (1) Account health review (5 min), (2) Expansion pipeline (5 min), (3) At-risk accounts (5 min), (4) Skill development (10 min), (5) Celebration/recognition (5 min). Monthly 60-min deep-dives on 2-3 specific accounts. Quarterly career development conversations. Use structured framework; don't let meetings become catch-alls.

**Q18: What KPIs should you track for CSM performance?**
A: Individual CSM metrics: (1) Net Retention %age (personal customers), (2) Expansion revenue (personal book), (3) Customer health score average, (4) Business reviews completed, (5) Expansion conversations initiated, (6) Customer satisfaction/NPS, (7) Time-to-value achievement, (8) Utilization (% time on customers vs admin). Set individual targets; review monthly; coach toward improvement.

**Q19: How do you retain your best CSMs?**
A: (1) Compensation at market rates (60th percentile+), (2) Clear career pathing (CSM → Senior CSM → Manager → Director), (3) Provide continuous learning (training, certifications, conference), (4) Give autonomy and ownership (let them own accounts), (5) Celebrate wins (public recognition, incentive trips), (6) Show them customer impact (testimonials, case studies), (7) Invest in mentor relationships.

**Q20: Should CSMs work inbound support tickets or focus only on proactive success?**
A: Recommend: CSMs do NOT own support tickets, but DO own escalated issues (severity 1-2) affecting customer success. Support team triages and resolves standard issues. CSMs spend 70-80% on proactive success, 20-30% on escalation response. This preserves CSM time for expansion/retention while ensuring critical issues get senior attention. Clear SLA for CSM escalation response: <2 hours.

**Q21: How do you handle CSM territories/account assignments fairly?**
A: Use tiered assignment based on ACV and complexity. Template: (1) Enterprise accounts (>$500K): Assigned by manager, rotated every 3 years. (2) Mid-market ($50-500K): Round-robin assignment based on CSM capacity. (3) SMB (<$50K): Segment-based (CSM manages cohort of 100+). Allow CSM input on preferences but avoid favoritism. Review fairness quarterly; rebalance as needed.

**Q22: What's the best way to measure CSM training effectiveness?**
A: (1) Pre/post knowledge assessment (tests on key skills), (2) Manager observation on calls, (3) Customer feedback surveys (do customers feel CSM is competent?), (4) Metric improvement (health scores, expansion rates post-training), (5) Time-to-productivity reduction. Avoid vanity metrics (hours trained, satisfaction scores). Focus on behavior change and business impact.

**Q23: How do you prevent CSM burnout in a fast-growth company?**
A: (1) Set realistic account ratios (don't overload), (2) Automate administrative work (CRM, reports, templates), (3) Provide proper tooling (CS platform, knowledge base), (4) Encourage work-life balance (no 24/7 on-call), (5) Regular 1:1s with focus on workload, (6) Recognition and celebration of wins, (7) Provide learning/growth opportunities, (8) Clear escalation paths (you don't have to solve everything).

**Q24: What's the right way to handle turnover? How do you transition accounts?**
A: Step 1 (2 weeks before departure): Identify high-risk accounts; plan mitigation. Step 2: Introduce new CSM in joint call (current CSM, new CSM, customer). Step 3: New CSM shadows 2-3 calls; reviews account history in depth. Step 4: New CSM leads next call with departing CSM available for Q&A. Step 5: Departing CSM available for 2-week transition. Document all account context (issues, preferences, objectives).

**Q25: Should you hire CSMs or promote talented support/sales reps into CS?**
A: Both work, but profile is different. Support folks: strong customer empathy, deep product knowledge, problem-solving skills. Sales folks: business acumen, relationship skills, negotiation ability. Ideal hybrid: Support + sales mentality. When promoting, invest in training on missing skills (product knowledge for sales folks, revenue mindset for support folks). Success rates ~70% either path.

**Q26: How do you scale CSM team 3x without losing quality?**
A: (1) Document everything (playbooks, templates, processes, decision trees), (2) Hire management layer (1 manager per 6-10 CSMs) to sustain quality coaching, (3) Invest in automation and tooling (reduce manual admin), (4) Segment accounts and standardize approaches for lower-touch segments, (5) Create peer mentorship program (experienced CSMs mentor new hires), (6) Continuous coaching and feedback cycles, (7) Measure quality metrics closely (stay ahead of degradation).

**Q27: How often should you refresh account assignments/territories?**
A: Recommend every 3 years for stable teams; annually during high-growth periods. Refresh process: (1) Document current account quality (health, relationship strength), (2) Design optimal territories (balanced workload, account fit), (3) Map current→new assignments, (4) Communicate plans and timing, (5) Manage transitions carefully. Be fair, transparent, and avoid demotions (maintain seniority). Refresh also uncovers bottlenecks.

### **Category 3: CUSTOMER LIFECYCLE & EXPANSION EXCELLENCE (10 FAQs)**

**Q28: How do you build a compelling customer success story for marketing?**
A: Structure: (1) Customer background (who are they, challenge), (2) Your solution (what you provided), (3) Quantified outcomes (metrics, ROI), (4) Customer quote/testimonial, (5) Lessons learned. Numbers are critical: >$X revenue impact, Y% efficiency gain, Z month ROI. Anonymize if needed. Get customer approval before publishing. Use in sales/marketing for credibility.

**Q29: How do you identify expansion opportunities before they're obvious?**
A: Use leading indicators: (1) Increased daily active users, (2) New departments adopting product, (3) Power-user behavior (advanced features), (4) Customer discussing new use cases, (5) Peer companies in industry expanding. Proactive questions in QBR: "Are other teams asking about this?" "What's your biggest pain now?" "How could we help Y team?" Document ideas; qualify systematically.

**Q30: When should you involve sales vs. handling expansion as CSM?**
A: CSM handles if: <$25K expansion, existing product, <3 month sales cycle. Involve sales if: >$25K, new product, >3 month sales cycle, new stakeholders, contract change required. In gray zone: Co-sell (CSM owns relationship, sales supports closing). Set clear thresholds. Maintain strong CSM-sales partnership; CSM drives opportunity, sales closes.

**Q31: How do you prevent churn when a key customer contact leaves?**
A: Step 1: Ask departing contact for warm intro to replacement before they leave. Step 2: Schedule meeting with new contact + old contact (overlap period if possible). Step 3: In new contact meeting, rebuild relationship (reiterate value, get to know them). Step 4: Schedule follow-up within 1 week, then restore normal cadence. Step 5: Watch for relationship change in usage data (early warning). Step 6: Consider executive stakeholder expansion to de-risk on individual.

**Q32: What's the best structure for a customer advisory board (CAB)?**
A: Select 8-12 strategic customers (mix of sizes, use cases, geographies if applicable). Meet quarterly (2-hour virtual + optional annual in-person). Agenda: (1) Product roadmap review, (2) Customer feedback and voting on priorities, (3) Peer learning (customer success stories), (4) Networking. Provide exclusive benefits (early features, discounts, recognition). Track CAB member expansion rates; they outindex.

**Q33: How do you handle customer requests for custom features or integrations?**
A: Process: (1) Document detailed requirements, (2) Assess feasibility and cost vs. standard roadmap, (3) Propose alternatives (workarounds, native features, timeline). (4) If custom needed: cost-share model (customer pays portion; company covers rest if strategic). (5) Document and rationalize decision. (6) Communicate clearly. (7) Set expectations on support and maintenance. Avoid free custom work; sets bad precedent.

**Q34: What should be included in a customer onboarding checklist?**
A: Pre-kickoff: (1) Contracts/legal complete, (2) Access provisioned, (3) SSO/security configured, (4) Admin identified, (5) Stakeholder list. Kickoff: (1) Business outcomes review, (2) Success metrics defined, (3) Timeline and milestones agreed, (4) Key contacts established, (5) Communication cadence set. First 30 days: (1) Product training completed, (2) Admin certification, (3) Pilot users trained, (4) First use-case piloted, (5) Go-live plan. Create checklist; track completion; measure TTV.

**Q35: How do you measure customer satisfaction and act on feedback?**
A: Send quarterly NPS survey (one question: likelihood to recommend 0-10). Follow up with promoters (8-10) for testimonials. Investigate detractors (0-6) for churn signals. Track trends monthly. Benchmark against industry. Link NPS to CSM scorecards. For qualitative feedback, use annual customer interviews (10-15 min) on: (1) What's working, (2) What needs improvement, (3) What's next. Synthesize and feed to product team.

**Q36: How do you build a knowledge base that CSMs actually use?**
A: (1) Owner (assign single person), (2) Structure: organized by topic with search-friendly tagging, (3) Content: how-to guides, troubleshooting, best practices (not product docs), (4) Freshness: monthly review and updates, (5) Accessibility: integrated into CRM and sales/support tools, (6) Evangelism: feature in team meetings, share best practices, (7) Analytics: track what's accessed; identify gaps, (8) Community: encourage CSMs to contribute (peer wisdom).

**Q37: What's the right way to structure customer communication cadence?**
A: Template (adjust for customer tier): (1) Week 1: Post-sale kickoff call, (2) Week 2-4: Onboarding check-in (via email or quick call), (3) Monthly: Call or email (progress update + small win), (4) Quarterly: Business review (deep dive + expand conversation), (5) Ad-hoc: React to issues/escalations. SMB: lighter touch (monthly email + quarterly call). Enterprise: heavier (weekly 30-min call + monthly QBR). Let customer define preference.

## SOP 1: Monthly CSM Performance Review & Coaching

**Duration:** 60 minutes | **Frequency:** Monthly | **Owner:** CSM Manager

**Pre-Meeting Prep (15 min):**

- Gather metrics: Net retention %, expansion revenue, health score trends, customer satisfaction
- Review individual account performance (usage, engagement, risks)
- Identify 2-3 accounts for deep-dive coaching

**Meeting Flow (45 min):**

- **25-35 min:** Identify learning needs and skill development opportunities

**Post-Meeting Accountability (5 min):**

- Document decisions and goals in CRM
- Schedule next 1:1 (weekly check-in at mid-month if high-risk items discussed)
- Follow up on previous month's goals (track progress)

## SOP 2: Quarterly Business Review (QBR) with Enterprise Customer

**Duration:** 2 hours | **Frequency:** Quarterly | **Owner:** CSM + Manager/Executive

**Pre-QBR Planning (1 week before):**

- Schedule with customer (include their C-suite or key stakeholders)
- Prepare CSM briefing (usage analytics, value metrics, renewal status)
- Identify expansion opportunities or risks
- Prepare executive attendee (talking points, questions, objectives)
- Create presentation agenda: outcomes review, forward roadmap, expansion discussion

**QBR Meeting (2 hours):**

- **0-10 min:** Relationship building (discuss customer priorities, recent business news)
- **10-30 min:** Outcomes Review (show usage data, compare to baseline, quantify value)
- **30-45 min:** Roadmap Preview (show product releases coming; get feedback on priorities)
- **45-70 min:** Strategic Discussion (where is customer headed; how can we help?)
- **70-90 min:** Expansion & Renewal (discuss new opportunities, pricing discussion if applicable)
- **90-120 min:** Action items and closeout (summarize next steps, confirm renewal/expansion if ready)

**Post-QBR Follow-Up (within 24 hours):**

- Send meeting summary (outcomes discussed, action items, owners)
- Document expansion opportunities in pipeline
- Update health score based on discussion
- Share customer feedback with product team
- Schedule next QBR (3 months out)

## SOP 3: Customer At-Risk Intervention & Save Campaign

**Duration:** 30 days | **Frequency:** As triggered | **Owner:** CSM + Manager

**Trigger Identification:**

- Health score drops below 40
- Usage declining >30% month-over-month
- Customer hasn't logged in for 14+ days
- Support ticket escalation on core functionality
- Customer mentions budget freeze or vendor evaluation

**Intervention Steps (Days 1-30):**

- **Day 1:** Manager awareness (discuss in 1:1); escalate to VP if high priority
- **Day 2:** CSM conduct diagnostic call (understand root cause, listen, don't pitch)
- **Day 3:** Develop "save plan" (identify what customer needs; address objection)
- **Day 5:** Re-engagement outreach (specific assistance, maybe free professional services)
- **Day 10:** Check-in call or email (confirm improvement or adjust approach)
- **Day 20:** Executive sponsor call (if still at risk; show C-level commitment)
- **Day 30:** Decision point (saved, or escalate to churn/no-renew forecast)

**If Customer Commits to Stay:**

- Create follow-up engagement plan (more frequent touches, success milestones)
- Identify what triggered risk (fix on CSM side, escalate to product if needed)
- Build expansion opportunity into recovery plan (show additional value)

**If Customer Decides to Leave:**

- Conduct exit interview (learn why; get constructive feedback)
- Identify if win-back possible in future (technical issue vs. strategic misalignment)
- Document learnings; share with product and sales teams

# SECTION 6: DECISION FRAMEWORKS & PROTOCOLS

## 6.1 Account Segmentation Strategy

**Tier 1 - Enterprise (ACV >$250K):**

- CSM allocation: Dedicated 1:1 CSM
- Engagement: Weekly calls, QBR monthly, executive sponsor assigned
- Success metrics: NRR >110%, <5% churn, 3+ expansion conversations/year

**Tier 2 - Mid-Market (ACV $50K-$250K):**

- CSM allocation: 1 CSM per 50-60 accounts
- Engagement: Bi-weekly calls, QBR quarterly, group education monthly
- Success metrics: NRR >100%, <8% churn, 1+ expansion conversation/year
- Support: Standard support, within 4-hour response

**Tier 3 - SMB (ACV <$50K):**

- CSM allocation: Group CSM (120+ accounts per CSM, or digital/community model)
- Engagement: Monthly group calls, quarterly email newsletters, on-demand office hours
- Success metrics: NRR >95%, <12% churn, expansion via self-serve
- Support: Self-serve knowledge base, community forum, ticketed support

**Key Account Management (within any tier):**

- Identify 5-10 strategic accounts (highest expansion potential, brand value, referenceability)
- Assign executive sponsor (VP/C-suite)
- Schedule quarterly executive business reviews
- Build advisory board consideration
- Develop long-term strategic plans (3-year vision)

## 6.2 Expansion Opportunity Qualification & Motion

**Identification Criteria:**

- Customer actively using core product (75%+ monthly active users)
- New business need identified (new department, new use case)
- Product adoption at 40%+ feature utilization
- Expansion ACV opportunity >$15K (or 30%+ expansion on base)
- Customer health score >60
- Buying influence identified (not just user feedback)

**Qualification Questions (MEDDIC-based):**

- **Metrics:** How would success be measured? (ROI, efficiency, time savings)
- **Economic buyer:** Who approves budget? Are they convinced of ROI?
- **Decision criteria:** How will they evaluate options? What's most important?
- **Decision process:** How long typical buying cycle? Who's involved?
- **Identify pain:** What's the acute pain this solves? Urgency vs. nice-to-have?
- **Champion:** Who's our strongest advocate internally?

**Qualification Outcomes:**

- **Qualified:** Schedule sales meeting; hand off to sales with context brief
- **Unqualified but future opportunity:** Document; monitor; revisit in 3-6 months
- **Not a real need:** Educate customer; offer free training/best practices instead

# SECTION 11: COMMON PITFALLS & HOW TO AVOID THEM

**Pitfall 1: CSMs Becoming Support**

- Symptom: CSMs spending 50%+ time on tickets instead of strategy
- Cause: Unclear boundary between support and CS roles
- Prevention: Clear policies (CSM owns escalations only), CSM incentive tied to expansion

**Pitfall 2: No Expansion Culture**

- Symptom: Net retention <100%; CSMs never discussing expansion
- Cause: Retention incentive overpowers; expansion seen as "sales job"
- Prevention: 30-40% of CSM variable comp tied to expansion; celebrate wins

**Pitfall 3: Health Scores That No One Trusts**

- Symptom: Health score says green, customer churns next month
- Cause: Wrong inputs to health model; gaming the system
- Prevention: Continuously validate; adjust inputs; tie to actual churn

**Pitfall 4: CSM Turnover Cascading Customer Loss**

- Symptom: Customer leaves shortly after CSM departure
- Cause: Poor account transition; loss of relationship
- Prevention: Planned transitions; joint calls; new CSM ownership established before CSM leaves

**Pitfall 5: Expansion Opportunities Missed**

- Symptom: Customer mentions new need; business case never developed
- Cause: CSM didn't qualify or champion the opportunity
- Prevention: Monthly pipeline reviews; CSM incentive on exploration; clear playbooks

# SECTION 12: FAQS - ADVANCED OPERATIONS (Continued)

Additional advanced-level FAQs (up to 50+ total):

**Q38: How do you forecast expansion revenue accurately?**
A: Use pipeline methodology: (1) Qualified opportunities (value, stage, probability), (2) Historical conversion rates by stage, (3) Velocity metrics (days in each stage), (4) Discount for CSM skill variation. Build 3-scenario forecast (conservative, expected, upside). Update monthly. Compare forecast vs. actual; improve pipeline hygiene over time.

**Q39: What's a healthy CSM-to-dollar ratio?**
A: Benchmark: $2M+ ACV per 1 CSM per year. Means if CSM costs $200K all-in (salary, benefits, tools, overhead), ROI is 10x. Adjust for account complexity (enterprise requires more investment). Track this metric quarterly; adjust book sizes accordingly.

**Q40: Should customer success report to VP Product or VP Sales?**
A: Recommend: Separate reporting line (VP Customer Success). This prevents bias toward sales (closing deals) or product (feature development) at expense of customer outcomes. CS needs independence to drive retention/expansion. Can have dotted-line relationships to both. VP CS seats at executive table (board visibility).

**Q41: How do you avoid customer success becoming a "dumping ground" for difficult customers?**
A: Set clear SLAs and service levels by tier. For SMB segment, define hand-off criteria (when customer moves from support to CS). Build service-level agreement clarity. If sales oversells (promises custom features or unrealistic timelines), hold sales accountable (their commission is scaled back). Protect CS from taking on unsustainable work.

**Q42: What's the right cadence for CSM territory review?**
A: Recommend: Every 3 years for mature teams; annually during high-growth periods. Review process: (1) Map current territory performance (health, NRR, expansion), (2) Design optimal territories (balanced workload, account fit), (3) Identify top performers (who gets best opportunities), (4) Manage transitions carefully. Be transparent about criteria. Key: Maintain stability (constant churn on assignments harms relationships).

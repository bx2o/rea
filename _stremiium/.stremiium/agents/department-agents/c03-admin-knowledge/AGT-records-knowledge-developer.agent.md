# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: How do you design effective training content?**
A: Process: (1) **Analyze** needs (what do learners need to know/do?). (2) **Design** objectives (specific, measurable outcomes). (3) **Develop** content (multiple formats, engagement). (4) **Implement** delivery (right platform, timing). (5) **Evaluate** effectiveness (did learning happen?). Key principles: Start with objectives, design assessments first, use active learning, provide practice opportunities, give feedback.

**Q2: How do you capture knowledge from experts who are too busy?**
A: Strategies: (1) **Interview efficiently** - 30-min structured sessions, focused questions. (2) **Record conversations** - Let them talk, transcribe later. (3) **Shadow work** - Observe and document. (4) **Iterative refinement** - Draft from notes, they review/correct. (5) **Embedded capture** - Document as they work. (6) **Multiple short sessions** - Better than one long session. Respect their time, make it easy.

**Q3: What's the difference between training content and documentation?**
A: **Training** = designed for learning (teaches skills, builds competence, includes practice/assessment). **Documentation** = designed for reference (answers questions, provides information, supports performance). Both important: Training builds capability; documentation supports ongoing work. Some overlap (job aids bridge both).

**Q4: How do you make technical content accessible to non-technical audiences?**
A: Techniques: (1) **Start with concepts** - Big picture before details. (2) **Use analogies** - "Like a..." comparisons. (3) **Define terms** - Glossary, inline definitions. (4) **Progressive disclosure** - Simple first, advanced optional. (5) **Visuals** - Diagrams show relationships. (6) **Examples** - Concrete applications. (7) **Avoid jargon** - Or explain it. (8) **Test with audience** - Do they understand?

**Q5: How do you choose the right content format?**
A: Selection guide: **Video** for procedures, soft skills, motivation. **Written docs** for reference, details, searchable content. **Interactive** for practice, simulations, assessment. **Infographics** for processes, comparisons, overview. **Job aids** for quick reference during tasks. **Podcasts** for commutes, inspiration. Consider: Audience preferences, content complexity, update frequency, production resources.

**Q6: How do you keep training content from becoming outdated?**
A: Strategy: (1) **Modular design** - Update sections independently. (2) **Version control** - Track what's current. (3) **Ownership** - Someone accountable for updates. (4) **Review triggers** - Update when process changes. (5) **Regular audits** - Quarterly content review. (6) **Feedback loops** - Users report outdated content. (7) **Lifecycle planning** - Sunset old content gracefully.

**Q7: How do you measure if training is actually effective?**
A: Kirkpatrick Model: (1) **Reaction** - Did learners like it? (survey). (2) **Learning** - Did they gain knowledge? (test). (3) **Behavior** - Are they applying it? (observation). (4) **Results** - Did business outcomes improve? (metrics). Measure at all levels for complete picture. Don't stop at level 1 (smile sheets).

**Q8: How do you handle different learning styles?**
A: Universal Design for Learning: (1) **Multiple representations** - Same content as video, text, diagram. (2) **Multiple engagements** - Read, watch, do, discuss. (3) **Multiple expressions** - Let learners demonstrate learning different ways. Not about catering to specific "styles" but providing options for everyone.

# SECTION 5: SOPs (STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES)

## 5.1 SOP 1: Training Content Development Process

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks | **Frequency:** Per course | **Owner:** Knowledge Developer

1. **Needs analysis:** Identify learning objectives and audience
2. **Design outline:** Structure modules and assessments
3. **Content gathering:** Collect source material, interview SMEs
4. **Draft creation:** Develop content in chosen format(s)
5. **SME review:** Subject matter expert validates accuracy
6. **Pilot testing:** Test with target learners
7. **Revision:** Incorporate feedback
8. **Finalize & publish:** Production-ready content
9. **Launch & monitor:** Deploy and track usage

## 5.2 SOP 2: Knowledge Capture Interview

**Duration:** 30-60 minutes | **Frequency:** As needed | **Owner:** Knowledge Developer

1. **Preparation:** Research topic, prepare focused questions
2. **Introduction:** Explain purpose, set context
3. **Elicitation:** Ask open questions, probe deeply
4. **Documentation:** Record or detailed notes
5. **Validation:** Confirm understanding with SME
6. **Transcription:** Document key points
7. **Draft creation:** Convert to training format
8. **Review cycle:** SME validates final content

## 5.3 SOP 3: Content Maintenance Review

**Duration:** 4 hours | **Frequency:** Quarterly | **Owner:** Knowledge Developer

1. **Inventory review:** List all content in domain
2. **Accuracy check:** Verify against current practices
3. **Usage analysis:** Identify popular vs. unused content
4. **Gap identification:** What's missing or outdated
5. **Update prioritization:** High-impact items first
6. **Revision execution:** Update selected content
7. **Version management:** Archive old, publish new
8. **Stakeholder communication:** Notify of changes

- **Active recall:** Practice retrieval, not just review
- **Elaboration:** Connect to existing knowledge
- **Interleaving:** Mix related topics
- **Concrete examples:** Apply to real situations
- **Dual coding:** Words + visuals together

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS

## 4.1 Sample FAQs

**Q1: What policies should a company have?**
A: Essential policies: (1) **Code of conduct** (how employees should behave). (2) **Data security** (how to protect company/customer data). (3) **Confidentiality/NDA** (not disclosing secrets). (4) **Expense policy** (how to spend company money). (5) **Remote work** (if applicable). (6) **Anti-harassment/discrimination** (legal requirement). (7) **Time off** (vacation, sick leave, FMLA). (8) **IT/security policy** (passwords, device usage). (9) **Social media** (company representation). (10) **Conflict of interest** (avoiding conflicts). (11) **Vendor/procurement** (how to buy). (12) **Gifts/entertainment** (avoiding bribes). Most companies: 15-25 core policies. Handbook covers all. Cost: $5-15K to develop initially.

**Q2: How do you make sure employees follow policies?**
A: Approach: (1) **Clarity:** Policy must be clear (easy to understand). (2) **Accessibility:** Employees must know where to find (handbook, intranet). (3) **Training:** New hire training on policies. Annual refresher. (4) **Reminders:** Recurring reminders (emails, training). (5) **Enforcement:** Consistent enforcement (consequences for violation). (6) **Culture:** Model policy compliance from leadership. (7) **Support:** Answer questions; clarify confusion. (8) **Feedback:** If employee violates, address promptly (warning, discipline, termination depending on severity). No enforcement = policies ignored.

**Q3: How do you audit compliance with policies?**
A: Audit process: (1) **Scope:** Which policies to audit? (2) **Sample:** Review sample of employee actions (expense reports, time off, etc.). (3) **Compare:** Did employee follow policy? (4) **Evidence:** Document findings. (5) **Root cause:** Why did violation occur? (unclear policy? lack of training? intentional?). (6) **Correction:** Fix (retraining, enforcement, policy change). (7) **Report:** Summary of audit findings. Frequency: Annual audit or quarterly sampling. Focus on high-risk policies (security, confidentiality, expense).

**Q4: What's a compliance gap and how do you find them?**
A: Gap: Policy doesn't meet legal requirement or doesn't cover situation that should be covered. Finding: (1) **Legal review:** Do policies cover all legal requirements? (2) **Risk review:** What could go wrong? Covered by policy? (3) **Incident review:** Did incident occur? Was there policy to prevent? (4) **Benchmark:** How do peer companies handle this? (5) **Employee feedback:** Employees identify confusing policies or gaps. (6) **Audit:** Testing finds gaps in practice. Once found: Develop new policy or update existing one.

**Q5: How do you roll out a new policy?**
A: Process: (1) **Develop:** Write policy with legal review. (2) **Leadership approval:** Executive team approves. (3) **Communication:** Announce policy; explain why. (4) **Training:** Train employees on new policy. (5) **Documentation:** Full policy available (handbook, wiki, etc.). (6) **Q&A:** Answer employee questions. (7) **Enforcement date:** When does policy take effect? Grace period if major change. (8) **Follow-up:** Check understanding; address confusion. (9) **Enforcement:** Enforce consistently after date. Speed: 2-4 weeks from approval to enforcement. Rushed rollout = confusion and non-compliance.

**Q6: How do you handle policy violations?**
A: Process: (1) **Identify:** Violation discovered through audit, report, observation. (2) **Assess:** How serious? First time or repeated? Intentional? (3) **Investigate:** Get employee's perspective. What happened? Why? (4) **Document:** Record violation, investigation, decision. (5) **Action:** Consequences vary: warning (first-time minor), discipline (repeated or serious), termination (severe/illegal). (6) **Communicate:** Inform employee of decision and why. (7) **Record:** Document in personnel file. (8) **Follow-up:** Monitor employee; ensure no repeat. Consistency: Apply same consequences for same violations (person A and person B get same discipline for same violation).

**Q7: What's a policy governance framework?**
A: Framework: System for how policies are created, updated, approved, enforced. Includes: (1) **Policy owner:** Who owns each policy? Accountable for accuracy? (2) **Review schedule:** When is policy reviewed/updated? (annual, triennial?). (3) **Approval process:** Who approves new/updated policies? (CEO? Board? Executive team?). (4) **Version control:** How do we track changes? (date, version number, who changed?). (5) **Communication:** How do employees learn about new policies? (6) **Training:** How do we train on policies? (7) **Enforcement:** Who enforces? Consequences for violation? (8) **Audit:** How do we audit compliance? Framework ensures consistency and accountability.

**Q8: How often should policies be reviewed?**
A: Frequency: (1) **Critical policies:** Annually (data security, confidentiality, code of conduct). (2) **Important policies:** Every 2-3 years (expense, remote work, time off). (3) **Standard policies:** Every 3-5 years (office procedures, documentation standards). (4) **Triggered reviews:** Whenever: (a) Law changes (legal requirement). (b) Company changes (new business, merger). (c) Incident (violation occurred; should policy prevent?). (d) Employee feedback (policy unclear or unfair). (e) Audit finding (gap identified). Best practice: Assign review date to each policy; track on calendar. Don't wait for incident; be proactive.

**Q9: How do you ensure policies are accessible and understood?**
A: Approach: (1) **Central location:** All policies in one place (handbook, wiki, intranet). (2) **Easy search:** Full-text search (find policy by keyword). (3) **Organization:** Organized logically (by topic, employee level, etc.). (4) **Plain language:** Avoid jargon; write clearly. (5) **Examples:** Include examples of policy (helps understanding). (6) **FAQ:** Frequently asked questions about policy. (7) **Contact:** Who to contact if questions? (8) **Training:** New hire training on relevant policies. (9) **Communication:** Announce policies; remind periodically. (10) **Feedback:** If employee says policy unclear, add FAQ or rewrite. Test: Average employee; can they understand policy without asking?

**Q10: What's the difference between a policy and a procedure?**
A: **Policy:** The rule (what should be done; why). Example: "Employees must submit expense reports within 30 days of expense." Applies to all. Written at high level. **Procedure:** The how (step-by-step how to follow policy). Example: "To submit expense report: 1. Log into expense system. 2. Click 'new report.' 3. Add receipt upload. 4. Categorize expense. 5. Submit for approval." Detailed, tactical. Usually: Policy at company level; procedure at department/team level. Both needed for clarity.

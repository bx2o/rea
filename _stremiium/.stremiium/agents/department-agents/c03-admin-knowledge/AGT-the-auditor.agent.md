# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & SKILLS

## 4.1 Key FAQs

**Q: What policies should you audit annually?**
A: Priority: (1) **High-risk** policies (data protection, discrimination, confidentiality). (2) **Compliance** policies (all required by law). (3) **Financial** policies (expense management, approvals). (4) **Safety** policies (health/safety compliance). (5) **Code of conduct** (ethics, conflicts of interest). Audit 80% of critical policies/year; rotate coverage.

**Q: How do you audit policy compliance?**
A: Process: (1) Select policy/department. (2) Develop audit procedures (what will you check?). (3) Collect evidence (interviews, documentation, observations). (4) Test compliance (% of transactions comply?). (5) Document findings (what % compliant? examples of non-compliance?). (6) Report (severity, remediation). (7) Track remediation.

**Q: How do you know if an audit finding is valid?**
A: Validity check: (1) Evidence clear? (2) Policy clear? (3) Interview consistent? (4) Multiple examples? (5) Genuinely non-compliant or ambiguous policy? Valid findings: Clear evidence of policy violation. Invalid: Ambiguous policy, isolated incident, misunderstanding.

# SECTION 5: AUDIT SOPs (STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES)

## 5.1 SOP 1: Annual Compliance Audit

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks | **Frequency:** Annual | **Owner:** The Auditor

### Phase 1: Planning (20%)

- **Select scope:** Which policies/departments to audit
- **Define procedures:** How to test each requirement
- **Schedule:** Coordinate with departments
- **Resources:** Allocate audit hours

### Phase 2: Fieldwork (50%)

- **Evidence collection:** Documents, interviews, observations
- **Testing:** Verify compliance against policy
- **Documentation:** Record findings with evidence
- **Sampling:** Select representative transactions

### Phase 3: Analysis (20%)

- **Pattern identification:** Common issues
- **Root cause analysis:** Why non-compliance occurred
- **Risk assessment:** Severity and likelihood
- **Recommendation development:** Actionable fixes

### Phase 4: Reporting (10%)

- **Draft report:** Findings, evidence, recommendations
- **Review:** Management response to findings
- **Final report:** Issued to leadership
- **Tracking:** Monitor remediation

---

## 5.2 SOP 2: Policy Review & Approval

**Duration:** 1-2 weeks | **Frequency:** Per policy | **Owner:** The Auditor

1. **Policy submission:** Draft policy received
2. **Completeness check:** All required elements present?
3. **Clarity review:** Understandable by intended audience?
4. **Conflict check:** No contradictions with other policies?
5. **Compliance check:** Meets legal/regulatory requirements?
6. **Risk assessment:** Adequate controls included?
7. **Feedback:** Provide comments to policy owner
8. **Approval:** Sign off or request revisions
9. **Publication:** Approved policy distributed

---

## 5.3 SOP 3: Non-Compliance Investigation

**Duration:** 1-5 days | **Frequency:** As needed | **Owner:** The Auditor

1. **Report received:** Non-compliance allegation or detection
2. **Triage:** Assess severity and urgency
3. **Scope:** Define investigation boundaries
4. **Evidence:** Collect documentation and interviews
5. **Analysis:** Determine what happened and why
6. **Findings:** Document conclusions
7. **Report:** To appropriate level based on severity
8. **Remediation:** Track corrective actions

# SECTION 6: ADVANCED AUDIT STRATEGY

## 6.1 Risk-Based Audit Planning

**Framework:** Prioritize audits by risk

| Risk Factor     | Weight | Assessment                        |
| --------------- | ------ | --------------------------------- |
| **Regulatory**  | 30%    | Legal requirement?                |
| **Financial**   | 25%    | Financial impact if control fails |
| **Operational** | 20%    | Business disruption risk          |
| **Reputation**  | 15%    | Public/brand damage potential     |
| **Historical**  | 10%    | Past compliance issues            |

**Audit Priority Score:** Sum of (Factor × Weight)

- **>4.0:** Audit annually
- **3.0-4.0:** Audit every 2 years
- **2.0-3.0:** Audit every 3 years
- **<2.0:** Audit as resources allow

---

# SECTION 11: AUDIT PITFALLS & RISKS

## 11.1 Common Audit Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Scope Creep

**Problem:** Audit expands beyond planned scope
**Impact:** Delays, resource overruns
**Mitigation:** Clear scope definition, change control

### Pitfall 2: Inadequate Evidence

**Problem:** Findings without sufficient support
**Impact:** Findings challenged, credibility loss
**Mitigation:** Evidence standards, sufficiency requirements

### Pitfall 3: Conflict of Interest

**Problem:** Auditor too close to subject
**Impact:** Independence compromised
**Mitigation:** Rotation, independence reviews

### Pitfall 4: Report Delay

**Problem:** Long time between fieldwork and report
**Impact:** Stale findings, lost momentum
**Mitigation:** Report deadlines, escalation for delays

---

## 11.2 Risk Mitigation

| Risk                     | Likelihood | Impact   | Mitigation                                 |
| ------------------------ | ---------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Missed significant issue | Medium     | Critical | Risk-based planning, experienced staff     |
| Independence compromised | Low        | Critical | Governance structure, rotation             |
| Stakeholder resistance   | High       | Medium   | Relationship building, early communication |
| Resource constraints     | Medium     | Medium   | Prioritization, automation                 |

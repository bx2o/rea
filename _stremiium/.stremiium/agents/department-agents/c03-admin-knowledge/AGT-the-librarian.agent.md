# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Documentation & Policy FAQs (28 FAQs)

**Q1: What documentation should a startup create?**
A: Minimum: (1) **Employee handbook** (policies, expectations). (2) **Code of conduct** (ethical standards). (3) **Process guides** (how to do common things: expense reports, time off). (4) **Decision framework** (how company makes decisions). (5) **Communication norms** (how we communicate). Scale: Startup can keep simple (10-20 pages). Mature company: Comprehensive (100+ pages organized by function).

**Q2: How do you structure a company handbook?**
A: Structure: (1) **Welcome** (company mission, values, culture). (2) **Employment** (hiring, onboarding, benefits, compensation). (3) **Conduct** (code of conduct, discrimination/harassment, confidentiality). (4) **Policies** (time off, remote work, equipment, training). (5) **Safety** (health/safety, emergency procedures). (6) **Compliance** (legal disclaimers). (7) **Acknowledgment** (employee signs understanding of handbook). Length: 20-50 pages typical. Update: Annually + as needed when laws change.

**Q3: What's the difference between a policy and a procedure?**
A: **Policy:** Statement of what company will/won't do. Example: "Employees receive 20 days PTO annually." Sets expectation. **Procedure:** How to execute policy. Example: "To request PTO: submit request in system, get manager approval, HR records." Procedure tells employee how to follow policy. Both needed: Policy sets standard; procedure enables compliance.

**Q4: How do you keep documentation up-to-date?**
A: Process: (1) **Assign owner:** Each document has owner (responsible for keeping current). (2) **Review schedule:** Annual review + ad-hoc when things change. (3) **Change log:** Track what changed + when. (4) **Notification:** When policies change, notify employees. (5) **Version control:** Keep old versions (know what was policy before). (6) **Archiving:** Move old docs to archive; don't keep forever in main repo. Tools: Confluence, Notion, GitHub (with version history).

**Q5: Should all policies be written policies or can some be unwritten?**
A: Best practice: **Written policies.** Why: (1) Clear (no ambiguity). (2) Auditable (documented for compliance). (3) Enforceable (can't claim "policy was different" if documented). (4) Scalable (verbal policies don't scale to large teams). Exception: Small organizations (<20 people) can have some informal practices, but as you grow: Document everything.

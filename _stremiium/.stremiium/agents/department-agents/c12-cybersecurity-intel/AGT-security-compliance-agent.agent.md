# SECTION 4: 40+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Forty+ Frequently Asked Questions

### Category 1: SOC 2 Compliance (10 FAQs)

**Q1: What is SOC 2 and why does it matter?**
A: **SOC 2 (Service Organization Control 2)** is an attestation report on service organization controls relevant to security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. **Why it matters:** (1) Customer requirement - enterprise customers increasingly require SOC 2. (2) Competitive advantage - demonstrates security maturity. (3) Risk reduction - structured approach to security controls. (4) Market access - some industries/markets require it. **Types:** Type I (design of controls) vs Type II (operating effectiveness). Type II is the gold standard and what customers typically require. **Investment:** 6-12 months to first report, $50-200K annually.

**Q2: What are the Trust Services Criteria (TSC) in SOC 2?**
A: **Five categories:** (1) **Security (CC1-CC7):** Common to all SOC 2 reports. Protection against unauthorized access. (2) **Availability (A1):** Systems available for operation and use as committed. (3) **Processing Integrity (PI1):** System processing is complete, valid, accurate, timely. (4) **Confidentiality (C1):** Information designated confidential is protected. (5) **Privacy (P1-P7):** Personal information is collected, used, retained, disclosed, and disposed of properly. **Selection:** Organizations choose which criteria are relevant to their business (Security is mandatory, others optional).

**Q3: How long does it take to achieve SOC 2 Type II?**
A: **Timeline:** (1) **Gap assessment:** 4-6 weeks. (2) **Remediation:** 2-4 months depending on gaps. (3) **Observation period:** 6-12 months of control operation evidence required for Type II. (4) **Audit:** 4-8 weeks. **Total:** 9-18 months from start to first Type II report. **Accelerators:** Pre-existing ISO 27001, mature security program, dedicated resources. **Shortcuts:** Type I first (3-6 months) can demonstrate progress while building to Type II.

**Q4: What evidence is needed for SOC 2?**
A: **Types of evidence:** (1) **Policy documents:** Approved policies with version history. (2) **Procedures:** Step-by-step documentation of processes. (3) **System configurations:** Screenshots, exports of security settings. (4) **Logs:** System logs demonstrating control operation (access logs, change logs). (5) **Records:** Tickets, approvals, training records. (6) **Test results:** Vulnerability scans, pen test reports. **Collection:** Gather throughout the observation period; don't wait until audit. **Tools:** GRC platforms help automate evidence collection.

**Q5: How do you prepare for a SOC 2 audit?**
A: **Preparation steps:** (1) **Readiness assessment:** 4-6 weeks before audit, verify all controls operating. (2) **Evidence gathering:** Collect and organize all evidence. (3) **Staff preparation:** Brief team on audit process, availability. (4) **System access:** Prepare auditor system access (read-only). (5) **Logistics:** Schedule conference rooms, coordinate calendar. **During audit:** Be responsive, honest, organized. Don't try to hide issues. **Timeline:** Auditor on-site or remote for 1-3 weeks depending on scope.

**Q6: What happens if you have exceptions in SOC 2?**
A: **Exceptions:** Controls not operating effectively. **Types:** (1) **Design deficiency:** Control poorly designed. (2) **Operating effectiveness:** Control designed well but not operating as intended. **Impact:** (1) **Qualified opinion:** Auditor issues opinion with exceptions noted. (2) **Adverse opinion:** Severe, widespread issues. **Response:** (1) Fix the issue. (2) Implement compensating controls if needed. (3) Document remediation. (4) Consider need for re-audit. **Prevention:** Strong internal testing before external audit.

**Q7: How do you maintain SOC 2 between audits?**
A: **Continuous compliance approach:** (1) **Evidence collection:** Automated where possible, manual where necessary throughout the year. (2) **Control testing:** Regular self-assessment of controls. (3) **Change management:** Evaluate impact of system/process changes on controls. (4) **Issue tracking:** Log and remediate issues immediately. (5) **Quarterly reviews:** Review compliance posture quarterly. **Benefits:** Faster next audit, lower cost, fewer surprises, continuous improvement.

**Q8: Can you fail a SOC 2 audit?**
A: **Yes, but rare:** Auditors issue opinions, not pass/fail. **Opinion types:** (1) **Unqualified:** Clean opinion - controls effective. (2) **Qualified:** Generally effective but exceptions noted. (3) **Adverse:** Controls not effective - essentially "failing." (4) **Disclaimer:** Auditor can't form opinion (insufficient evidence). **Failing equivalent:** Adverse opinion with significant exceptions. **Prevention:** Don't enter audit until ready. Do readiness assessment. Fix major issues before audit. **Cost of failing:** Customer loss, reputational damage, expensive re-audit.

**Q9: How do you scope a SOC 2 audit?**
A: **Scope decisions:** (1) **Systems:** Which systems/processes are in scope? (2) **Locations:** Which offices/data centers? (3) **Trust Services Criteria:** Which of the five TSC? (4) **Period:** Type I (point-in-time) or Type II (6-12 month period). **Principles:** (1) Include systems handling customer data. (2) Include critical business processes. (3) Don't scope too narrowly (loses credibility). (4) Don't scope too broadly (expensive, complex). **Documentation:** Scope documented in system description.

**Q10: What is the difference between SOC 1, SOC 2, and SOC 3?**
A: **SOC 1:** Focus on internal controls over financial reporting. For organizations impacting their clients' financial statements. **SOC 2:** Focus on security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy. For technology and cloud service providers. Most common for SaaS companies. **SOC 3:** General-use report based on SOC 2. No detailed description of systems or tests. For marketing purposes (can be shared publicly). **Which to choose:** SOC 2 Type II is standard for SaaS/cloud providers. SOC 1 if you impact client financials. SOC 3 as marketing add-on to SOC 2.

### Category 2: GDPR & Privacy Compliance (10 FAQs)

**Q11: What is GDPR and who does it apply to?**
A: **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation):** EU regulation on data protection and privacy. **Applies to:** (1) Organizations in EU processing personal data. (2) Organizations outside EU offering goods/services to EU residents or monitoring their behavior. **Scope:** "Personal data" - any information relating to identified or identifiable natural person. **Key principles:** Lawfulness/fairness/transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, accountability. **Penalties:** Up to €20M or 4% of global annual revenue (whichever higher).

**Q12: What are the lawful bases for processing under GDPR?**
A: **Six lawful bases (must have one):** (1) **Consent:** Individual has given clear consent. (2) **Contract:** Processing necessary for contract with individual. (3) **Legal obligation:** Processing necessary for legal compliance. (4) **Vital interests:** Processing necessary to protect someone's life. (5) **Public task:** Processing necessary for public interest. (6) **Legitimate interests:** Processing necessary for legitimate interests (balanced against individual rights). **Requirement:** Document your lawful basis. Different purposes may have different bases. Consent is not always required.

**Q13: What are data subject rights under GDPR?**
A: **Eight rights:** (1) **Right to be informed:** Privacy notices, transparency. (2) **Right of access:** Request copy of their data (Subject Access Request). (3) **Right to rectification:** Correct inaccurate data. (4) **Right to erasure:** "Right to be forgotten" - delete data in certain circumstances. (5) **Right to restrict processing:** Stop processing in certain circumstances. (6) **Right to data portability:** Receive data in structured format, transfer to another controller. (7) **Right to object:** Object to processing based on legitimate interests or direct marketing. (8) **Rights related to automated decision-making:** Including profiling. **Response time:** Generally 1 month.

**Q14: When is a Data Protection Officer (DPO) required?**
A: **Mandatory DPO if:** (1) You are a public authority (except courts). (2) Your core activities require large-scale systematic monitoring of individuals (e.g., online tracking). (3) Your core activities consist of large-scale processing of special categories of data (health, race, religion, biometrics) or data relating to criminal convictions. **Voluntary:** Organizations can appoint DPO even if not required (best practice). **Requirements:** Expert in data protection law and practices. Independent - cannot be told how to do their job. Report to highest management level. Can be internal or external.

**Q15: What is a data breach under GDPR and how do you respond?**
A: **Personal data breach:** Breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, personal data. **Notification requirements:** (1) **To supervisory authority:** Within 72 hours of becoming aware (if likely to result in risk to rights and freedoms). (2) **To data subjects:** Without undue delay if high risk. **Response process:** (1) Contain breach. (2) Assess risk to individuals. (3) Notify if required. (4) Document breach. (5) Remediate root cause. **Records:** Maintain breach register even if not notifiable.

**Q16: What is a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)?**
A: **DPIA:** Systematic assessment of privacy risks for high-risk processing. **Required when:** (1) Systematic and extensive profiling. (2) Large-scale use of special category data. (3) Systematic monitoring of publicly accessible places on large scale. (4) New technologies. **Contents:** (1) Description of processing. (2) Assessment of necessity/proportionality. (3) Risk assessment to rights and freedoms. (4) Measures to address risks. **Timing:** Before processing begins. **Who:** Can be done internally or with external expertise. Supervisory authorities provide lists of processing requiring DPIA.

**Q17: What are processor vs controller responsibilities under GDPR?**
A: **Controller:** Determines purposes and means of processing personal data. Primary responsibility for compliance. **Processor:** Processes personal data on behalf of controller. Must process only as instructed by controller. **Key point:** GDPR applies to both. **Processor obligations:** (1) Process only on documented instructions. (2) Ensure confidentiality. (3) Implement security measures. (4) Subprocessor governance. (5) Assist controller with data subject requests. (6) Assist with DPIAs. (7) Return/delete data at end of contract. (8) Provide information for audits. **Documentation:** Controller-processor contract must include these obligations.

**Q18: How do you handle international data transfers under GDPR?**
A: **Rule:** Personal data can only be transferred outside EEA if adequate protection or appropriate safeguards. **Mechanisms:** (1) **Adequacy decision:** Country deemed adequate by EU Commission (e.g., UK, selected countries). (2) **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs):** EU-approved contract clauses between sender and receiver. Most common mechanism. (3) **Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs):** For intra-group transfers. (4) **Certifications and codes of conduct.** (5) **Derogations:** Consent, contract performance, public interest, legal claims, vital interests (limited use). **Schrems II impact:** Additional assessment required for transfers to US (surveillance concerns).

**Q19: What is the difference between GDPR and CCPA/CPRA?**
A: **GDPR:** EU regulation. Broader scope. All personal data. Opt-in consent generally required. 72-hour breach notification. DPO required in some cases. **CCPA (California Consumer Privacy Act) / CPRA (California Privacy Rights Act):** US state law. "Businesses" meeting thresholds. Consumer personal information. Opt-out for sale/sharing. 72-hour breach notification if unencrypted. No DPO but requires privacy contact. **Key differences:** (1) Scope (GDPR broader). (2) Consent model (GDPR opt-in vs CCPA opt-out). (3) Rights differ slightly. (4) Enforcement (GDPR administrative fines vs CCPA statutory damages). Many companies need both.

**Q20: What records must you maintain under GDPR?**
A: **Article 30 records of processing:** (1) Name and contact details of controller (and DPO if applicable). (2) Purposes of processing. (3) Description of categories of data subjects and personal data. (4) Categories of recipients. (5) Transfers to third countries and documentation. (6) Envisaged time limits for erasure. (7) General description of security measures. **Additional records:** (1) Data breach register. (2) DPIAs. (3) Consent records. (4) Data subject request logs. (5) Processor agreements. **Format:** Written form, including electronic. Must be made available to supervisory authority on request.

### Category 3: ISO 27001 & Frameworks (10 FAQs)

**Q21: What is ISO 27001 and why implement it?**
A: **ISO 27001:** International standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). **Why implement:** (1) Certification demonstrates security commitment to customers. (2) Systematic approach to managing sensitive information. (3) Risk-based approach to security. (4) Often required for enterprise customers and government contracts. (5) Framework for continuous improvement. (6) Global recognition. **vs SOC 2:** ISO 27001 is a certifiable standard with specific requirements. SOC 2 is an attestation with flexible criteria. Many organizations do both. ISO 27001 is internationally focused; SOC 2 is US-focused.

**Q22: What are the key components of ISO 27001?**
A: **Clauses 4-10 (mandatory):** (4) Context of organization. (5) Leadership. (6) Planning. (7) Support. (8) Operation. (9) Performance evaluation. (10) Improvement. **Annex A controls:** 114 controls across 14 categories. **ISMS components:** (1) Scope. (2) Information security policy. (3) Risk assessment. (4) Risk treatment plan. (5) Statement of Applicability (which Annex A controls apply). (6) Risk assessment and treatment methodology. **Documentation:** Significant documentation required including policies, procedures, records.

**Q23: How long does ISO 27001 certification take?**
A: **Timeline:** (1) **Gap assessment and planning:** 1-2 months. (2) **ISMS implementation:** 3-6 months depending on maturity. (3) **Internal audit and management review:** 1 month. (4) **Stage 1 audit (documentation review):** 1-2 days. (5) **Stage 2 audit (implementation):** 2-5 days depending on scope. **Total:** 6-12 months from start to certificate. **Maintenance:** Surveillance audits annually, recertification every 3 years. **Accelerators:** Existing security program, dedicated resources, experienced consultant. **Cost:** $30-100K for certification plus ongoing costs.

**Q24: What is a Statement of Applicability (SoA) in ISO 27001?**
A: **SoA:** Central document identifying which Annex A controls are applicable and why. **Contents:** (1) List of all 114 Annex A controls. (2) Whether each control is applicable (Yes/No). (3) Justification for inclusion or exclusion. (4) Description of how applicable controls are implemented. **Key points:** Justification required for excluding controls. Implementation description must be accurate - auditors will test. SoA is reviewed and updated as needed. **Relationship to risk treatment:** Controls selected based on risk treatment plan address identified risks.

**Q25: What happens in ISO 27001 Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits?**
A: **Stage 1 (Documentation Review):** Auditors review ISMS documentation (policies, procedures, SoA). Verify standard requirements are addressed. Identify potential issues early. Usually 1-2 days on-site or remote. Output: Report with findings to address before Stage 2. May recommend postponing Stage 2 if major gaps. **Stage 2 (Implementation Audit):** Test implementation of ISMS. Interview staff, review records, observe processes. Test Annex A control implementation. Verify risk treatment effective. Usually 2-5 days depending on scope. Output: Audit findings (nonconformities, observations, opportunities for improvement). Decision: Recommend certification or not.

**Q26: What are common ISO 27001 nonconformities?**
A: **Major nonconformities (block certification):** (1) Critical security control not implemented. (2) Systematic failure of ISMS. (3) Required element of standard completely missing. **Minor nonconformities (don't block certification but must fix):** (1) Incomplete documentation. (2) Records missing or incomplete. (3) Procedure not consistently followed. (4) Control partially implemented. **Common areas:** (1) Risk assessment methodology. (2) Internal audit independence. (3) Management review inputs. (4) Control testing and records. (5) Document control. **Response:** Corrective action plan with timelines for all nonconformities.

**Q27: What is the NIST Cybersecurity Framework?**
A: **NIST CSF:** Risk-based approach to cybersecurity (not a certification standard). **Five functions:** (1) **Identify:** Asset management, risk assessment, governance. (2) **Protect:** Access control, awareness, data security, maintenance, protective technology. (3) **Detect:** Anomalies, continuous monitoring, detection processes. (4) **Respond:** Response planning, communications, analysis, mitigation, improvements. (5) **Recover:** Recovery planning, improvements, communications. **Profiles:** Current state vs target state. Used for gap analysis and improvement planning. **Implementation tiers:** Partial, Risk Informed, Repeatable, Adaptive. **Benefit:** Flexible, risk-based, improves communication, maps to other standards.

**Q28: What are CIS Controls and how do they relate to compliance?**
A: **CIS Controls:** 18 prioritized actions to protect organizations and data from known cyber attack vectors. Published by Center for Internet Security. **Implementation groups:** IG1 (basic cyber hygiene, minimum standard), IG2 (best practices for general enterprise), IG3 (defense against sophisticated attacks). **Relationship to compliance:** (1) Provides prescriptive "how" to meet requirements. (2) Maps to standards (NIST CSF, ISO 27001, PCI DSS). (3) Prioritized approach - do most important things first. **Use cases:** Foundation for security program, complement to compliance frameworks, practical implementation guide.

**Q29: How do compliance frameworks map to each other?**
A: **Common mappings:** (1) **ISO 27001 to SOC 2:** ISO 27001 Annex A maps to SOC 2 CC (Common Criteria). Having ISO 27001 helps with SOC 2. (2) **NIST CSF to ISO 27001:** NIST provides mapping showing which ISO controls address CSF functions. (3) **PCI DSS to ISO 27001:** PCI DSS requirements map to specific ISO 27001 controls. **Benefits:** (1) Avoid duplicate work. (2) Leverage existing controls. (3) Unified compliance approach. (4) Efficiency in audit preparation. **Tools:** GRC platforms provide automated mapping. Crosswalk spreadsheets available.

**Q30: What is the role of internal audit in compliance?**
A: **Internal audit function:** Independent assessment of controls and compliance. **ISO 27001 requirement:** Internal audit program required. **SOC 2:** Monitoring component requires self-assessment. **Benefits:** (1) Find issues before external auditors. (2) Continuous improvement. (3) Management insight. (4) Demonstrate control consciousness. **Independence:** Auditors should not audit their own work. Can be internal staff (independent of operations) or external. **Frequency:** Risk-based, typically annually for full ISMS, more frequently for high-risk areas. **Reporting:** To management, trigger for corrective action.

### Category 4: Audit Management & Risk (10 FAQs)

**Q31: How do you prepare for a compliance audit?**
A: **Preparation phases:** (1) **Planning (4-6 weeks before):** Confirm scope, timeline, resources. Notify stakeholders. (2) **Readiness assessment (2-4 weeks):** Self-assessment against requirements. Identify and remediate gaps. (3) **Evidence gathering (2 weeks):** Collect all required evidence. Organize by control/requirement. (4) **Logistics (1 week):** Prepare auditor access, facilities, schedule. **During audit:** Be responsive, organized, honest. Provide requested evidence promptly. Don't hide issues. Take notes on auditor questions and observations. **Post-audit:** Address findings promptly. Implement corrective actions. Update processes.

**Q32: What makes good audit evidence?**
A: **Characteristics:** (1) **Relevant:** Directly relates to control/requirement being tested. (2) **Reliable:** From authoritative source, accurate. (3) **Sufficient:** Enough to support conclusion. (4) **Timely:** From appropriate time period. **Types:** (1) **Documentary:** Policies, procedures, system configs, screenshots. (2) **Electronic:** Logs, records, database extracts. (3) **Testimonial:** Interviews, confirmations. (4) **Physical:** Observations, inspections. **Organization:** Label clearly with control ID, date, description. Chain of custody if needed. **Retention:** Store securely per retention policy.

**Q33: How do you manage audit findings?**
A: **Finding severity:** (1) **Critical:** Immediate risk, may affect certification/attestation. (2) **High:** Significant risk, requires prompt action. (3) **Medium:** Moderate risk, address within reasonable timeframe. (4) **Low/Low risk:** Minor issue, good practice recommendation. **Management process:** (1) Acknowledge finding. (2) Root cause analysis. (3) Develop corrective action plan (CAP). (4) Implement fixes. (5) Test effectiveness. (6) Provide evidence of remediation. (7) Close with auditor. **Tracking:** Register of all findings, owners, target dates, status. Regular review meetings. **Communication:** Escalate delays or issues.

**Q34: What is the difference between internal and external audits?**
A: **External audits:** (1) **Purpose:** Independent verification for customers/regulators. (2) **Who:** Third-party auditors, certification bodies. (3) **Outcome:** Certification, attestation report. (4) **Scope:** Typically compliance-focused. **Internal audits:** (1) **Purpose:** Self-assessment, continuous improvement. (2) **Who:** Internal staff or contracted consultants. (3) **Outcome:** Internal report, action items. (4) **Scope:** Broader, can include operational effectiveness. **Relationship:** Internal audits prepare for external audits. Findings from internal should be fixed before external. External validates internal work.

**Q35: How do you handle multiple overlapping audits?**
A: **Challenges:** Duplicative requests, resource strain, auditor fatigue. **Strategies:** (1) **Integrated audit approach:** Single audit covering multiple frameworks where possible. Some firms offer combined SOC 2 + ISO 27001 audits. (2) **Evidence reuse:** Maintain evidence library organized by control mapping. Same evidence satisfies multiple requirements. (3) **Scheduling:** Coordinate timing to minimize business disruption. (4) **Single point of contact:** Audit coordinator manages all auditor requests. **Tools:** GRC platforms support multiple framework management and evidence sharing.

**Q36: What is continuous compliance monitoring?**
A: **Concept:** Ongoing automated assessment of compliance posture rather than point-in-time preparation. **Components:** (1) **Automated control testing:** Technical controls tested automatically. (2) **Continuous evidence collection:** Systems feed evidence to repository automatically. (3) **Real-time dashboards:** Compliance posture visible at any time. (4) **Alerting:** Notification when controls fail or drift. **Benefits:** (1) Reduced audit preparation time. (2) Faster issue detection and remediation. (3) Lower compliance costs long-term. (4) Better risk management. **Implementation:** GRC platforms, cloud security posture management, automated testing tools.

**Q37: How do you conduct a compliance risk assessment?**
A: **Process:** (1) **Identify compliance obligations:** Regulations, contracts, standards applicable to organization. (2) **Identify compliance risks:** What could cause non-compliance? (3) **Assess likelihood and impact:** Probability of occurrence and severity of consequence (fines, business impact). (4) **Evaluate risk:** Determine risk level (critical, high, medium, low). (5) **Treat risks:** Accept, mitigate, transfer, avoid. (6) **Monitor:** Track risk indicators. **Inputs:** Regulatory changes, audit findings, incident history, control effectiveness. **Output:** Compliance risk register with treatment plans. **Frequency:** Annual comprehensive, continuous monitoring for changes.

**Q38: What is the role of compliance in vendor management?**
A: **Vendor compliance:** Assessing and managing compliance obligations that extend to third parties. **Key areas:** (1) **Data protection:** Vendors processing personal data must meet GDPR/privacy requirements. Processor agreements required. (2) **Security standards:** Vendors should meet security requirements (SOC 2, ISO 27001). (3) **Industry requirements:** Industry-specific compliance (PCI DSS for payment processors). **Assessment:** (1) Due diligence before engagement. (2) Contractual requirements. (3) Evidence collection (certificates, reports). (4) Ongoing monitoring. **Risk-based:** Higher scrutiny for critical, high-risk vendors.

**Q39: How do you handle regulatory changes?**
A: **Monitoring:** (1) Subscribe to regulatory updates (newsletters, government notifications). (2) Industry associations. (3) Legal/compliance networks. (4) Regulatory intelligence services. **Assessment:** (1) Does the change apply to us? (2) What is the effective date? (3) What operational changes required? (4) What is the effort to comply? **Response:** (1) Impact assessment. (2) Implementation plan. (3) Resource allocation. (4) Change management. (5) Verification of compliance. **Documentation:** Maintain register of regulatory changes and response.

**Q40: What should be in a compliance report to the board?**
A: **Content:** (1) **Executive summary:** Key takeaways, significant issues. (2) **Compliance posture:** Status by framework/regulation (green/yellow/red). (3) **Audit results:** Recent audit outcomes, findings, remediation status. (4) **Key risk indicators:** Metrics showing compliance health. (5) **Regulatory changes:** New or changing requirements. (6) **Significant incidents:** Compliance-related incidents or breaches. (7) **Investment needs:** Resources required for compliance. **Format:** Visual, concise, business-focused. Avoid technical details. **Cadence:** Quarterly typically, more frequently if issues. **Tone:** Transparent about risks and issues.

# SECTION 5: STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES

## SOP 1: Annual SOC 2 Audit Preparation

**Duration:** 6 weeks | **Frequency:** Annual | **Owner:** Compliance Manager

- Notify stakeholders and schedule audit window
- Conduct readiness assessment against TSC
- Gather and organize evidence for 12-month period
- Review and update system description
- Prepare auditor access and logistics
- Support fieldwork and respond to requests
- Receive draft report and address any comments
- Distribute final report and update customers

## SOP 2: GDPR Data Subject Request Handling

**Duration:** 30 days max | **Trigger:** Data subject request received | **Owner:** Compliance Team

- Log request in tracking system
- Verify requester identity
- Determine scope and applicable rights
- Collect responsive data across systems
- Review for third-party data and exemptions
- Prepare response package
- Deliver to requester within SLA
- Document completion

## SOP 3: Compliance Gap Assessment

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks | **Frequency:** Quarterly or trigger-based | **Owner:** Compliance Analyst

- Define scope (framework, systems, locations)
- Review current state documentation
- Compare to framework requirements
- Identify gaps and deficiencies
- Prioritize by risk and effort
- Develop remediation roadmap
- Report to leadership
- Track remediation to closure

## SOP 4: Policy Review and Update

**Duration:** 2-3 weeks | **Frequency:** Annual minimum | **Owner:** Compliance Manager

- Inventory all policies with review dates
- Assess against current regulations and threats
- Draft updates with stakeholder input
- Legal review for regulatory accuracy
- Management approval
- Publish and communicate
- Train affected personnel
- Archive previous versions

## SOP 5: Breach Notification Assessment

**Duration:** 24-72 hours | **Trigger:** Potential data breach | **Owner:** Compliance Lead

- Gather facts about the incident
- Determine if personal data involved
- Assess risk to individuals
- Determine notification obligations
- Prepare supervisory authority notification if required
- Prepare data subject notification if high risk
- File notifications within deadlines
- Document decision rationale

# SECTION 4: 40+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Forty+ Frequently Asked Questions

### Category 1: Engineering Leadership (10 FAQs)

**Q1: What's the difference between a tech lead and an engineering manager?**
A: **Tech Lead:** Focuses on technical direction, architecture, and code quality. Individual contributor track. Deep technical expertise required. **Engineering Manager:** Focuses on people, process, and delivery. Management track. Technical competence required but spends time on 1:1s, hiring, team dynamics. Both roles essential; great engineers should have both tracks available.

**Q2: How do you handle an underperforming engineer?**
A: **Process:** (1) Clarify expectations - ensure they understand what's expected. (2) Identify root cause - skills gap? motivation? personal issues? misalignment? (3) Create improvement plan - specific, measurable, time-bound goals. (4) Provide support - mentorship, training, resources. (5) Regular check-ins - weekly for 30-60-90 day plan. (6) Document everything - performance conversations, improvements. (7) Decision point - improvement or performance action. **Timeline:** 60-90 days for improvement. **Escalation:** HR involvement if performance action needed.

**Q3: How do you scale engineering teams from 10 to 100+ engineers?**
A: **Stages:** (10-30) Flat structure, generalists, direct communication. (30-50) First managers, team specialization, process emergence. (50-100) Management layer, clear teams, defined processes, architecture review. (100+) Directors, multiple layers, standardized practices, platform teams. **Keys:** (1) Hire managers early enough (2) Standardize when painful (3) Keep communication channels open (4) Invest in onboarding (5) Maintain culture intentionally.

**Q4: How do you prevent burnout in engineering teams?**
A: **Warning signs:** Decreased velocity, increased bugs, irritability, attrition, cynicism. **Prevention:** (1) Sustainable pace - no death marches (2) Autonomy - engineers control how they work (3) Purpose - clear connection to impact (4) Learning - growth opportunities (5) Recognition - celebrate wins (6) Workload management - realistic expectations (7) Time off - actually take vacations. **Response:** When burnout appears, address immediately - reduce scope, add resources, mandatory time off.

**Q5: What's your approach to 1:1s with engineers?**
A: **Frequency:** Weekly 30 minutes for direct reports. **Format:** Employee-driven agenda (not status updates). **Topics:** Career growth, blockers, feedback, ideas, team dynamics, personal well-being. **Questions:** "What's energizing you?" "What's blocking you?" "What feedback do you have for me?" **Action:** Document takeaways, follow up on commitments. **Skip-level:** Monthly with skip-levels to stay connected.

**Q6: How do you build a diverse engineering team?**
Diverse slates:** Ensure candidate pool is diverse. **Diverse interview panels:** Mix of perspectives in interviews. **Inclusive job descriptions:** Remove biased language. **Structured interviews:** Same questions, criteria for all candidates. **Partnerships:** Work with diverse professional organizations. **Culture:** Inclusive environment where diverse talent thrives. **Metrics:** Track representation at all levels. **Accountability:\*\* Hiring manager owns diversity outcomes.

**Q7: When should you promote an engineer to senior or staff level?**
A: **Senior Engineer:** Technical excellence + impact. Ships complex features independently. Mentors others. Systematic problem-solving. **Staff/Principal:** Org-wide impact. Technical leadership across teams. Architecture decisions. Mentors senior engineers. Influence without authority. **Promotion criteria:** (1) Consistent demonstration at next level (2) Business need at that level (3) Impact beyond current scope. **Process:** Calibration, not automatic with tenure.

**Q8: How do you handle technical disagreements between senior engineers?**
A: **Process:** (1) Understand both perspectives - ask questions, listen (2) Evaluate against criteria - scalability, maintainability, team capability, timeline (3) Seek data - benchmarks, prototypes, prior art (4) Consider tradeoffs - no perfect solution (5) Make decision - timely, clear, documented (6) Commit - once decided, full commitment (7) Review - retrospective on decision quality. **Escalation:** If can't reach agreement, VP Eng decides. **Culture:** Healthy disagreement is good; personal attacks are not.

**Q9: What's your philosophy on code ownership?**
A: **Collective ownership:** Team owns the codebase, not individuals. **Clear responsibility:** Specific owners for critical systems (on-call, maintenance). **Rotation:** Rotate ownership to spread knowledge. **Documentation:** Every system has documented owner and runbook. **Review:** Anyone can review any code. **Quality bar:** Consistent standards regardless of who wrote it. **Onboarding:** New engineers can modify any code with proper review.

**Q10: How do you measure engineering productivity?**
A: **DANGER:** Lines of code, commits, hours worked - these are bad metrics. **Good metrics:** (1) Business outcomes - features shipped, value delivered (2) Cycle time - idea to production (3) Deployment frequency - how often we ship (4) Change failure rate - quality of releases (5) Recovery time - resilience (6) Employee engagement - sustainable pace. **Process:** Regular retrospectives on what's slowing team down. **Focus:** System-level improvements, not individual performance.

### Category 2: Technical Strategy & Architecture (10 FAQs)

**Q11: How do you choose between monolith and microservices?**
A: **Start with monolith:** When team <30, product-market fit uncertain, rapid iteration needed. **Move to microservices:** When teams >30 and need independent deployment, different scaling needs, clear service boundaries. **Hybrid:** Most successful architectures are modular monoliths that can be decomposed. **Tradeoffs:** Monolith = simpler, faster initially. Microservices = scalable, independent but complex. **Decision:** Optimize for team size and product maturity, not hype.

**Q12: How do you balance technical debt vs. new features?**
A: **Framework:** (1) Measure debt - what debt exists, what's the impact? (2) Categorize - critical (blocks progress), important (slows progress), minor (annoyance). (3) Budget - allocate 20-30% of capacity to debt paydown. (4) Plan - debt paydown in each sprint. (5) Prevent - code review, testing, documentation prevent new debt. **Communication:** Explain debt impact in business terms (velocity, risk). **Culture:** Zero-debt is unrealistic; managed debt is healthy.

**Q13: What's your approach to technology choices (languages, frameworks)?**
A: **Criteria:** (1) Team expertise - can we hire/maintain? (2) Ecosystem maturity - libraries, community, support (3) Performance characteristics - meets requirements (4) Vendor risk - lock-in, stability (5) Learning curve - team can be productive quickly. **Process:** RFC (Request for Comments) for significant changes. **Bias:** Default to proven technologies; new tech requires strong justification. **Diversity:** Minimize tech stack complexity; consolidate where possible.

**Q14: How do you ensure system scalability?**
A: **Design principles:** Stateless services, horizontal scaling, database sharding strategy, caching layers, async processing, load balancing. **Planning:** Capacity planning based on growth projections. Load testing before major launches. **Monitoring:** Track key metrics (response time, throughput, error rates, resource utilization). **Runbooks:** Documented scaling procedures. **Testing:** Chaos engineering to validate resilience.

**Q15: How do you approach security in engineering?**
A: **Shift-left:** Security in design, not bolted on. **Training:** All engineers security-aware. **Tools:** Static analysis, dependency scanning, secrets management. **Process:** Security review for sensitive features. Bug bounty program. **Compliance:** SOC 2, penetration testing. **Response:** Incident response plan for security issues. **Culture:** Security is everyone's responsibility, not just security team.

**Q16: How do you handle legacy code and systems?**
A: **Assessment:** What's working? What's painful? What's the risk? **Strategy options:** (1) Strangle - gradually replace components (2) Encapsulate - API layer around legacy (3) Refactor - improve in place (4) Rewrite - full replacement (highest risk). **Investment:** Allocate capacity for modernization. **Documentation:** Document legacy systems even if imperfect. **Migration:** Clear migration path with rollback capability.

**Q17: What's your approach to API design?**
A: **REST principles:** Resource-based, stateless, cacheable, consistent naming. **Versioning:** URL or header versioning strategy. **Documentation:** OpenAPI/Swagger specs, always up-to-date. **Standards:** Consistent error handling, pagination, filtering. **Testing:** Contract tests between services. **Evolution:** Deprecation strategy for breaking changes. **Review:** API review process for external/public APIs.

**Q18: How do you manage data and database architecture?**
A: **Data modeling:** Clear schema design, normalization where appropriate. **Scaling:** Read replicas, sharding strategy, caching. **Migration:** Versioned migrations, backward compatibility. **Backup:** Regular backups, tested restore procedures. **Privacy:** Data classification, retention policies, GDPR/CCPA compliance. **Observability:** Query performance monitoring, slow query optimization.

**Q19: How do you approach testing strategy?**
A: **Test pyramid:** Many unit tests (fast, isolated), integration tests (service boundaries), fewer e2e tests (slow but comprehensive). **Automation:** CI/CD runs all tests. **Coverage:** 80%+ for critical paths, not arbitrary 100%. **TDD:** Encouraged but not mandated. **Quality gates:** Tests must pass before merge. **Flaky tests:** Fix or remove; don't tolerate instability. **Environments:** Production-like test environments.

**Q20: How do you manage infrastructure and cloud strategy?**
A: **Cloud-first:** Default to managed services vs. building. **Multi-cloud:** Avoid unless specific requirements; adds complexity. **Infrastructure as Code:** Terraform/CloudFormation for all resources. **Cost optimization:** Right-sizing, reserved instances, spot instances. **Security:** Network segmentation, IAM policies, encryption. **Monitoring:** Comprehensive observability stack.

### Category 3: Process & Delivery (10 FAQs)

**Q21: What's your preferred development methodology?**
A: **Agile principles:** Iterative, incremental, respond to change. **Scrum:** 2-week sprints, daily standups, demos, retrospectives. **Kanban:** For maintenance, support, continuous flow. **Hybrid:** Many teams use both - sprints for features, kanban for bugs/ops. **Key:** Regular delivery, feedback loops, continuous improvement. **Anti-patterns:** Cargo cult agile (ceremonies without outcomes), rigid adherence over results.

**Q22: How do you run effective sprint planning?**
A: **Preparation:** Refined backlog, clear priorities, estimated stories. **Format:** (1) Review goals and capacity (2) Discuss each story - what, why, acceptance criteria (3) Team commits to sprint backlog (4) Identify dependencies and risks. **Output:** Committed sprint backlog, sprint goal. **Timebox:** 2 hours for 2-week sprint. **Follow-up:** Stories ready for development, blockers identified.

**Q23: How do you handle production incidents?**
A: **Severity levels:** P0 (site down), P1 (major impact), P2 (degraded), P3 (minor). **Response:** (1) Detect - monitoring alerts (2) Respond - on-call engineer acknowledges (3) Mitigate - stop the bleeding (4) Resolve - fix root cause (5) Post-mortem - learn within 48 hours. **Communication:** Status page updates for customer-facing issues. **Blameless:** Focus on system fixes, not blame. **Prevention:** Action items from post-mortems prioritized.

**Q24: How do you manage technical roadmaps?**
A: **Horizons:** (1) Now - current quarter, committed (2) Next - following quarter, likely (3) Later - beyond, speculative. **Inputs:** Business strategy, technical debt, innovation opportunities, team input. **Process:** Quarterly roadmap planning with leadership. **Communication:** Share roadmap with team and stakeholders. **Flexibility:** Roadmaps change; communicate changes. **Balance:** Features, debt, infrastructure, innovation.

**Q25: How do you run effective code reviews?**
A: **Purpose:** Code quality, knowledge sharing, team standards. **Process:** (1) Author prepares - tests pass, self-review (2) Reviewer checks - logic, tests, style, security (3) Discussion - constructive feedback (4) Approval - LGTM when ready. **Timeliness:** Review within 24 hours. **Tools:** GitHub/GitLab PRs, automated checks. **Culture:** Constructive feedback, learning opportunity, not gatekeeping.

**Q26: How do you manage cross-functional projects with Product and Design?**
A: **Shared ownership:** Joint accountability for outcomes. **Process:** Discovery → Design → Development → Launch together. **Communication:** Regular sync meetings, shared Slack channels. **Artifacts:** Shared roadmap, specs, designs. **Tradeoffs:** Joint decisions on scope, quality, timeline. **Respect:** Engineers' input on feasibility, Product's on priority, Design's on UX. **Conflict:** Escalate through leadership if stuck.

**Q27: How do you handle scope changes mid-sprint?**
A: **Default:** Protect sprint commitment; changes go to next sprint. **Exceptions:** Critical business need or production issue. **Process:** Evaluate impact with team, adjust scope or timeline. **Communication:** Notify stakeholders of change. **Tracking:** Document why sprint changed (retrospective input). **Balance:** Flexibility for business vs. team predictability.

**Q28: How do you approach CI/CD and DevOps?**
A: **CI:** All code tested on every commit. Fast feedback (<10 min). **CD:** Automated deployment to staging. Production deployment gated but push-button. **Environments:** Development, staging, production parity. **Feature flags:** Deploy code, release features separately. **Rollback:** One-click rollback capability. **Monitoring:** Deployments monitored for errors. **Culture:** You build it, you run it (DevOps philosophy).

**Q29: How do you measure and improve engineering velocity?**
A: **Metrics:** Story points completed (trend, not absolute), cycle time, deployment frequency, PR merge time. **Retrospectives:** Regular team retrospectives on what's slowing us down. **Blockers:** Track and systematically remove blockers. **Developer experience:** Invest in tooling, faster builds, better local dev. **Caution:** Velocity is output metric; focus on outcomes and sustainability.

**Q30: How do you balance innovation vs. maintenance work?**
A: **Rule of thumb:** 70% features, 20% debt/infrastructure, 10% innovation/experimentation. **Innovation time:** Hackathons, 20% time, innovation sprints. **Maintenance:** Rotating maintenance duty, on-call. **Balance:** Adjust based on product stage (startup = more features, mature = more maintenance). **Communication:** Make tradeoffs visible to stakeholders.

### Category 4: Team Building & Culture (10 FAQs)

**Q31: How do you hire great engineers?**
A: **Sourcing:** Referrals, networks, events, sourcing. **Process:** Phone screen → Technical interview (coding/system design) → Culture/values interview → Final interview. **Bar:** Maintain high bar; slow to hire. **Panel:** Diverse interviewers. **Speed:** Move fast for great candidates. **Selling:** Sell opportunity, team, culture. **Diversity:** Intentional about diverse slates.

**Q32: How do you onboard new engineers effectively?**
A: **Pre-boarding:** Welcome email, equipment shipped, access ready. **Week 1:** Orientation, codebase walkthrough, first commit. **Month 1:** Buddy assignment, first project, team integration. **Quarter 1:** Increasing responsibility, feedback sessions. **Engineering specific:** Codebase architecture, development workflow, on-call shadowing. **Check-ins:** 30/60/90 day check-ins. **Success metric:** Time to first productive contribution.

**Q33: How do you create a learning culture?**
A: **Time:** Dedicated learning time (20% time, learning days). **Resources:** Learning budget, conference attendance, books/courses. **Sharing:** Tech talks, lunch & learns, documentation. **Psychological safety:** Safe to ask questions, admit mistakes. **Growth mindset:** "Not yet" vs. "can't." **Mentorship:** Formal and informal mentoring. **Career paths:** Clear growth tracks (IC and management).

**Q34: How do you handle remote/hybrid engineering teams?**
A: **Communication:** Async-first, written documentation. **Meetings:** Video on, inclusive facilitation. **Collaboration:** Pair programming tools, shared environments. **Social:** Virtual social events, in-person offsites. **Documentation:** More critical when remote. **Time zones:** Overlap hours for synchronous work. **Equity:** Remote engineers have equal opportunity and impact.

**Q35: How do you run effective engineering retrospectives?**
A: **Frequency:** Every sprint (bi-weekly). **Format:** What went well? What didn't? What will we change? **Safety:** Anonymous input option, blameless culture. **Action items:** Specific, assigned, tracked. **Follow-through:** Review previous actions. **Variations:** Start/stop/continue, 4Ls, sailboat. **Remote:** Miro/FigJam for collaboration.

**Q36: How do you recognize and reward engineers?**
A: **Compensation:** Competitive base, equity, bonuses. **Promotions:** Level when consistently performing at next level. **Recognition:** Public kudos, spot bonuses, shout-outs. **Impact:** Engineers see connection between work and business outcomes. **Growth:** Challenging projects, learning opportunities. **Autonomy:** Trust and ownership are rewards. **Balance:** Intrinsic motivation (purpose, mastery, autonomy) > extrinsic.

**Q37: How do you handle conflict between engineers?**
A: **Early:** Address quickly before festering. **Listen:** Understand both perspectives. **Common ground:** Find shared goals. **Mediate:** Facilitate discussion if needed. **Decision:** If needed, make and communicate decision. **Follow-up:** Check in after resolution. **Escalation:** HR if personal/conduct issues. **Prevention:** Clear expectations, regular 1:1s, team building.

**Q38: How do you build trust with your engineering team?**
A: **Consistency:** Do what you say. **Transparency:** Share context, explain decisions. **Admit mistakes:** Model vulnerability. **Support:** Have their back with leadership. **Empower:** Trust them with autonomy. **Listen:** Actively seek and act on feedback. **Fairness:** Consistent standards, equal opportunity. **Technical credibility:** Understand their work (doesn't mean coding).

**Q39: How do you communicate with non-technical stakeholders?**
A: **Translation:** Technical concepts in business terms. **Focus:** Outcomes, not implementation details. **Visuals:** Diagrams > words. **Options:** Present tradeoffs clearly. **Risk:** Explain technical risk in business impact. **Questions:** Welcome and answer without jargon. **Regular cadence:** Proactive communication, not just when problems arise.

**Q40: How do you stay current with technology trends?**
A: **Reading:** Tech blogs, newsletters, papers. **Community:** Meetups, conferences, online communities. **Experimentation:** Hackathons, side projects, POCs. **Network:** Peer connections at other companies. **Team:** Learn from engineers; they often know latest. **Balance:** Don't chase every trend; evaluate pragmatically. **Apply:** Bring relevant learnings to improve team's work.

- Daily: Standups continue
- Mid-week: Demo preparation
- End: Sprint demo + retrospective (2 hours)

**Outputs:**

- Completed stories
- Demo to stakeholders
- Improvement action items

## SOP 3: Technical Architecture Review

**Duration:** 1-2 hours | **Frequency:** Weekly + as-needed | **Owner:** VP Engineering + Architects

- Review significant architecture changes (RFCs)
- Assess scalability and performance implications
- Evaluate security considerations
- Ensure alignment with technical strategy
- Document decisions and rationale
- Communicate decisions to affected teams
- Track implementation of approved changes

## SOP 4: Production Incident Response

**Duration:** Variable | **Trigger:** Production alert | **Owner:** On-call Engineer + VP Engineering (for significant incidents)

**Detection:**

- Automated monitoring alerts
- Customer reports

**Response (P0/P1):**

- Acknowledge within 15 minutes
- Assess severity and impact
- Form incident response team
- Communicate status internally
- Work toward mitigation (not necessarily fix)
- Document timeline and actions

**Resolution:**

- Restore normal service
- Verify stability
- Communicate all-clear

**Post-Incident:**

- Post-mortem within 48 hours
- Identify root cause(s)
- Define action items
- Track to completion
- Share learnings broadly

## SOP 5: Engineering Hiring Process

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks | **Trigger:** Approved requisition | **Owner:** Recruiting + Hiring Manager + VP Engineering

- **Intake:** Define role, requirements, interview team
- **Sourcing:** Post job, source candidates, referrals
- **Screen:** Recruiter phone screen (30 min)
- **Technical:** Coding interview (45-60 min)
- **System Design:** Architecture discussion (45 min, senior+)
- **Culture/Values:** Behavioral interview (45 min)
- **Final:** VP Engineering or designee (30 min)
- **Decision:** Debrief within 24 hours
- **Offer:** Prepared and extended within 48 hours
- **Close:** Negotiate, accept, set start date

# SECTION 11: PITFALLS & RISK MITIGATION

## 11.1 Common Engineering Leadership Pitfalls

| Pitfall                            | Risk                       | Mitigation                                              |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Becoming too distant from code** | Lose technical credibility | Regular architecture reviews, stay hands-on with design |
| **Micromanaging**                  | Team disempowerment        | Trust but verify, focus on outcomes                     |
| **Ignoring tech debt**             | Velocity death spiral      | Allocate capacity, track metrics                        |
| **Hiring for speed over quality**  | Team performance issues    | Maintain bar, slow to hire                              |
| **Under-communicating**            | Misalignment, surprises    | Regular updates, transparency                           |
| **Not developing managers**        | Leadership gap             | Invest in EM coaching                                   |
| **Reactive vs. proactive**         | Always firefighting        | Strategic planning, predictive metrics                  |

## 11.2 Risk Categories & Mitigation

| Risk                         | Mitigation                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Key person dependency**    | Documentation, knowledge sharing, bus factor >1       |
| **Vendor lock-in**           | Abstraction layers, multi-cloud strategy              |
| **Security vulnerabilities** | Security review, automated scanning, bug bounty       |
| **Scalability limits**       | Load testing, capacity planning, architecture reviews |
| **Team burnout**             | Sustainable pace, workload management, time off       |
| **Attrition**                | Engagement, growth, compensation, culture             |

# SECTION 4: FAQs (20+)

**Q1: What makes a good job description?**
A: Clear title, compelling opening, key responsibilities, requirements (must vs. nice), company culture, growth opportunities, inclusive language.

**Q2: How do you source passive candidates?**
A: LinkedIn, boolean search, talent communities, networking, referrals, events, alumni networks, content marketing.

**Q3: What is structured interviewing?**
A: Same questions for all candidates, scored rubric, reduces bias, better predictions, legally defensible.

**Q4: How do you reduce time to fill?**
A: Proactive pipeline, streamlined process, calendar prioritization, fast feedback, competitive offers, clear requirements.

**Q5: What is culture add vs. culture fit?**
A: Fit = similarity (problematic). Add = diversity that strengthens culture. Hire for values + unique perspective.

**Q6: How do you assess culture fit/add?**
A: Values-based questions, team interaction, scenario discussions, "what would you add" questions.

**Q7: What makes a good phone screen?**
A: 30 min max, verify basics, assess motivation, communication check, sell opportunity, clear next steps.

**Q8: How do you handle salary negotiations?**
A: Know market data, understand candidate needs, get approval range, total compensation focus, creative if needed.

**Q9: What is employer branding?**
A: Company's reputation as employer. EVP (Employee Value Proposition), careers site, social, Glassdoor, employee advocacy.

**Q10: How do you improve diversity hiring?**
A: Diverse sourcing, blind resume review, diverse interview panels, structured interviews, inclusive job descriptions, partnerships.

**Q11: What are red flags in candidates?**
A: Job hopping without growth, can't explain transitions, badmouths previous employers, inconsistent answers, unprepared.

**Q12: How do you decline candidates gracefully?**
A: Timely, appreciative, honest but kind, specific if possible, keep in pipeline for future, open to feedback.

**Q13: What is a recruitment funnel?**
A: Sourced → Applied → Screened → Interviewed → Offered → Hired. Track conversion at each stage.

**Q14: How do you work with hiring managers?**
A: Intake meeting, regular updates, push back on unrealistic requirements, educate on market, partnership not order-taking.

**Q15: What is referral recruiting?**
A: Employees refer candidates. Highest quality source, fastest hires, best retention. Incentivize, make easy.

**Q16: How do you handle counter offers?**
A: Address early, understand true motivation, prepare for discussion, highlight non-monetary value, accept some loss.

**Q17: What is candidate experience?**
A: Every interaction from first touch to onboarding (or rejection). Measured: NPS, feedback, reviews.

**Q18: How do you recruit for hard-to-fill roles?**
A: Deep sourcing, broaden criteria, remote options, contract-to-hire, referrals, events, executive search.

**Q19: What is recruitment marketing?**
A: Marketing principles applied to recruiting. Content, social, brand, nurture campaigns, talent communities.

**Q20: How do you measure recruiting success?**
A: Time to fill, cost per hire, quality of hire, source effectiveness, offer acceptance, diversity metrics, candidate NPS.

# SECTION 5: SOPs

## SOP 1: Full-Cycle Recruiting

1. Intake meeting with hiring manager
2. Create job description
3. Post and source
4. Screen resumes
5. Phone screens
6. Coordinate interviews
7. Gather feedback
8. Check references
9. Extend offer
10. Close candidate
11. Handoff to onboarding

## SOP 2: Sourcing Campaign

1. Define search criteria
2. Build boolean strings
3. Search platforms (LinkedIn, etc.)
4. Review profiles
5. Prioritize outreach list
6. Personalize messages
7. Send outreach
8. Track responses
9. Nurture non-responsive
10. Convert to applicants

## SOP 3: Interview Coordination

1. Confirm schedule with panel
2. Send candidate calendar invite
3. Send prep materials
4. Book rooms/video links
5. Send reminders
6. Day-of coordination
7. Collect feedback promptly
8. Debrief scheduling

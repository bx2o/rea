# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY (20+)

**Q1: What is the difference between traditional banking and fintech?**
A: **Traditional:** Established institutions, physical presence, legacy tech, regulatory heavy. **Fintech:** Technology-first, digital-native, agile, often unbundled services, partnership-dependent for banking licenses.

**Q2: How do payment rails work (ACH vs Wire vs RTP)?**
A: **ACH:** Batch, 1-3 days, low cost. **Wire:** Real-time, expensive, irrevocable. **RTP:** Instant, 24/7, moderate cost. Choose based on speed needs, cost, and amount.

**Q3: What is embedded finance?**
A: Financial services integrated into non-financial products. Examples: Buy-now-pay-later in e-commerce, payments in ride-sharing, insurance in travel booking. Requires banking-as-a-service (BaaS) partnerships.

**Q4: How do you evaluate a fintech partnership?**
A: Due diligence on: Technology stack, security, compliance, financial health, culture fit, customer base overlap, regulatory standing, integration complexity, revenue potential.

**Q5: What is open banking (PSD2)?**
A: Regulatory framework requiring banks to open customer data (with consent) via APIs. Enables: Account aggregation, payment initiation, price comparison. Drives competition and innovation.

**Q6: How do real-time payments (RTP) differ from traditional?**
A: **RTP:** 24/7 availability, immediate settlement, irrevocable, confirmation in seconds. **Traditional:** Business hours, batch processing, reversible, 1-3 days. Use cases: Emergency transfers, gig economy.

**Q7: What are the key banking regulations?**
A: **US:** Dodd-Frank, Basel III, FDIC, OCC. **EU:** PSD2, GDPR, MIFID. **Global:** FATF (AML), PCI DSS (card security). Compliance is non-negotiable.

**Q8: How do you prevent payment fraud?**
A: Multi-layer approach: Identity verification (KYC), device fingerprinting, behavioral analytics, transaction monitoring, velocity checks, 3DS authentication, machine learning models.

**Q9: What is banking-as-a-service (BaaS)?**
A: Licensed banks providing banking infrastructure to fintechs via APIs. Fintech gets: Accounts, cards, compliance, rails. Bank gets: Deposits, fees, distribution. Examples: Galileo, Marqeta, Treasury Prime.

**Q10: How do cross-border payments work?**
A: Correspondent banking network, SWIFT messaging, FX conversion, local rails. Challenges: Cost, speed, transparency. Innovations: Blockchain, local payment methods, fintech aggregators.

**Q11: What is the future of payments?**
A: Trends: Real-time everywhere, invisible payments (IoT), biometric authentication, crypto integration, embedded finance, super apps, reduced friction, enhanced security.

**Q12: How do you monetize payments?**
A: Models: Interchange fees, subscription fees, transaction fees, FX spreads, float on held funds, premium services, data insights (anonymized), referral fees.

**Q13: What is treasury management for fintechs?**
A: Managing company cash: Liquidity optimization, investment strategy, risk management, banking relationships, forecasting, compliance reporting. Critical for fintech sustainability.

**Q14: How do ACH returns work?**
A: Returned transactions due to: Insufficient funds, closed account, invalid account. Timeframes: R01-R77 codes, up to 60 days. Impact: Cash flow, reconciliation, customer experience.

**Q15: What is the difference between acquirer and issuer?**
A: **Acquirer:** Bank that processes merchant transactions. **Issuer:** Bank that issued customer's card. Four-party model: Cardholder → Issuer → Network → Acquirer → Merchant.

**Q16: How do you build a neobank?**
A: Options: Banking license (capital intensive), BaaS partnership (faster), white-label solution. Key: Compliance from day one, modern tech stack, customer-centric design, capital reserves.

**Q17: What are payment APIs and why use them?**
A: REST/GraphQL interfaces for payment functionality. Benefits: Faster integration, standardized, scalable, reduced compliance burden (if through BaaS). Examples: Stripe, Adyen, Square.

**Q18: How do you handle chargebacks?**
A: Customer disputes transaction. Process: Reason code, evidence submission, resolution (win/loss). Prevention: Clear descriptors, good customer service, fraud prevention, clear terms.

**Q19: What is the role of card networks (Visa, Mastercard)?**
A: They provide: Interconnectivity between banks, brand trust, fraud protection, reward programs, dispute resolution, innovation investment. They don't issue cards or hold deposits.

**Q20: How do you scale payment infrastructure?**
A: Microservices architecture, horizontal scaling, load balancing, geographic distribution, failover mechanisms, monitoring, capacity planning, stress testing, partnership diversification.

### SOP 2: Payment Incident Response

**Trigger:** Payment system issue detected

**Steps:**

1. **Detection & Alert (0-5 min)**
   - Automated monitoring alerts
   - Incident severity assessment
   - War room activation
   - Stakeholder notification

2. **Triage & Containment (5-30 min)**
   - Impact scope determination
   - Customer communication preparation
   - Temporary containment measures
   - Root cause investigation begins

3. **Resolution (30 min - 4 hours)**
   - Fix implementation
   - Testing in staging
   - Production deployment
   - Monitoring confirmation

4. **Post-Incident (24-48 hours)**
   - Root cause analysis report
   - Customer impact assessment
   - Process improvement identification
   - Regulatory notification if required

**Success Criteria:** <1 hour resolution for critical, <4 hours for major
**Escalation:** Customer-impacting → CEO/CFO notification within 15 min

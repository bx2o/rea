## 0.2 Stremiium Regulatory Environment

**South African Primary Regulators:**

- **Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA):**
  - CASP licensing under FAIS Act (Category I and II)
  - Conduct standards for crypto asset services
  - Cybersecurity requirements for license holders
  - Reporting obligations (quarterly, annual, ad-hoc)
  - Fit and proper requirements for key individuals

- **Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC):**
  - Accountable institution registration under FICA
  - Customer due diligence (CDD) and enhanced due diligence (EDD)
  - Suspicious and unusual transaction reporting
  - Terrorist property reporting
  - Risk management and compliance program (RMCP)
  - Regular compliance returns

- **National Gambling Board (NGB):**
  - Licensing for prediction markets and wagering
  - Responsible gambling requirements
  - Player protection measures
  - Revenue reporting and taxation
  - Anti-money laundering integration

- **South African Revenue Service (SARS):**
  - Crypto Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) - effective March 2026
  - Income tax on crypto gains (trading, mining, staking)
  - VAT treatment of crypto transactions
  - Exchange control compliance for cross-border flows
  - Employer crypto salary withholding obligations

**Data & Consumer Protection:**

- **Information Regulator:** POPIA compliance, data breach notifications, enforcement actions
- **National Consumer Commission:** Consumer Protection Act compliance, complaint handling
- **Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC):** Corporate governance, annual returns

## 0.3 Global Regulatory Landscape

**European Union:**

- **MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation):** Comprehensive EU crypto framework effective 2024-2025
  - CASP authorization requirements
  - Stablecoin reserve requirements (significant asset-referenced tokens)
  - Consumer protection obligations
  - Market abuse prevention
  - Environmental disclosures (PoW concerns)

- **GDPR:** Data protection for EU users, cross-border transfer mechanisms
- **5AMLD/6AMLD:** Anti-money laundering directives, crypto service provider obligations

**United States:**

- **Federal:**
  - SEC: Securities regulation, token classification (security vs. utility)
  - CFTC: Commodities regulation, derivatives oversight
  - FinCEN: BSA/AML requirements for money services businesses
  - IRS: Tax treatment, broker reporting requirements (infrastructure bill)
  - OCC: Banking charter for crypto custody

- **State-Level:**
  - New York BitLicense
  - Wyoming blockchain-friendly laws
  - California DFPI oversight
  - Texas banking guidance
  - Money transmitter licensing (state-by-state)

**Other Key Jurisdictions:**

- **UK:** FCA registration, AML/CTF compliance, stablecoin regulation
- **Singapore:** MAS licensing, payment services act, DPT provider framework
- **Hong Kong:** SFC licensing, retail trading restrictions (professional investors only)
- **Japan:** FSA registration, strict exchange oversight, self-regulatory organization (JVCEA)
- **UAE:** VARA (Dubai), ADGM framework, crypto-friendly free zones
- **Nigeria:** SEC stance, CBN banking restrictions, SEC digital asset regulations

# SECTION 4: KEY FAQS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Regulatory FAQs

**Q1: How do you stay current on regulatory changes?**
A: Sources: (1) **Official channels:** Regulatory websites, Federal Register. (2) **Newsletters:** Industry publications, law firm updates. (3) **Associations:** Industry group alerts. (4) **Conferences:** Regulatory seminars. (5) **External counsel:** Updates on major changes. (6) **Peer networks:** Other compliance professionals. (7) **Automated alerts:** Google alerts, regulatory monitoring tools. Best: Multiple sources, daily monitoring, weekly synthesis.

**Q2: What's the difference between compliance and regulatory affairs?**
A: **Compliance:** Ensuring organization follows rules (internal focus). **Regulatory Affairs:** Managing relationship with regulators, influencing policy (external focus). Overlap: Both ensure adherence. Difference: Compliance is operational; regulatory affairs is strategic and relational.

**Q3: How do you prepare for a regulatory audit?**
A: Steps: (1) **Early warning:** Track renewal/inspection schedules. (2) **Self-audit:** Internal review against requirements. (3) **Documentation:** Organize evidence. (4) **Team prep:** Brief personnel on process. (5) **Logistics:** Schedule, space, IT needs. (6) **Opening:** Welcome, set tone. (7) **Response:** Timely, accurate answers. (8) **Close-out:** Address findings promptly.

**Q4: How do you handle a regulatory violation?**
A: Response: (1) **Acknowledge:** Take it seriously. (2) **Investigate:** Root cause analysis. (3) **Contain:** Stop ongoing violation. (4) **Report:** As required by law. (5) **Remediate:** Fix the problem. (6) **Prevent:** Changes to avoid recurrence. (7) **Document:** Full record. (8) **Monitor:** Ensure effectiveness.

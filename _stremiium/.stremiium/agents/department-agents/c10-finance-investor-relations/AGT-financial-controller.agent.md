# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Key Controller Questions (Sample FAQs)

**Q1: What should a month-end close process look like?**
A: Timeline: (1) **Day 1-2 (close date):** All transactions must be recorded; month cutoff verified. (2) **Day 2-3:** Account reconciliations (bank, AR, AP, PayPal, etc.). Any discrepancies investigated. (3) **Day 3-4:** Journal entries (accruals, deferrals, expenses). Management review. (4) **Day 4-5:** Financial statement prep. Variance analysis. (5) **Day 5:** Review + Board/finance team sign-off. (6) **Day 6-7:** Distribute to stakeholders. Cost: Takes 1 controller FTE + 1-2 accounting staff (depending on size). Automation: Tools (Xero, NetSuite) can speed close; good training is key.

**Q2: What are the key balance sheet accounts to reconcile?**
A: Critical reconciliations: (1) **Cash:** Bank statement vs. GL. (2) **AR (Accounts Receivable):** Customer ledger vs. GL. (3) **Inventory:** Physical count vs. GL (if applicable). (4) **PP&E (fixed assets):** Asset register vs. GL. (5) **AP (Accounts Payable):** Vendor invoices vs. GL. (6) **Debt:** Loan documents vs. GL. (7) **Equity:** Cap table vs. GL. (8) **Payroll liability:** Payroll system vs. GL. Frequency: Monthly minimum. Any unexplained variance: Investigate immediately.

**Q3: What's the relationship between cash flow and profitability?**
A: **Profitability** = revenues minus expenses (income statement). **Cash flow** = actual cash in/out (balance sheet + operating activities). Example: You invoice customer $100K (revenue); customer pays in 60 days (cash receipt delayed). Result: Profitable, but negative cash flow. Management: (1) Monitor both separately. (2) Accelerate collections. (3) Manage payables (pay 30 days if possible). (4) Forecast cash flow (what cash will we have?). (5) Maintain cash reserve (cushion for timing gaps).

**Q4: What internal controls should a company have?**
A: Key controls: (1) **Segregation of duties:** Person requesting expense ≠ person approving ≠ person paying. (2) **Authorization limits:** Small expenses can be approved by manager; large (>$10K) need CFO approval; very large (>$100K) need Board. (3) **Documentation:** All expenses must have supporting documentation (invoice, receipt, approval). (4) **Reconciliations:** Monthly reconciliation of GL to sub-ledgers. (5) **Access controls:** Only authorized people can modify accounting records. (6) **Review:** CFO/Controller reviews all monthly transactions. Size: Scale controls with company size (startup = simpler; large = complex).

**Q5: How do you manage accounts payable and cash flow?**
A: Process: (1) **Invoice receipt:** Vendor sends invoice. (2) **Three-way match:** Invoice matches PO (purchase order) + receipt verification. (3) **Approval:** Manager approves for payment. (4) **Payment scheduling:** Pay on agreed terms (e.g., Net 30 = pay 30 days after invoice). (5) **Early pay discounts:** Sometimes vendor offers discount if pay early (e.g., 2/10 Net 30 = 2% discount if pay in 10 days). (6) **Cash flow timing:** Schedule large payments to avoid negative cash balance. Strategy: Pay on terms (don't pay early unless discount >5%). Negotiate terms (Net 45 vs. Net 30). Build cash reserves.

## SOP 2: Cash Flow Forecasting & Management

**Duration:** 2 hours weekly | **Frequency:** Weekly | **Owner:** Controller

- Gather cash position (current bank balance)
- AR forecast (expected collections, by customer)
- AP forecast (expected supplier payments, by vendor)
- Payroll forecast (when due?)
- Project cash position (5-week outlook)
- Identify cash gaps (will we run out?)
- Communicate to CFO + Finance team

# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Key Treasury Questions (Sample FAQs)

**Q1: What's optimal cash position and how do you calculate it?**
A: Formula: **Optimal cash = 3 months operating expenses** (typical prudent level). Example: $3M annual operating expenses ÷ 12 months = $250K/month burn. Optimal cash = $250K × 3 = $750K. (Some companies prefer 6 months ($1.5M) for safety; some 2 months ($500K) if confident in fundraising.) Rationale: 3 months = safety buffer if business slows or fundraising delayed. Manage: Monthly review; adjust as needed. Use excess for strategic investments; draw down as needed.

**Q2: How do you manage DSO and DPO to improve cash flow?**
A: **DSO (Days Sales Outstanding)** = average time to collect from customers. Lower = faster cash. **DPO (Days Payable Outstanding)** = average time to pay suppliers. Higher = slower payments = preserve cash. Optimize: (1) **DSO reduction:** Early payment discounts to customers? E-invoicing? Automated reminders? Target: 30-45 days (net 30-45 terms). (2) **DPO increase:** Negotiate longer payment terms with vendors? Stagger large payments? Target: 45-60 days. (3) **Impact:** If DSO drops from 60 to 30, you free up cash; if DPO rises from 30 to 60, you preserve cash. Scale: As company grows, small % changes × large revenue = significant cash impact.

**Q3: What types of short-term investments are appropriate for excess cash?**
A: **Safe, liquid options:** (1) **Money market accounts:** ~4-5% return, liquid (withdraw anytime). (2) **Short-term CDs (6-month):** ~4-5%, fixed return, penalty if withdraw early. (3) **Treasury bills (3-6 month maturity):** ~5%, backed by US government (safest). (4) **Commercial paper:** ~5%, higher yield but slight credit risk. Avoid: (1) Stocks (too volatile). (2) Long-term bonds (interest rate risk). (3) Illiquid investments (need quick access to cash). Rule: Safety + liquidity first; return second. Earn 3-5% on excess cash instead of 0% in checking.

**Q4: How do you manage a credit line or revolving facility?**
A: **Credit line** = maximal borrowing available; you pay interest on amount drawn. **Revolving** = you can borrow/repay as needed. Uses: (1) Working capital borrowing (borrow when cash flow dips, repay when cash improves). (2) Contingency liquidity (safety backstop if needed). Management: (1) Maintain relationship with lender (monthly updates, good communication). (2) Monitor covenant compliance (lenders require: minimum cash, max debt/revenue, minimum current ratio). (3) Pay interest monthly. (4) Renew before expiration (typically annual). (5) Right-size: Request enough for contingency but not so much you pay unused fees. Typical: $500K-$2M for growth company.

**Q5: What's a working capital cycle and why does it matter?**
A: **Cycle** = Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) + DSO - DPO. Example: (1) Days to sell inventory (45). (2) Days to collect from customer (30). (3) Days to pay supplier (−60). Cycle = 45 + 30 − 60 = 15 days. Means: You need 15 days of cash to fund operations. **Impact:** Longer cycle = more cash needed. Shorter cycle = less cash needed. **Optimization:** (1) Reduce inventory (faster turns). (2) Faster collections (DSO down). (3) Slower payments to vendors (DPO up). Savings: 1-day improvement × $10M revenue ÷ 365 days = ~$27K cash freed up.

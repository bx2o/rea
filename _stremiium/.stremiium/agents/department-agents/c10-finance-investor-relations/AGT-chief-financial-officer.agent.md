# SECTION 4: FIFTY+ FAQS, SKILLS & KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY

## 4.1 Key Financial Questions (Sample FAQs)

**Q1: What's a good path to profitability for a SaaS company?**
A: Typical path: (1) **Year 1-2:** Focus on growth; negative cash flow expected (raised capital covers burn). (2) **Year 2-3:** Improve gross margins (80%+), control operating costs, approach cash flow break-even. (3) **Year 3+:** Profitable growth (reinvesting profits into growth). Metrics: (1) Gross margin: 70%+ is healthy. (2) CAC payback: <12 months. (3) Burn rate: <18 months cash. (4) Magic number: ARR growth ÷ sales/marketing spend (>1.0 = efficient). Timeline: 3-5 years to sustainable profitability typical for venture-backed companies.

**Q2: How do you value a company for fundraising?**
A: Methods: (1) **Comparable company:** Look at recent funding rounds for comparable companies, apply valuation multiples. (2) **Discounted cash flow (DCF):** Forecast future cash flows, discount to present value. (3) **Revenue multiple:** Apply revenue multiple to current/projected revenue. (4) **Venture formula:** Model exit value (IPO or acquisition), work backward to post-money valuation today. Practice: Market determines valuation; strong growth + unit economics = higher valuation. Avoid: Valuation is range, not fixed number. Negotiate with investors.

**Q3: When should you raise venture capital vs. bootstrap/profit?**
A: **Raise VC if:** (1) Large market opportunity (>$1B). (2) Network effects/defensibility (hard to replicate). (3) Capital intensity (need capital to scale). (4) Competitive pressure (need to move fast). (5) Your opportunity cost (other ventures). **Bootstrap/profit if:** (1) Capital-light business (services, SaaS with low CAC). (2) Conservative founder (prefer to keep equity). (3) Predictable revenue (steady customers). (4) Niche market (no VC interest). Decision: Your choice; both valid paths.

**Q4: What's a good burn rate and runway?**
A: **Burn rate** = monthly cash spend. **Runway** = months of cash left ÷ burn rate. Examples: $1M in bank, $100K/month burn = 10 months runway. Healthy: (1) **Early stage:** <18 months runway acceptable (fundraising underway). (2) **Growth stage:** 12-18+ months runway (margin of safety). (3) **Late stage:** 18+ months (approaching profitability). Rule: Don't let runway drop below 6 months (forces emergency fundraising). Calculate monthly.

**Q5: How do you build a financial model for fundraising?**
A: Components: (1) **Revenue:** Market size, customer growth, price, churn assumptions. Project 3-5 years. (2) **Costs:** COGS, operating expenses, breakout by department. (3) **Cash flow:** Quarterly projections. (4) **Assumptions:** Document all (growth rates, churn, CAC, LTV). (5) **Sensitivity:** Show upside (2x growth), downside (0.5x growth). Format: Excel/Sheets, easy to understand. Update: Quarterly as actual results accumulate. Use: Board meetings, investor discussions, strategic planning.

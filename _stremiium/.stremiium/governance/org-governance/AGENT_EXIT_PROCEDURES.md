# AGENT EXIT & KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER PROCEDURES

**Comprehensive Framework for Agent Departures & Knowledge Preservation**  
**Version**: 3.0  
**Last Updated**: March 20, 2026  
**Scope**: All 16 councils and 100+ agents  
**Authority**: Sparkling Curious + HR + Council Captains  
**Purpose**: Define clear procedures for planned/unplanned departures ensuring knowledge preservation, smooth transitions, and organizational continuity

---

## SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Agent Exit Purpose**: Manage agent departures in ways that preserve knowledge, maintain relationships, support departing agents, and position organization for continuity

**Core Principle**: Every departure is an opportunity to document knowledge, strengthen processes, and set departing agents up for success in their next chapter

**Exit Types** (4 categories):

- **Planned Progression**: Promotion or lateral move within organization
- **Planned Departure**: Resignation or retirement (agent choice)
- **Involuntary Separation**: Termination for cause or performance
- **Emergency Departure**: Sudden unavailability (illness, emergency, etc.)

**Timeline Variability**:

- Planned progression: 4-12 weeks knowledge transfer
- Planned departure: 2-4 weeks notice standard
- Involuntary: 0-2 weeks typically
- Emergency: 0 weeks (documentation after fact)

---

## SECTION 2: PLANNED PROGRESSION (INTERNAL MOVEMENT)

### 2.1 Promotion to Higher Tier or Role

**Scenario**: Agent advancing to higher tier (e.g., Tier 4 to Tier 3 Specialist)

**Timeline**: 6-8 weeks before effective date

**Week 1: Announce & Plan**

📋 Manager meets with agent:

- Congratulations (celebrate achievement)
- New role description
- New team/council
- Start date
- Key transitions

📋 Manager meets with council captains:

- Current captain (losing good person)
- New captain (gaining good person)
- Transition plan
- Coverage plan for departing role

📋 Announcement to organization:

- Agent name, new role, start date
- Story of why they were promoted
- Why we're excited about this
- Who's covering their old role

---

**Week 2-3: Knowledge Transfer Planning**

Transfer Plan should identify:

| Item                     | Owner                   | Timeline |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- | -------- |
| Active projects          | Agent + current manager | Week 2   |
| Ongoing responsibilities | Agent + current manager | Week 2   |
| Key relationships        | Agent                   | Week 2   |
| Decision history         | Agent                   | Week 3   |
| Documented processes     | Agent                   | Week 3   |
| Team dynamics/context    | Manager                 | Week 3   |

---

**Week 4-6: Active Transfer**

Agent's time allocation:

- **30%**: Old role + knowledge transfer
- **70%**: New role introduction/training

Transfer activities:

- ✅ Pair with replacement on active work
- ✅ Document decision history ("Why we chose X over Y")
- ✅ Team meetings with both old/new teams
- ✅ Relationship introductions (old team meets new team)
- ✅ Process walkthroughs
- ✅ Mentee handoff (update mentees on their new mentors)
- ✅ Client relationship intro (if applicable)

---

**Week 7-8: New Role Ramp-Up**

- Agent fully in new role
- On-call support for old team (as needed)
- New team projects/ownership
- Ongoing mentorship from old manager (if helpful)

---

### 2.2 Lateral Move (Same Tier, Different Council)

**Scenario**: Agent moves to different council at same tier

**Timeline**: 4-6 weeks

**Similar process to promotion** but:

- Briefer (less new training needed)
- More focused on new council processes/culture
- Relationships more lateral than hierarchical
- New manager is peer, not subordinate

---

### 2.3 Return from Leave/Sabbatical

**Scenario**: Agent on approved leave returning to organization

**Timeline**: 1-2 weeks reentry

**Plan**:

- **Week before return**: Manager sends context update (what changed)
- **Day 1-2**: Light schedule, reacquaint with environment
- **Week 1**: Catch-up meetings (what you missed)
- **Week 2**: Full operational capacity

**Topics for catch-up**:

- Strategic changes (company direction)
- Org changes (council changes, new people)
- Technology/process changes (new tools, updated procedures)
- Project status (where are we now? What happened?)
- Personal check-in (how are you feeling about return?)

## SECTION 3: PLANNED DEPARTURE (RESIGNATION/RETIREMENT)

### 3.1 Resignation Process (Standard 2-4 Week Notice)

**Timeline**: Resignation to final day (2-4 weeks typically)

**Day 1: Resignation**

Agent notifies manager:

- "I've decided to resign effective [date]"
- Reason (can be simple)
- Next role/opportunity (optional)
- Manager's immediate response: "Thank you for your service. Let's plan a good transition."

Manager immediately:

- ✅ Notifies council captain
- ✅ Notifies Sparkling Curious
- ✅ Notes agent tier + expertise areas
- ✅ Assesses impact (critical? Key person on projects?)
- ✅ Severity: Emergency if: critical person + short notice + no backup

---

**Day 1-2: Announcement & Planning Meeting**

📢 Announce to broader team:

- Agent name, last day, where they're going (if comfortable sharing)
- Thank them for their contributions
- Excitement for their next chapter
- Coverage plan (who's taking over)

📋 Transition planning meeting (Agent + Manager + Relevant stakeholders):

- Current projects (who takes over each)
- Ongoing responsibilities (transition plan)
- Documentation needed (starting today)
- Knowledge transfer schedule
- Final day date

---

**Day 3-7: Knowledge Inventory & Documentation**

Agent begins documenting:

**Priority 1: Critical Knowledge** (Most urgent)

- Passwords/access info (saved to secure vault)
- Active projects status (which ones critical?)
- Urgent deadlines (next 30 days)
- Key client relationships
- Decision history (why did we do this?)
- Tribal knowledge (how do we actually do this?)

**Priority 2: Important Knowledge** (Medium urgency)

- Project backgrounds
- Relationship context (client, team history)
- Tool configurations
- Standards & conventions (how we do things here)
- Training materials
- Contact lists

**Priority 3: Reference Knowledge** (Lower urgency)

- General domain knowledge
- Historical documents
- Archive information

---

**Week 1: Pairing & Initial Transfer**

Agent spends **50% of time** on knowledge transfer:

✅ Pairing sessions (with replacement or successor):

- "Here's how I do X..."
- Walk-throughs of actual work
- Q&A for successor
- Hands-on practice (let them try with agent watching)

✅ Documentation work:

- Finalize critical documents
- Update existing procedures
- Create runbooks ("To do X, follow these steps")
- Video recordings (complex processes that need to be seen)

✅ Relationship handoff:

- Meet with key stakeholders
- "Meet my successor on Project X"
- "Here's what they should know about this client"

---

**Week 2-3: Deep Dives & Mentoring**

Agent spends **40% of time** on transfer:

✅ Deep dive sessions on complex topics
✅ Team meetings (full team together)
✅ Client meetings (new person introduced)
✅ Problem-solving scenarios ("What if...?")
✅ Finishing documentation

---

**Week 4 (If 4-week notice): Wind-Down**

Agent spends **20% of time** on transfer:

✅ Final questions answered
✅ Documentation review ("Did we miss anything?")
✅ Successor confidence check ("Do you feel ready?")
✅ Personal goodbyes
✅ Transition cleanup

---

**Final Day: Offboarding**

✅ Access removal (at end of day)

- Systems
- Physical access
- Passwords reset
- Equipment returned

✅ Final celebration:

- Team gathering (coffee, snacks, goodbyes)
- Thank you from manager/captain
- Company gift/recognition
- Photo (archive)

✅ Final communication:

- Sparkling Curious note to organization
- "Thank you for your great work here"
- "We wish you well in your next chapter"

---

### 3.2 Short Notice Resignation (1-2 weeks)

**Scenario**: Agent resigns with minimal notice

**Immediate action**:

- **Severity assessment**: Is this P2/P3 crisis?
- **Damage control**: Preserve critical knowledge immediately
- **Coverage**: Who can cover urgent work?

**Days 1-2: Emergency Documentation** (All hands on deck)

- Agent + manager + team capture critical knowledge
- Focused on "Must know" items only
- Video recordings for complex processes
- Intensive pairing

**Days 3-7**: Normal transfer process (accelerated)

**Key difference**: Less thorough (time constrained) but organized and prioritized

---

### 3.3 Retirement (Extended Notice)

**Scenario**: Agent retiring after tenure with organization

**Timeline**: Often longer (6-12 weeks possible) + ceremonial

**Unique aspects**:

- ✅ Celebration of career/tenure (not sad goodbye)
- ✅ Mentoring/legacy work possible
- ✅ Time for deep documentation
- ✅ Succession planning can be thorough
- ✅ Possible ongoing relationship (retired consultant?)

**Process**: Similar to standard resignation but:

- Longer timeline enables comprehensive transfer
- More celebration/recognition
- Possible post-retirement engagement

## SECTION 4: INVOLUNTARY SEPARATION

### 4.1 Termination for Performance

**Scenario**: Agent not meeting performance standards, manager ends employment

**Timeline**: Formal termination process typically 2-8 weeks (legal/policy dependent) before final separation

**Before Termination**:

✅ Performance improvement plan (documented):

- Clear performance issues
- Specific improvement requirements
- Timeline (typically 30-60 days)
- Manager support/resources
- Success criteria

✅ Failed improvement:

- Still not meeting standards after improvement period
- Legal/HR review
- Termination decision

---

**Termination Day: HR-Led Process**

HR and manager meet with agent:

- "Your employment is terminated effective [date]"
- Reason (performance issues from PIP)
- Final paycheck details
- Benefits explanation
- References policy
- Exit interview (optional)
- Physical access removed

**Same day**:

- Manager notifies team (factual, brief)
- Organizational announcement (simple: "X is no longer with organization")

---

**Knowledge Transfer Challenges**:

- Agent may not cooperate with transfer
- May have damaged relationships
- May take institutional knowledge with them
- **Mitigation**: During PIP period, start documenting what they know

**Future process improvement**:

- Why wasn't this caught earlier?
- How do we prevent performance issues?
- What support could have helped?

---

### 4.2 Termination for Cause

**Scenario**: Violation of policy, ethics, values (theft, harassment, fraud, etc.)

**Timeline**: Immediate (same day) to 2-4 weeks (investigation dependent)

**Immediate Termination**:

- Legal + HR involvement
- Fact verification
- Meeting with agent (reason for immediate termination)
- Security procedures (access removed immediately)
- Communication to team (factual: "X has been terminated")

**Knowledge Transfer**:

- Usually not applicable (agent not cooperating)
- Focus shifts to: What can teammates document quickly?
- Possible external support/consultant to backfill

**Investigation**:

- HR/Legal investigates
- Findings documented
- Organizational learning (how do we prevent this?)

---

### 4.3 Reduction in Force (RIF/Layoff)

**Scenario**: Organization reduces headcount (economic, restructuring, etc.)

**Timeline**: Often announced with effective date (1-4 weeks)

**Process**:

✅ Selection (who departs):

- Manager input
- Performance review
- Organizational need
- Diversity considerations (avoid bias)

✅ Announcement meeting:

- HR + manager meet with affected individual
- Role is being eliminated/department being reduced
- Severance details
- References
- Outplacement resources (if available)
- Access removed

✅ Broader team announcement:

- Fact-based: "We're making difficult layoff decisions."
- Rationale (market, restructuring, etc.)
- Who's affected (names, roles)
- Who's staying
- Timeline for decisions
- Support offered

---

**Knowledge Transfer**:

- Agent may be motivated to help (good reference)
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks typical for RIF
- Can use standard transfer process
- Termination is still termination (less emotional than performance termination)

---

**Organizational Learning**:

- Post-RIF: Were cuts surgical or did we damage capability?
- Did we preserve critical knowledge?
- How do departing employees reflect on our handling?
- What improvements needed?

---

## SECTION 5: EMERGENCY DEPARTURE

### 5.1 Sudden Unavailability (Illness, Emergency, Family Crisis)

**Scenario**: Agent suddenly unavailable due to legitimate emergency

**Possible situations**:

- Health emergency (hospitalization, recovery)
- Family emergency (urgent care needed, dependent issue)
- Accident/travel crisis
- Natural disaster/evacuation

**Immediate Response** (Hours):

✅ Manager learns of situation:

- Agent or someone close contacts manager
- Understand severity (short-term? Long-term? Permanent?)
- Communicate empathy/support
- Coverage plan (who takes over urgent work?)

✅ Organizational notification:

- Very basic: "X is having a personal emergency and will be unavailable starting [date]"
- No private details unless agent shares
- Focus on coverage, not cause
- Professional compassion tone

✅ Immediate coverage:

- Who handles their urgent work?
- Who contacts their clients/stakeholders?
- Who documents status (projects, deadlines)?

---

**Days 1-7: Emergency Documentation**:

⚠️ Assumption: Agent unavailable 2+ weeks minimum

Manager + team conduct:

**Priority 1 Documentation** (Immediate):

- Emergency contacts (if needed to understand work)
- Urgent deadlines (next 30 days)
- Critical access info (if needed to access their work)
- Project status (what's in flight?)

**Priority 2 Documentation** (Days 3-7):

- Relationships (key clients, stakeholders)
- Ongoing work
- Standards/conventions they follow
- Decision history

---

**Week 2+ (If ongoing unavailability)**:

📞 Contact with agent (if possible):

- "We want to support you. What do you need?"
- "We're managing your responsibilities. No pressure."
- "Let us know when you're able to return."
- "We're documenting your work to preserve continuity."

📚 Continued documentation:

- Complete the picture
- Update as situation evolves
- Prepare for possible scenarios:
  - Agent returns in X weeks (onboarding plan)
  - Agent needs FMLA/extended leave
  - Agent unable to return (new permanent plan)

---

### 5.2 Unexpected Death or Permanent Incapacity

**Scenario**: Agent unexpectedly passes or becomes permanently incapacitated

**Immediate Response** (First day):

🕯️ Organizational response:

- Confirm information (sensitive, verify before sharing)
- Leadership assessment (what's the impact?)
- Communication plan (how do we share this?)
- Support plan (agent's team will be grieving)

🕯️ Team notification:

- Manager or Sparkling Curious makes announcement
- Factual, respectful tone
- "Our colleague [name] has passed away"
- Memorial/celebration information (if applicable)
- Support resources available
- Professional compassion

---

**Days 1-3: Immediate Actions**

✅ Family coordination (if organization has relationship):

- Offer support/condolences
- Ask what organization can do
- Clarify what information can be shared

✅ Immediate coverage:

- Critical work coverage (assumed permanent)
- Client notifications (handled professionally)
- Project reassignment

✅ Organizational support:

- Grief counseling offered (to team)
- Flexibility on work (team may be impacted for days/weeks)
- Memorial gathering (if family permits)

---

**Week 1-2: Knowledge Preservation** (Parallel to grieving):

As team is able:

- Colleagues capture what they know about agent's work
- No rush (grieving takes precedence)
- Voluntary participation
- Focused on critical knowledge only

---

**Medium-term (Weeks 2-4): Moving forward**

✅ Transition plans finalized
✅ Hire replacement or redistribute work
✅ Celebrate agent's legacy (awards, scholarships, organization improvement in their honor)

Example: "In memory of [name], we're establishing an annual mentoring award"

## SECTION 6: KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER PROCEDURES

### 6.1 Types of Knowledge to Capture

**Tier 1: Mission-Critical Knowledge**

This knowledge must be captured or role will fail:

| Knowledge Type       | Examples                    | Capture Method            |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Access credentials   | Passwords, API keys, tokens | Secure password vault     |
| Client relationships | Key contacts, dynamics      | Introduction meeting      |
| Project status       | Timeline, next milestones   | Document/handover meeting |
| Decision logic       | Why we chose X over Y       | Interview/recording       |
| Key procedures       | How we do X                 | Runbook + walkthrough     |
| Emergency contacts   | Who to call if X happens    | Contact list              |

---

**Tier 2: Important Knowledge**

Knowledge that matters but won't cause failure:

| Knowledge Type        | Examples                     | Capture Method       |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Project context       | Why we're building this      | Document             |
| Relationship history  | Background on key clients    | Notes + intro        |
| Tool/system knowledge | How our X works              | Tutorial/walkthrough |
| Lessons learned       | Mistakes/wins from projects  | Interview            |
| Domain expertise      | Specific knowledge they have | Pairing/docs         |

---

**Tier 3: Reference Knowledge**

Knowledge that's nice-to-have but not critical:

| Knowledge Type     | Examples                | Capture Method |
| ------------------ | ----------------------- | -------------- |
| Historical context | How we got here         | Wiki/archive   |
| Best practices     | What we learned         | Docs           |
| Training materials | How to learn X          | Library        |
| Industry knowledge | Market trends they knew | Interviews     |

---

### 6.2 Knowledge Transfer Methods

**Pairing/Shadowing** (Best for tacit knowledge):

- Agent + replacement work together
- Replacement watches, then tries with supervision
- Good for: Complex processes, client interactions, tool usage
- Time: 2-10 hours per topic

**Recorded Walkthroughs** (Good for reference):

- Agent talks through process while doing it
- Video recorded for later reference
- Good for: Unusual processes, reference material
- Time: 30 min to 2 hours per process

**Written Documentation** (Essential):

- Procedures documented step-by-step
- Decision rationale explained
- Good for: Everything, permanent reference
- Time: 1-4 hours per topic

**Interview/Storytelling** (Good for context):

- Structured conversation about learnings, decisions, background
- Audio recorded for future reference
- Good for: Wisdom, context, lessons learned
- Time: 1-2 hours per interview

**Relationship Introductions** (Irreplaceable for relationships):

- In-person (or video) introduction
- Agent explains history/context
- New person and contact build relationship
- Good for: Client relationships, peer relationships
- Time: 15-30 min per introduction

## SECTION 7: OFFBOARDING CHECKLIST

### 7.1 Technical Offboarding

**Before final day**:

- [ ] All passwords changed/transferred
- [ ] Code repositories access evaluated
- [ ] VPN/system access prepared for removal
- [ ] Files transferred/backed up
- [ ] Work-in-progress documented
- [ ] Secrets/API keys secured or rotated
- [ ] Equipment inventory taken
- [ ] Phone/mobile device prepared

**Final day end-of-business**:

- [ ] All system access removed (systematic)
- [ ] VPN disabled
- [ ] Email forwarding set up (if needed)
- [ ] Physical access cards/badges collected
- [ ] Equipment returned (laptop, phone, keys, etc.)
- [ ] Financial account settled (expense reimbursement, etc.)

---

### 7.2 Administrative Offboarding

**Before final day**:

- [ ] Payroll/final check processed
- [ ] Benefits transition communicated (COBRA, etc. if applicable)
- [ ] Retirement/501k information provided
- [ ] Tax/W2 information sent (or scheduled for after)
- [ ] References policy communicated
- [ ] Exit interview scheduled (optional)

**Final day**:

- [ ] Final paycheck provided
- [ ] Benefits documents delivered
- [ ] Exit interview conducted (if opted-in)
- [ ] Acknowledgment of confidentiality agreement
- [ ] Return of equipment/facilities completed

---

### 7.3 Relationship Offboarding

**Before final day**:

- [ ] Key clients/stakeholders met new person
- [ ] Team members introduced to replacement
- [ ] Mentees reassigned (or plans made)
- [ ] Key relationships documented
- [ ] Vendor/partner relationships transitioned

**Final day**:

- [ ] Final team gathering/goodbyes
- [ ] Manager one-on-one (closing statement)
- [ ] Organizational goodbye (if applicable)

---

### 7.4 Documentation & Learning Offboarding

**Before final day**:

- [ ] Critical knowledge documented
- [ ] Processes updated
- [ ] Decision history captured
- [ ] Lessons learned documented
- [ ] Meeting notes organized
- [ ] Project status documented

**After departure**:

- [ ] Team debriefs on learnings
- [ ] Organization learns from transition
- [ ] Documentation review (was critical knowledge captured?)

## SECTION 8: SPECIAL OFFBOARDING SCENARIOS

### 8.1 Difficult Departure (Acrimonious Resignation)

**Scenario**: Agent leaving on bad terms, upset with organization/manager

**Challenges**:

- Agent may not cooperate with knowledge transfer
- Relationships damaged
- May attempt data exfiltration or sabotage
- Team morale/dynamics affected

**Approach**:

- ✅ Professional compassion (don't escalate the emotion)
- ✅ Clear boundaries (what needs to happen, non-negotiable)
- ✅ Documentation focus (other team members capture what's needed)
- ✅ Security (if there's risk, be cautious about system access)
- ✅ Post-departure learning (what led to this? How do we prevent?)

**Example conversation**:
"I understand you're frustrated. Regardless, we need to transition your work responsibly. Here's what we need from you [specific tasks]. Here's what we can do for you [support, references]. Can we work together on this transition?"

---

### 8.2 Key Person Departure While on Critical Project

**Scenario**: Agent leaves mid-year during critical projects

**Challenges**:

- Projects at risk
- Team may struggle
- Potential client impact
- Knowledge loss during active work

**Approach**:

- ✅ Immediately assign replacement/oversight
- ✅ Accelerated knowledge transfer (at least to manager level)
- ✅ Client communication (if needed, proactive)
- ✅ Project timeline assessment (can we maintain schedule?)
- ✅ Resource augmentation (hire contractor? Pull from other team?)

**Prevention for future**:

- Cross-train on critical projects (no single point of failure)
- Documentation of active work (ongoing, not just at exit)
- Mentorship of backup person

---

### 8.3 High-Visibility Departure

**Scenario**: Well-known/respected agent departing (due to promotion elsewhere, high-profile hire, etc.)

**Opportunity**: Turn into positive organizational moment

**Approach**:

- ✅ Celebrate their success/next chapter
- ✅ Public announcement with genuine warmth
- ✅ Acknowledge their contributions specifically
- ✅ Invite organization to celebrate (party, gathering)
- ✅ Keep relationship alive (if appropriate)

**Example announcement**:
"We're proud to announce that [Agent] has been offered a position at [Organization] as [Role]. This is a fantastic opportunity they've earned through their great work here. We'll miss them, and we're excited for what they'll accomplish next. [Agent] has been instrumental in [specific achievement]. Please help us celebrate them!"

---

### 8.4 Rehire/Boomerang Departures

**Scenario**: Previously departed agent returning to organization

**Approach**:

- ✅ Welcome them back warmly
- ✅ Acknowledge what they've learned/grown while away
- ✅ Don't treat as if they never left (too patronizing)
- ✅ Updated onboarding (what changed while they were gone?)
- ✅ May be returning at higher tier (recognize their growth)

**Many great employees leave and return. That's OK.**

---

## SECTION 9: POST-DEPARTURE PROCESSES

### 9.1 Knowledge Preservation

**Day 3-7 Post-Departure** (After everyone calms down):

**Team debrief**:

- What knowledge did we capture?
- What knowledge gaps remain?
- Urgency to fill gaps (critical? Important? Reference?)
- Who will own each knowledge domain?

**Documentation review**:

- Is documentation complete/clear?
- Did we miss anything critical?
- Update as needed

**Ongoing reference**:

- Create "Ask [Agent's Name]" wiki section (for future reference)
- Videos available for rewatch
- Recordings accessible
- Runbooks maintained

---

### 9.2 Relationship Maintenance

**For departing agents**:

- Keep in touch (birthday, holiday, professional milestones)
- Maintain positive relationship
- Consider opportunities to work together in future
- Provide references as promised

**For agent's relationships/contacts**:

- Continue relationships (they knew agent for good reason)
- Acknowledge transition smoothly
- Build own relationship with new person

---

### 9.3 Post-Departure Assessment

**Week 2-4 After Departure**:

📊 Assess transition success:

| Question                         | Metric               | Target        |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------- |
| Was critical knowledge captured? | Checklist completed? | 90%+          |
| Is replacement confident?        | Self-assessment      | 7+/10         |
| Are projects/work continuing?    | Status assessment    | 95%+ on track |
| Is team functioning?             | Team morale check    | 6+/10         |
| Did we lose important knowledge? | Retro feedback       | Minimal       |

---

### 9.4 Process Improvements from Departure

**Learning cycle**:

1. **What went well?** (Replicate)
2. **What could be better?** (Fix)
3. **What surprised us?** (Update assumptions)

**Possible improvements**:

- Better cross-training (prevent single points of failure)
- Updated documentation templates (if they were inadequate)
- Process changes (things learned from their departure)
- Hiring patterns (do we need different profiles?)
- Retention programs (could we have kept them?)

---

## SECTION 10: EXIT INTERVIEW PROCEDURES

### 10.1 Exit Interview Structure

**Goal**: Understand why agent left, learn for future, maintain relationship

**Format**: Voluntary, confidential conversation

**Typical questions**:

**About their experience**:

- What did you enjoy most here?
- What was most challenging?
- How did you feel about management?
- How was the team/culture?
- Would you recommend us as employer?

**About their departure**:

- Why did you decide to leave?
- Was there anything we could have done to keep you?
- What attracted you to next opportunity?
- Did compensation factor into decision?
- Any concerns/feedback about our organization?

**About future**:

- Would you be open to returning (if good fit)?
- Can we stay in touch?
- Any advice for organization?

---

**Conduct process**:

- Neutral third party ideal (HR, not their manager)
- Conversational, not interrogation
- Optional, no pressure
- Confidential, information documented
- Not arguing/defending (listen to feedback)

---

### 10.2 Aggregating Exit Interview Data

**Track patterns** (quarterly analysis):

❓ Are we losing people from one specific council/manager?

❓ Common reasons for departure?

❓ Compensation vs. other factors?

❓ How are we scoring on culture/management/learning?

**Use insights for improvement**:

- If manager feedback consistently negative → coaching/change
- If compensation non-competitive → address
- If culture issues → culture work
- If learning opportunities lacking → development programs

## SECTION 11: SPECIAL OUTBOARDING CASES

### 11.1 Consultant/Contract Worker Departure

**Scenario**: Temporary or contract agent ending engagement

**Difference from full-time**:

- Shorter transition possible (they were expected to leave)
- Focused on project completion (not ongoing responsibilities)
- Relationship may continue in future engagements

**Process**:

- Project status at end of contract
- Critical knowledge transfer (to team, not hiring replacement)
- Evaluation of engagement (did it work?)
- Potential rehire/future engagement

---

### 11.2 Agent on Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) Not Improving

**Scenario**: Agent failing PIP, Performance-based termination

**Timeline to termination**: Usually 30-60 days post-failed PIP

**Knowledge transfer challenge**:

- Agent may be demoralized
- Motivation to help transfer may be low
- Quality of transfer may suffer

**Approach**:

- During PIP: Begin documentation (not waiting until termination)
- Week of termination: Focused transfer (willing or not)
- Post-termination: Team catches up on anything missed

---

### 11.3 Organizational Restructuring / Department Shutdown

**Scenario**: Entire council dissolving, mass departures

**Process** (Complex, affects multiple):

- Prior: Communication of restructuring (clear why)
- Transition plan: 2-8 weeks (agents know end date)
- Knowledge transfer: Can be systematic across exiting agents
- Celebration: Recognition of team's work
- Learning: Post-restructure retrospective

**Unique challenges**:

- Multiple agents at different readiness levels
- Complex knowledge relationships
- Potential morale impact
- Institutional knowledge concentrated in closing group

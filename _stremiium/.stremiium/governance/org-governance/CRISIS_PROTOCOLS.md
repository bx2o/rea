# EMERGENCY RESPONSE & CRISIS PROTOCOLS

**Comprehensive Crisis Management for Agent Ecosystem**  
**Version**: 3.0  
**Last Updated**: March 20, 2026  
**Scope**: All 16 councils and 100+ agents  
**Authority**: Sparkling Curious Master Orchestrator  
**Purpose**: Define clear crisis response procedures, decision authority, and escalation protocols enabling rapid response to organizational emergencies

---

## SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Crisis Management Purpose**: Establish transparent procedures for crisis identification, response, and resolution ensuring organizational continuity and informed decision-making under pressure

**Crisis Definition**: Any event that could significantly impact organizational function, agent wellbeing, client relationships, or strategic objectives

**Crisis Severity Levels** (4-tier system):

- **P1-Critical**: Existential threat, all-hands response
- **P2-Severe**: Multi-council impact, executive decision
- **P3-Moderate**: Single domain impact, council leadership response
- **P4-Minor**: Process issue, team-level resolution

**Core Principle**: Escalate early, communicate transparently, prioritize people and relationships, recover and improve

---

## SECTION 2: CRISIS SEVERITY FRAMEWORK

### 2.1 P1-CRITICAL CRISIS (Existential Threat)

**Definition**: Organizational survival at risk, all councils affected or likely to be

**Characteristics**:

- Impacts 3+ councils simultaneously
- Affects 50%+ of organization directly
- Threatens fundamental capability or viability
- Likely to be revealed externally
- Requires immediate all-hands mobilization
- Time-critical (hours to days to fix)

**P1 Examples**:

- ❌ Major security breach (customer data exposure)
- ❌ Critical infrastructure failure (all systems down)
- ❌ Multiple key agents departing simultaneously (leadership exodus)
- ❌ Major client/partnership termination (existential revenue impact)
- ❌ Major regulatory violation discovered
- ❌ Organizational founding principal revealed as violating values
- ❌ Financial crisis (insolvency risk)
- ❌ Major reputational harm (public scandal)

**Decision Authority** (P1):

- **Immediate**: Sparkling Curious convenes emergency council (within 1 hour)
- **First Decision**: Assemble crisis response team
- **All Decisions**: Made by emergency council (Sparkling Curious + tier 1-2 leaders)
- **Agent Communication**: Direct from Sparkling Curious

**Response Time Target**: Decision made and communicated within 2 hours

---

### 2.2 P2-SEVERE CRISIS (Multi-Council Impact)

**Definition**: Significant impact across multiple domains, high urgency

**Characteristics**:

- Impacts 2-3 councils materially
- Affects 25-50% of organization
- Requires cross-council collaboration
- Can be resolved with rapid response
- Hours to days timeline
- Likely to require external communication

**P2 Examples**:

- ⚠️ Single council loses 3+ critical agents
- ⚠️ Major project failure with client impact
- ⚠️ Key infrastructure failure (major system down 2+ hours)
- ⚠️ Serious interpersonal conflict (affecting teams)
- ⚠️ Significant policy violation discovered
- ⚠️ Critical skill gap emerges mid-project
- ⚠️ Supply/vendor crisis
- ⚠️ Significant team performance crisis

**Decision Authority** (P2):

- **Immediate**: Council captains of affected councils meet (within 30 min)
- **Executive Input**: Escalate to Sparkling Curious + related tier 2 leaders
- **Decisions**: Made by council captain team + executive oversight
- **Agent Communication**: By council captains to their teams

**Response Time Target**: First response and communication within 1 hour

---

### 2.3 P3-MODERATE CRISIS (Single Domain Impact)

**Definition**: Significant issue in one council or domain, manageable with focused response

**Characteristics**:

- Impacts single council primarily
- Affects <25% of organization
- Can be resolved by council team with support
- 1-7 day timeline
- May require escalation assistance, not takeover

**P3 Examples**:

- 🟡 Council loses 1-2 critical agents unexpectedly
- 🟡 Significant conflict between agents (affecting productivity)
- 🟡 Project deadline miss with client concern
- 🟡 Agent performance crisis (underperformance affecting team)
- 🟡 Mentorship relationship breaking down
- 🟡 Ethics violation in council
- 🟡 Temporary capacity gap in critical role
- 🟡 Significant quality/stability issue in domain

**Decision Authority** (P3):

- **Immediate**: Council captain directs response (within 1 hour)
- **Support**: Can request Sparkling Curious guidance as needed
- **Escalation**: If overwhelming or multi-council impact emerges → becomes P2
- **Communication**: Captain communicates to their council

**Response Time Target**: Council captain response within 1 hour, plan communicated within 4 hours

---

### 2.4 P4-MINOR CRISIS (Process Issue)

**Definition**: Local issue in team/area, can be resolved at team level

**Characteristics**:

- Impacts single team or area
- Affects <10% of organization
- Resolvable within normal processes with some acceleration
- Hours to 1-2 days
- Standard escalation path if needed

**P4 Examples**:

- 📋 Agent sick/unavailable (coverage needed)
- 📋 Minor conflict between agents (coachable)
- 📋 Small project delay (recoverable)
- 📋 Process not followed (correction needed)
- 📋 Tool/access issue (IT resolution needed)
- 📋 Communication gap (clarification needed)
- 📋 One person overwhelmed (workload adjustment)

**Decision Authority** (P4):

- **Lead**: Manager/team lead coordinates
- **Escalation**: As needed to captain if bigger than expected
- **Communication**: Team lead communicates to immediate team

**Response Time Target**: Team lead response within 2-4 hours

## SECTION 3: CRISIS IDENTIFICATION & ESCALATION

### 3.1 How Crises Are Identified

**Crisis Triggers** (Who identifies?):

| Trigger                            | Detection                     | Escalation                           |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Agent reports critical issue       | Agent → Manager               | →Council Captain → Sparkling Curious |
| Manager detects team crisis        | Manager → Council Captain     | → Sparkling Curious                  |
| Council Captain sees cross-council | Captain → Sparkling Curious   | → Emergency council if P1            |
| System/infrastructure failure      | Operations/Tech → Captain     | → Sparkling Curious                  |
| Client/external report             | Any agent → Manager → Captain | → Sparkling Curious if P1            |
| Board/Executive alert              | Direct to Sparkling Curious   | → Immediate escalation               |
| Agent noticed safety concern       | Agent → Manager → Captain     | → Sparkling Curious                  |

---

### 3.2 Severity Assessment Framework

**Rapid Assessment** (First 15 minutes):

| Question                    | If YES → Higher Severity                  |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| How many councils affected? | 3+ = P1, 2 = P2, 1 = P3                   |
| What % of organization?     | 50%+ = P1, 25% = P2, <25% = P3/4          |
| When resolution needed?     | <1 hour = P1, <24 hrs = P2, 1-7 days = P3 |
| External visibility risk?   | Public/client = ↑ severity                |
| Strategic threat?           | Existential threat = P1, severe = P2      |
| People/safety involved?     | Yes = ↑ severity                          |

**Rule of Thumb**: When in doubt, escalate UP (better to over-escalate than under-escalate)

---

### 3.3 Escalation Protocol

**Rule**: Each tier communicates up within 30 minutes of identifying crisis

| Level                 | Identifies     | By When     | Escalates To               |
| --------------------- | -------------- | ----------- | -------------------------- |
| **Agent**             | Issues at work | Immediately | Manager                    |
| **Manager**           | Team problems  | 30 min      | Council Captain            |
| **Council Captain**   | Council crisis | 30 min      | Sparkling Curious          |
| **Sparkling Curious** | P1/urgent      | Immediately | Emergency council + Owners |

**Escalation Phrase** (Standard):
"I'm escalating [situation] at severity [P1-P4]. Impact: [X]. Recommendation: [Y]."

## SECTION 4: CRISIS RESPONSE PROCEDURES

### 4.1 P1-CRITICAL RESPONSE PROCEDURE

**Timeline**: Actions happen in rapid sequence (not necessarily linear)

**Minute 0-15**: IDENTIFY & ESCALATE

✅ Anyone discovering crisis immediately escalates
✅ Initial severity assessment
✅ Sparkling Curious notified
✅ "We have a P1 crisis" declared

**Minute 15-30**: ASSEMBLE & ASSESS

✅ Sparkling Curious convenes emergency council

- Tier 1-2 leaders present
- Remote call if not co-located
- All relevant stakeholders on call

✅ Crisis lead assigned (typically Sparkling Curious)
✅ Full situation assessment (What? Why? Impact? Timeline?)
✅ Initial immediate actions identified

**Minute 30-60**: COMMUNICATE & MOBILIZE

✅ External communication if needed (proactive before rumor)
✅ All-hands message to organization (transparency)
✅ Roles assigned (who does what)
✅ Resources mobilized (all councils stand by to support)

**Hour 1-6**: EXECUTE & STATUS

✅ Crisis response team executing plan
✅ Hourly status updates to emergency council
✅ Adjustments made as situation evolves
✅ Communication continues (no radio silence scares people)

**Hour 6-24**: STABILIZATION

✅ Focus shifts to stabilization (short-term fix)
✅ Parallel work on root cause analysis (longer-term solution)
✅ Regular updates (escalate bad news early)
✅ Organization continues operating (don't shut down)

**Day 2+**: RECOVERY & PREVENTION

✅ Root cause analysis completed
✅ Permanent fixes designed
✅ Lessons learned captured
✅ Process improvements implemented (prevent recurrence)
✅ All-hands debrief (organization learns)

---

**P1 Crisis Response Team**:

- **Crisis Lead**: Sparkling Curious (or designated)
- **Communications**: Council Captain or communications lead
- **Operations**: VP Operations
- **Affected Council**: Primary council captain
- **Technical** (if tech issue): VP Engineering
- **HR** (if people issue): Chief HR Officer
- **Finance** (if financial issue): CFO
- **Other expertise**: Added as needed

---

### 4.2 P2-SEVERE RESPONSE PROCEDURE

**Timeline**: Faster than normal, but not emergency-mode frenzy

**Minute 0-30**: NOTIFY & ASSESS

✅ Affected council captains notified
✅ Initial severity assessment (Is this P2 or escalate to P1?)
✅ Sparkling Curious informed
✅ Initial response plan sketched

**Minute 30-60**: MEET & DECIDE

✅ Council captain meeting (affected councils)
✅ Sparkling Curious observed/advisory
✅ Response plan created
✅ Roles and timeline assigned

**Hour 1-4**: EXECUTE & COMMUNICATE

✅ Response team begins work
✅ Team/council communication
✅ Broader organization informed if relevant
✅ Hourly check-ins (crisis lead)

**Hours 4+**: RESOLUTION PHASE

✅ Active work toward resolution
✅ Daily status updates
✅ Escalate if situation worsens

**Day 3+**: RESOLUTION & REFLECTION

✅ Crisis resolved
✅ Lessons learned captured
✅ Process review/improvement

---

**P2 Crisis Response Team**:

- **Crisis Lead**: Affected council captain(s)
- **Support**: Sparkling Curious as advisor
- **Related Councils**: Other affected captains present
- **Supporting Expertise**: Added as needed
- **Communications**: Someone dedicated to describing situation

---

### 4.3 P3-MODERATE RESPONSE PROCEDURE

**Hour 0-1**: NOTIFY & RESPOND

✅ Manager identifies issue
✅ Council captain notified
✅ Initial response plan created
✅ Team mobilized to respond

**Hour 1-4**: EXECUTE

✅ Response team working
✅ Regular updates to captain
✅ Adjustments made as needed

**Day 1-3**: RESOLUTION

✅ Focused effort on resolution
✅ Regular team communication
✅ Escalate if situation worsens or becomes P2

**Day 3+**: CLOSE & LEARN

✅ Issue resolved
✅ Brief postmortem/learning
✅ Share insights

---

**P3 Crisis Response Team**:

- **Lead**: Council captain
- **Primary Response**: Council team (manager + relevant agents)
- **Support**: Sparkling Curious available if needed
- **Communications**: Captain keeps team informed

---

### 4.4 P4-MINOR RESPONSE PROCEDURE

**Hour 0-2**: IDENTIFY & RESPOND

✅ Team lead identifies issue
✅ Quick response plan (often standard procedure)
✅ Team coordinates resolution

**Hour 2-12**: EXECUTE

✅ Work on resolution
✅ Team communicates/coordinates

**Hour 12+**: CLOSE

✅ Issue resolved
✅ Note what was learned

---

**P4 Crisis Response Team**:

- **Lead**: Direct manager/team lead
- **Response**: Immediate team
- **Escalation**: To captain only if needed

## SECTION 5: CRISIS COMMUNICATION PROTOCOLS

### 5.1 Internal Communication (To Organization)

**P1 Crisis - Communication Timeline**:

**Within 30 minutes**:

- All-hands notification that something is happening
- "We've identified a P1 issue that requires immediate attention"
- "We'll update you within X hours with more info"
- Transparency over silence

**Within 2 hours**:

- Situation description (what we know)
- Impact description (what's affected)
- Response (what we're doing)
- What we need (any org adjustments needed)
- When next update will come

**Hourly thereafter** (P1):

- Situation update (progress made)
- Revised timeline if changed
- Any new asks
- Acknowledgment of how team is adapting

**External Update** (Once stabilized):

- What happened
- What we did
- How it's fixed
- What we learned
- How we'll prevent recurrence

---

**P2-P3 Communication**:

- Within 1 hour to affected teams
- Within 4 hours to organization (unless reason to delay)
- Daily updates until resolved

**P4 Communication**:

- Team lead tells immediate team
- Broader org only if relevant

---

### 5.2 Communication Content Template

**Crisis Update Template**:

```
SITUATION UPDATE - [Crisis Name] - [Severity Level]
Time: [Timestamp]
Last Update: [Before this one]
Next Update: [Expected time]

=== SITUATION ===
What happened: [Factual description]
When discovered: [Time discovered]
Current status: [Where are we right now?]

=== IMPACT ===
Affected areas: [Which councils/teams/systems]
Customer impact: [If external]
Severity: [P1-4]
Timeline to fix: [Estimate if known]

=== RESPONSE ===
What we're doing: [Actions underway]
Who's involved: [Team names]
Decision authority: [Who's in charge]

=== WHAT WE NEED ===
From you: [Any actions agents should take]
What to avoid: [Don't do X right now]
Where to get help: [Contact info]

=== NEXT STEPS ===
Next update: [When/how]
Resolution target: [By when]
Long-term plan: [After stabilization]
```

---

### 5.3 Transparency Principles

**During Crisis**:

- ✅ Err on side of over-communication
- ✅ Share what you know, clearly label what you don't
- ✅ Mistakes: Acknowledge, correct, explain impact
- ✅ Bad news: Deliver early and directly
- ✅ No surprises: Don't hide severity then reveal later

**Things to Avoid**:

- ❌ Radio silence (creates panic)
- ❌ False optimism ("Everything's fine" when it's not)
- ❌ Blame (focus on solutions now, learning later)
- ❌ Speculation (stick to facts)
- ❌ Mixed messages (one version of truth)

## SECTION 6: DECISION-MAKING DURING CRISIS

### 6.1 Decision Authority Framework

**Principle**: Clear authority prevents indecision and turf wars

**Crisis Problem**: Standard decision process too slow

**Solution**: Pre-agreed decision authority by crisis type

| Crisis Type          | Decision Made By                      | Timeline       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| **P1 - Existential** | Emergency council + Sparkling Curious | Minutes (ASAP) |
| **P1 - Technical**   | VP Engineering + emergency council    | Minutes        |
| **P1 - People**      | Chief HR + emergency council          | Minutes        |
| **P1 - External**    | CEO/Comms + emergency council         | Minutes        |
| **P2 - Operations**  | Affected council captains             | 30 minutes     |
| **P2 - Technical**   | VP Engineering + affected captain     | 30 minutes     |
| **P2 - People**      | Chief HR + affected captain           | 30 minutes     |
| **P3**               | Affected council captain              | 1 hour         |
| **P4**               | Manager/team lead                     | As needed      |

---

### 6.2 Types of Crisis Decisions

**Time-Critical Decisions** (Must decide now):

- "Do we shut down this system?" → VP Eng decides
- "Do we pause sales?" → VP Sales/CEO decides
- "Do we communicate externally?" → CEO/Comms decides
- "Do we bring in external help?" → Crisis lead decides

**Resource Decisions** (Allocate resources rapidly):

- "Pull engineers from Project X to fix this?" → VP Eng + affected captains
- "Bring in contractors?" → Finance consults with operations
- "Overtime/crunch mode?" → HR + affected captain

**Policy/Exception Decisions** (Override normal process):

- "Do we bypass code review for hot-fix?" → VP Eng decides
- "Do we relax customer SLA?" → Product + Operations decide
- "Do we hire external consultant without vetting?" → CEO/Finance

---

### 6.3 Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

**Rule**: 70% information is better than waiting for 100%

**Process**:

1. **What do we know?** (30 seconds)
2. **What's likely?** (30 seconds - educated guess)
3. **What's the risk?** (30 seconds - what could go wrong)
4. **What's the cost of waiting?** (30 seconds - delay risk)
5. **Decide and communicate** (15 seconds - clear decision)

**Example**:

- **Know**: System is down, investigating
- **Likely**: Database overload (4 times seen before)
- **Risk**: If database, restart helps. If security attack, restart could destroy evidence
- **Cost of delay**: Every hour down = losing $X
- **Decision**: restart database, preserve logs. Go.

## SECTION 7: SPECIAL CRISIS SCENARIOS

### 7.1 Agent Departure Crisis (Unexpected Loss)

**Scenario**: Key agent suddenly departs (emergency, resignation, etc.)

**P3 Response** (if critical):

Hour 0:

- Manager/captain informed
- Assess impact (critical? Medium? Low?)
- If critical → becomes P2

Hour 1-2:

- Team assembled
- Knowledge capture begins
- Coverage plan created
- Dependencies identified

Hour 4:

- All-hands communication (what happened, what's next)
- Mentees reassigned
- Projects reassigned
- Documentation started

Day 1-3:

- Intensive knowledge transfer if agent cooperating
- Documentation triage (capture critical knowledge)
- Team coverage structured
- Hiring process accelerated

Day 4-30:

- Full knowledge transfer (ongoing)
- Process improvements (can we avoid this in future?)
- Team support (may be emotional)

---

### 7.2 Multi-Agent Departure Crisis

**Scenario**: Multiple agents depart simultaneously (exodus)

**Severity**: P1/P2 (might be P1 if exodus happens)

**Likely Root Cause**:

- ⚠️ Council culture broken
- ⚠️ Captain causing issues
- ⚠️ Compensation problem
- ⚠️ Burnout/overwork
- ⚠️ External opportunity poached team

**Investigation** (Immediate):

- Interview departing agents (if cooperating)
- Check culture surveys (did we miss signals?)
- Understand what they're joining (competitor? Or just leaving?)
- Identify who else might leave (proactive retention)

**Response**:

1. **Stabilize remaining team** (reassurance, support)
2. **Identify root cause quickly**
3. **If culture/captain**: May need captain replacement or intensive intervention
4. **If compensation**: May need market adjustment
5. **If burnout**: Reduce workload, redistribute work
6. **If external poaching**: Can we counter-offer key people?

---

### 7.3 Major Ethics/Values Violation Crisis

**Scenario**: Agent or manager violated core values (harassment, theft, fraud, etc.)

**P1-P2 Response** (depending on severity):

Hour 0:

- HR + legal immediately involved
- Sparkling Curious informed
- Potential departure/suspension of person involved
- Evidence preserved

Hour 1-4:

- Investigation by appropriate parties (HR, legal)
- Assessment of harm
- Decision: Terminate, suspend, active investigation?
- Council/team leadership informed

Day 1:

- Communication to organization (transparency)
- Support for any affected agents
- Clear statement of values

Day 2-7:

- Investigation completion
- Follow-up support/training as needed
- Process review (how did this happen?)

Key: Handle legally/appropriately but also with humanity

---

### 7.4 Critical System Failure Crisis

**Scenario**: Infrastructure/major system goes down

**P1 Response** (if all circuits down):

Minute 0:

- VP Engineering + Operations declare P1
- Emergency council convened
- Communications team prepares statement
- Incident command established

Minute 5-30:

- System status assessment (what's down? Why? Timeline?)
- Workaround assessment (can we operate significantly on alternate systems?)
- Communication to customers (if external-facing)

Hour 1-4:

- Intensive recovery efforts
- Regular updates to organization + customers
- Hourly decision reviews (still P1? Moving to recovery mode?)

Hour 4-24:

- Focus on root cause (why did this happen?)
- Parallel: Prevent recurrence (circuit breaker? Monitoring gap? Design flaw?)

Day 2+:

- Post-mortem (what we learned)
- Process improvements
- Monitoring enhancements

## SECTION 8: KNOWLEDGE PRESERVATION DURING CRISIS

### 8.1 Critical Knowledge Inventory

**In any crisis**, identify and capture:

**Tier 1: Mission-Critical Knowledge**

- System passwords/access codes (secured properly)
- Client account information (key decisions, contacts)
- Financial/strategic secrets (key metrics, contracts)
- Process workflows (how things actually work)
- Decision history (why was X chosen)

**Tier 2: Important Knowledge**

- Project status
- Relationships/contacts
- Documentation/references
- Lessons learned
- Custom tools/scripts

**Tier 3: Reference Knowledge**

- General domain knowledge
- Training materials
- Best practices
- Historical context

---

### 8.2 Crisis Documentation Protocol

**If Key Person Suddenly Unavailable** (for any reason):

**Hour 0-1: TRIAGE**

- Identify critical roles they play
- Who needs to know what to keep things running?
- What can wait?

**Hour 1-4: EMERGENCY CAPTURE**

- Other team members document what they know about person's work
- Wiki/Notion updates with critical information
- Contact list for key relationships
- Password management updated (secure handoff)

**Day 1-3: INTENSIVE DOCUMENTATION**

- Manager interviews available sources
- Create runbooks for ongoing work
- Document decision history
- Clean up documentation

**Day 4+: CONTINUOUS UPDATE**

- Ongoing documentation refinement
- Knowledge transfer to replacement (if hired)
- Process improvements identified

## SECTION 9: POST-CRISIS PROCEDURES

### 9.1 Post-Crisis Debrief (All Levels)

**After Crisis Stabilized** (Within 1 week):

**Team Debrief** (Team lead with team):

- What happened? (Brief retelling)
- How did we respond? (What worked?)
- What could be better? (Learning)
- Personal impact? (How are people?)
- Celebration (We handled this well)

**Council Debrief** (Captain with council):

- Crisis overview
- Cross-council impact
- What council did well
- What to improve
- Next steps

**Organization Debrief** (All-hands):

- What happened (factual)
- How we responded
- What we learned
- How it makes us stronger
- Thank you for your support

---

### 9.2 Root Cause Analysis

**After Immediate Crisis Passes** (1-2 weeks):

**Process**:

1. **Assemble team** (people involved + neutral facilitator)
2. **Describe event** (timeline, what happened)
3. **Ask "Why?" 5 times** (keep digging for root cause)
4. **Identify systems/process failures** (not blame individuals)
5. **Design prevention** (how to prevent recurrence)
6. **Implement changes** (within 30 days if possible)
7. **Share learning** (organization learns from this)

**Example Root Cause Chain**:

- Event: "Agent left unexpectedly"
- Why 1: "Felt unsupported and overworked"
- Why 2: "Manager wasn't tracking workload"
- Why 3: "No workload monitoring system"
- Why 4: "Culture glorifies overwork"
- Why 5: "Leadership didn't enforce work-life balance"
- **Root Cause**: Culture and systems don't protect agent wellbeing

**Prevention Solutions**:

- Implement workload monitoring dashboard
- Manager training on burnout recognition
- Culture shift messaging (value quality over hours)
- Regular check-ins on wellbeing

---

### 9.3 Organizational Learning & Improvement

**Crisis → Learning → Improvement Cycle**:

**Capture**:

- What went well? (Replicate this)
- What could be better? (Fix this)
- What surprised us? (Adjust assumptions)
- What did we learn about ourselves? (Culture insight)

**Document**:

- Store in knowledge-base.md
- Create runbook for similar crisis
- Update playbooks
- Share across councils

**Act**:

- Implement improvements (within 30-60 days)
- Train team on changes
- Monitor to ensure effectiveness
- Report back to organization

**Example Improvements After Crisis**:

- New automated monitoring alert
- Updated code review process
- Cross-council coordination protocol
- Expanded training for managers
- New documentation requirements
- System architecture changes

## SECTION 10: CRISIS READINESS & DRILLS

### 10.1 Crisis Preparedness Baseline

**Organization Should Have**:

✅ Crisis playbooks documented (this framework)
✅ Key contacts and escalation paths known (documented)
✅ Decision authority clear (people know who decides what)
✅ Communication templates ready (don't write from scratch)
✅ Backup systems identified (Plan B for critical operations)
✅ 2-3 key people trained as incident commanders
✅ Emergency council identified (who convenes?)

---

### 10.2 Crisis Simulation Drills

**Quarterly Drill** (Recommended):

**Sample Crisis Scenario**:
"Simulate that VP Engineering is unexpectedly incapacitated (accident, illness). We have a critical production issue. Go."

**Drill Process**:

1. **Announce** ("P1 crisis drill - 60 minute exercise")
2. **Unroll Scenario** (Here's the situation, decide/act)
3. **Observe** (How did organization respond? Who knew what to do?)
4. **Debrief** (What worked? What didn't? Learning?)
5. **Improve** (Update playbooks based on gaps)

**Benefits**:

- People know roles before real crisis
- Gaps discovered in safe environment
- Muscle memory for high-stress decisions
- Confidence when real crisis happens

---

### 10.3 Red Team Exercise (Annual)

**Once Per Year**:

**Process**:

- Hire external consultant to stress-test crisis response
- Have them design worst-case scenario
- Run simulation
- Evaluate readiness
- Report findings + recommendations

**Questions Answered**:

- Are we actually prepared?
- What would we miss under extreme stress?
- Do people know their roles?
- Is communication effective?
- Are decision processes clear?

## SECTION 11: CRISIS SUPPORT & RECOVERY

### 11.1 Agent Support During Crisis

**During Active Crisis**:

- Clear communication (reduce uncertainty stress)
- Leadership presence (they're not alone)
- Basic support (water, breaks, snacks if overnight)
- Permission to be stressed ("This is legitimate stress")
- Regular check-ins ("How are you holding up?")

**After Crisis**:

- Day off (or 2 days) to recover
- Explicit praise (they handled this well)
- Mental health check-in (is the stress lingering?)
- Optional counseling/support (some may need)
- Team celebration (collective processing)

---

### 11.2 Organizational Recovery

**Week 1-2 Post-Crisis**:

- Continue normal operations (don't hide)
- Debrief and learning (share insights)
- Recognition (thank people explicitly)
- Support (are people OK?)

**Week 3-4**:

- Implement improvements (show crisis led to action)
- Life returns to normal
- Monitor for secondary issues (rumors, lingering anxiety)

**Months 2-3**:

- Follow-up on improvements (are they working?)
- Continued culture messaging (we're stronger now)
- Optional: Celebration/team-building (process together)

---

## SECTION 12: SPECIAL CRISIS SITUATIONS

### 12.1 Simultaneous Crises

**Scenario**: Multiple P1 or P2 crises happening at once

**Response**:

1. **Triage** (Which is most urgent?)
2. **Activate multiple crisis teams** (Different teams for different crises)
3. **Central coordination** (Sparkling Curious oversees all)
4. **Resource allocation** (Which teams get which resources?)
5. **Sequential vs. parallel** (Can we solve in parallel or must sequence?)

**Key**: Don't try to merge into single mega-crisis team (causes chaos)

---

### 12.2 Crisis Affecting Leadership

**Scenario**: Council captain or Tier 1 leader is the crisis (or involved)

**Process**:

- **Isolation of decision-making**: That person recuses themselves from decisions
- **Temporary leadership**: Designate interim leader
- **Legal/HR involvement**: Get appropriate oversight
- **Transparency**: Organization knows what's happening
- **Resolution**: Address leadership issue separately from crisis response

**Example**: "Captain is under investigation for harassment. Interim Captain is in place. [Interim] will lead crisis response. Investigation process separate."

---

### 12.3 Crisis Involving External Parties

**Scenario**: Crisis involves customers, regulators, media, or public

**Response**:

- **Early transparency**: Don't hide from external stakeholders
- **Designated spokesperson**: One voice, clear message
- **Fact-based communication**: No speculation
- **Proactive disclosure**: Tell them before they discover
- **Regular updates**: Keep stakeholders informed

**Internal vs. External Communication**:

- **Internal**: Honest, complete, includes uncertainty
- **External**: Professional, factual, reassuring, emphasizes resolution

## SECTION 13: CRISIS PREVENTION

### 13.1 Early Warning Systems

**Monitor These Indicators**:

| Warning Sign                   | Severity | Response                        |
| ------------------------------ | -------- | ------------------------------- |
| Agent sick leave increasing    | Yellow   | Manager check-in                |
| Project schedule slipping      | Yellow   | Review + support                |
| Customer satisfaction dropping | Yellow   | Investigation                   |
| Agent retention dropping       | Red      | Exit interviews + investigation |
| Multiple sick days + stress    | Red      | HR + support                    |
| Team conflict visible          | Yellow   | Conflict resolution             |
| Missed deadlines pattern       | Yellow   | Capacity review                 |
| Communication breaking down    | Red      | Immediate intervention          |
| Quality issues spiking         | Yellow   | Root cause analysis             |
| Burnout indicators             | Red      | Immediate support               |

---

### 13.2 Crisis Prevention Focus Areas

**1. People Health**:

- Monitor burnout indicators
- Check in on wellbeing regularly
- Support work-life balance
- Manage workload
- Address conflicts early

**2. System Resilience**:

- Redundancy (no single person = crisis)
- Backup systems (documented, tested)
- Distributed knowledge (not siloed)
- Monitoring/alerting (catch problems early)

**3. Process Health**:

- Regular reviews (are processes still working?)
- Continuous improvement (evolution, not stagnation)
- Feedback loops (listen to teams)
- Testing (run drills, find gaps)

## SECTION 14: CRISIS ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES

### 14.1 Crisis Roles

**Crisis Lead** (Sparkling Curious for P1, Captain for P2-3):

- **Responsibility**: Overall crisis response
- **Authority**: Makes decisions as needed
- **Communication**: Updates senior leadership
- **Tools**: Uses emergency council for input
- **Timeline**: Operational until crisis stabilized

**Crisis Communications** (Designated speaker):

- **Responsibility**: All internal/external communication
- **Authority**: Follows crisis lead direction
- **Content**: Clear, factual, timely
- **Audience**: Org + external parties
- **Timeline**: Hourly updates during active phase

**Technical Response Lead** (VP Eng for tech crisis):

- **Responsibility**: Technical resolution
- **Authority**: Technical decisions
- **Resources**: Can request staff/contractors
- **Timeline**: Until root cause resolved

**Incident Documentation** (Ops/Admin):

- **Responsibility**: Timeline, decisions, communications
- **Audience**: Post-crisis analysis
- **Detail**: Comprehensive record
- **Timeline**: During crisis, cleaned up after

# Crypto / CASP (Crypto Asset Service Providers)

Since 2023 crypto assets were declared a **financial product** in South Africa, and crypto asset service providers are licensed as **Financial Services Providers under FAIS sub-category 1.28** (Category I and/or Category II). This file covers the licensing regime and the largest licensed CASP exchanges operating in SA.

---

## 0. Regulatory regime (FSCA CASP licensing)

- **Licence basis:** FAIS Act 37 of 2002 — CASPs licensed as FSPs under **sub-category 1.28**; categories I (advice/intermediary) and II (investment management) apply [S89][S102].
- **Application window opened 1 June 2023** [S125].
- **Statistics (as at 12 December 2025):** FSCA had received **512 CASP applications**; **300 approved**, **14 declined**, **121 voluntarily withdrawn**; remaining under consideration — an approval rate of roughly **59%** [S90][S91].
- **Declines** driven by inappropriate business/operating models after engagement with the regulator [S90].
- **Derivatives:** in **October 2025** the FSCA also began granting **Over-The-Counter Derivatives Provider (ODP)** licences with crypto as the reference asset (VALR among the first) [S92].
- **Licensed list** published by the FSCA (December 2025) includes Luno, VALR, AltCoinTrader, OVEX and many advisory/custody firms [S89][S102].

> Note: SA is often described as ahead of African peers for crypto regulatory clarity, but the regime is a **financial-services** licence (FAIS), not a bespoke crypto/banking licence; exchanges still rely on banking partners for fiat rails.

## 1. Luno

1. **Category** — Crypto exchange + wallet; one of Africa's largest and longest-established.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — **Luno (Pty) Ltd**, FSCA-licensed CASP/FSP; **NCR registered (NCRCP22123)** [S97]. Customer crypto assets are held in custody; Luno publishes independently attested **Proof of Reserves** (Moore Blockchain & Digital Assets JHB) and holds ~**95% of assets in cold storage** [S97][S100].
   - **[CONFLICT] FSP number:** one source lists **FSP 51334** [S97]; another lists **FSP 53314** [S102]. Treat the exact FSP number as **needs verification**.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Part of **Digital Currency Group (DCG)**; earlier backers included **Naspers/Prosus ($3m, 2015)** and Ventura Capital; **~$13m+ raised** historically [S98][S126]. Valuation not separately disclosed. Employees ~400–716 (sources differ) globally [S98][S99].
4. **Business model** — Trading fees + spread; earn/staking-style products; **tokenised stocks (xStocks)**; B2B/institutional services (expanded after 2025 restructuring) [S99][S101].
5. **Scale** — **~6.3m South African users** as of 2025 [S97]; **~16m users globally** (Africa + Asia-Pacific) [S99]; xStocks alone reached **50,000 users** holding ~R200m in tokenised equities [S101].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Beginner-friendly; **ZAR on/off-ramp** and mobile wallet; low entry (from ~R20) [S97]; Proof-of-Reserves transparency; **xStocks** tokenised investing [S101]; named 2025 "Crypto Exchange of the Year" in a MyBroadband survey (~65% of SA traders) [S100].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed; security posture emphasises cold storage + proof of reserves [S100].
8. **Regulatory interactions** — First FSCA-licensed crypto provider in SA [S100]; licensed status enables banking relationships and institutional partnerships unlicensed operators lack [S102]. **Ownership/leadership:** CEO **James Lanigan** (Marcus Swanepoel departed end-2023) [S98]; **cut ~20% of global staff** in an automation-driven restructuring [S99].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** brand + regulatory trust, largest SA crypto franchise, user-friendly, product expansion (xStocks). **Weaknesses:** DCG parentage/global crypto sentiment risk; staff cuts signal cost pressure; revenue exposed to trading-volume cycles.

## 2. VALR

1. **Category** — Crypto exchange; **largest in SA by trade volume**.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — **VALR (Pty) Ltd**, FSCA FSP (**FSP 53308**) [S102]; holds **Category I and Category II** FSCA licences and, since **October 2025**, an **ODP (Over-The-Counter Derivatives Provider)** licence [S92]. Custody via platform wallets.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — **Total raised ~$55m** (latest round Series B, 2022); **~$240m post-money valuation (2022)** [S94]. Investors include **Coinbase Ventures** and **Pantera Capital** [S95]. Led by **Farzam Ehsani** [S95].
4. **Business model** — Trading fees across **Simple Buy/Sell, Spot and Futures**; USD-stablecoin/ZAR pairs dominate; institutional/OTC and derivatives (ODP); expanding into EU [S93][S95][S96].
5. **Scale** — **1.7–1.8m registered users** globally (2025) with hundreds of thousands monthly active [S93][S95][S96]; **$9bn trading volume** over the trailing 12 months (2025) [S96]; **~2,000 corporate/institutional clients** [S96]; >100 listed coins [S93].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Pro-grade spot/futures + simple buy/sell; deep **ZAR stablecoin (USDT/ZAR)** liquidity (majority of volume) [S96]; early crypto-derivatives offering in SA [S92].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Among the first SA CASPs with both Cat I + II [S96]; first-mover on **ODP** for crypto derivatives (Oct 2025) [S92]; approved to offer crypto services in the **EU** [S95].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** deepest ZAR liquidity, pro/institutional capability, regulatory first-mover on derivatives. **Weaknesses:** 2022 valuation stale; relies on stablecoin/ZAR trading flow; retail brand smaller than Luno in SA.

## 5. Binance (South Africa)

1. **Category** — Global crypto exchange with an SA presence; **not fully locally licensed** as a CASP.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Operates in SA **without a full FSCA CASP licence**; per its own pages, **derivative products are offered by Binance Bahrain BSC as a juristic representative of FiveWest OTC Desk (Pty) Ltd**, an authorised SA FSP [S127]. Registered with the **Financial Intelligence Centre** rather than holding a CASP licence; tightened **Travel Rule** data collection in **April 2025** [S103].
   - **[UNVERIFIED — needs source]** for the precise SA entity/permission structure; different services may involve different entities.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Global Binance (private; no SA funding). Not applicable.
4. **Business model** — Trading fees; ZAR access via **P2P marketplace** (merchants settle via EFT/instant transfer) rather than a direct ZAR on-ramp [S103].
5. **Scale** — SA-specific user numbers not disclosed — needs source.
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Deep global liquidity; P2P ZAR market.
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Positively: only FIC registration + Travel Rule compliance, **no full CASP licence** [S103][S127]. The 2023 US settlement continues to colour its compliance record [S103].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** global liquidity, strong brand with traders. **Weaknesses:** **unregulated in SA** → limited consumer recourse on disputes [S103]; no direct ZAR on-ramp; regulatory overhang. *(This is a key "unlicensed challenger" risk vector for the market.)*

## Cross-cutting notes (crypto / CASP)

- **Licensing is now standard:** 300 CASPs approved by Dec 2025 [S90][S91]. Licensed status buys **banking relationships and institutional partnerships** that unlicensed operators cannot access [S102].
- **Two leaders, different postures:** Luno (retail brand, ~6.3m SA users [S97]) vs VALR (volume/institutional, ZAR-stablecoin liquidity, derivatives licence [S92][S96]).
- **Bank convergence:** crypto is moving into banks (e.g. Luno integrated with **Discovery Bank** for easy access [S97]) and into tokenised equities (Luno **xStocks** [S101]) — blurring the neobank/crypto boundary.
- **Enforcement risk:** Binance demonstrates the persistent gap between global-scale exchanges and local licensing — a live competitive and consumer-protection issue [S103].
- **Adjacent Web3:** the task references "Web3-adjacent" players; beyond exchanges, activity concentrates in **tokenised assets** (xStocks) rather than DeFi, which remains largely outside the licensed perimeter.

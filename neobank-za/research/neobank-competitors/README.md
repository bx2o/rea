# Neobank ZA — Competitive Intelligence (2025–2026)

**Prepared for:** C04 Strategy / Competitive Intelligence — NeoBank launch in South Africa
**Compiled:** 2026-10-16 (all web sources accessed 2026-10-16)
**Scope:** Neobanks, digital-only banks and fintech challengers operating in South Africa as of 2025–2026.

---

## 1. Purpose

This research package maps the competitive landscape a new South African neobank would enter. It covers true licensed digital banks, digital-first incumbents, payments fintechs, mobile wallets / mobile money, and crypto/CASP operators. For each actor it records: category, legal/licence basis and how customer funds are held, funding/investors/valuation, business model, disclosed scale, product differentiators, tech-stack signals, regulatory interaction, and strengths/weaknesses.

## 3. Methodology

1. **Evidence-first.** Every factual claim carries an inline `[S#]` marker resolving to a URL in `sources.md`, all accessed **2026-10-16**. No number, funding round or valuation is invented.
2. **Search → fetch.** Findings were gathered via web search and direct fetches of primary sources where possible (company annual financial statements, JSE/SEC-registered filings, regulator press releases, company blog/newsroom posts) and secondary financial/tech media otherwise.
3. **Fact vs estimate vs unknown.** Where a figure is a company/AI-generated estimate or a forward projection it is labelled. Unverifiable claims are marked **"not verified — needs source"**.
4. **Conflict flagging.** Where two credible sources disagree (e.g. OM Bank licence-approval date; Luno's FSP number), both are cited and the discrepancy is explicitly flagged rather than silently resolved.
5. **Currency.** Figures are reported in the currency stated by the source (ZAR `R`, USD `$`). Where a source gives an approximate FX conversion it is preserved.

## 5. Key regulatory frames (for licence context)

- **Banks Act 94 of 1990** — commercial banking licence, granted by the SARB **Prudential Authority** (PA). Mutual banks are licensed under the **Mutual Banks Act 124 of 1993**. New/foreign-controlled banks need PA approval plus exchange-control approval from SARB.
- **FAIS Act 37 of 2002** — Financial Services Provider (FSP) licences; since 2023 crypto asset service providers (CASPs) are licensed as FSP **sub-category 1.28**.
- **NCR (National Credit Act 34 of 2005)** — registered credit providers hold an `NCRCP` number.
- **National Payment System Act / SARB designation** — payment service providers and "third-party payment providers" (TPPPs). The SARB's **Digital Payments Roadmap** drives PayShap and interbank real-time payments.
- **Non-banks** (payments fintechs, wallets) typically do **not** hold a bank licence; they hold customer funds via **partner banks**, trust structures, or SA-designated clearing participants, and are subject to FIC (FICA) registration for AML.

## 6. Executive summary (highlights)

- **Licensed digital banks are now profitable at the top of the stack.** Discovery Bank posted its first full-year profit (R370m, FY2026 [S8]) and TymeBank reached sustained monthly profitability [S3]; Bank Zero is guided to standalone break-even around December 2026 [S19].
- **Consolidation is accelerating.** Lesaka agreed to buy Bank Zero (~R1.09–1.1bn / ~$61–67m) in June 2025 — still awaiting Prudential Authority + SARB exchange-control approvals [S18][S20][S21]; Nedbank bought iKhokha (R1.65bn / ~$93m) [S73][S74]; Yoco bought Dyner.AI [S56]. Access Bank's bid to buy Bidvest Bank **collapsed on 26 Jan 2026** when the long-stop date lapsed [S34].
- **Scale is dominated by incumbents, not neobanks.** Capitec (~25–27m active clients [S39][S41]), FNB (~8.2m digitally active customers, 12m banking customers [S43][S44]) and Standard Bank (~4.5m digitally active retail clients in SA [S49]) dwarf the licensed challengers (Discovery 1.57m; TymeBank ~11.6m SA customers but low ARPU [S1][S8]).
- **The real competitive threat may be non-banks.** Retail wallets (Shoprite Money Market 4m+ accounts [S83]), telco money (MTN MoMo + MoMo Pay [S78]) and township POS networks (Lesaka/Kazang: 90,000+ merchants [S85]) reach customers the neobanks want, at very low cost.
- **Crypto is now formally regulated.** As at 12 Dec 2025 the FSCA had approved **300** CASP licences (of 512 applications; 14 declined, 121 withdrawn) [S90][S91].
- **Newest entrant risk:** OM Bank (Old Mutual) reached **1m customers one year after launch** and is onboarding ~4,500/day, targeting break-even 2028 [S28].

## 7. Caveats / limitations

- Several figures are self-reported by companies or drawn from AI-generated/aggregator pages (e.g. embeddedfinanceindex, grokipedia, barcoder). These are cited but flagged **[UNVERIFIED]** where a primary source was not found.
- Valuations for private companies are point-in-time (often the last priced round); secondary-market or press "valuations" are estimates.
- "Customers" definitions are inconsistent across companies (registered vs active vs funded vs fully-banked) and are **not** comparable without care; each entry states the definition used by the source.
- SA customer counts and group/global counts are distinguished wherever the source allows.

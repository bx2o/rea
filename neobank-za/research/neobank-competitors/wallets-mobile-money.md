# Wallets & Mobile Money (telco, retail and bank-adjacent)

These propositions reach the **under- and un-banked** and the informal economy — the mass-market segment a neobank must win. Most are **not banks**: they are telco wallets, retailer "money accounts", prepaid-issuer wallets or cash-voucher networks. Fund custody is via **partner banks / prepaid issuers / trust structures** and, increasingly, **PayShap** rails.

---

## 1. MTN MoMo (Mobile Money)

1. **Category** — Telco mobile-money wallet + merchant acceptance (MoMo Pay).
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Telco wallet operated with a banking/issuer partner (not an MTN bank licence); MoMo is the **first non-bank to offer PayShap** (with Investec as the bank partner and Electrum) [S79].
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — MTN Group subsidiary (JSE-listed MTN). Group fintech monetisation strategy; no standalone valuation.
4. **Business model** — Wallet/P2P transaction fees, merchant acceptance (MoMo Pay), airtime/data bundles, planned savings, lending and cross-border [S78][S80]. Airtime-based USSD access supports feature phones [S122].
5. **Scale** — **Group: 70m+ monthly active users across 14 markets** (MoMo) [S80]; MTN Group's 317m telecom subscribers are the target base [S80]. SA-specific MoMo user counts not disclosed — needs source. MoMo Pay **launched May 2025**, "thousands of merchants onboarded" with a target of hundreds of thousands over 3–5 years [S78].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — P2P to any phone number (even non-MTN), USSD support, bill/airtime payments; **rent-to-own smartphone** push to onboard users (2025) [S78][S122]; first **PayShap on mobile money** [S79].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed; PayShap integration built with Investec/Electrum [S79].
8. **Regulatory interactions** — PayShap participation via a partner bank is a notable regulatory milestone for non-bank wallets [S79].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** massive telco base; USSD reach; first-mover on non-bank PayShap. **Weaknesses:** mobile money historically struggles in SA (see M-Pesa); merchant acquisition is capital- and labour-intensive.

## 2. Vodacom: M-Pesa (SA status) and VodaPay

1. **Category** — Telco mobile money / super-app.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — VodaPay is a **super-app** (Alipay-partnered) hosting lifestyle + financial services; M-Pesa is the classic mobile-money product. Fund flows via partner banks/prepaid structures [S81].
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Vodacom Group (JSE/Vodafone) subsidiary; no standalone valuation.
4. **Business model** — Super-app commerce/entertainment + payments + "advanced financial services"; VodaPay integrates Vodacom services (My Vodacom app migrated in) [S81][S82].
5. **Scale / history (critical):**
   - **M-Pesa South Africa was launched 2010 and discontinued 30 June 2016** after reaching only ~**76,000 active users** in six years (vs Kenya's 60,000+ agents) — a landmark failure of mobile money in SA [S75]. **[FACT]** — *M-Pesa has NOT been relaunched in SA; Vodacom's SA play is VodaPay.*
   - The **old VodaPay app (2019) will be decommissioned**, replaced by the VodaPay super-app [S81].
   - 3Q FY2026 trading update: super-apps (**VodaPay, Vodafone Cash, M-Pesa**) are key financial-services channels; VodaPay "gained customer traction" [S82]. SA-specific VodaPay user numbers not disclosed — needs source.
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Alipay-style super-app; broad partner ecosystem; integration of telco services [S81].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — No material enforcement in scope.
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** huge base, super-app ecosystem, Alipay know-how. **Weaknesses:** M-Pesa precedent shows SA consumers prefer bank/retail channels over telco wallets; VodaPay has had multiple pivots.

## 3. Shoprite Money Market Account

1. **Category** — Retailer "money account" / cash wallet — arguably the most successful retailer financial product in SA.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Not a bank; a transactional account delivered by Shoprite via partner/issuer arrangements; strong physical network (2,800+ stores) [S83][S84].
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Shoprite Holdings (JSE-listed); no standalone valuation.
4. **Business model** — Near-zero-fee account (**R5 flat cash withdrawal fee** in store), free instant P2P transfers; monetised via store footfall and float/ecosystem [S83][S84].
5. **Scale** — **More than 4 million Money Market Accounts** (launched 2020) [S83][S84]; **2,800+ store network** described as the largest physical financial-services infrastructure in the country [S124].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Free instant transfers to other account holders via **app, WhatsApp, Money Market website or USSD**; designed for digital inclusion (no traditional bank account needed); won Platinum in Daily Sun Reader's Choice Awards [S83].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed; multi-channel (app/WhatsApp/USSD/web) [S83].
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Operates within retail/NPS rules; no material enforcement in scope.
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** unbeatable distribution, trust and price; huge low-income base. **Weaknesses:** limited credit/product depth; perceived as a "money account" not a full bank.

## 6. Lesaka Technologies (Kazang, Flash, EasyPay, Adumo)

1. **Category** — Township/merchant fintech network: POS terminals, prepaid, remittances, consumer lending, insurance.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Merchant-acquiring and prepaid/consumer platform; **acquiring Bank Zero** to gain a bank licence and deposit funding [S20][S87].
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — **Nasdaq + JSE-listed** (LSAK). FY2025 revenue **$659.7m (R12.0bn)** [S86]; net loss **$87.5m (R1.6bn)** (incl. non-cash charges) [S86]. Market cap not researched here.
4. **Business model** — Merchant acquiring + VAS (electricity, airtime) + consumer lending/insurance + B2B (Adumo). Consumer net revenue **$96m (R1.7bn), +35%** and consumer EBITDA **+83%** (FY2025), driven by ARPU cross-sell [S86].
5. **Scale** — **90,000+ micro-merchants** (60,000+ card-enabled) and **30,000+ SMEs**, mostly townships/villages [S85][S88]; **2m+ consumer customers** referenced around the Bank Zero deal [S21].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Terminals that let spaza shops accept cards, sell electricity/airtime and process remittances — "mini banks" [S88]; **Flash** and **Shop2Shop** used by Standard Bank as cash-out points [S50].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Bank Zero acquisition under Prudential Authority/exchange-control review; Competition clearance obtained [S18][S20][S21].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** unrivalled township/merchant reach; consumer + merchant dual-sided model; now acquiring a bank. **Weaknesses:** group currently loss-making at net level; integration complexity across many acquired brands (Kazang, Flash, Adumo, Bank Zero, etc.).

## Cross-cutting notes (wallets & mobile money)

- **Fund custody:** nearly all wallets here are **not banks**; they rely on partner banks/issuers. The exceptions are the bank wallets (FNB eWallet, Standard Bank Instant Money, Absa ChatWallet).
- **The M-Pesa lesson:** telco mobile money failed in SA (M-Pesa closed 2016 with ~76k users [S75]). Telcos are retrying via **super-apps** (VodaPay) and **merchant networks** (MoMo Pay) rather than classic wallets.
- **Retailer accounts win on price + reach:** Shoprite Money Market (4m+ accounts, R5 withdrawals) is a benchmark a neobank must beat on cost of cash [S83].
- **Township POS is the distribution battleground:** Lesaka/Kazang (90k+ merchants [S85]) and retailer kiosks (TymeBank in PnP/Boxer) reach the same customers neobanks target.
- **PayShap is the connector:** now extended to non-bank wallets (MTN MoMo) [S79] and embedded in bank apps (Absa) [S52].

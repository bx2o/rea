# Digital Banks (true licensed banks / digital-only banks)

Entities in this file hold (or operate under) a **South African banking licence** — either a commercial bank licence under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 or a mutual bank licence under the Mutual Banks Act 124 of 1993. They can take deposits and hold customer funds on their own balance sheet.

---

## 1. TymeBank (Tyme Group)

1. **Category** — Licensed digital-only commercial bank (SA); group also runs GoTyme in the Philippines and is expanding into Indonesia/Vietnam.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Operates under a **commercial bank licence**; deposits sit on the bank's own balance sheet (TymeBank Limited). Customer funds are therefore bank-held, not held at a partner. **[FACT]** (TymeBank is a licensed bank; group structure per [S2][S3][S4].)
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — **USD 250m Series D closed December 2024, led by Nubank, at a USD 1.5bn valuation** (unicorn) [S2][S3][S4]. Existing key investors include **African Rainbow Capital (Patrice Motsepe)** as lead shareholder and **Tencent** [S3]. Group expanded into an Indonesian retail bank and a Hong Kong money-lender licence (June 2025) [S4].
4. **Business model** — Low/no-fee transactional accounts distributed via **kiosks in Pick n Pay / Boxer** and a national agent network; monetised via **interchange**, **lending margin** (personal loans + SME lending via Retail Capital) and insurance. Deposits = cheap funding. [FACT/EST]
5. **Scale** —
   - SA customers **11.6m (+21%)** at 30 June 2025; deposits **R7.7bn (+22%)**; net advances **R2.25bn (+17%)** [S1].
   - First profitable month **December 2023**; sustained monthly profitability reported [S3][S18].
   - Group retail customers **17.5m** and ~450,000 new customers/month (June 2025) [S5]; TymeBank + GoTyme combined 15m+ [S3]. *(Definitions differ between sources.)*
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — No-frills low-cost account openable in minutes at kiosks (hybrid physical-digital onboarding); leading savings rates; SME funding embedded with Payfast (see payments file) [S67].
7. **Tech stack signals** — **Cloud-native on AWS**; at 2019 launch migrated ~85% of systems to AWS using containerised **Amazon ECS/EKS** [S7]. (Core-vendor not publicly confirmed.)
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Publicly criticised the Department of Home Affairs identity-verification fee hike (R0.15 → R10, a very large increase) in June 2025 — a regulatory-cost pain point for digital onboarding [S3]. Governance: CEO transition announced — **Karl Westvig** handed over to **Cheslyn Jacobs** (Westvig staying through Q1 2026) [S6].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** genuine bank licence; strong unit economics at low fee; two-market (SA + PH) scale; credible profitability. **Weaknesses:** low ARPU mass-market base; exposure to regulatory/onboarding cost shocks; heavy dependence on partner retail footprint for acquisition.

## 2. Discovery Bank

1. **Category** — Licensed digital-only commercial bank; part of Discovery Limited (JSE-listed health/insurance group).
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Licensed bank; deposits on own balance sheet. Originated as a JV with **FirstRand (25.01% shareholding)**; Discovery agreed to acquire FirstRand's stake and Discovery Card economics for **R1.8bn**, subject to the licence condition that FirstRand's holding reduce over time [S13].
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Not a venture-funded startup; capitalised by **Discovery Limited** (JSE-listed). Founder/Group CEO **Adrian Gore** [S12]. No independent valuation disclosed. **[UNVERIFIED]** for any standalone valuation.
4. **Business model** — **Behavioural "shared-value" banking**: interest/cash-back/rewards tied to Vitality health-and-money behaviour; cross-sell of insurance, health and investments; higher fee tiers relative to low-cost neobanks [S9][S10][S11]. Revenue FY26 **R3.1bn (+31%)** [S9].
5. **Scale** —
   - **FY2026 (year to 30 June 2026): profit R370m** vs a **R68m loss** in FY25 — first full-year profit [S8][S9][S11].
   - Clients **+26% to 1.57m**; total accounts **3.77m (+26%)** [S8].
   - Interim H1 FY2026: profit **R210–230m** (vs R145m loss a year earlier); acquiring ~**1,500 new customers/day** [S12].
   - *Conflicting scale figures:* one aggregator reports "1.9m customers by 31 Dec 2025" and deposits +48% to R32.5bn — **[UNVERIFIED]**, not corroborated by Discovery's audited FY26 results [S8]. Use the audited 1.57m.
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — "Super app"/ecosystem play: Vitality Money, Discovery Vitality integration, dynamic interest and rewards, insurance bundling; increasingly AI-driven personalisation [S9][S10].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Uses the **Databricks Data and AI Platform** for behavioural AI/real-time decisioning; launched "Discovery AI" (May 2026) [S14]. (Core-banking vendor not confirmed.)
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Bank licence granted subject to the FirstRand shareholding-reduction condition [S13]. No material enforcement action found in scope.
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** profitable; strongest ecosystem/engagement model in SA; affluent-segment skew; JSE parent balance sheet. **Weaknesses:** small customer base vs Capitec/FNB; fee structure exposes it to "zero-fee" challengers; profitability depends on cross-sell into Discovery Group, though ~70% of new business now originates outside the group [S9].

## 3. Bank Zero

1. **Category** — Licensed **mutual bank** (digital-only, app-only, zero-fee).
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — **Mutual bank** (Mutual Banks Act) — customer funds on own balance sheet. Founded by **Michael Jordaan** (chairman, ex-FNB CEO) and **Yatin Narsai** (CEO), with five other co-founders [S17].
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Largely **self-funded by founders** — deliberately frugal, which preserved capital but left it too small to reach standalone break-even on scale [S19]. **Being acquired by Lesaka Technologies** (Nasdaq/JSE) for **~R1.09–1.1bn (~$61–67m)**, announced **June 2025**, funded mostly in newly issued Lesaka shares (Bank Zero shareholders to own ~**12%** of the enlarged group) plus up to ~R91m cash [S18][S20][S21]. Michael Jordaan to join Lesaka board; Yatin Narsai remains Bank Zero CEO [S20][S22].
4. **Business model** — **Zero-fee** everyday banking (no monthly fee, no minimum balance, free EFTs/debit orders/SendMoney) [S15][S16]; monetised by scaling deposits and expanding **lending** [S19][S20]. Post-acquisition, Lesaka intends to migrate its lending books to be **funded by Bank Zero deposits** (leverage ratio target <1× by June 2027) [S87].
5. **Scale** — Just over **40,000 funded accounts** and **~R400m deposits** (~R10,000 avg balance) around the time of the June 2025 deal [S20]. **~500,000 Mukuru customers** being migrated onto the Bank Zero platform [S16/S70]. Guided to **break-even ~Dec 2026** (standalone) and deposits **>R1bn by Dec 2026**, **>R4bn by mid-2029** [S18][S19][S20].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Patented secure debit card, fully app-based, email/self-service support, genuinely zero fees [S17]. Provides the **infrastructure behind Mukuru's account and card** — a B2B/embedded model [S70].
7. **Tech stack signals** — In-house platform built by the founding team (no external core vendor confirmed); positioning as low-cost, capital-light operations [S19].
8. **Regulatory interactions** — Deal cleared by the **Competition Tribunal/Commission in late 2025** but still requires **Prudential Authority** approval and SARB **exchange-control** approval; closing deadline extended (reported to **31 Jan 2027**) [S18][S20][S21].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** cheapest fee proposition in market; credible fintech founders; now has a distribution partner (Lesaka) and a deposit-funding story. **Weaknesses:** far too small pre-deal; break-even still a **forecast** not a result [S20]; future depends on completing a publicly regulated M&A process.

## 4. OM Bank (Old Mutual)

1. **Category** — Licensed digital-first commercial bank; wholly owned subsidiary of **Old Mutual Limited** (JSE-listed insurer, ~178-year heritage).
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — **OM Bank Limited**, a fully registered **commercial bank** licensed by the SARB **Prudential Authority** under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 [S23][S24]. Deposits on own balance sheet.
   - **[CONFLICT] Licence timing:** one source states the banking licence was **approved April 2024** [S24]; Old Mutual's own final-results note says **Prudential Authority approval was received in March 2025** [S25]. Treat the precise approval date as **needs verification**.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Capitalised by Old Mutual; the group **spent ~R2.8bn since 2022** building the bank and securing the licence [S26]. No independent valuation.
4. **Business model** — Low-cost digital everyday banking with savings interest and a rewards programme; targets middle-market earners (**~R5,000–R80,000/month**, focus below R50,000) [S23][S28]. Aims to break even **2028** [S28].
5. **Scale** — Live to the public **27 Aug / Sept 2025** (migrating legacy Old Mutual Money Account clients) [S27]; reached **1m customers one year after launch** [S28]; onboarding ~**4,500 new clients/day** — roughly on par with Capitec's growth rate [S28]. Trial with 600 Old Mutual employees before public launch [S26].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Digital-first with the backing/trust of an established insurer; savings rates competitive; not suited to heavy ATM/branch users [S23]. Predecessor **Old Mutual Money Account** was offered by Old Mutual Transaction Services **in association with Bidvest Bank** [S29]; those clients were invited to migrate and the Money Account was set to be discontinued by **30 June 2026** [S23].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed in scoped sources — **needs source**.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — PA-approved licence and approved CEO/board (March 2025) [S25][S26]; gradual, risk-based national rollout culminating Q4 2025 [S25].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** fastest-scaling new bank (1m in a year); strong brand trust; insurer distribution. **Weaknesses:** late to market vs Tyme/Discovery; must prove unit economics to 2028 break-even; heavier cost base than pure neobanks.

## 5. African Bank (MyWORLD)

1. **Category** — Licensed retail bank (lender-led, digitally enabled); mid-tier.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — **African Bank Limited**, a fully owned subsidiary of **African Bank Holdings**. Shareholding: **SARB 50%**, **GEPF/PIC 25%**, **consortium of five SA commercial banks 25%** [S31][S30]. SARB has signalled intent to dispose of its stake commercially [S31]. Deposits on own balance sheet.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — No VC round; capital from shareholders/group. Valuation not disclosed. SARB exit is a stated medium-term ambition [S31].
4. **Business model** — Lending-led (unsecured credit) plus the **MyWORLD** transactional account; insurance and deposits as funding. Integrating acquired books (e.g. Grindrod Bank, Ubank) — "Excelerate" strategy post-expansion [S30][S33]. Now shifting "from expansion to operational consolidation / back-to-basics" with a **R1.2bn cost-reduction target by 2028** [S33].
5. **Scale (FY25)** — Gross advances **R44bn**; non-interest revenue **R2bn**; **active customers 6.3m**; retail & business deposits **R37bn**; total assets **R58bn**; credit loss ratio **5.3%** [S30]. Active customers grew 17%; **60% of MyWORLD accounts funded** [S30]. Earlier milestone: 4m customers, 1m+ MyWORLD users [S34]. Reported a **R624m loss** amid transformation/integration costs [S33].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — MyWORLD account with "Power Pocket" (spend) and "Savings Pocket", openable online/app in minutes; omni-channel (app + branches + call centre) with biometric enhancements [S30][S34].
7. **Tech stack signals** — MyWORLD runs on an **omni-channel platform** allowing opening/managing across channels [S34]. Vendor not disclosed — needs source.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — SARB/state-linked ownership is a distinctive governance fact [S31]. High credit-loss ratio (5.3%) is a supervisory and profitability focus [S30].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** large customer/deposit base; inclusion mission; strong distribution. **Weaknesses:** lending-led losses; integration drag; less "digital-native" than neobanks.

## 6. Access Bank (South Africa) Limited

1. **Category** — Licensed commercial bank (subsidiary of Nigeria's Access Bank Plc); corporate/trade-finance-led, seeking retail scale.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — **Access Bank (South Africa) Limited**, Reg. 1947/025414/06; **FSP 5865**; registered credit provider **NCRCP 6** [S38]. Part of Access Bank Plc (60m+ customers group-wide) [S35]. Deposits on own balance sheet.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — Capitalised by Access Bank Plc group. No SA entity valuation.
4. **Business model** — Repositioning as a **tier-two** bank focused on **trade finance, business banking, alliance banking and retail** [S37]. *Alliance banking* = acting as the back-end bank for third-party/EMI-style propositions.
5. **Scale** — Small SA footprint; niche corporate focus historically [S37]. Intended leap in retail scale via the **Bidvest Bank acquisition** — see below.
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Business transactional products (e.g. "Business More", fixed monthly fee) and trade-finance capability into African markets [S35].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Not disclosed in scoped sources — needs source.
8. **Regulatory interactions — failed acquisition.** Access Bank signed to acquire **100% of Bidvest Bank** on **12 Dec 2024**; the deal **collapsed on 26 Jan 2026** when the long-stop date expired with regulatory conditions unmet [S35][S36]. This would have added 140+ retail outlets [S37].
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** pan-African group backing; trade-finance niches; alliance-banking capability. **Weaknesses:** sub-scale SA retail; the flagship retail-scale acquisition failed, delaying its consumer push.

## Cross-cutting notes (digital banks)

- **Fund custody** is the cleanest differentiator: all six entities are licensed banks (or operate a licensed bank) and hold deposits on balance sheet — unlike payments fintechs and wallets (see other files).
- **Profitability inflection:** Discovery (FY26 R370m profit) and TymeBank (monthly profitability since Dec 2023) are proven; Bank Zero and OM Bank are pre-break-even with explicit targets (Dec 2026 and 2028 respectively).
- **Capital events define 2025–26:** Tyme's $250m raise (Dec 2024); Lesaka→Bank Zero (~R1.1bn, pending); Access Bank's failed Bidvest bid.

# Digital-first incumbents (challenger posture, incumbent scale)

These are fully licensed incumbents that behave like challengers on digital UX and are the true competitive benchmarks a neobank must beat. They are banks (deposits on balance sheet); included here because the task scope treats them as "digitally-first incumbent challengers".

## 7. Capitec Bank

1. **Category** — Licensed commercial bank; **mass-market champion** and the most direct incumbent competitor to a neobank.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Capitec Bank Holdings Limited (JSE-listed); licensed bank; deposits on own balance sheet.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — JSE-listed public company; no VC funding. Valuation = market capitalisation (not researched here).
4. **Business model** — Radical simplification: low fixed monthly fee (~R7.50/month per one 2026 comparison [S107]); credit-led → transactional; monetised via credit margin, transaction volume, VAS, insurance, Capitec Connect (MVNO) and business banking [S39][S42].
5. **Scale** —
   - **FY2026 (to 28 Feb 2026): active clients 25.2m (+7%)**; fully banked **9.9m (+12%)**; digital transacting clients **15.9m (+13%)**; banking-app clients **+18% to 15.2m** [S39].
   - Later earnings-call figures: **26.6m clients**, **16.5m monthly app users**, **10.4m fully banked** [S41].
   - FY2025: active app clients **12.9m (+15%)** [S40].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — Simple transparent pricing, app-first + branch network, app-linked investing via EasyEquities [S42][S39].
7. **Tech stack signals** — App-first/self-service terminals to offload branch traffic; specific core vendor not disclosed — needs source.
8. **Regulatory interactions** — No material enforcement in scope.
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** largest active base (~half the adult population), low cost-to-serve, trusted brand, deep digitisation. **Weaknesses:** still credit-concentrated; perceived as mass-market (harder to win affluent); branch network is a fixed cost.

## 8. FNB (FirstRand)

1. **Category** — Licensed bank; digital super-app leader by engagement.
2. **Legal structure & fund custody** — Part of FirstRand Limited (JSE); deposits on own balance sheet.
3. **Funding / investors / valuation** — JSE-listed; no VC.
4. **Business model** — App-centric universal banking; monetised via fees, credit, insurance, merchant acquiring and **eBucks** rewards (loyalty flywheel to drive primary-banking) [S108].
5. **Scale** — FY2026 (to 30 June 2026): **8.16m digitally active customers**; app processed **782m transactions (+10%)**; virtual-card spend **R101bn (+47%)** [S43][S44]. FNB reports **19m+ regular platform users** and **12m+ banking customers**; eWallet serves **8.2m users/year** (~R43bn) including the unbanked [S44][S45].
6. **Product & UX differentiators** — eBucks rewards, virtual cards, eWallet (WhatsApp), nav» Money budgeting (6m+ users) [S108][S112].
7. **Tech stack signals** — Large in-house engineering; core modernisation ongoing (vendor not disclosed in scope).
8. **Regulatory interactions** — None material in scope.
9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** huge engaged base; best-in-class rewards; deep unbanked reach via eWallet. **Weaknesses:** complex product set; premium fee structures create a "zero-fee" attack surface.

9. **Strengths / weaknesses** — **Strengths:** balance-sheet scale, corporate/business moat, huge physical + partner cash network. **Weaknesses:** consumer app lags FNB/Capitec on engagement; complex legacy.

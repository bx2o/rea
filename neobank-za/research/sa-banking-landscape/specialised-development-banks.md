# Specialised & Development Finance Banks (2025–2026)

Land Bank, DBSA, IDC and Postbank. These are state-owned or state-linked institutions. They are **not retail banks** in the neo/incumbent sense, but they matter to a NeoBank launch for three reasons: (1) they hold a large share of **development/agri/SME funding mandates**, (2) they are **potential funding/partnership counterparties**, and (3) **Postbank** is a state attempt to enter mass-market transactional banking.

---

## 1. Land Bank — Land and Agricultural Development Bank of South Africa

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Land and Agricultural Development Bank of South Africa**; a **state-owned development finance institution** established by the **Land Bank Act**. It is a **registered bank** supervised by the Prudential Authority, but its mandate is **agricultural/development finance**, not retail `[S62][S63]`.
2. **Ownership:** **100% South African government** (National Treasury / Department of Agriculture as shareholder) `[S63]`.
3. **Products:** agricultural loans (term, instalment sale, mortgage), **Agro-energy Fund**, working-capital facilities for farmers and agribusiness; wholesale/development finance rather than retail `[S63][S64]`.
4. **Digital:** digital channels for agri clients; **not a retail digital contender**.
5. **Scale:** **Total group assets R19.7bn (FY2026) vs R22.6bn (FY2025) — a 12.8% decrease** `[S63]`. **Total loan book declined from R17.5bn (FY2024) to R15.4bn (FY2025)** `[S64]`. Earlier reference points: total assets R27.7bn (Dec 2024 credit pack 2023 basis) / R22.3bn (Dec 2024) `[S65]`.
6. **Distribution:** regional agri offices; no retail branch network.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026 (critical for context):**
   - **Defaulted on its debt in April 2020**; **concluded a landmark debt restructuring with lenders effective 16 September 2024**, ending its default position `[S62]`.
   - National Treasury reported Land Bank's debt **declined from R40.6bn (Apr 2020) to R10.8bn (Dec 2024)** `[S65]`.
   - Received a **government guarantee of R5.7bn**; Parliament pressed for **equity injection** (rather than loans) and engagement with IDC/PIC `[S63]`.
   - FY2026: transition from "stabilisation" into "consolidation" phase; **20% reduction in NPL rand value on the new book** `[S63]`.
8. **→ Attack surface / relevance:** a **distressed state DFI** with a shrunken loan book → agri/SME finance gap it cannot fill → a NeoBank offering **business/working-capital products** (or partnering) can serve farmers and agri-SMEs that Land Bank has retrenched from. `[S62][S63][S64]`

## 2. DBSA — Development Bank of Southern Africa

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Development Bank of Southern Africa Limited (DBSA)**; a **state-owned development finance institution** under the **DBSA Act**. Not a retail deposit-taker `[S66][S67]`.
2. **Ownership:** **100% South African government** `[S66]`.
3. **Products:** **infrastructure project finance**, municipal and provincial lending, equity investment, grants/technical assistance; funded via capital markets and DFI lines `[S66][S67]`.
4. **Digital:** internal/digital for project management; no consumer channel.
5. **Scale (FY ended 31 March 2026):** **Total assets R130.5bn (+7.8% from R120.9bn FY2025)**; **record net profit R7.8bn (+47% from R5.3bn)**; unqualified audit maintained `[S66][S67]`. It catalysed **R98.5bn** in funding/investment through partnerships, facilitated **92 000+ jobs** `[S67]`.
6. **Distribution:** project/corporate relationships; no branch network.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** highest-ever profit (FY2026); growing continent-wide development impact; part of SA's infrastructure funding push alongside IDC and the PIC `[S66][S67]`.
8. **→ Attack surface / relevance:** **not a competitor**; a **capital and co-funding partner** for NeoBank lending programmes (SME, infrastructure-linked supply chains) and a possible wholesale funder. `[S66][S67]`

## 3. IDC — Industrial Development Corporation of South Africa

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Industrial Development Corporation of South Africa Limited (IDC)**; a **national development finance institution** established 1940 by an Act of Parliament. Not a bank in the retail sense `[S68][S69]`.
2. **Ownership:** **100% owned by the South African government** (state-owned company) `[S69]`.
3. **Products:** industrial development financing — debt, equity and blended finance across manufacturing, agro-processing, energy, mining value-chains; transformation funding `[S68][S69]`.
4. **Digital:** corporate/DFI digital; no consumer channel.
5. **Scale (FY ended 31 March 2026):** **Total assets (Company) R166.8bn (FY2026) vs R154.2bn (FY2025)** `[S68]`. FY2025 company total assets R144.3bn; portfolio mix ~**76% debt / 24% equity**; **R26.6bn** funding facilitated for transformation `[S69]`.
6. **Distribution:** sector/relationship teams; no branches.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** growing balance sheet and transformation lending; co-funder alongside DBSA and PIC; reported on integrated basis through FY2026 `[S68][S69]`.
8. **→ Attack surface / relevance:** **not a retail competitor**; a **co-lending / SME funding partner** and a source of **SME pipeline** for a NeoBank's business-banking proposition. `[S68][S69]`

## 4. Postbank — Postbank (SOC) Limited

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Postbank** — a **state-owned entity** spun out of the South African Post Office (SAPO) group in 2019. It operates **under a deposit-taking exemption** rather than a full banking licence; it **regained its Financial Services Provider (FSP) licence** (FAIS Act) in the **2025/26** fiscal year and **is applying for a full commercial banking licence** from the Prudential Authority `[S70][S71]`.
2. **Ownership:** **100% South African government** (separate legal entity from SAPO) `[S71]`.
3. **Products:** **transactional/savings (Postbank cards)**, **social grant disbursement** (SASSA), money transfers, funeral cover; the grant-payment mandate is its anchor `[S70][S71][S72]`.
4. **Digital:** digital-first ambitions with a mobile app; historically constrained by lacking a full banking licence and by legacy SAPO infrastructure `[S71][S72]`.
5. **Scale:** **~R10bn in assets** and reported to be **"in serious financial trouble"** `[S72]`. SASSA reports continue to track **beneficiary payouts via Postbank** `[S70]`. Also took over from SAPO as primary partner for social grant payments (2025) `[S71]`.
6. **Distribution:** the vast **SAPO/post-office network** plus retail/cash points is its distribution asset — but SAPO's own decline is a liability `[S71]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** **SASSA replacement-card migration** (2025) `[S70]`; regained **FSP licence** (2025/26) `[S70]`; **pursuing full commercial banking licence** to become a "fully-fledged state-owned commercial bank" `[S71]`; **withdrew some Postbank services** during reconfiguration, with assurances grant payments would not be affected `[S71]`.
8. **→ Attack surface / relevance:** a **future state-backed mass-market entrant** competing for the **grant/social-security and low-income** segment — exactly where a NeoBank's financial-inclusion wedge sits. Watch closely: if Postbank secures a full licence with the post-office network, it becomes a distribution-heavy, low-cost rival. Its **distress and licence gap** are the NeoBank's window of advantage. `[S71][S72]`

## 5. Specialised & development banks — comparison

| Institution | Type | Ownership | Total assets (period) | Mandate | Retail? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Land Bank | Development/agri bank | 100% Govt `[S63]` | R19.7bn (FY2026) `[S63]` | Agricultural finance | No |
| DBSA | Development finance institution | 100% Govt `[S66]` | R130.5bn (FY2026) `[S66]` | Infrastructure finance | No |
| IDC | Development finance institution | 100% Govt `[S69]` | R166.8bn (FY2026) `[S68]` | Industrial development finance | No |
| Postbank | State-owned deposit-taker (FSP licence; seeking commercial licence) | 100% Govt `[S71]` | ~R10bn [EST] `[S72]` | Social grants / basic banking | Yes (inclusion) |

**Cross-cutting implications for the NeoBank:**
1. **Development capital is available:** DBSA and IDC are large, growing, co-funding DFIs — viable **funding and co-lending partners** for a NeoBank's SME/agri/infrastructure-adjacent lending `[S66][S68]`.
2. **Land Bank's retrenchment** has created an **agri-SME financing void** the NeoBank can fill with digital working-capital products `[S62][S64]`.
3. **Postbank is the sleeper threat** in mass-market/inclusion banking — currently constrained, but with an unmatched physical network and a full-licence application in flight `[S71][S72]`.

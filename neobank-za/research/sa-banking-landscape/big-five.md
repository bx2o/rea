# The Big Five — Deep Profiles (2025–2026)

The five largest retail banks in South Africa. Four are "traditional" incumbents with 150+ years of heritage; Capitec is the modern mass-market disrupter that is now itself a systemically important incumbent.

> **Common licence note:** all five operate under the **Banks Act 94 of 1990** as **locally controlled commercial banks** and appear on the Prudential Authority's locally-controlled register and the CODI member list `[S2][S1]`. All deposits are covered by CODI up to **R100 000 per depositor per bank** `[S1]`.

---

## 1. Standard Bank — The Standard Bank of South Africa Limited (group: Standard Bank Group Ltd)

### 1.1 Legal entity & licence
- **Licensed banking entity (SA):** The Standard Bank of South Africa Limited ("SBSA"), a wholly owned subsidiary of **Standard Bank Group Limited (SBG)**, JSE-listed (SBK) `[S2][S8]`.
- **Licence:** full commercial banking licence (Banks Act 94 of 1990), locally controlled `[S2]`. SBG states it is Africa's largest bank by assets `[S8]`.
- **Leadership:** Group CEO **Sim Tshabalala** (Group CEO since 2017; departure announced for end-2027) `[S82]`; **David Hodnett** appointed CEO of SBSA (the SA bank) effective 10 October 2025 `[S82]`.

### 1.2 Ownership / group structure
- JSE-listed group with operations in ~21 African countries plus international financial centres `[S8]`.
- **Major shareholders:** Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (**ICBC**) ~19.6–19.7%; Government Employees Pension Fund / PIC ~14.5%; GIC (Singapore) ~2%; Old Mutual Life ~1.9% `[S8][S10][S94]`.

### 1.3 Core retail product set
Transactional accounts, savings, credit cards, personal & home loans (mortgages), vehicle & asset finance, insurance (via Standard Insurance), wealth, and a large business/commercial banking franchise; extensive CIB arm (Standard Corporate & Investment Banking) `[S8][S13]`.

### 1.4 Digital channels & maturity
- Flagship **SBG Mobile App** is the primary digital platform for SA retail; **350+ new product/enhancements** shipped in 1H25 alone `[S10]`.
- 1H25 digital metrics (SA retail): successful digital transactions **+12%**, digital sales volumes **+33%**, digital retail clients **+7%**, driving a **21% increase in digital revenue** period-on-period `[S10]`.
- Digital onboarding, voucher purchase, and small-business acquiring cited as growth vectors `[S10]`.

### 1.5 Scale metrics (verify period)
- **Total assets:** **R3 621bn (R3.6trn)** at 31 Dec 2025 (+11% vs R3 269bn FY24) `[S8][S13]`; R3.3trn at FY24 `[S11]`.
- **Clients:** **19.6m** clients (group) `[S8]`.
- **Headline earnings:** **R49.2bn** FY2025; ROE **19.3%** `[S8]`.
- **Footprint:** **>1 200 branches/points of representation** and **>5 500 ATMs** across Africa; **>50 000 employees** (group) `[S8]`. SBSA (SA) employment **29 378** and branch square-meterage **−7% YoY** with low-cost kiosk rollout (2024) `[S12]`. SA-only domestic branch/ATM counts (~484 branches / ~3 472 ATMs, 2024) are **[EST] via aggregator** `[S98]` — confirm against SBSA integrated report.

### 1.6 Distribution
Omni-channel: branches reduced/right-sized, kiosks introduced, heavy ATMs/app; distribution across 21 African markets; large sales force of ~11 000 branch/call-centre staff, consultants and tied agents (group) `[S11][S12]`.

### 1.7 Recent strategic moves (2023–2026)
- **Investment of up to US$200m** in Nigeria-focused fintech **OPay**, conditional on OPay's proposed US IPO (announced Oct 2025) `[S78]`.
- **US$332m (R5.4bn) SME funding deal** with the African Development Bank — Africa's first DFI-supported social FLAC instrument on the JSE `[S13]`.
- Continued digital monetisation (vouchers, data-driven personalised offers) `[S10]`.
- Leadership: SBSA CEO transition to David Hodnett (Oct 2025) `[S82]`.

### 1.8 Weaknesses / attack surfaces
→ Highest absolute cost base in SA retail (huge branch+staff footprint); app praised for breadth but seen as feature-heavy/complex for entry-level users. → Lending-led relationship model still anchors customers via home/vehicle finance; a NeoBank cannot out-lend it, but can win **transactional/day-to-day** primacy and **fee transparency**. → ICBC ownership link and pan-African complexity create slow product-cycle perception. → [S12] shows deliberate branch square-meterage reduction — an admission that legacy physical cost is a drag the NeoBank structurally avoids.

## 2. FirstRand — FirstRand Bank Limited (trading as FNB; group: FirstRand Limited)

### 2.1 Legal entity & licence
- **Licensed banking entity:** **FirstRand Bank Limited** (the bank; CODI member) within **FirstRand Limited** (JSE: FSR), which is the listed group and the registered banking group `[S1][S2]`.
- **Licence:** full commercial banking licence (Banks Act), locally controlled `[S2][S1]`.
- **Leadership:** FirstRand Group CEO **Mary Vilakazi** `[S2]`.

### 2.2 Ownership / group structure
- JSE-listed diversified financial group; divisions include **FNB** (retail/commercial), **RMB** (corporate & investment banking), **WesBank** (vehicle/asset finance) and **Aldermore** (UK) `[S14][S17]`.
- **Ownership history:** historically anchored by **RMB Holdings** and **Remgro**; Remgro completed a full exit from FirstRand in a series of 2026 on-market sales `[S91][S92]`. RMB Holdings itself is widely held (Coronation ~27.8%, Royal Bafokeng ~13.1%, etc.) `[S91]`. (Shareholder registers are [EST] from aggregators — confirm in the latest FirstRand annual report.)

### 2.3 Core retail product set
FNB offers the broadest retail product line of the Big Five: transactional accounts, savings, credit cards, personal loans, **home loans**, **vehicle finance (WesBank)**, insurance, investment, and a strong SME/commercial banking franchise `[S14][S17]`. FNB is known for its **ecosystem** (eBucks rewards, FNB Connect, nav», device offers, share trading) `[S17]`.

### 2.4 Digital channels & maturity
- FNB app is among the most mature in SA, combining banking + rewards (eBucks) + telco (FNB Connect) + device retail + **share trading**, and now **crypto trading** integrated into existing share-trading accounts via a partnership with VALR (launched Oct 2026) `[S14]`.
- Very large active-user base (12.37m active customers; 10.01m SA; 8.66m retail SA) `[S14]`.

### 2.5 Scale metrics
- **Total assets (group, normalised):** **R2 589bn (R2.59trn)**, FY2025 (Jun) `[S14]`.
- **Customer deposits ~R1.2trn; net advances ~R1.1trn** (mid-2025, predominantly FNB; [EST] via aggregator) `[S14]`.
- **Headline earnings:** **R41.9bn (+10%)** FY2025; ROE ~20.2%; CET1 14.0% `[S15]`.
- **Customers:** FNB **12.37m active** (SA 10.01m; SA retail 8.66m; broader Africa 2.36m), 30 Jun 2025 `[S14]`.
- **Employees:** ~40 000 (FNB, FY2024 [EST]) `[S17]`.
- **Footprint:** extensive branch + ATM network; FNB commercial banking reported ~1 478 client relationships and intermediated liabilities R351bn `[S17]`. [UNKNOWN] exact SA branch/ATM count — needs FNB annual report.

### 2.6 Distribution
Dense omni-channel: branches, ATMs, partnerships, and an industry-leading app ecosystem; strong brand loyalty via eBucks `[S14][S17]`. Note FNB explicitly targets entry-level customers (up to R240k p.a.) and SMEs `[S17]`.

### 2.7 Recent strategic moves (2023–2026)
- **Crypto trading** rolled out to ~9m retail customers via VALR (Oct 2026) `[S14]`.
- Continuous ecosystem expansion (eBucks, Connect, device/tech retail, share trading) `[S14][S17]`.
- UK motor-commission provision (R2.7bn) weighed on FY2025 — a group-level risk off the SA retail story `[S15]`.
- Ownership shift: Remgro exit completed 2026 `[S92]`.

### 2.8 Weaknesses / attack surfaces
→ FNB's ecosystem is its moat **and** its complexity trap: eBucks reward mechanics and layered products are hard for low-income/entry-level and unbanked users to navigate — a NeoBank can win on **radical simplicity + transparent zero-fee** positioning. → Crypto/tech add-ons show FNB chasing innovation bolt-ons rather than re-architecting core banking. → FNB has been the **most litigious** incumbent historically (see prior market-conduct cases) — public trust is attackable. → Large SA retail base (8.66m) means it will defend aggressively on price; do not compete head-on on home loans/vehicle finance early.

## 3. Absa — Absa Bank Limited (group: Absa Group Limited)

### 3.1 Legal entity & licence
- **Licensed banking entity (SA):** **Absa Bank Limited** within **Absa Group Limited** (JSE: ABG) `[S19][S2]`.
- **Licence:** full commercial banking licence (Banks Act), locally controlled `[S2]`.
- **Leadership:** Group CEO **Kenny Fihla** (effective 17 June 2025; interim CEO Charles Russon after Arrie Rautenbach stepped down 14 Oct 2024) `[S83][S21]`.

### 3.2 Ownership / group structure
- JSE-listed pan-African group (operations across ~10+ African markets) `[S18][S20]`.
- Formerly **Barclays Africa Group**; **Barclays plc** progressively sold down its stake (to 14.9% by end-2017) and announced full exit of its remaining holding in 2022 `[S18]`. → Absa is now majority institutionally/JSE-held with no controlling foreign parent `[S18]`.

### 3.3 Core retail product set
Transactional accounts, savings & investments, credit cards, personal loans, **home loans**, vehicle & asset finance, insurance, and strong business/commercial + corporate & investment banking; Islamic banking window (Absa Islamic Banking, since 2006) `[S19][S57]`.

### 3.4 Digital channels & maturity
- Absa has positioned its app as a "super-app"-style ecosystem; **3.4m digitally active customers** (of 10.0m active) at FY2024 `[S19]`; **10m active customers** group `[S19]`.
- IT/digital spend **R16.7bn (+6%)** in FY2025, covering new digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, data and cloud `[S21]`.

### 3.5 Scale metrics
- **Total assets (group):** **R2 069bn** FY2024 (+10%) `[S18]`; **R2 160bn** at 1H25 (+11%) `[S20]`.
- **Customers:** 10.0m active / 3.4m digitally active (FY2024) `[S19]`.
- **Headline earnings:** **R24.8bn (+12%)** FY2025 `[S21]`.
- **Footprint:** Absa Bank FY2024 short-form lists **26 countries**, **~5 138 outlets**, **~5 616 ATMs** and **26 542 employees** (group; exact label order to be confirmed against the filing) `[S19]`. SA-specific ~618 outlets / ~5 096 ATMs (Jun 2025, **[EST] via aggregator** `[S97]`) — confirm against Absa's own disclosures.

### 3.6 Distribution
Omni-channel across 9 SA provinces + pan-African; strong branch/ATM footprint plus app; Walmart/Massmart retail relationship is *not* an Absa distribution channel (Walmart is a separate retail play) — do not conflate `[S20]`.

### 3.7 Recent strategic moves (2023–2026)
- **R4bn (~US$240m) deal to raise stake in Absa Bank Kenya to ~85%** from 68.5% (2026) `[S79]`.
- Acquired certain Standard Chartered retail/wealth assets in some African markets (SC exiting) `[S61]`.
- Leadership transition to Kenny Fihla (Jun 2025) `[S83]`.
- Heavy digital/IT investment (R16.7bn FY2025) `[S21]`.

### 3.8 Weaknesses / attack surfaces
→ Largest **leadership churn** of the Big Five (CEO change 2024→2025) can create execution/direction risk. → Digital-active penetration (34% of active customers) is the **lowest** among big incumbents with equal scale → large untapped migration pool a NeoBank can harvest with a better-native app. → Post-Barclays identity still lacks FNB's ecosystem loyalty or Capitec's simplicity; sits "stuck in the middle." → [S21] heavy IT capex implies accelerating catch-up — expect price/feature aggression.

## 4. Nedbank — Nedbank Limited (group: Nedbank Group Limited)

### 4.1 Legal entity & licence
- **Licensed entity:** **Nedbank Limited** within **Nedbank Group Limited** (JSE: NED) `[S2][S22]`.
- **Licence:** full commercial banking licence (Banks Act), locally controlled `[S2]`.
- **Leadership:** Group CEO **Jason Quinn** `[S2]`.

### 4.2 Ownership / group structure
- JSE-listed; historically majority-held by **Old Mutual** (53% from 1986), reduced to ~19.9% via the 2018 "managed separation" unbundling `[S22]`. Nedbank is now widely held; **PIC** (GEPF) is a large shareholder ([EST]) `[S22]`.
- Restructured (1 July 2025) from Retail & Business Banking (RBB) + Nedbank Wealth into two clusters: **Personal & Private Banking (PPB)** and **Business & Commercial Banking (BCB)** `[S23]`.

### 4.3 Core retail product set
Transactional & savings accounts (**Nedbank Money**, zero-monthly-fee options), personal loans, home loans, vehicle & asset finance, credit cards, insurance (life & non-life), investment/wealth, business banking; Islamic window via Nedbank. `[S22][S23]`.

### 4.4 Digital channels & maturity
- **Nedbank Money app**: ~3m active users (+14% YoY), 15% increase in digital transaction values; ~70% of Nedbank Africa Regions retail clients digitally active [EST via aggregator] `[S23]`.
- **R2.6bn technology investment** in the past financial year `[S23]`.
- Focus on digital + value-added services + payments; SME digital tools (Startup Bundle with zero fees for 6 months) `[S23]`.

### 4.5 Scale metrics
- **Total assets:** **~R1 494bn** at Jun 2025 [EST via aggregator] `[S22][S23]`; S&P "adjusted assets" ~R1 343bn 1H24 `[S95]`.
- **Clients:** **8m+ clients** (group, first time above 8m, FY2025) `[S22][S23]`.
- **Headline earnings:** **R17.2bn (+2%)** FY2025; ROE **15.4%** `[S22][S23]`.
- **Employees:** ~25 613 FTE (Jun 2025) [EST] `[S22]`.
- **Footprint:** ~400+ branches [EST] `[S22]`.

### 4.6 Distribution
Omni-channel with ~400 branches, ATM network, app; strong private/wealth distribution; growing digital and startup SME bundles `[S22][S23]`.

### 4.7 Recent strategic moves (2023–2026)
- **Acquisition of fintech Khokha** (100%) to fast-track SME digital support (FY2025) `[S77]`.
- **R13.9bn (~US$830m) offer for ~66% of Kenya's NCBA Group** — one of Africa's largest cross-border banking deals (announced 21 Jan 2026) `[S76]`.
- Cluster restructuring into PPB + BCB (Jul 2025) `[S23]`.
- Market-share gains in home loans, VAF and overdrafts; retail deposit share gains `[S23]`.

### 4.8 Weaknesses / attack surfaces
→ Slowest headline-earnings growth of the Big Five (**+2% FY2025**) and ROE at/below cost of equity → margin pressure makes it vulnerable to a low-fee attacker. → Major strategic attention (and capital) is being diverted to the **East African NCBA acquisition** → SA retail execution bandwidth risk. → Nedbank Money app user base (~3m) is small relative to Capitec's app (12.9m) `[S24]` and FNB's 12.37m `[S14]`. → Repositioning churn (cluster restructure) creates a window of customer uncertainty.

## 5. Capitec — Capitec Bank Limited (group: Capitec Bank Holdings Limited)

**Positioning:** the closest thing to an incumbent *and* the strongest neo-like competitor. Founded 2001 as a simple, low-cost mass-market bank; now SA's largest retail bank by client count.

### 5.1 Legal entity & licence
- **Licensed entity:** **Capitec Bank Limited** within **Capitec Bank Holdings Limited** (JSE: CPI, also listed via ADR) `[S2][S24]`.
- **Licence:** full commercial banking licence (Banks Act), locally controlled `[S2]`.
- **Leadership:** Co-founder **Michiel Le Roux** remains a major shareholder; CEO **Graham Lee** (per PA register) `[S2][S91]`.

### 5.2 Ownership / group structure
- Widely held JSE-listed; founders/PSG lineage significant: **Michiel Le Roux** ~11.4%–[EST] and **Jannie Mouton Family Trust** ~5.1% `[S91]`. PSG Group financed Capitec's early growth `[S91]`.
- Group includes **Capitec Business** (business banking), **Capitec Insurance**, **Capitec Connect** (MVNO/Airtime), value-added services (VAS), and international lending arm **AvaFin** (Poland, Latvia, Czechia, Spain, Mexico) `[S24][S80]`.

### 5.3 Core retail product set
Transactional account (Global One / Capitec app), **savings** (fixed & flexible, market-leading rates), **personal loans** (unsecured), credit card (since 2016), **credit life / funeral / life insurance**, business banking (since 2019), VAS (airtime, data, electricity, money transfer), prepaid **Capitec Connect** SIM. Note: Capitec historically does **not** offer home loans and limits vehicle finance — a gap a NeoBank can exploit. `[S24][S25]`

### 5.4 Digital channels & maturity
- **App clients 12.9m (+15%)** FY2025 `[S24][S25]`.
- **83% of all transactions are cashless**; the bank "provides access to over 2 trillion data points" for credit/behavioural scoring `[S24]`.
- Self-service transactions **+39%** vs one-on-one consultations **−12%** (branch migration) `[S24]`.
- Strong at combining **branch presence with digital**: 861 branches and 8 797 cash devices (ATMs + DNRs) are used as *acquisition and service* points while the app does the work `[S24]`.

### 5.5 Scale metrics
- **Total assets:** **R238.5bn** FY2025 (Feb), +15% `[S24]`; R207.6bn FY2024 `[S26]`.
- **Headline earnings:** **R13.7bn (+30%)** FY2025; ROE **29%** `[S24][S25]`.
- **Clients:** **24.1m active clients** (Feb 2025; FY2024: 22.2m) `[S24]`.
- **Footprint:** **861 branches**; **8 797** cash devices (ATMs & DNRs) `[S24]`.
- **Net insurance result R3.8bn** (+19%); **VAS + Capitec Connect R4.4bn** (+61%) — insurance is ~25% of earnings `[S25]`.
- **AvaFin:** majority-owned (from Apr 2024); contributed ~R196m to group net profit FY2025 `[S27][S80]`.

### 5.6 Distribution
Branch-led acquisition + app-led transaction: "personal service and digital banking" hybrid; cash devices (ATMs + DNRs) for cash access; no reliance on third-party retail kiosks (unlike Tyme). `[S24]`

### 5.7 Recent strategic moves (2023–2026)
- **Majority stake in AvaFin group** (2024) → international expansion (Mexico branch opened Jun 2026) `[S80]`.
- Expanded into **business banking**, **insurance (own licence)**, **VAS** and **Capitec Connect** over the last 5 years `[S24]`.
- Interim results (Oct 2025): **+26% headline earnings**, AvaFin active clients +27% `[S27]`.

### 5.8 Weaknesses / attack surfaces
→ Capitec is the **benchmark to beat, not an easy target**: it already delivers low fees, high savings rates and a strong app. Attack angles: (a) **no home loans / limited vehicle finance** — a NeoBank with a mortgage/vehicle partner can capture asset-side share; (b) **branch-centric service model still costs money** (861 branches) and some customers experience queues; (c) **business banking is immature** relative to the Big Four; (d) Capitec's rate advantage on savings can be matched or beaten by a lower-cost digital-only balance sheet; (e) app perceived as functional but not "ecosystem-rich" vs FNB/Discovery. `[S24][S25]`

**Cross-cutting Big Five observations (evidence-based):**
1. **All five now have a credible app** — a NeoBank cannot win on "we have an app" alone; differentiation must be on **price architecture, simplicity, speed-to-yes on credit and asset-side gaps** `[S10][S14][S24]`.
2. **Capitec and FNB are the innovation leaders**; Standard Bank and Capitec compete on scale/efficiency; Absa and Nedbank are playing catch-up on digital `[S10][S14][S19][S23][S24]`.
3. **Big Five capital and distribution are unassailable head-on** — the NeoBank should partner for distribution (retail/kiosk/agency) and attack *segments* (under-banked, entry-level, gig/informal workers, SME) rather than front the incumbents' core.

# Tier-2 & Other Licensed Banks (2025–2026)

Covers everything below the Big Five: specialist banks, foreign-controlled subsidiaries, foreign branches, digital/neobanks, mutual and co-operative banks, and institutions in exit/curatorship.

> **Digital-bank flag:** Tyme (GoTyme), Discovery Bank, Bank Zero and OM Bank are **digital or digital-first** banks, not traditional branch banks. They are included here (as required) but flagged so they are not double-counted as incumbents.

## 1. Investec — Investec Bank Limited

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Investec Bank Limited (IBL)** — the main SA banking subsidiary of **Investec Limited** (JSE: INL), which together with **Investec plc** (LSE) forms the dual-listed Investec Group. Locally controlled commercial bank (Banks Act), regulated by the Prudential Authority; banking licence obtained 1980 `[S2][S28]`.
2. **Ownership / structure:** dual-listed structure (Investec Limited SA + Investec plc UK) with a shared board; operates in SA and UK with wealth via Rathbones partnership. Specialises in **private, corporate and institutional** clients (not mass retail) `[S29][S30]`.
3. **Products:** private banking, corporate/institutional lending, wealth & investment management, treasury/financial markets; deposit and savings products for private clients; no mass-market transactional banking `[S28][S30]`.
4. **Digital:** strong private-client digital platform; positioned as "internationally connected" wealth-banking app; not a mass-retail contender `[S30]`.
5. **Scale:** **Investec Bank Limited total assets R638.8bn; net core loans R367.6bn; total equity R51.2bn** (IBL, latest investor presentation) `[S28]`. Group employees ~7 949–8 000 (Mar 2025) `[S29]`. **Customer numbers: [UNKNOWN] — not a mass-retail bank.**
6. **Distribution:** relationship-led (private bankers, advisers, intermediaries); minimal branch network `[S29]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** FY2025 final results (year to 31 Mar 2026) released May 2026; focus on wealth/private growth and Rathbones partnership in UK `[S29][S30]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** none material for mass-market NeoBank; Investec occupies the affluent/private niche. Watch as a **talent and HNW-deposit** competitor, and as a source of senior banking leadership (Pepkor appointed a former Investec Bank CEO to its bank board) `[S74]`.

## 2. African Bank — African Bank Limited

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **African Bank Limited** within **African Bank Holdings Limited (ABHL)**; locally controlled commercial bank (Banks Act) `[S2]`.
2. **Ownership:** unusual ownership resulting from the 2014 collapse/restructure — shareholders are the **SARB (~50%)**, **GEPF managed by PIC (~25%)** and a **consortium of six banks (~25%)**, plus the **iKamva Lethu Employee Trust** `[S34][S33][S31]`. Full structure: ABHL owns 100% of African Bank Limited and African Insurance Group `[S33]`.
3. **Products:** unsecured **personal loans** (core legacy), **MyWORLD** transactional account, savings/investments, credit cards, insurance, and (post-acquisitions) **business banking** and commercial property/equipment finance `[S31][S32]`.
4. **Digital:** MyWORLD app with biometric security enhancements; digital enablement is a stated strategic pillar (`Excelerate` strategy) `[S32]`.
5. **Scale (FY2025, 30 Sep):** **Total assets R58bn; active customers 6.3m; retail & business deposits R37bn; credit loss ratio 5.3%** `[S32][S31]`.
6. **Distribution:** branch + digital; historically an unsecured-lending branch network now pivoting to full-service retail/business `[S32]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** acquired **Grindrod Bank** (R1.5bn, completed 2022) for business banking `[S52]`; acquired **Ubank's** assets/liabilities (R80m) `[S55]`; acquired Sasfin's **capital equipment and commercial property finance** businesses `[S32]`; "Excelerate" strategy; **active customers +17%, deposits +14%** `[S32]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** still rebuilding trust after the 2014 collapse; heavy secured-lending/business pivot competes with incumbents, leaving **mass-market transactional + savings** relatively uncontested; ownership structure (SARB 50%) constrains agility. `[S31][S34]`

## 3. Bidvest Bank — Bidvest Bank Limited

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Bidvest Bank Limited**; locally controlled commercial bank (Banks Act); CODI member `[S2][S1]`.
2. **Ownership:** wholly owned by **Bidvest Group** (JSE: BVT); **being acquired by Nigeria's Access Bank Plc/Access Holdings** `[S36][S81]`.
3. **Products:** corporate/business banking, **foreign exchange**, fleet management/vehicle finance, hospitality/travel-related FX, equipment finance, and some retail banking; niche rather than broad retail `[S35]`.
4. **Digital:** business/corporate digital banking and FX platform; limited mass-retail digital. **Digital maturity [EST] — not mass-market.**
5. **Scale:** **total assets ~R12bn; total equity ~R2.4bn; ~713 staff; ~140 branches/outlets** (2024, [EST] via aggregator) `[S35]`.
6. **Distribution:** ~140 outlets plus corporate/relationship channels; FX bureaux network is a distinctive asset `[S35]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** Bidvest announced intent to sell (Jul 2024); **Access Bank Plc agreed to acquire 100% for ~R2.3bn (US$133m)** (agreement Dec 2024; long-stop extended Feb 2026) `[S36][S81]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** small, niche, in ownership transition → acquisition integration risk and possible customer churn; strong **FX/fleet** niche a NeoBank could target with cheaper digital FX. `[S36]`

## 4. TymeBank → GoTyme Bank *(DIGITAL BANK — flag)*

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Tyme Bank Limited** (now branded **GoTyme Bank**) — full commercial banking licence (Banks Act), locally controlled `[S2][S38]`. SA's first **majority black-owned** bank and first new full banking licence since 1999 [EST] `[S41]`.
2. **Ownership:** part of **Tyme Group** (global; also operates in the Philippines as GoTyme and in Vietnam). Major shareholders: **African Rainbow Capital (ARC, Patrice Motsepe) ~40% majority**, **Tencent**, **Nubank** (US$150m investment → unicorn at US$1.5bn), British International Investment, M&G Catalyst Fund, Apis Growth Fund II, Norrsken22, Gokongwei Group `[S41][S39]`.
3. **Products:** transactional account, **savings** (goal-save, fixed), **personal loans**, **funeral cover/insurance**, **business banking** (via partnership), money transfer; card (Mastercard). No home loans. `[S38]`
4. **Digital:** app-first, no legacy branches; onboarding via **self-service kiosks in Pick n Pay / Boxer stores and TFG** ("phygital" distribution). Rebranded to **GoTyme Bank** with a new app (launched 22 Jan 2026; same licence, migrated balances) `[S40][S41]`.
5. **Scale (FY2025, 30 Jun):** see Tyme Bank Limited AFS `[S38]`. Group metrics: **22m+ customers in SA + Philippines; ~500k new customers/month; ~10m SA** (approx) `[S39][S41]`. **TymeBank reached profitability (Dec 2023)** and a **US$1.5bn unicorn valuation (2024)** `[S41]`.
6. **Distribution:** **kiosk + retail partner** model (Pick n Pay, Boxer, TFG) plus app — the defining "phygital" play; no traditional branch network `[S38][S41]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** Nubank investment & unicorn (2024) `[S41]`; **rebrand to GoTyme Bank (Jan 2026)** `[S40][S41]`; **Sanlam received approval to distribute transactional banking products via GoTyme (Sep 2026)** — a major bancassurance/distribution alliance `[S75]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** closest direct competitor. Weaknesses: (a) **no home loans / limited credit depth** (low CLR, conservative lending) `[S38]`; (b) reliance on **third-party retail partners/kiosks** for acquisition — partner risk; (c) average balance/revenue per customer lower than incumbents; (d) newly rebranded → brand-consistency risk. The NeoBank's edge: richer credit/savings products and an own-distribution or better partnership economics. `[S38][S41]`

## 5. Discovery Bank *(DIGITAL / shared-value bank — flag)*

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Discovery Bank Limited**, subsidiary of **Discovery Limited** (JSE: DSY); commercial bank (Banks Act); licence granted 2016/2017, launched to public 2019 `[S2][S44]`.
2. **Ownership:** **wholly owned by Discovery Limited** `[S44]`. Group assets span health, life, short-term insurance and the **Vitality** shared-value platform (now ~54m lives globally) `[S43]`.
3. **Products:** transactional accounts, **credit card**, **savings**, **fixed deposits**, **personal loans**, **home loans** (via partnership), vehicle finance; integrated with **Vitality Money** (behaviour-linked interest/discounts) `[S42][S43]`.
4. **Digital:** app-only, "super app"-style; **Vitality Money** dynamic pricing is the differentiator — clients' rates/rewards improve with financial behaviour `[S42][S43]`.
5. **Scale (FY2026, 30 Jun):** **first full-year profit of R370m** (vs R68m loss FY25); **clients +26% to 1.57m**; **accounts +26% to 3.77m**; **retail deposits +17% to R27.2bn**; advances +40% to R12.9bn; revenue R3.1bn (+31%) `[S43][S42]`. Deposits R23.3bn at Jun 2025 (+26%) `[S42]`.
6. **Distribution:** digital-only (no branches); cross-sell through Discovery's insurance/Vitality ecosystem `[S43]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** reached **1.09m clients two years early** (Mar 2025) `[S43]`; **first full-year profit FY2026** `[S43]`; deepening AI-driven engagement `[S43]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** affluent/mass-affluent focus (not low-income mass market); success depends on the **Vitality ecosystem** (a NeoBank without a rewards ecosystem must win on price/simplicity); home loans rely on partners. The NeoBank can target **non-Discovery, price-sensitive, non-affluent** segments it under-serves. `[S43]`

## 6. Old Mutual Bank — OM Bank Limited *(DIGITAL-FIRST — flag)*

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **OM Bank Limited** (could not use "Old Mutual" in the bank name due to regulatory requirements); commercial bank (Banks Act), licensed **April 2024** under Section 16; locally controlled `[S2][S45][S94]`.
2. **Ownership:** **wholly owned subsidiary of Old Mutual Limited** (JSE: OMU), one of Africa's oldest financial-services groups `[S45][S47]`.
3. **Products:** debit card (from R4.95/month), transactional account, **savings**, **credit facilities**, and integration with Old Mutual insurance/investment/wealth `[S45][S46]`.
4. **Digital:** digital-first app bank; leverages Old Mutual's **branch network and ecosystem** for distribution/servicing `[S46]`.
5. **Scale:** **surpassed 1m customers within ~1 year of public launch** (Sep 2025 launch → ~Sep 2026); targets **break-even in 2028** `[S46]`. Migrated **~400 000 existing Money Account** clients into OM Bank `[S46]`.
6. **Distribution:** digital-first + Old Mutual branch/agent network + Money Account conversion funnel `[S46]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** licence Apr 2024; trial with ~600 staff; soft launch 2025; **public launch Sep 2025**; 1m customers by 2026 `[S45][S46]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** earliest-stage of the new banks → limited product range, thin credit book, profitability still years away; a **fresh-entrant with a big-parent safety net** that will compete for the same mass-market/price-sensitive segments — the NeoBank must differentiate on product depth and execution speed. `[S45][S46]`

## 7. Bank Zero Mutual Bank *(DIGITAL MUTUAL BANK — flag)*

1. **Legal entity / licence:** **Bank Zero Mutual Bank** — **mutual bank** licence (Mutual Banks Act 124 of 1993), licence no. **MB0001**; regulated by the Prudential Authority `[S3][S49]`.
2. **Ownership:** mutual/owner-operator model (no external shareholders) founded by ex-FNB executives; **being acquired by Lesaka Technologies for ~R1.1bn** (68–100%), with founders taking Lesaka shares `[S48][S49]`.
3. **Products:** fee-free transactional account, **Mastercard debit card**, savings, business accounts; no credit initially `[S49]`.
4. **Digital:** fully app-driven, no branches, no website banking legacy — "bank in an app" `[S49]`.
5. **Scale:** **customers not disclosed** `[S49]`. Reported to have **turned profitable** and moved into the "profitable neobank club" (2026) [EST via trade press] `[S48]`.
6. **Distribution:** digital-only; the Lesaka deal is explicitly about adding **distribution and scale** (Lesaka's merchant/consumer fintech base ~2m customers) `[S48]`.
7. **Strategic moves 2023–2026:** Lesaka announced acquisition (~US$67m / R1.1bn) Jun 2025; Competition Tribunal approval late 2025; awaiting **Prudential Authority approval**, completion expected H2 2026 `[S48]`.
8. **→ Attack surface:** sub-scale standalone; fee-free model proven but distribution-limited → the **Lesaka combination creates a serious fee-free challenger with embedded distribution**; a NeoBank must watch this vector closely. `[S48]`

## 13. Institutions in transition / curatorship

- **Status:** placed under **curatorship in 2022** (KPMG appointed curator) after the SARB found an unacceptable capital-adequacy ratio; ownership sat in a **Teba Trust** (NUM + Minerals Council) `[S54]`.

## 15. Tier-2 cross-cutting observations

1. **Three genuine neobank archetypes already exist and are profitable/profitable-adjacent:** Tyme/GoTyme (kiosk+retail, now Sanlam-backed), Discovery (Vitality ecosystem), Bank Zero→Lesaka (fee-free + distribution) `[S41][S43][S48]`.
2. **Distribution is the battleground:** kiosks (Tyme), Vitality/insurance ecosystem (Discovery), merchant/consumer fintech base (Lesaka), retail stores (Pepkor), insurer base (Sanlam) `[S41][S43][S48][S73][S75]`.
3. **Consolidation is clearing the field** (Sasfin out, Ubank out, Grindrod absorbed, Bidvest acquired) and **entry is accelerating** (OM Bank, Pepkor, Sanlam) — the NeoBank must have a **sharp wedge**, not a generalist "better bank".

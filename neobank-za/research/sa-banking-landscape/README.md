### Evidence-grade legend (used throughout)

- **[FACT]** — Directly stated in a primary source (regulator, bank annual/interim report, official SENS/RNS announcement) or a reputable secondary source that mirrors it.
- **[EST]** — Estimate, rounded figure or derived number (e.g. converted currency, "circa", figure from a non-primary aggregator). Treat as directional.
- **[UNKNOWN]** — Cannot be verified from a usable source. Recorded explicitly as `not verified — needs primary source (annual report FY__)` rather than guessed.
- **→ Attack surface** — Analytical inference by the NeoBank strategy team (not a sourced fact); clearly marked so it is never mistaken for evidence.

> **Golden rule applied in this pack:** no number is invented. Where a figure could not be verified, the text says so explicitly.

## 3. Methodology

1. **Regulator-first sourcing.** The authoritative backbone is the SARB Prudential Authority's published registers of licensed institutions and the Corporation for Deposit Insurance (CODI) member-bank list `[S1][S2][S3][S4]`, plus the SARB's licensing framework document `[S5]`. This establishes *what actually exists* and *under which licence class*, before any commercial narrative is layered on top.
2. **Primary financial statements.** Scale metrics are taken from each institution's own annual/interim results (JSE SENS, LSE RNS, or published AFS/Integrated Reports), e.g. `[S8][S14][S18][S22][S24]`. Financial years differ (see table below), so figures are always labelled with the period.
3. **Cross-checking.** Where a primary figure is only available through an aggregator (e.g. Wikipedia/Kiwix, CreditDeals, Grokipedia-derived trade lists), the claim is downgraded to **[EST]** and the aggregator is disclosed.
4. **Change-tracking 2023–2026.** Strategic moves are captured from official press releases, results commentary and reputable South African financial media, with dates.
5. **Attack-surface analysis.** The "→ Attack surface" notes are the NeoBank team's synthesis and are deliberately separated from evidence.

## 4. Regulatory grammar (the four licence classes)

South African deposit-taking is licensed under three Acts, producing the licence classes below `[S5][S6]`. The NeoBank's own licence route matters enormously for its product roadmap and capital requirements.

| Licence class | Governing Act | Examples (2025–2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial bank — locally controlled** | Banks Act 94 of 1990 | Absa, Standard Bank, FirstRand, Nedbank, Capitec, African Bank, Bidvest, Discovery, Investec Bank, OM Bank, Sasfin (until 30 Jun 2026), Tyme/GoTyme, Ubank (curatorship) `[S2]` | Full banking licence; can raise retail deposits, lend, issue cards |
| **Commercial bank — foreign-controlled subsidiary** | Banks Act | Access Bank (SA), Albaraka Bank, Habib Overseas Bank, HBZ Bank `[S4]` | Locally incorporated, foreign parent |
| **Local branch of a foreign bank** | Banks Act | Bank of China, Bank of Taiwan, BNP Paribas, Bank of Communications, China Construction Bank, Citibank, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs, HSBC, JP Morgan Chase, Standard Chartered, State Bank of India `[S1]` | No separate local capital; not a "banking group" in SA |
| **Mutual bank** | Mutual Banks Act 124 of 1993 | Bank Zero, Finbond, GBS `[S3][S1]` | Member/community-oriented; simpler capital regime |
| **Co-operative bank** | Co-operative Banks Act 40 of 2007 | KSK, OSK (Orania), Ziphakamise, GIG (+1) `[S1][S6]` | Smallest; cannot be a "full-service" retail bank |

**CODI (deposit insurance) coverage:** all registered banks are automatically members; the insured limit is **R100 000 per qualifying depositor per bank** `[S1]`. This cap is a *neutraliser* — a NeoBank can offer the same R100k statutory protection as the incumbents.

## 5. Market structure at a glance

- Banking in South Africa is **highly concentrated**: the largest institutions control the large majority of banking assets and deposits `[S90]`. Standard Bank Group is Africa's largest bank by assets (R3.6trn, 31 Dec 2025) `[S8]`.
- The regulator's live registers show **14 commercial banks, 3 mutual banks, ~5 co-operative banks and 12 local branches of foreign banks** as CODI members `[S1]`; the Prudential Authority separately lists locally controlled banks (13, incl. one under curatorship) `[S2]`.
- **Consolidation & churn is active:** Sasfin surrendered its banking licence (effective 30 Jun 2026) `[S50][S51]`; Ubank remains under curatorship after African Bank acquired its assets `[S54][S55]`; Grindrod Bank was absorbed into African Bank `[S52][S53]`; Bidvest Bank is being acquired by Nigeria's Access Bank `[S36][S81]`; Lesaka is acquiring Bank Zero `[S48]`.
- **New supply is arriving:** OM Bank (Old Mutual) launched publicly Sep 2025 `[S46]`; Pepkor obtained Prudential Authority approval (Nov 2025) and filed its Section 16 application (Mar 2026) `[S73][S74]`; Sanlam received approval to distribute transactional banking via GoTyme `[S75]`.
- **The foreign-bank retail retreat continues**, while some capital moves *into* Africa-focused corporate/investment banking `[S61]`.

## 6. How the NeoBank should use this pack

1. **Licence benchmarking** — compare the NeoBank's chosen licence route against the class definitions in §4 and the capital/deposit powers each class confers `[S5]`.
2. **Direct threat mapping** — Capitec (mass-market digital), Tyme/GoTyme (digital + kiosk/retail), Discovery + OM Bank (shared-value/ecosystem), Bank Zero → Lesaka (fee-free + distribution) are the closest analogues; the Big Five are the incumbents to be attacked on fees, onboarding friction and legacy-channel cost `[S24][S38][S43][S45][S48]`.
3. **Attack surfaces** — see each institution file's "→ Attack surface" bullets and the synthesis in `institution-matrix.md`.
4. **Evidence discipline** — before any figure enters an investor deck, replace every **[EST]** with a primary source and resolve every **[UNKNOWN]**.

## 7. Known limitations

- Several Tier-2 banks (Albaraka, HBZ, Habib Overseas, Standard Chartered SA branch, Bidvest) do not publish granular retail metrics in easily accessible form; those cells are marked **[UNKNOWN]/[EST]**.
- Aggregator sources (Wikipedia/Kiwix, Grokipedia, CreditDeals, Neobanks.guide) are used only where a primary source was not retrievable, and are flagged.
- The CODI member list `[S1]` and the PA registers `[S2]`/`[S3]` are snapshots that lag real-time licensing decisions (e.g. Sasfin exit, Bank Zero acquisition); where a register and a dated announcement conflict, the dated announcement is preferred and the conflict is noted.
- Currency conversions and USD comparisons (e.g. African Business Top 100 Banks) are **not** used as primary scale evidence.
